## Bringing Bad News

Winter passed all too quickly this year.  After the travails of the previous summer, the peaceful idyll we had seemed so short.

My fortune to have such a lovely, loving and lovable wife has always amazed me.  But now I have my darling son Caradoc who is so full of joy that it overflows to those he is with.  Arthur, too, is a source of joy for me.  He is so level-headed for such a young stripling.  Although he has a fine temper, it rarely shows itself - truly a wondrous thing for a future king.  And, to my amazement, I am also glad to have the company of Morgaine.

I had been totally unsettled by her during the first Winter of her fosterage.  She appeared to be like a younger Jeanne.  Although young in mien, she was so settled, so ... adult-like.  Unfortunately, my attempts to be companionable and fatherly must have seemed strained.  But just before adventuring that year, I thought that my problem seemed to be settling itself.  Now, I find that I am comfortable with treating like another adult.  She is quiet and also level-headed, like Arthur, and she has an underlying current of fire, like Arthur.  Thankfully, she has forgiven my foolishness of the previous Winter and we spend some times of an evening playing the harp.  She seems to have some ability with it, so I am tutoring her.

Raven's Gate, too, seemed to be growing more comfortable.  There were more people residing here now.  In preparation for my plans of building a larger castle from the remains of Broad, more servants and craftsmen and men-at-arms were arriving.  Also, to protect the things dearest to my heart, 10 knights had been gathered to my bastion.  With this many knights, I called for Sir Gilbert to be my Knight Commander at Raven's Gate.  This would give Sir Gilbert some purpose, give my knights a good commander and give Sir Edar a rest from the ongoing strain of having the previous Steward of Marlborough (and his wife) always there to constrain him.

It was but 6 days after the Feast of Beltane when a servant found me with a stonemason with whom I was discussing the plans for the new fortress.  He told me of the arrival of _[[010-Knights-of-Arthurian-Britain#Sir Arcavius|Sir Arcavius]]_.  Excusing myself, I strode purposefully out to greet my new adventuring companion.

Arcavius was outside the gate with his squire and some monks.  How Christian of him, adventuring with his own prelate.  Chuckling to myself, I bid Arcavius welcome to Raven's Gate and bid him enter.  But then he strangely declined and entreated that we discuss this matter outside of the gates.  Strange request indeed, but ... ah well.

Ordering some servants to bring out some food and wine, I received my guests in a veritable early morn outdoor meal. Arcavius had come with his squire, _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^70a562|Gessius]]_, and a monk named _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^77e1aa|Father Bawdwin]]_ who mysteriously announced to the world that he was no relation to Bishop Bawdwin of Briton (who was a high-ranking Christian priest, I think).

They had come to request my aid to investigate some disturbances in a village named Medbourne, which was closer to Lincoln than to Sarum.  A fair distance north.  The monk kept up a most impassioned speech about his devil being loose in the village, but he failed to explain precisely what was happening up there.  This was not impressing me at all, until Arcavius told me of the same dream that he and his squire had on the way to Raven's Gate.

The dream was of a large serpent which went to a village where it circled widdershins around the village, before it stopped and raised its head up high whereupon it turned into walls around the village and a tower above it.  A gate opened and many smaller serpents exited and went out into the surrounding forest.  These smaller serpents then returned with animals and objects and babies in their mouths.  All this sounded very strange, but the serpent circling widdershins .... I think I remember that has something to do with a powerful enchanted item ... one of those I had promised _[[Oberon|Oberon]]_ I would find, if I could.  This should be looked into.

Telling my guests to stay but a while, I called for my servants to prepare my adventuring accoutrements and for Sir Hywel, Nai, Buddfannyn and Tathyl to prepare for a journey.  Jeanne had just gone out hunting not an hour before, glad to spend some time out in the woods after spending the Winter indoors.  So I sounded a hunting horn, and went to give my farewells and hugs to my child and foster-children.  Soon enough, Jeanne returned and took matters in hand.  The time we knew would happen was here, and we parted regretfully.

## An Audience with Earl Robert

Our party left and rode to Sarum.  I requested an audience with Earl Robert from Sir Jaradan, and was soon admitted to the Earl.  I advised him I was going to be adventuring up north.  My plan was to investigate the supposed disturbance in Medbourne.  He was surprised as he had been organizing a troop of knights to do the very same thing.  Earl Robert then granted me command of the troop of four knights and their squires to aid me in the venture.

Before proceeding, I discussed how we should proceed to Medbourne.  There were two main routes we could take - either up the West Trade Road or the East Trade Road.  Fr. Bawdwin had come south along the East Trade Road, managing to avoid Saxons a number of times on the way.  This would mean that there would be many delays if that Road was taken.  Delays that could prove fatal for many villagers if there were trouble in Medbourne.  So my decision is to first go to Levcomagus to enlist Sir Cynon in our mission.  Then to go to Cirencester, and up to Lambor Castle.  After we found a guide to take us through the Wilds of the Sauvage Forest, we would arrive at Medbourne.

## On the Road to Medbourne

Leaving the next morn and riding hard took us to Levcomagus.  Sir Cynon welcomed us right royally.  Unfortunately, he was still caught in his Stewardly duties, and he was duty-bound to remain behind.  (I still don't know whether he really was duty-bound, or he just gave that as an excuse to not to accompany Fr. Bawdwin who kept pestering him.)  So after a feast and a good night's rest, we rode hard again and spent the night at Mildenhall.  Before we were feasted by my steward and friend, Sir Edar, we were shown how much progress had been made on the hospice that I had ordered to be built.  I was pleased by the amount of work that had been done, and expected it to be completed over the summer.

The next day was an easy ride to the city of Wandborough, held by the Duke of Leicester.  After an uneventful night, we rode to the primary seat of the Duke of Clarence, the old Roman city of Cirencester.  On the way, after crossing the Isis River, we saw some standing stones on a mound off the road.  Hywel told us that that was Tor Barrow Hill, an ancient site which supposedly had a tough guardian protecting "Great Magic".  Maybe this was another place where the Fae Great Treasures.  Well ... we can find out when we return.

From Cirencester, we were now on the West Trade Road.  The next two days saw us travel through Burton to Longcompton.  Here we noticed many signs of arcane nature.  Hywel requested a halt, and advised me that this was a town of witches.  If we would stay here for a short while, he would find us a place to stay.  After a short while, Hywel returned with a couple of neatly dressed peasants.  I was not surprised to learn that they were willing to let us stay as I was the husband of Jeanne.  They led us into the village and we saw many signs of hex symbols, and everyone was healthy and clean.

Upon noticing the monk was getting upset, I took him aside and I told him in no uncertain terms that he would cause no trouble in this village.  He would not rest amongst this evil, as he called it.  If he wanted to stay outside in the cold, then he would stay outside.  It angered me that he would be so intolerable.  As he left the village, I went to the other knights and told them they would cause no trouble in this village or they would answer to me.  Thankfully for them, they don't.

Next morning, we find the monk outside on the road, and we ride on.  During the morning, we ride past a horse carved into the red clay hillside.  Hywel tells us of the Red Horse by baiting Arcavius with a short tale.  Passing through Kyneton, a part of the Earl of Wuerensis lands, we arrive at Lambor Castle at dusk.

We were welcomed by the _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^511c23|Baron of Lambor]]_, who was beholden to none, himself when he found out who I was.  The Baron had heard of some of the tales of the Battle of Lyndhurst where I and Brion had captured the Saxon standard and earned our knighthood's on the field of battle.  During the meal, I gave news of the last seasons events.  The Saxons had raided St. Albans, where Duke Ulfius had led his forces to fight them off.  The Steward of Levcomagus had been committing treason with the Saxons of Sussex, and had been deposed by forces under my command.  That _[[010-The-Seasons#^9a36d4|Sissa]]_, the son of the King of the Sussex Saxons, had been killed in single combat with me.  That a band of flesh-eaters and their giant master terrorizing Silchester had been killed by a band of knights under my command.

After dinner, the knights of the Castle are a rough but quiet bunch.  The Baron asks me what are our plans, and I tell him of our mission to investigate and eliminate the disturbance in Medbourne.  To do so, we have need of a guide.  The Baron tells me of a number of villagers of Medbourne have fled to Lambor, one of whom may help us.  We soon meet with an obvious woodsman named _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^2f9db0|Marcus]]_ who agrees to aid us after sizing us up.

The next morn, we leave the hospitality of the Baron riding our fine horses into the chill morning air.  Marcus takes us on a slightly northerly trail, which he says will take us in a round-about way to the tormented village.  But nigh on noontime, Marcus halted our progress and pointed out a young boy standing by the side of the track.  Our woods guide is quite timid for some reason, as he seems unwilling to continue.  Motioning for him to make way, I move my horse to the boy.  If the boy was being used by bandits as bait in a trap, then they would be no match for such a band of knights.

As I approach the unknown lad, he greets me by name!?  Although my surprise puts me back, I continue to converse.  Just as I begin to realize who it is, Merffyn tires of the game and declares himself as he changes back to his true (?) form.  He has learnt of my mission to investigate the disturbances of Medbourne, and has come to inform of the true nature of the blight afflicting the land.

Medbourne was the fief of Sir Stetorus for a number of years.  From his nearby manor-keep, he held suzerainty over the village of Medbourne.  He administered to his people hard, but in a fair manner.  In time, Sir Stetorus had a daughter quite fair, who came of age in body, if not in wisdom.  For this lovely lass strayed one Samahain Night to a defiled well, and after a time it was noticed that she was with child.  In its due time, she gave birth to a "child" named Gorbodok, but she died in child-birth.  This "child" grew prodigiously as the years passed.  After but a mere 7 years, this devil-child grew to manhood!  The grieving grandfather paid no heed to the demon that he called his grandchild.

After 2 more years passed, Sir Stetorus paid the forfeiture for his benightedness as Gorbodok killed him and burned down the village church of St. Helen of the Waters.  In the time since, Gorbodok has gathered an evil band of brigands to follow him; an evil band which has gathered many villains to build a new tower in Medbourne.  Merffyn avows we must show no mercy on any of them in stopping them as Gorbodok's plan is to gather villages of women and take them to the same well where his evil came from and to grow an army of more monsters just like him.  At the end of this dismal tale of woe and gloom, Merffyn vanishes.

Filled with new resolve, I ask Marcus if the trail leads directly to Medbourne.  After a while of silence, he closes his mouth and meekly agrees.  Truly, he must have difficulty dealing with humans .. poor lad.  Waving the rest of the party to follow, we ride on to the crest of the looming hill.  As we reach the pinnacle, we see laid before us the village of Medbourne.  Cleared land surrounds the village and Motte castle of the demon-child.  In the centre of the village, many men are working on constructing a tower ... men guarded by 5 brigands.

## Battle with Gorbodok

Although I am normally a peaceable sir, I was filled with a righteous wrath at this rising tower of evil.  It was with a shiver of shock that I dimly heard Arcavius counselling to give them a chance.  Recollections welled in my mind of similar words being used long ago ... by myself with Brion!  But even with the counsel freely given, I thought on what Merffyn had said about Gorbodok, his brigands and of my knowledge of battle.  We saw but 5 brigands of men-at-arms quality, that is if the brigands were at their best.  Undoubtedly there were many more nearby, and also Gorbodok and some higher quality warriors would arrive soon enough.  It would be best to attack and eliminate this bit of opposition as quickly as possible.

I begin to charge my destrier down the hillside, and I soon hear the rest of my knightly followers charging behind me.  As we roll down the hill, hooves pounding harder and louder, villagers begin to turn heads in our direction.  All to soon, the guards hear us charging nearer.  The villagers who are about to be run over suddenly awaken to their danger and leap into their hovels, knowing that blood was soon to flow and praying that theirs would not be among the flood to follow.  As we charged through the village, 12 more guards were appearing in various states of dress out of some of the hovels.  Yelling at them to submit or die, I spy a large brigand armed with sword, shield and spear and charge at him.  As I do so, I see that he is setting his spear butt on the ground so he can impale my horse - Gods rot his soul.

Pulling my horse up just in time, I find myself going over the left shoulder of my steed directly at the brigand.  Mithras aids the just, for he let me knock down my opponent and land on my feet, with only my shield grip loosened.  As I gripped my shield firmly, waiting for the brigand to rise and then let him have the first blow.  I use my shield to drive his swipe to the side and lay him open.  As he falls, I see that Arcavius has just swung a powerful strike which has cleaved his opponent in twain.  Looking further, I see we have killed four of these brigands and that the survivor is running away towards the nearby Motte castle.

Now the rest of the brigands arrive.  The mad swirl of battle settles on us all.  I come to my senses standing over some dead bodies with visions of ravens slowly vanishing from my sight.  Nearby, Arcavius also seems to be coming back to himself but he is still so tight-lipped that his mouth is outlined in white.  Battle is still raging, however. Hywel is standing over his squire, Tathyl, and is fighting two of the brigands at once.  Then, the sounds of horses coming sound.  Yelling at Arcavius to aid Hywel, I gather the surviving knights from Sarum and prepare for the brigands master, Gorbodok, to arrive.  After dispatching the 2 remaining bandits in an uncharacteristically ungraceful display by Hywel, Arcavius aids the limping Hywel to his horse, as he then also mounts.

Gorbodok is leading 3 knights in a charge at us, from the direction of the Motte castle.  We are of equal numbers, so honour will be met in single combats.  I charge at Gorbodok, and we meet near to where I fought the first big brigand.  I swing a hefty blow at him which just bounces of his armour, and I see him easily swinging his sword from alongside his horse in a rising blow and then a tearing, rending pain blotted the light from my eyes and I knew no more.

## Aid for Rhodri

It is only afterwards that I found out what occurred to everyone else after I was struck down.  Gorbodok slung my body over his horse and rode with me to the forest.  Arcavius and the others saw this, but could not follow until they dispatched their opponents.  Soon they are chasing the demon-child through the forest.  As they ride hard, they are separated by the speeds of their horses, and Arcavius knows I will be lost if he doesn't find me.  Calling on one of his Christian saints, the local St. Helen of the Waters to aid him, and something leads him to where I am.

Unfortunately, I was laying in a ring of standing stones, placed there by Gorbodok.  Gorbodok tried to beguile Arcavius, but Arcavius saw through his tricks, but after threats were exchanged Gorbodok stepped into the circle and he and my body vanished from sight, just as Hywel arrived.

With nothing else to do at the circle, the surviving members of the party regather at Medbourne.  The squires had gathered all dropped equipment, and had treated all wounds.  One of our companion knights was able-bodied, another was dead, and the remaining two required litters to travel.  Also there was to be a parley with the peasants which found they all wanted to travel away from the accursed place.  After organizing for travel for everyone to Lambor Castle, Hywel and Arcavius go in search of Merffyn.

These two stalwart friends go to seek the evil well which began the demon-child.  There they find Merffyn who tells them the well is an evil wound in the side of the Dragon.  It must be closed with the strength of faith.  When he is told of my capture, he also advises them that they need another friend's help.  Hywel knows that Merffyn speaks of the mysterious Lucienne of Avebury.  However, Arcavius believes the strength of faith to be a reference to _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^606b3d|Bishop Bawdwin of Briton]]_.  So they go to follow the trail back to Lambor Castle to ensure that the rest of the party arrived there.

Then the two split to go their separate ways.  Sixteen days passed before Arcavius returned with the militant prelate, where he finds Lucienne and Hywel waiting for him.  Getting Arcavius to delay the Bishop, Lucienne dives into the pool and soon appears out of it with me carried before him.

But I think I get ahead of myself now, for I remember quite vividly what happened to me after I awoke ...

## In Gorbodok's Realm

I was suddenly aware I was on the top of a extremely tall cliff.  The sky was a smoky kind of red, and smelled abysmal.  Storm clouds were crossing the sky, but they didn't cross all in the same direction, or at the same speed.  In the great distances below, I could barely see small figures moving; many, many, many small figures.  And it was then that I began to feel that something was behind me..  I started to feel something, maybe a sense of not wanting to look back at whatever it was, but it was something which angered me.  I spun around quickly, reaching for the sword I no longer had handy.

I saw before me an enormous black as basalt figure.  As large as Arcavius was, he would have been dwarfed by this creature.  My mind whirled at more than just the scenery.  The monster had four arms, each larger than a strong man's thighs, and its face was that of twisted goat.  This?!  This was the true form of Gorbodok!

Gloatingly, I was told that I had been brought to this Other Realm (oh so unlike the fair Fae kingdom I had seen) to be the commander of this gathered horde.  I was to train the creatures on the plain below to be an army.  As much as my pride and anger at this monster wanted me to say nay to it, I knew that I had no way back ... yet.  I had no weapon ... yet.  Battles and combats teach you many things, not least is the knowledge that there are times to attack, and other times when you must regroup before you can attack (or defend).

Gorbodok grabbed my arm, and suddenly we were right in front of the horde.  With the time of decision at hand, I knew I had to do something with these ... well ... dumb-looking horse-sized beasts.  I could not train these things to attack purposefully at the land Arthur would soon rule, so I must hamper their training as much as I can get away with.  Turning to Gorbodok, I declared that the first thing an army needs to learn is discipline, and the Romans showed that the best way to learn discipline was to learn how to march.  And after but a single moment, I saw that I had been given a gift by Mithras.  These beasts had no concept of left or right ... in fact, I would not doubt they had no concept of anything except food, fighting and ....

A long time was spent achieving no results.  Well, just by thinking about it, there should have been a long time passing but nothing had changed.  I had begun to feel hungry but found that I wasn't feeling hungry - I was feeling I should be hungry.  Gorbodok's patience wore thin with no apparent results with the training, and he grabbed me again and I found we were in a plush ... palace.  Ornate as the walls and cushions were, I found I had no taste for this place.  When it noticed I was interested in the doorway leading out of the room, Gorbodok offered me freedom to explore as much as I wanted.

And so I began, but almost immediately I seemed to hear in my mind, or was it just a memory of something I heard by someone I knew, the words "Look with your heart; not with your head."  Not having the patience to think this through, I go looking around for something which I can use to escape.  No matter how much I looked I could not find anything useful.  I found much riches and much ruin, but neither were of interest to me.

"Time" was a mystery here.  Gorbodok came and fetched me to train his forces, and I would get them marching or fighting each other.  It was as if they forgot what I had tried to teach them before .. if they were the same group.  Even fighting each other seemed pointless for no matter how damaged they appeared, they still got up afterwards.  I felt no hunger, no need for sleep and no need to visit any midden.  In fact, the only two things I felt consistently were the wish not to be here, and the wish to be at home.

Once I noticed some sort of swirl on the horizon.  Since this is something different, I get on one of these beasts and order everything to march in that direction.  It wasn't really marching, but it certainly covered the distance.  I eventually found myself at the bottom of a large pinnacle of rock.  The beasts wouldn't approach near it, so I set them to running around the pinnacle.  I thought that I had found the way out for above the pinnacle, was what looked to be an upside-down waterspout.  So I climbed up the slope to the shale and on up to the vertical sides, until I reached the very peak.  At this point I come to realize I have no idea of how to reach that "water-spout"; and the fact that I have a feeling that there is something I am supposed to do or find here.  Then Gorbodok appears.

No sooner than I can notice the fiend, then we are back in his "palace".  "Do you think you can leave here, mortal?" taunts Gorbodok.  How dare he!  I am no-one to be made fun of!  I am not to be denied my wife.  Nor am I to be denied the new Roman Empire!  Before I can think past my wrath, the bastard vanishes, leaving me to force my ire down.

Needing to calm down, I begin to pray to Mithras to aid my temper and give me my justice.  However, as soon as I begin to do so, Gorbodok returns in his own rage.  "Do not ever do that here ... in MY realm!"  Whereupon he vanishes again.  Obviously, he fears the justice from Mithras.  Therefore, I must continue and give him no respite!

But I must calm myself, run through some of the calming Mithraic chants.  Already, I feel myself beginning to relax, to prepare myself for the chants when I seem to hear ... music?

## Music of the Harp

Jumping up, I try to look around, straining my ears to catch that sound again.  But I don't hear anything else.  And after another indeterminate period of time, I begin to accept I won't hear it and again try to focus myself ... and I hear it again.  I will not be distracted!  Focus ... Calm .... relax ...The music.

I can hear the music .. the tune.  But only when I'm composing my thoughts, when I'm relaxed.  I can recognize the tune, too.  It's a harp tune.  It's the same piece that Hywel played at the Fae Feast last year; some otherworldly Fae tune that Hywel played for the Fae lady he was with.... It's louder over here.

The melody begins to fade as my excitement rises.  I am doing something!  But I must calm myself to follow this "trail".  And so, from room to hallway to room to hallway to room I travailed.  Just like the endless plain that had lain beneath the cliff, so I spent "time" relaxing as I sauntered through this endless palace.  Never tiring nor feeling hunger.  Until "eventually", I found the room where the song was playing.

This particular room had it's far corners ceiling collapsed with wreckage strewn from that gaping hole.  The music was coming from under some of the beams and dirt.  Moving some of the debris, I found a normal-sized 3' harp.  It was battered and had no strings on it, yet I could still hear music coming from it.

Now that I hold it, the music begins to fade.  Not knowing exactly what I am doing, I try to play the tune I had been hearing on this harp with no strings.  As I play, I reflect on my deep, abiding love for my Jeanne and through that love is a sharp sword of grief at our separation.  I don't know how long I played, or even how I continued to play through that intense, bitter-sweet, intermingling blaze of love and grief.  As I finish the final chords, I am finally aware that something has changed in everything around me.  It doesn't look any different, but I know something has changed drastically.  Wrung out inside, I knew that it was time for me to go home.

## A Downcast Devil

Carrying the harp back the way I came, I enter the original chamber and see Gorbodok kneeling in the middle of the room with both sets of hands crossed before him and with downcast eyes.  I know I should be surprised, if not shocked, by what I'm seeing, but I can't muster that up in me.  I should be shocked for I understand precisely what Gorbodok is going through.  He is totally abject for he feels the total loss of love, and he has the never-ending grief of having no-one to turn to.

Going to Gorbodok's kneeling figure, I look up at his teary, downcast eyes and tell him to take me to where I can leave his realm and emotions that are raging in him will evanesce away.  Slowly, getting to his feet, our surroundings change from an opulent palace to the bare peak that I had climbed earlier.  Still with tears pouring down his face, Gorbodok bids me to go and never return.  With a gladdening heart at my imminent return to my love, I see Gorbodok vanish for the last time.

After a while standing at the top of the pinnacle, looking up at the upside-down whirlpool, trying to think how I can get from here to there, I begin to see lightning and hear cracks of thunder from that whirlpool.  It builds in intensity, and rage, until an earth-shattering kaboom signals the finale of the sky show.  Picking myself up, and looking back up I can just see a bird coming down out of the whirlpool, and come spiralling down.  A bird which is getting bigger and bigger as it gets closer and closer.  It must be something big like ... like Gorbodok.  But almost as soon as I realize that, I can see that it is red ... like Lucienne was when we fought together!

## On Lucienne's Wings

Lucienne lands heavily nearby.  Feeling so happy that soon I would be continuing back home to Jeanne, I beamed at Lucienne.  Stomping to me, he grabs me fiercely by the arm and then says something confusing about always causing trouble?  Well, whatever.  Before I could say anything, he hunched down and leapt straight up and flexed his wings in great flails of his wings.  A good thing that being startled, I instinctively clutched my sword hand and didn't drop the harp.

Flying in sharp spirals up, we enter the whirlpool and enter a murky, loud, lightning-split maelstrom.  My breath is taken from me in one more shock, but in the next I am enjoying this thrilling, exciting ride.  No horse ride has ever been this exhilarating.  All to soon, we pass through the eye of the maelstrom, and through the surface of a rotten, foul-smelling, spouting spring.  Nearby stands an open-mouthed Edar and Hywel.

As Lucienne lands us, he whispers loudly to my ear "That's one more favour you owe! .... Lady Leda sends her greetings."  Returning the salutations, I am still caught in the exhilaration of the flight and my overwhelming desire to go home when I realize that I have said something which has wounded Lucienne in some way.  Unfortunately, I cannot recall what I have said but I know it is not right for me to ask what I have said to trouble him so.  Hywel welcomes me back and informs me that Arcavius has gone to distract Bishop Bawdwin. (Bawdwin ... here?)  So if Lucienne and I would follow him to a hidden copse of trees.

We are there but a short while, when Arcavius and the Bishop arrive with some servants bearing some Christian things.  The Bishop looks like a gaunt, non-military man with a military bearing.  Doing "Christian" ritual things, I guess.  Whatever he did, it sure had spectacular results.

The spring closes, but it looks to me like a blockage at the top of a gateway.  I can see the Bishop looking smug at what he's done, but I can also see Hywel and Lucienne are backing up faster and faster.  Barely moments later, a violent eruption arose before me where the spring used to be.  Yelling for the people by the spring to run, I saw the Bishop bravely scrambling for his Relics while all his helpers were running away.  Thankfully for the Bishop's life, Arcavius halts those attendants and sends them back to help him.  Bare seconds after the small crowd of Christians has left the scene, the largest noisy outburst yet happens.  Picking ourselves up, all that remains of the spring is a deep, dry crater with an odorous, sulphurous smell of hell wafting from it.

After such a conclusion, the rest seemed to be spiritless.  The Bishop gathered his relics and servants and headed east through the forest to Lonazep, and then home.  I just want to go home to Jeanne, so we head west back to Lambor Castle.  Marcus is still timid.  He should learn to keep his mouth closed, though ... something might fly in if he leaves it open like that.  He doesn't even prattle like he did before.

## A Little Harp Music

We reached Lambor Castle that evening, just in time for the day's feast.  Preparing for the feast, I planned to use my travel harp after the feast and tell the story of how the beast of Medbourne was brought low.  But ... I don't know, I took the harp I had brought from Gorbodok's realm out of the bag I was carrying it in and looked at it.  Mithras!  It was the most exquisite harp I had ever seen.  It was golden and seemed strung with gossamer ... it was just so ... Fae.  But I would probably cause trouble bringing that to the feast.  So I put the harp away.

After the feast, I sent Buddfannyn for my harp.  When he returned with the travel harp and I was tuning it, I felt for the first time as if I had lost my affinity with it.  It felt lifeless ... dead ... just a piece of wood.  As musical as the sound of swords clashing.  For once I think I will indulge myself; I call on Buddfannyn to return my harp to my room and bring the bag I had brought back with me.  Meanwhile, I await my turn to provide the entertainment.  I obviously didn't tell the story aright, for the retelling of the adventure wasn't received with rapt attention.

Finally Buddfannyn returns with my new harp.  Eagerly I draw the harp from its cocoon.  But before I can begin to play, Sir Hywel and Arcavius wish to speak with me straight-away.  They try to gull me with some tall tale that it would be perilous if I were to play the harp. Arcavius kept wanting me to play my travel harp, obviously something had been done to it.  That must be why it doesn't feel like an extension of my fingers anymore.  As they continue to vex me, Hywel asks me to play a song for him.  He begins to hum a tune, which I think is familiar.  After but a couple of notes, I realize I know the tune, but I just can't seem to place the name of it.  I begin playing, and find myself lost in the music.  When I come to the natural end and am aware of myself again, I look around in amazement at the sudden silence.

Everyone is asleep where they were.  Servants have fallen to the floor with their trays.  Baron Lambor and his knights are all in various postures in their seats.  Arcavius is asleep on the floor, looking quite uncomfortable.  Hywel has managed to fall asleep seated between two servant girls?!  Even the dogs are heavy with sleep.

Arcavius and Hywel were right!  I had doubted my friends.  I had endangered everyone ... what could have happened?  My thoughts were in a whirl, in their own whirlpool going down ... maybe I had better go to sleep.  Yes ... that sounds the thing to do.  I reeled to my room and went to sleep on my bed amidst the eerie silence of a castle.

Next morn I awoke early, but I still lay abed, thinking.  The harp was a Fae thing.  An awe-inspiring instrument.  But it was a danger to everyone around me.  It was with shock that I realized my hand had reached under the bed and was opening the bag ... but I hadn't wanted to do that!  What if I played at Chalke Castle!  Mithras!  I could not let anything endanger Arthur, nor Jeanne or Caradoc.  What must I do?  Do ...

It was a Fae harp ... I should return it to the Fae.  On our return trip, I could call on Puck and get him to return it.  It seemed that was the right decision for the castle slowly seemed to come awake.

Soon Nai was up to get me ready for the day.  As he dressed me, I bade him pack away our train and ready our horses for our return home.  I went down to see Lord Lambor.  He was much put out with what had happened.  I ask for his pardon for showing him what I had recovered from Gorbodok's realm.  With all the grace he could muster, Baron Lambor bid me forget it, but he thought it would be best if I travelled home.  Understanding fully, I made no comment but bade him fare well.  When I saw Hywel and Arcavius I told them prepare for our departure.  Hywel made no comment but Arcavius did so sullen-faced.

## Angus the Bard

We travelled south along the King's Road, but each morning arrived and I hadn't stepped out into the woods to call on Puck.  It was not until we were between Cirencester and Wandborough that Arcavius and Hywel came to me to return the harp to the Fae.  They seemed determined to continue on and on, but I said it was to be done that night at camp.  And that night, I led Arcavius and Hywel into the woods.  Arcavius seemed a bit apprehensive, and seemed astonished when I started calling out for Puck.  He shouldn't have been for Puck appeared in his small glowing spritely form.

He was happy to see me.  I knew instinctively who to give the harp to.  _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^468229|Angus]]_ the Fae Bard was who I should return this to.  So I requested he please lead me to Angus.  As he led us deeper into the forest, he talked of how everybody was charmed by what had happened at my last performance.  My heart had been breaking in two - how could I bring myself to let go of Jeanne ... the harp I meant - oh, what did I mean anymore.  I had to let go of it for it was dangerous to Arthur.

We must have passed members of the Fae court, but it was all a blur to me.  Before I was ready, if I was ever going to be ready, Angus appeared before my eyes.  His mellifluous voice rolled over me in greeting.  I told him I had something of his and opened the bag and drew out the harp.  Looking Angus in the eyes, locking my gaze with his, I strove inside myself to save Arthur.  I released the harp into his hands and felt my heart tear itself apart.  I must have been still astir but I can't recall what I did or what happened, but I must have remembered not to be there when Angus played his harp in celebration or we would be trapped there in the Fae realm by the music.   
We travelled home and I was overcome with joy on my return for I was home and with Jeanne again.

At the end of the summer, the signs of Jeanne's oncoming pregnancy were starting to become obvious to even me.  I felt that Jeanne, the children and our household should tour slowly through our holdings while Jeanne was still able to travel comfortably.  At Sarum, I told the Earl all that had happened at Medbourne, and at Lambor.  Also, I got to spend some time with my radiant, gorgeous wife at the court of Earl Robert.  We travelled on to Levcomagus and visited Sir Cynon and spent a couple of days enjoying his hospitality and then travelled to Mildenhall and Marlborough.  It was here that we found that Aneurin was with child as well, and was but two months from birthing.  And so the summer drew to a close ....
