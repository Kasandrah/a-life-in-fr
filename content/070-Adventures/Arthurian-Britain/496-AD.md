## Mid-February - Late Winter

### A Task from Merffyn

As I entertained my beloved milady during a fine winter's morn, one of the young stableboys, _Cedric_, sped into the hall and skidded to a halt just within.  He awaited with great agitation until he was beckoned forward and then gasped out that a stranger was approaching Raven's Gate through the deep snow.

This alone is of interest, as surely someone travelling during the dead of winter must have some truly compelling reason to suffer so.  Also, if the stranger did not come to attack, then the stranger would bring news and fresh entertainment into the hold.

However, the further news that the stranger was approaching in the company of a large wolf made this most interesting.  I knew of only one person who had a wolf companion.  A person of moment and renown (infamy to many including my wife).  _[[Merlin|Merffyn]]_!  He who had made Stonehenge.  Merffyn!  He who had the Future King, the little lad Arthur, son of Uther Pendragon, under his protection.

I have mixed feelings for the man.  When I first met him, he seemed fascinating, captivating.  Was it only last summer that he guested in our camp, and shared bread?  Hmmm, that shooting star that night was a sign of momentous times.  For surely a Saxon invasion deserved such a herald.

The second meeting was when he wanted to meet me in Cornwall.  That was my first meeting with the small Fae creature nomened _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^23db36|Puck]]_.  A truly amazing creature he is too.  At once amusing and vexing....  Ah yes.  That Fae led me through a most strange and wondrous path, into a tree and on a Ley line' to Tintagel.  There Merffyn showed me a child.  Such a child, though. I knew he was my King.  I felt it, deep in my soul.  A message from Mithras, my god of truth.  All felt right.  But even as I swore to myself, to Mithras, and even to Merffyn, that I would follow my King when he would lead, and to protect him until he could, I felt a little disquiet within me about Merffyn.  I admit to myself and Mithras I did feel great pride that Merffyn was entrusting this knowledge of my King to me, to involve me in his defence.

But now I felt some feelings which weren't complimentary to Merffyn or to myself.  My beautiful wife ... could it be possible that we have only been married mere months? ... feels much anger and is most upset with Merffyn.  She too is capable of much that can gather tales like Merffyn, but she hasn't sought to make herself known.  Part of her feelings against Merffyn come from his actions of manipulating others.  Upon reflection, his manipulation of me becomes more apparent to me.  However, her feelings appear to have other causes as well.  My disquiet came partly from his manipulative ways, but mostly from the feelings that he aroused in my wife.  My wife should not have such ugly emotions forced on her.

Now Merffyn was here again.  And I knew he wanted me to do something.

Upon his arrival, I bade the man welcome.  I gave what I would give to anyone who did not come to fight - my hospitality.  I could see the clouds gathering in my love's frown, even as Merffyn was approaching our Keep.  Now they promised snow storms and bleak blizzards.

I thought he showed a smidgen of understanding when he left he his four-legged companion without.  Merffyn came to give me a task, I knew.  But such a task!  He brought word that the King must be moved from Tintagel to safety.  By boat even.  The King was in danger!  Of course I must help in this task.  But ... but I had said to my dear that I would not leave her this early.  Could I leave her?

My sworn duty was to my King, to safeguard him.  Especially in his time of need, when he could not even protect himself.  As these worries assailed me, Merffyn complimented my wife, or so I thought at first.  He made mention of how radiant my beloved looked, now she was my dear companion, and I couldn't help but look at her and feel some pride.  Even the glower she had was attractive.  But she was looking even more angry.  I felt a slight flush of anger at Merffyn, as it came to me that he was baiting my wife.  How dare he?  Jeanne looked even more angry when Merffyn asked why hadn't she told me.  Told me what?  Obviously, she would tell me when she felt the time was right.  I trusted her sensibilities.

I asked Merffyn if he would like to see to his companion for a little while, and Merffyn agreed to leave me to talk with my wife.  Although I knew it was what Merffyn wanted, I had to protect the King.  Protect the King!  Of course!  The safest possible place for the King ... the place I could protect him the most ... was here.  I knew of no more secure place than Raven's Gate.  But, I would not force this on my lady.  This task could only be fulfilled if my beloved thought it was something she thought was worth doing.

I spake with my wife on this matter.  At first, she could not agree as Merffyn was raising the child and she wanted nothing to do with Merffyn.  But, I thought on what would convince her.  Merffyn had care of the boy, and was teaching him everything.  If she believed that Merffyn was too manipulative, then she should ensure that Merffyn was not the only caretaker of the future King.  My Sweet acquiesced to this, after some thought.  I thought my arguments were most sage and germane, but she had another reason - one which I would only work out later.

I was about to ask Hywel to ask Merffyn to return, when I remembered that I had made him my vassal knight on my wedding day.  Even now, the memory of his expression on that occasion brought a twitch to my lips.  I looked around, and none of the servants were in sight.  Beginning to feel some irritation, I then thought that they knew my beloved well and most probably absented themselves when they saw how she was feeling about Merffyn.  I got up, and fetched Merffyn from the yard.

I agreed to help.  Merffyn bade me to bring my boon companions to aid me in this adventure.  I bade him to enjoy my hospitality while I went to fetch my companions, but he stated he had other matters to see to, but he would return in two weeks.  Frankly, I felt relief that he did not accept my hospitality, as I would not vouch for the safety of peace within the household if he had accepted.

Before I would go though, I had to speak to my wife.  She was still wroth with Merffyn.  I felt a sore need to take this anger from her.  I tried to find the source of this anger.  My Jeanne explained when I asked, how Merffyn followed another path of power.  One which was more manipulative of others than what Jeanne practiced.  There was another reason in the past, which my dear would not explain.  A kernel of anger was rekindled in me at Merffyn.  How dare he do this to my wife!

My beloved was with child!  I was to be a father!  I had asked what tidings Merffyn had alluded to.  My beloved was wroth that Merffyn had forced her hand to tell me these glad tidings, when she had wanted to tell me in a more private moment.  Ah, beloved, you have no need to hold anger on this cause, for I am eager for this word to be given to me again and again in a much closer circumstance.

For the first time, my beloved saw her husband ride off to begin an adventure.  Although my heart was brought low by this, I wished it would happen many times in the future as that would mean that I would return many times to my dear wife.  I also knew that my campaigning for this year was beginning early, but I must be home in the middle of summer in time to see my first born child being brought into the world, to be with my dear wife when she needs me most.

### To Sir Brion at Longcott

I sped with only Hywel in tow to Sir Brion's new demesne Longcott.  We made travel as fast as we were able.  I was glad to see my boon companion, but I felt that he was even more glad than I to see me.  A welcoming feast was laid out for me, as Brion asked me what task I bore with me.  I told him that Merffyn had brought news that the son of Uther must be moved to safety, and that we must do so.  Brion was happy to do so, but he seemed a bit put-off by the fact that Merffyn didn't say what the danger was.  Ah Brion, what matters is that the King is in danger.  This means that our actions must be urgent.  We will all discuss this with Merffyn when we return to Rav.... Broad Chalke.

The next morn, the party had doubled in size with the addition of Sir Brion and his squire, _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^baa3eb|Tathyl]]_.  We now rode hard for Rhiwallon.  For our party to be complete, we must have Sir Edar and possibly _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^0711d0|Lady Aneurin]]_, if she so be inclined to come as her ruse de amour, squire Aneurin.

### To Sir Edar at Rhiwallon

That fine blustery, wintry afternoon that we arrived at Rhiwallon, we were greeted by Sir Edar, and his lady Aneurin.  There too was Lady Aneurin's mother, _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^607441|Lady Margolin]]_.  I had only learnt last summer that she had once had a dalliance with _[[Oberon|Oberon]]_, the Lord of the Fae, which resulted in our companion, Aneurin.  Even now, I dare say she had lost none of her attractiveness.  It was an effort to ignore this, but my love for my Jeanne and the imminence of danger to my young liege gave me strength.  Also, she seemed quite amiable ... talkative even.  Welcomed we were to Rhiwallon.

The urgency of the task at hand and the delicacy of the information led to some quick thinking (and possibly great fortitude) by squire Tathyl, as he veritably leapt forward to walk with Lady Margolin through some of her gardens.  (I must admit that I wish Jeanne were here with me when time did not press so, for I would love to wander these gardens with her in my arms.)  Tathyl's backward look gave some evidence of the weightiness of his task, but it allowed our close-knit company to discuss the urgency of our new task.  I find Edar and Aneurin also impressed with the urgency.  Upon Lady Margolin's return, we inform her of the unfortunate need for haste, and that we must depart on the morrow.  She seems sad, but is then taken up with organising a veritable banquet.

The feast was truly wondrous.  The food was truly delicious, and the entertainment was extremely varied and never dull.  I got caught in one of my muses while listening to one of the tunes another bard was playing, and I think I did a very enchanting melody, at least so I felt the response showed.  Even the other bards showed appreciation.  I felt that I had done well, and thought I could gather some information of the demesne amongst some of the entertainers.  I learnt of Lady Margolin's interest's in the Fae (as if I hadn't known why concerning Oberon and Aneurin).  I was just learning about the current state of affairs between Lady Margolin and her current husband, _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^607441|Lord Bliant]]_, when I heard what the current story being recited was about.

It was Brion recklessly pursuing some perverse Irish blood trait.  I had known he would recite some story, and in fact he wasn't telling it badly, but ....  But, indeed, he was telling a story he should not be telling.  Of how 4 knights had travelled through some woods when they were attacked by werewolves, and although they beat them off, their leader had become infected.  Of how the 3 other knights had to resort to contacting the Fae to help destroy the lupins to save their lord.

This would probably have been aright to tell almost anywhere else, but Brion had to recklessly invoke the Fae into the story.  I looked quickly at Lady Margolin and saw my worst fears were being realized.  Not being able to stop Brion without making matters worse, I waited for blizzard to blow out so I could see the new lay of the land.

Lady Margolin gave some signals towards Brion that I think I would have seen even with my eyes closed.  I looked at Brion quickly enough to catch an expression of what I could not name other than as mischief incarnate flash across his face, and grinning lust replace it.  A flush of irritation came to me as I thought that he would involve my wife in his madness, but my reason returned when I thought that even he could not be that foolhardy, could he?  Although I was worried that Brion would do something that I might come to seriously regret, I trusted that Brion would not bring us into danger.  However, Edar and Aneurin headed me off as I was about to continue my intriguing amongst the entertainers.

Aneurin was mightily concerned about her mother.  Of course, I wasn't the only observant one tonight.  She was concerned with what her mother was apparently planning to do with Brion and asked if I could do something about it.  She was concerned that her step-father would probably be obliged to step in and challenge Brion.  With Brion being so skilled in his use of the sword and his absolute apparent lack of sense, I and Aneurin and Edar foresaw the impending result.  They also saw that Edar's current relations to Aneurin made him obliged to follow up with a challenge of Brion, with the same result.  I could also see that this would endanger our defence of Arthur.  How could we possibly protect him if we fought amongst ourselves.

With action upon my part necessary to prevent grave peril to the safety of our land, I strode boldly to the disappearing couple with my mind racing to think of something to convince them to break of this intended amorous affair.  As I reached them I asked if I could speak urgently to Brion about some matters that must be arranged for our early departure on the morrow.  At first, Brion's attention and awareness of my desire to speak to him privately must have been trickling through layers and layers of lust for he seemed not to understand.  Before things got too tense, he seemed to understand and asked Lady Margolin to wait for him.

As we moved to an outer, unoccupied room, I asked Brion if he was fully aware of what he was doing?  He states his and her desire to be alone together.  I argue with him about what this will result in.  I explain Lord Bliant's obvious resulting actions, and our companion Edar's regrettable, yet most honourable, obligations.  I tried to get him to think on his wife and his fidelity to her.  Finally, I laid my most puissant blow in this battle of words, that what he was threatening to do would endanger the King even more.  I would leave the matter up to his capable judgment.  He was silent for a very long moment, but then glared and left me to explain to Lady Margolin that he was feeling ill.

Then he started to storm off, but then transformed that into a staggering reel out the door to his chambers.  I marched to the currently irate Lady Margolin to explain that Sir Brion was come over quite suddenly ill, some vapours or such.  She had been looking quite disbelievingly at me until she suddenly repeated something I had said.

"Bilious! Not the meat!?"  She suddenly started, and raced towards the kitchens muttering some quite perturbing things.  I felt that I had avoided a whole thicket load of wild boars while I was bound and gagged and smeared in honey.  When I returned to my seat at the feast, I mentioned how I had averted the impending disaster.  Aneurin knew that her mother must be tearing her kitchen staff apart for allowing some bad meat to be served.  I offered to take the cooks and their families within my hold if need be, as I was quite taken with the food they had prepared, and that I was obliged as I had placed them in this position.  Lady Aneurin agreed that she saw the need existed.  It was the best solution to this situation causing the least harm, but still harm was caused.  The evening ground to an eventual halt, very late.

Early the next day as we were preparing to depart, we were being seen off by Lord Bliant and not Lady Margolin.  He concentrated on me, and in his cold way wished me well.  However, he didn't say anything to Edar and Aneurin, but he blatantly snubbed Brion.  I knew that he had heard somewhat of the happenings, or near-happenings, of last night.  I thanked him for our stay, and for the feast, and then we departed.

Although we travelled as a party on a great adventure, we seemed to be frozen by the weather, at the least.  Brion tended to ride by himself, a little ahead, and he didn't speak at all to me, and only sullenly sometimes to the others.  Not a totally auspicious beginning to our journey, but a lot better than it might have been.

## Start of March - Early Spring

### An Old Ritual

On the second day out from Rhiwallon, we camped early.  For it was the eve of Spring, when thoughts turn to growing and new life ... and to loved ones.  I had not dallied on my journey, but I was not with my beloved.  Soon after we had the horses haltered, Edar and Aneurin wandered away from the camp.  He he!  Look at Edar now.  He's fostering a ritual that is older than the faith he holds to.  I know he doesn't hold to it as firmly as he used to; at least, not if Aneurin had anything to do with it.  Being the half human - half Fae daughter of Lord Oberon does give you a firm opinion on the beliefs of a religion which disavows any possibility of virtue in the Fae.

Just after dawn spread its grey light through the clouds of the night, a very wet, bedraggled, bleary-eyed and yet looking very happy couple wandered back from the deeper forest and gathered their tack and harness to prepare for the days ride.  All were hard pressed not to laugh outright, but we all had the discipline to limit ourselves to grins.  Well ... at least Hywel, Brion and I did.  Tathyl kept sniggering the whole morning's ride.  I must admit though that Edar's red, embarrassed face did keep everyone amused.  Even Brion didn't have a glower on his face some of the time.

We sped south with nary a halt, but when it was dark.  That is, until we drew nigh on Stonehenge.  It was a week after the coming of Spring, yet there was still a feeling of power there.  I felt the hair on the nape of my neck and the back of my hands stand up straight.  Such power remaining after a week.  I couldn't resist the opportunity to perform one of the advanced rituals of Mithras.  Normally, I would only perform this on special feast days, like I had the week before, but it felt right to do it now ... here in this place.  As I beseeched Mithras to aid me in my quest to protect Arthur, to give me strength of arm to aid my King, I saw Aneurin walking very gingerly, almost as if she was walking on hot sands.  I knew she would be more sensitive to the power here, but that sensitive?

The sight of Aneurin put me in mind of my beloved.  I also petitioned my god to champion my true love, my beloved.  To protect her, the priestess of another faith, and to protect, aid and guide my child.  As my libation poured to the ground, I felt my faith and devotion to my god once more reaffirmed in the rightness of the moment.  That moment stretched out to rest of the afternoon, even as we left and headed to Sarum, to inform Earl Robert of our mission.

### To Earl Robert at Sarum

On our arrival at Sarum, we were welcomed with the generous hospitality that I had come to appreciate of the Earl, and a great sense of curiosity.  Many welcomed us by name, and much backslapping was given us as we passed.  I could barely believe that this time last year, I had been barely recognized as one of the new squires, who had just arrived in the previous autumn.  And now I was a knight, just like my father.  But unlike my father, I had demesnes, lived amongst friends, and had a loving wife.

I was shown directly to Earl Robert, upon my request.  The tales of the past summer had grown and borne fruit as glory, and renown, and boons such as this.  The others had other tasks on their platters, and we would only be staying for a short while.

He greeted me warmly and tried to be totally decorous and hospitable, but curiosity was marching to war with that.  I bade the Earl greetings, and quickly assuaged his itching for knowledge.  I informed my Lord of the visitation of Merffyn to Raven's Gate, and of his tasking myself and my companions with the moving of the future King to safety.  Immediately, my lord showed his great loyalty to his true liege by offering men to aid us, but this was a task which needed a small band of worthy champions who could move quickly and quietly.  Reluctantly, Earl Robert could see the sense of this.

I had wished to keep my lord current with matters of state.  With that pressing task of duty duly done, I could now inform the Earl of other mighty matters such as the coming of my first so ... child in the summer.  I also informed the Earl that I had made Hywel my vassal knight.  I told him of my views that Hywel had been the perfect squire, and such diligence should make Hywel a most worthy fellow in the brotherhood of chivalry.  I could see that this aroused some contemplation within my lord, and felt that I had taken up enough of the Earl's time and we should continue with our journey as it was but a few hours to my home.  I bid the Earl a fair morrow, and left to find my companions.

All were ready to continue, although I found Brion looking most smug and looking quite delighted.  This caused me some concern, in that I also found two other knights who I distantly remembered as being bullies to the squires were nursing some minor wounds.  However, I felt that it was almost worth it to have my boon companion Brion back.

### Return to Raven's Gate

We were but a bare hour from Raven's Gate when we began to overtake another knight.  It was a mercenary knight with a scar lengthwise across his face.  We stopped to talk with him, as I wondered what he was doing coming to my home.  It appears he had gotten lost as he was trying to get to Cornwall as the self-titled _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^258f90|King Mark of Lyonesse]]_ was offering a very high fee for knights in his service.  In spite of our friendly help of giving him directions and such, he failed to offer us his name.  However, I had a feeling that we would meet again as he had given me the knowledge of what was causing Merffyn's concern for Arthur.

Although he had not said so, it was obvious that King Mark was attempting to make himself the paramount lord of Briton by spreading his claims through Cornwall, including Tintagel; the current abode of Arthur, and Uther Pendragon's last home.  It was a powerful, rich castle astride the trade routes to Gaul, Iberia and further afield.  In fact, it was a surprise to think that no-one had tried to do this in the two years since Uther's passing.  All this made me more eager to reach home to plan the defence of my liege.

Upon arrival back at Raven's Gate, I bade everyone welcome, and hied the servants to take care of their needs as I left them and sped to my beloved.  It made me so joyous to see her.  She gave me welcome with open arms and we spoke of many things that loving ones speak of, as well as pithy things seen on the journey, and deductions from them.

It was nigh on time for supper when my dear blanched and went tight-lipped as she said, "He's here!"  And so Merffyn had arrived.  How, without passing the gates, only he and my Lady Jeanne knew.  It was now time to talk of Arthur, of Cornwall, and most importantly, to my mind, where should Arthur's new home be.  So we all, bar my beloved who could not countenance meeting with Merffyn today, gathered in the main hall.  Since we were about sup, I bid Merffyn join us before he would do so anyway.

It was now that Merffyn sought to inform us of the occurrences in Cornwall.  He probably felt piqued when news of King Mark appeared to be known already.  Merffyn also answered as to why he couldn't move Arthur by more preternatural means, which of course at once gained my approval, and then progressed to my ire.  I had mentioned my travel through the Ley lines, but had forgotten how harrowing the travelling was.  It was definitely not something for Arthur to undergo at such a young age.  But then Merffyn said that there was only one other of his level of power, who was opposed to him.  He had the temerity, the gall to say that milady was not on his level.  How dare he insult my wife in my home?  Is this how he uses the bounds of hospitality?  But ... but my King is in danger ... so I must swallow my pride and take this insult.

It was the anger, and my passion for serving my liege which must have hindered my tongue, for when I broached the idea of moving Arthur to Raven's Gate for safety, I could not seem to find the right words to convince Merffyn.  But then Brion stepped in and rallied the argument and we counterattacked with very good reasons.  We had the approval and support of Earl Robert, and so we could get a garrison of as many men as necessary.  Similarly, if it did become known that Arthur was here, then he would be defended by a truly worthy band of knights and men and by my wife.  How much more secure could he possibly be?

We would be able to give Arthur a more varied education, the variety of which would help Arthur to be able to make his own decisions wisely.  I thought, but didn't say that it would give my King some views that would balance the somewhat amoral ones that Merffyn held.  Maybe amoral might not be the right word ... perhaps total incapability to take others opinions and emotions into account.  Along with that, Arthur would need to have some companions and teachers who would be kith and kine for him.  It would not be fair on Arthur, or on those he would rule, if he was unable to relate to anyone.  All in all, I thought it was most convincing, but Merffyn said he would think on it.

We then went on to plan Arthur's extraction from Tintagel.  Merffyn suggested that my party should make its way, as quickly as possible to Padstowe Bay, which was on the Cornish coast south-west of Tintagel.  There he would meet us with a merchant vessel that he would hire.  We would then sail to Tintagel, which would have already have been invested by King Mark.  There we would pick up Arthur and his mother, the _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^e0aea7|Lady Ygraine]]_, and we would set sail for Bath.  Lady Ygraine wanted to go to a convent for the remainder of her days.  (Absenting oneself from the world seemed a dishonourable, cowardly way of reacting to your fates, but it was something that Christians seemed fond of.)  We would then move Arthur to his new abode.

This plan was left open as to how our party would reach Padstowe Bay, but in trying to help that rude mercenary knight who had become lost, we had already worked out a route to follow to Cornwall.  This was fortunate, as Merffyn was not concerned with this and departed after completing his meal.  We would head up the Ebble River valley, north past Wilton, then west on the main road through Morgaine's Forest to Wells, and then south-west on the main road through Jagent to Exeter.  From there, we travel partly along the south coast, hopefully skirting King Mark's army, eventually striking north-west to the Bay.  With speed being of the essence, we resolved that this was a good plan, and concluded our meals and prepared to leave on the morrow.

Before the night was through, I held my love in my arms and bid my farewells, for I would not force a protracted parting on her.  It would not be just to inflict that on my lady, when it could be done gently and in a more tender way than could be done amongst a crowd preparing to depart.  Also, I wanted this to be a private moment anyway.  I must admit, though, that I was a little bewildered when she didn't seem too concerned that I was going away into danger, and might not return.  But then I was flushed with delight that my wife was at once confident in my abilities, and in her own abilities to cope with my absence and in her goddess to keep an eye on me.  For wasn't I married to a priestess of the Morrigan in one of Her ceremonies, as well as the Earl's Christian "masses".  Truly, I was fortunate to earn the love of such a woman.  It was with such gladdened hearts that we went to our rest for the night; in spite of our upcoming parting in the morn.

And in the morn, we bade each other a fond farewell as our party left on our Spring campaign.  For all preparations had been made, and we had a King to redeem.

### Vagon Castle

So our band of champions set forth to rescue Arthur from the avaricious grasp of King Mark, and to keep him in safety until he would be ready to rule in hid own right.  As we had planned we set forth eastwards along the Ebble River valley to the main road to Sarum.  We rode hard, barely sparing our chargers.  A few miles north on the main road, we took the turn-off past the fortified town of Wilton that had grown in the corner of the Nabbe River and the Lower Wylie River.  Although it was a town directly beholden to Earl Robert, we had no need to spend time here and so we sped through with nary a pause.

After passing through Wilton, we'd continued north until we passed from the dirt track from Wilton to the well-paved Roman road west from Sarum, through Morgaine's Forest to Wells.  We raced westward, at last.  In our speed, we passed up camping outside of the old fortifications of Grovely.  This barely irked me as, although I dearly wanted to examine the Roman ruins and perhaps find a shrine to Mithras, the urgency of Arthur's need for my protection could leave little in its wake.

As night fell, we approached the Castle just off the road named Vagon.  Vagon Castle appeared to have spent its best years earlier than when I was born.  It was a standard box keep of a style of but fifty years or so ago.  It also appeared fairly neglected, and in need of some decent stewardship and stipend, in my considered opinion.  It protected a few villages of the surrounding area.  I vaguely recalled that it had a new lord of the manor, the son of the previous one, though to my mind I could not recollect any more details.

It being dark, we banged on the gate demanding entry.  The sentry at the gate seemed old and quite querulous.  I put it down to being miffed at being woken in the night, but Brion's temper seemed to be reddening his face to suit his hair.  When we asked to be let in, he did so after a truly insulting desultory discourse, and Brion was working up a temper on himself when I noticed that the gatekeeper acted the simpleton.  Afflicted by the gods, poor lad.  As Brion seemed not to notice, I thought I should step in and get the gatekeeper to direct us to his superiors.  He had already told us that _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^20db7f|Sir Sagamore]]_, lord of this castle, was not in residence tonight.  I also found out that his instructions as he understood them was to watch the gate and let any one through the gates who wanted to.

It was now that I saw Hywel and Tathyl beginning to gather the horses and take them to the stables; this distraction probably saved my anger from being vented on someone who didn't deserve it, unfortunately for Hywel.  I took Hywel aside and instructed him once again that that is a squire's task, not his.  I regret to say, I realized I had failed Hywel in that I had forgotten to ask Earl Robert for two new squires.  I'd see what I could do here, but from what I had seen so far, I had grave doubts.

As we reached the main door to the keep, I came face-to-face with one of the serving-maids.  In a manner intended not to startle her, for I think she looked quite open-eyed now, I told her that we were knights from Sarum come to stay the night in passing through, and that we would like to have some food.  Silently, she passed the platter of food that she had been holding into my hands, and then stood there.  Feeling a little upset, I sought to clarify my instructions for fear that this simple-mindedness might be quite contagious.  "Take us to your lord's steward?" seemed fairly straightforward enough.  And she seemed to latch onto that instruction quite eagerly and, unfortunately sped off by herself back into the keep.  I did feel annoyed, but said a prayer to Mithras to not cloud my mind with anger from clarity of the truth, and felt more focused.  As I took one of the pieces of meat from the platter and passed the platter back amongst the party, I followed the silent wide-eyed wench.

The introduction to the steward and the prayer to Mithras clarified everything.  We met him coming down the stairs and introductions were made.  It was obvious now, with more light available of the likeness of visage between the steward, the maid and the gatekeeper.  Ah, the simple-mindedness was contagious ... in this family.  He greeted us and made us welcome in the absence of his lord.  Sir Sagamore was away on a patrol against bandits.  We explained who we were, and of our need to stay the night.  The steward seemed caught in a quandary.  At a loss of what to do, this riled me so that I snapped for him to call a servant to show us to the hall where we would eat while he went to the cook to cook some vittles.  This seemed to have the desired effect.

The steward explained the absence of his lord in that a party of his knights were ambushed a days ride West from Vagon.  Eight knights had been attacked, leaving only one survivor.  Sir Sagamore had set out two days ago with the rest of his knights in chase of these bandits who had attacked his men.  No matter how hard we pressed, we could not get any more pertinent information from him.  So before we were shown to our rooms for the night, I discussed with Brion as to setting up a watch of our own this night.  Tathyl would stand first watch, Brion wanted to stand second watch, and so on.  We made sure to send Tathyl a meal to warm him for his task.

But I also thought to take Lady Aneurin aside.  I asked if she could continue with us in her male squire personae, as that would attract less attention than travelling with a female companion would.  She gracefully acquiesced to my suggestion, but I did notice that she seemed more relaxed than she had.  I consider myself gifted with Mithras sight at times like this when I could tell that Aneurin had been in the horns of indecision as to how she should act amongst us : as male squire or female companion?  With the decision taken from her ... or forced on her ... she felt better.  With such precautions taken, I felt I could rest the night.

Later that night, I was awoken by one of the servants with the news of Sir Sagamore's return.  I sent the lad off to awaken Sir Brion, but warned him to do so no closer than swords length as Brion could awaken in the most disagreeable of moods.  As the lad left, I donned my garments and, with caution bred of years of training with my father, also my armour.  Fully accoutred, I headed to the main hall of the keep where I met Sir Sagamore upon the stairs.  Actually, close to where we had encountered the steward earlier in the night.

He seemed at a loss at finding a strange knight in his keep ... actually, a lot like the steward ...  Could it be ...?  He even looks a bit like the steward.  A little irritation crept loose from my just-awakened mind and I sought to control the irritation by controlling the conversation, as my father had taught me.  Brion emerged, also fully armoured, thus ensuring I must dominate the discussion most urgently before Brion takes it to places we have no need to travel.  I greeted Sir Sagamore and introduced ourselves to him, and bid him welcome in his demesnes.  A little desultory conversation began, until I thought to ease the situation by inviting the recently returned knight to have a drink in his own hall.  I sought to drain this young knight of all information, as many dangerous things were afoot in the land and preparations must be made in the lands of Earl Robert for the defence of our liege, Arthur.

It is at times like these when I see how of an age I can be with some of the nobility, but how different of thought.  My upbringing following the proper British heritage which was once of Roman origins, following my father as he fought for different lords and the guidance of Mithras has combined to give me a different sense of the present than some of my fellow knights.  This Sir Sagamore has been given this demesne of Vagon by the recent demise of his father, but a visiting guest of equal rank has the ability to take charge of him and his household.  I pray to all the gods, to the Morrigan and mostly to Mithras that Arthur will follow more in my footsteps than that of Sir Sagamore's.

Arthur must be able to take charge of all situations as he finds them, and to wrest them to how he desires them.  An ability to see the truth of what is as Mithras provides is essential.  He must also have the honour and intelligence to see the virtues of matters as they stand and not abuse them.  Ahh, Mithras aid the lad to see and judge truly and rightfully.

This pause was but momentary, and not noticed by anyone.  The hall was filling with Sir Sagamore's knights and my hopes for this demesne was failing just as rapidly.  They were mere boys, some barely suitable to be squires and the rest barely suitable to be servants.  My concerns for the road ahead were relaxing, just as quickly as my consideration was rising on the future of this keep.  Sir Sagamore could give us little more information than we already had.  As I and Brion had suspected, it was not bandits who had attacked them, it was mercenary knights.  But they numbered only four.

After seeing Sir Sagamore's retinue of household knights, I considered that maybe the household knights showed more promise than I had thought, ... and that had been none.  Other than that fact, they could give no information, which I considered a greater fault than their lack of fighting ability.  With so little to learn from them, I instructed everybody to get some rest until the morn as our party would ride within the hour after dawn.  Before we left I wanted Sir Sagamore to guide me through his keep and I would give him instructions as to how to defend his stronghold, while Sir Brion would try and teach the household knights some semblance of defence, as much as he could do in such a short time.

As dawn breaks, I already have Sir Sagamore marching through his "stronghold" with a little crowd following, almost like a fair.  I see the points where he should place his men and what parts could do with strengthening, and I explain them to the almost permanent fish-stare of the keep's lord.  The thought that there is at least another fortified town between here and Earl Robert's western border, gives me little relief, and so I ponder what I will need to talk about with Earl on my return.  As we return to the courtyard, I order Sir Sagamore to send a messenger to the Earl informing him of the occurrences of the past week.  A little part of me fades away as the response comes back of what a truly good idea that is.  I sigh at how surprised he sounds.  Surely the land needs more responsible war leaders, if Arthur is to be protected.  When we reach the courtyard, I see Brion has reached a similar feeling as he seems to be close to tears as well.  He has been testing these household knights and appears to have been greatly frustrated.

After our band of companions has gathered and prepares to ride off, I tried to instil some backbone and inspiration to these people.  Unfortunately, I give an impassioned speech of loyalty to the Earl but I still see an unchanging sea of blank faces.  As I leave slightly disheartened, I warn Sir Sagamore that I will return in the months to come, and that I expect to see some improvement.  However, I hold to no hope that improvement will occur.

### Edar's Indecision

Not even a day's ride from Vagon, we came across the scene of the ambush.  Thinking of where I would set four men to waylay travellers on this road, I tracked where they had hidden two men on each side of the road.  It looked quite professionally done, which made what had happened sickening to me.  How in the name of Mithras could anyone who would hold to the name of knighthood do such things to a band of dressed-up boys?

We camped but an hour later, and Brion and myself went our separate ways to pray to our patron gods.  I prayed as hard as I could that Mithras would aid me in the defence of Arthur, and to make the Earl recognize the truth of how his lands lie, and to guide me to the guilty-handed perpetrators who had acted so disgracefully, and that he aid me in dispatching these knights who could be so untrue to their vows.  As I prayed, I felt a touch of something that I could only attribute to Mithras.  He had responded, and I felt content that he would inspire me and guide me to the law-breakers who had betrayed their honour.  When I returned to camp, I found that Brion must have had a similarly inspiring prayer as he was pacing back and forth around a corner of the fire ranting and raving about fighting and blood, and other things that the Morrigan gives inspiration for.

On the other side of the fire, I see the others of the party looking quite worriedly at Brion.  However, Edar looks very perturbed, as if more than just by Brion.  I go to him and ask him what disturbs him.  He says that he has lost his faith and he can find nothing to hold onto.  Knowing that I must get Edar to find a truth to cling to if he is to fulfil any part of his own life, I started talking about my beliefs.  While I begin, Brion sees what I am about so storms in front of us and, to my mind, continues with his ranting of impassioned fighting and blood and vengeance.  He must realize that he is not succeeding with Edar, as he stamps off to the other side of the fire and continues his pacing and muttering.

Still feeling close with Mithras, I give a quite impassioned sermon, which Mithras aids me in allowing me to speak quite fluently and succinctly.  I feel quite pleased with what has resulted, and Edar has listened to all I have spoken of.  However, I feel he shied away from my mentions of Mithras (obviously a reflexive action from his dying Christian beliefs) but really liked what I said about Mithraic values of truth and knightly values.  With this in mind, I leave him with the parting words of : "At the very least, hold to the true ideals of knighthood!"  This Edar clung to most vigorously, as he seemed more relaxed than he had been before.  So with this, I was satisfied.

Brion stood watch through almost the whole night, pacing back and forth non-stop, thankfully only muttering to himself.  He woke me for the final watch, so he could at least have some rest for the night.  I stoked the fire, for it was still a cold spring night.  And I heard Aneurin talking to herself, or so I thought, when I heard her distinctly say that there would be no more religious discussions tonight.  I quietly approached her prone form as it lay facing away from the fire in a suddenly stiff and patently asleep figure.  I then quietly said to the figure who I hadn't seen, but knew to be somewhere nearby; "Yes, no more religious discussions tonight. But if you wish to continue the conversation later, I am willing to later and you know where I live, Puck."

The next day, we forge ahead until late in the day we reach the main road north to Bath and south to Jagent.  Rather than be sidetracked westwards to Wells for a few hours, we speed southwards hoping to catch up with the band of four unseemly mercenary knights.  Unfortunately we must set up camp as night falls.

### Knights of the Northern Witch

The next nightfall would see us at the gates of Jagent.  A town that was originally part of Salisbury, but the family lords of the town had been extremely militant in the past.  Earl Robert had accepted this if they didn't move eastwards.  This was also acceptable to the lords of Jagent as that would take them through Morgaine's Forest.  However, we were but a scant hour from Jagent when we started overtaking a band of four knights, who had no other followers.  As we began to overtake them, they parted two to a side to let us pass ... just like the ambush!  It felt like them, but we had to know.  I was overcome with a feeling I don't recall feeling before: disgust and slight sense of sickness.  It was them.  Brion knew it as well, for he taunted them in that infuriating Irish way of his.  When they charged at us in a line of four on their horses, I felt glad to show them how true knights behave.

Myself and Edar swing around either side of the charging four and attack them from the side.  Brion and Hywel continue charging them from in front.  And our two squires stayed of the road and to the side.  In that first clash of arms, with my cries to Mithras and Brion's to the Morrigan ringing clear, Brion and myself unhorse our opponents.  Unfortunately, Hywel is also unhorsed.  Hywel is endangered while my opponent has been unhorsed.  His opponent is not getting off his horse to continue to fight Hywel; the effrontery of the barbarian.  As I rush to aid Hywel, I see that Edar has knocked his opponent out (well slung blow!) and Brion takes a nasty blow that has drawn blood.

I knock Hywel's opponent to the ground to teach him to fight fair, and then I get off my horse to show him how to fight properly.  As I fight him, I still see Brion ahorse fighting his opponent on foot.  I receive but two minor blows as I defeat this varlet.  And as I turn from his fallen form, I see Brion dispatch his with a mighty swing and a cry to the Morrigan.  The fourth villain runs, but Edar is still ahorse and chases after him and knocks him out with the flat of his blade.  But he waits until I arrive on my horse. I put the unconscious form on my horse and took him back to the battle site.

When we return to the road of contention, I see Brion taking the armour off the body of his opponent.  The body has a throat-wound that I swear hadn't been there before, but maybe I'm mistaken... yay, verily.  As I go to aid Brion, I sense that the armour has a real sinister and corrupt feel to it.  As I mention this to Brion, Aneurin rides up and agrees.  The armour itself has green tinges to it.  We all agree that it must not be left behind for anyone to find, so we carry it on the horse that used to carry its previous master.  The rest of the swords and armour we gather on our new horses.  These would be useful for Tathyl, and Aneurin.  We also gather a bronze torc and two bronze rings.

We rouse one of the dying knights to get information.  The dead knight with the evil armour had a tattoo on him, which Brion recognized as of Pictish origin.  We question him as to who the dead fellow was, but he seems to unable to speak.  I dimly recall that I have heard of some spell that could take your voice away.  I mention this quietly to Brion, and Brion asks who cast this spell on him?  He looks scared.  I try to force an answer from him by first asking "Who is he?" which drew a blank look.  A little thought brought my next question a little more certainty, " Who ... is ... she?!"  His face paled, and sweat began pouring from him.  You could smell the fear in him.  Fear not of us, but of her ... whoever she might be.

Brion pulled me aside and asked if we should try to get M here by saying his name, just as Merffyn had mentioned about his opponent back at Raven's Gate.  I considered it, but thought that our mission was too important to endanger by attracting more attention than we should now.  We would be seeing Merffyn in ten to fifteen days from now, anyway.  Surely, he could wait that long to be informed.  With this decision, we leave the dying men unarmed and untended by the side of the road, showing the spoils of abusing the privileges of knighthood.  Just as Merffyn had sent us to help him in Tintagel, his opponent had sent these refuse to hinder him.  We must make haste once again.  That night we camped outside the gates of Jagent, and with the morn we rode past it.

## Mid March - Early Spring

### The Red Cap

We rode up to and around Jagent with all due haste.  We had no time to tarry, and Jagent was a place of the Earl's foes who were only held in abeyance by the forest.  Although they hadn't done much recently to oppose the Earl, why place temptation in their laps?

Nothing untoward occurred until that evening.  We had made good time, and had passed a few mercenary knights on the road.  However, during Aneurin's watch that night I was awakened by Edar, who had been awakened by Aneurin.  We had a guest at our campsite.  He (I presumed) looked like a very young boy, although slightly shorter than Brion.  He was leaning quite comfortably on a pole arm with a very sharp looking blade on the end, which glinted alarmingly.

Bound by the rituals of knightly hospitality, I welcomed him to the fire, even though that raised some groans from Aneurin.  Aneurin took me aside and told me he was a _[[010-The-Seasons#^25fcff|Red Cap]]_.  Looking at the boy, I could see the obvious truth of what she said, but then I gathered what she trying to tell me.  He was of the Fae for he had that kind of cast to his face.  But then Aneurin told me that he was not of her Father's side of the Fae, a thought that raised the spectres of all the evil stories of the Fae from folk lore.  He was of the Unseelie Court, and an extremely violent and unstoppable kind.

I could confront him, but his speed would see that to be futile, and thus end Arthur's rescue before it could begin for him.  So I sought to prevaricate and divert his attention from us by making us but extremely well-accoutred merchant band, and thus too boring to attract the attention of.  But I was hindered by my lack of experience with lying, my failure to hide the urgency of our task, and the Red Cap's obvious pleasure in causing Aneurin discomfort just by his presence.

After a little while of consideration, I concluded that our unwanted guest required of us a great risk in travelling now, at night, to hopefully leave him behind.  This, in turn, proved to be unsuccessful when the Red Cap came with us.  What was also unfortunate was that in our fatigue and in overcast darkness, Tathyl's horse stepped in a hole and broke its leg.  Tathyl was hurt and we could not continue until he was able to move about, so we made camp until the morn.  It was also fortunate that we had some spare horses for the morrow's travel.

The next day saw everyone feel better for the Red Cap was no longer with us as dawn broke.  We travelled as hard as the horses could bear.  That is, until the sun was at its peak.  It was then that we came across a peasant family travelling in their wagon with everything they owned.  Resting the horses, we questioned the peasant as to news of what lay ahead.  They had fled their land as the spectre of warfare raised its goried and gloried banner ... again.  It was not yet three years since the dragons fought last in Cornwall.  It was what I had seen before when I travelled with my father ... while their betters fought, everyone else fled before they were caught in between two combatants who could slay them in passing without them even knowing what the affray was about.

### To Exeter

Continuing on to Exeter, a place we reached just before nightfall, we encountered more and more of these commoners who obviously had common sense.  I hazarded a guess as to where there might be some rooms for us in Exeter.  While we travelled through the town, I was constantly reminded of what we had abused of our rightful past.  Old buildings built when we were still part of civilization, one we should learn from and create our own, were interspersed among much newer buildings.  Newer, but much less inspiring buildings.  The old ones were more in disrepair, more ... cannibalised. ...  Sometimes I felt such despair for our fair Britannia.  As always, I turned to Mithras to guide us to civilization once more ... through our King-in-waiting Arthur ... if he could be taught aright.

Although I filled myself with determination, it failed to materialize rooms for us in the town.  So we camped outside of the town that night.  Fortunately for us, it appeared we had left our Red Cap behind, for he did not appear that night.

With the dawn, we went to the ferry on the other side of Exeter.  The river Exe was wide enough for us to need a ferry and we had no time to go around, so Edar was good enough to provide us the fare.  Once across it appeared that Aneurin was waylaid by an elderly pair of peasants seeking alms to pay for their fare.  As I approached the group, I noticed that Aneurin was trying to get information of what lay ahead.  Whereupon she surreptitiously paid them for their aid.

What she had learned was there were two roads west.  One led southwards from here, and followed the coast mostly.  The other road passed through the Morris Forest before that petered out as the road passed through the moors.  Although it would be the rougher path, it was much more direct and therefore quicker.  Also, King Mark's army had marched from the castle on the south coast of Cornwall, directly south of Tintagel.  With this in mind, we travelled the north road and again we travelled hard.  By nightfall, we had left the forest but hours before and were in the moors.

### The Haunted Moors

We camped in a little vale in the moors.  The moors were a rough, rolling land with no vegetation larger than a stunted, windswept bush of a tree.  I had no concept of bleakness until I crossed this moor.  But I know our mission is worth gaining this insight.  However, as it got darker and darker and we readied to sleep, we began to hear things at the edges of the night.  Worrying things.

It was not till Mithras granted me a true vision that they were the wailings and clashing of the many who died fighting on the moors.  This truly horrifying sight and sound was made truly tragic when I saw some Roman legionnaires caught in this endless combat in the moors.  I was driven to play some of the old Roman tunes my grandfather taught me, but I fear I did not do them justice.  Though, initially I thought I had succeeded in putting the restless at peace for it quickly went quiet.  But things had gone quiet for someone else ... our Red Cap had returned.

It was a querulous Red Cap who had found us.  He seemed a bit upset that we had left before he had returned to our old campsite, but seemed appeased when we told him we thought he had left us then.  Brion informed us he was going to pray to the Morrigan, and as he turned to go some of us noticed that the Red Cap, who was looking at the departing Brion, looked a lot older and less human.  This was not going to be a restful night.  And, in fact, so it turned out.  We passed the rest of the night in an uncomfortable rest, which left everyone surly in the morn.  It seemed the right mood, for it was very overcast and boded to rain before long ... just what we needed.

And rain it did.  Long and fairly hard, hard enough that I almost felt lost at times as our horses bore us through the mud of the moors.  In that rain, we could have passed King Mark's entire army by no more than a couple of yards, and there was no way we could have known.  I would say that by about midday, the rain eased off and then stopped.  After travelling a little more, we saw a plume of smoke rising from the direction of Tintagel.  My heart beat faster as I called for us to head for the smoke.

It was a very long distance from us, so we had travelled no more than a few minutes in that direction when the rain began again quite suddenly.  It had gone from no rain to a downpour that was much heavier than it had been before.  It only lasted ten minutes, but it seemed to have rained the same amount of water that the mornings rain had given us.  After this torrential downpour, the smoke was no longer in evidence.  I took this as a sign that Merffyn was working hard to protect the King, and that we must hurry.  Another reason was that we had lost our Red Cap, and I had no longing for his return.

As we continued back to the road and headed west again, we came across three knights coming from the other direction.  We stopped to talk with each other and found out that the three mercenary knights had come to join King Mark's army, but had gotten completely turned around in the heavy rain of the morning.  They joined us as we were also travelling west, and as far as they were concerned, we had the same purpose.

### Crossing the Tamar River

We continued west and soon found ourselves at the ford across the Tamar River.  Although, it was now a very full river with icy cold water flowing quickly past us, Brion volunteered Tathyl to see if the water was shallow enough for us to get across, but he was swept off his feet and he lost the pole he was using to test the depth.  Thankfully we had some rope, so we managed to get him out of the water.  Then Brion volunteered himself and he tried, although he too began to be swept away so we pulled on the rope that was tied to him, but Edar lost his footing and he went into the water as well.  He didn't let go of the rope; otherwise he would have been swept away.  Other than that, it was an uneventful crossing with us all getting wet again but no more so than when it had rained before.

We travelled a few hours more when we came across the scene of a battle, but no ... that is not what it was.  It was a scene of pure butchery.  We could make out that there had been three knights on horseback, originally.  Now ... there were enough chunks of meat to feed King Mark's army for a day.  It was obvious that two of the knights had died side by side and one had managed to run a few yards away before he too was killed.  But what was then done to the bodies.  Brion knew that it was a pole arm that must have caused some of the wounds, and we could not find any tracks.

I hadn't needed these clues to tell me what had done this.  What, I wondered, was the reason why this hadn't happened to us?  Was it possibly for this reason that we had not met any of King Mark's army patrols?  Who, or what could be powerful enough to make something like the Red Cap "protect" us like this?  Could Merffyn stoop this low ... or could something else want us too succeed?  Again we travelled on, and soon we met again with our Red Cap "friend".  It was then while we were riding that Aneurin noticed something about him.  She pointed it out to us, and that was that he was not leaving any tracks behind.

### The Hurlers

The next day saw us approaching some henge of two circles of obelisks, one ring inside the other.  As we drew nearer to them, the Red Cap named them the Hurlers.  This close to our destination, Padstowe Bay, we needed no delays for we were to meet with our ship today or on the morrow.

As I headed our party around the stones in a wide circle, Brion gave a glad cry and spurred his horse to the henge.  It was but a matter of moments before I found out what had happened, but Brion had reached the henge, had dismounted and strode to the centre of the henge.  Once there he began a little rite to the Morrigan using his own blood as sacrifice.  As he began that rite, the Red Cap was standing by one of the obelisks and he looked back at me.  He gave me a full-blooded stare of such malevolence and pure ... evil, that it rocked me on my horse.  But it also enraged me!

In my rage, I ignored that the Red Cap could slice me to pieces before I could even use my abilities, and spurred my horse in a gallop at my foe.  Crying to Mithras, aye and to the Morrigan, I sped to save my friend.  But as I swung at him, he laughed and stepped into the stone.  My sword clanged against the stone, drawing a cry of frustration from me.

Brion had disappeared from within the henge while I had charged, and not having any knowledge of how to save my friend, my comrade-in-arms, I jumped from my horse and began a sped-up rite to Mithras.  Using my sword I slashed my arm severely and prayed to Mithras.  Redeem my friend ... and at once I felt the answer of my god.  It was one that I wasn't sure I understood exactly, but it was Mithras and his shining truth that reached me.  HE said "Yes ... fight! But fight with honour!".

They said that after I had made my prayers to Mithras that I keeled over and within a blink of an eye, my boon companion was returned in a similar condition to me.  Our three temporary companions fled at this, thus relieving me of the decision on how to rid ourselves of them.

We were but half a day from our rendezvous with the trader ship at Padstowe Bay.  On that ride, I noticed that the others rode a bit behind us and kept looking askance at us two.  While we were riding, I learnt from Brion that he too had had a meeting with his goddess.  A meeting which I could infer shed some light on what Mithras had told me.  Any contact with the Morrigan would involve fighting - either doing it or talking about it.  My God agreed with what She might have said, with the qualification that it be honourably.  But in time, I will learn what Brion was told.

### Padstowe Bay

When we arrived at the bay, we organized a hovel to stay-in for our ship hadn't arrived yet.  As we were waiting, I thought we all could do with some sword-training.  A decision it seemed only Brion agreed with as everyone else seemed tired.  It was only then that I realized myself and Brion seemed charged with plenty of energy.  As the night progressed, Brion and I spent our time on watch for we found it hard to sleep, so filled with energy were we.  And in the morning, while it was still dark, the ship was sighted at the neck of the bay, but it would be midday before it docked.

After we saw most of the unloading from the ship, we boarded and presented ourselves to the captain and stated we were his passengers.  The captain was very swarthy, dark-skinned almost.  I did feel suspicious about him, but Merffyn should know what he was doing.  The King's safety rested on this.  We had interesting discussion on horses, one that raised my interest in purchasing some of his but he said he wouldn't sell them until he knew we were going to treat them well.  I felt he had just been jesting at my expense, insulting our breed of horses.  It was a good thing that Merffyn arrived soon after.  We took him aside and told him of our travels, of the Pictish knight and of the Red Cap.  And, I hoped our sea-journey would start soon.

We set sail at high tide, or so Merffyn claimed.  We sailed on until nightfall, and then continued on.  It wasn't full-moon, so we couldn't see land, but eventually we were told we were off Tintagel.  Merffyn came on deck and told us we would land tonight and rescue the Lady Ygraine and our lord Arthur.  Merffyn called my band of companions to him, and gave us the order to get our horses saddled, and to ride to the castle.

### A Fine Mist

As we gathered our steeds on deck, and saddled them, Merffyn began to do something.  As we watched Merffyn, a cry from one of the deckhands drew us to the side of the ship.  A mist, which had not been there but moments before, covered the water like a shroud; a shroud that was rising up the side of the ship, all around the ship.  Soon, the white fog was all around us, and we could only see a couple of lengths away.  I knew Merffyn had called forth this fog, but I couldn't look at him until his voice called back my wandering mind.  "Ride forth!", he cried.

I looked at him, the thought of being betrayed flickered and then died as I remembered that for all Merffyn's annoyances and goals, endangering the King could not be one of them....  At least at this stage of Arthur's life.  The King is all potential for his schemes ....  Yes, he would not endanger the King.  I strode to my horse and mounted.  As I forced my horse towards the edge of the ship, I saw Brion and the others heading their horses after me.

I flew over the side, half-expecting to drop into the sea, but half-way down the horse landed on something solid and continued on, but without a sound being made!  I continued on, galloping to the beach knowing my friends followed.  Time passed as I rode through the fog, until suddenly I could just see some light fires burning through the fog.  The sound of my horse's hooves arrived at the same time.  When I heard another horse arriving on the beach, I also heard the rousing cries of alarm from King Mark's men.  I rode to the east, to reach the caves from which I had seen Arthur play on this very beach last summer.  Surely, there was some means of entrance from those caves.

A group of barely armoured men strove to get in our way, but no just-awakened men-at-arms could hope to stand before Brion, Edar, Hywel and myself.  We brushed them aside easily.  We poured into the largest cave, but that narrowed too.  We got off the horses, but there was only room for one person at a time.  Brion had gotten in the lead, and he eventually came across some guards.  He quickly dealt with the three, leaving the last his life, if not his honour.  They had been guarding a stairway upwards.  This had to be the postern gate.  Taking only Edar, Hywel and Aneurin, and leaving Brion to guard our exit, I climbed the stairs.

### Tintagel Castle

At the top of the stairs was a thick door, obviously bolted on the other side.  I pounded on the door with the hilt of my dagger until someone answered.  When a muffled voice asked who we were, I stated our names and why we were here.  "Merffyn has sent us."  Eventually, the door opened showing us the scowling visages of three men-at-arms and a knight.  The knight gave instructions that only I and Edar could enter.  As we had little time, I had no choice but to agree.  The knight's name was _[[010-Knights-of-Arthurian-Britain#Lord Cynon|Sir Cynon]]_ as we found out on the way to Lady Ygraine's hall.  As we arrived, I saw Ygraine.  I knew it had to be her, as she was a beautiful, almost ageless, lady of regal bearing.

"Milady!  We have come to rescue you and our Sire from Cornwall!"

When Ygraine arched her eyebrow, blushingly I thought to add that Merffyn had chosen us to guide them to safety.  (It was not till later that I thought maybe she had been questioning my referral to Arthur as Sire.)  Asking pointedly if we intended her to leave her daughters behind, I sought to put her mind at ease on this and other matters.  I informed that Merffyn was on a ship out to sea from Tintagel, and that we would ride to him.  This time the raised eyebrow brought my reminder to her that we were talking of Merffyn.  In spite of Sir Cynon's admonitions that this was likely to be a trick by King Mark, Lady Ygraine agreed to come with us.

Following my behests that it was not a large vessel, Lady Ygraine chose for Arthur, _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^463384|Margawse]]_, _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^11871f|Morgaine]]_, a lady-in-waiting for a nursemaid, and Sir Cynon to come with us.  The rest of the Tintagel garrison could then yield to King Mark and would not suffer more, as they were not of gentle birth.  We gathered the few horses left in Tintagel, with most riding double, and joined Brion in the cave below the postern gate.  Now it was time for us to charge our way to freedom.

Brion grinned ferally as I ordered him to join me in leading our rush, and to ensure that the rest of our party would not be detained.  Outside of the cave, the remaining men had been joined by a few more, but more were on their way.  This was no time to delay!

Charging out, yelling out our war cries, we hoed into the men-at-arms.  They would not trouble Arthur, not while I lived.  As Brion and I split the Cornish men, Sir Hywel and Sir Cynon rushed the rest of our party between them and out to water.  As we hewed our hapless opponents down, I saw that the torches held by more of King Mark's men were almost down on the beach.  It was then that I dimly heard the sound of hooves striking on wood - the wood of the ship.  Yelling at Brion to come on, I pulled the horse around and rode out to the water and onto the water.  Looking behind me I could see Brion close behind.

In the mist, the side of the boat appeared suddenly and I pulled the horse into a leap that cleared the side.  As Brion's horse cleared the side, I could see Merffyn stumble and hold onto the mast nearby....  Rushing to his side, I saw he was almost as pale as the mist.  He told me to take Arthur to safety.  Merffyn was greatly weakened by the casting he had done.  He had to return to his home to rest.  Getting Hywel and Tathyl to help Merffyn to below decks, I ordered the captain of the ship to take us to Bath.

### A Vision of Avalon

The next day showed clear skies, with no signs of the mists from last night.  Merffyn came on deck and said, in a tired and strained voice, that he had to rest for a while.  As he spoke of the need to return to Avalon, I began to see some sort of vision.  To the south of us, the land was boggy and miry.  But at the same time, I began to see a green, fertile land with meadows and fine buildings rising quite high.  Merffyn pointed to where I was staring, and told me that was where he had to go.  With the words that he would see me and Arthur anon, he vanished!  As did the vision of Avalon.

### To Bath

The next day was when I broached the subject of the Lady Ygraine's disposition.  I told her that based on discussions with Merffyn, that I would be the guardian of Arthur until he reached his majority.  She then regally enquired as to what would happen to Margawse and Morgaine.  She informed that _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^463384|King Lot of the Orkneys]]_, some barbaric isles to the north I believe, had treated with her to wed Margawse.  I concurred with what had been done, and gave my oath to ensure that this was made so.

Of Morgaine, though, I was a bit mystified, as she seemed so precocious.  I swore that I would earnestly seek to ensure that Morgaine was looked after now, and when it came time for her to wed.  With these important considerations taken care of, the Lady Ygraine only wished to reside peacefully in an abbey.  She was tired of being fought over as a prize, rather than being treated as someone.  I recalled that Amesbury in Salisbury held an abbey and a monastery, and she found my suggestion agreeable.

The captain of the ship said we would make the port of Bath on the morrow, and the dawning new day showed his skill of piloting.  A river led through the Somerset marshes.  Passing the village of Bristol, we came on the main city port of Somerset, Bath.

Our ship docked at midday, and our party left the vessel to stay at a nearby house.  After thanking the captain, I spoke with Sir Cynon as to our travel plans.  We would require more horses for our party, as well as some travel supplies.  We would stay here until the morning.  Sir Cynon said that he was quite gifted with horses, so he should go to the hostellers to pick the best horses.  Sending Hywel for the supplies, and knowing a good deal about horses myself, I went with Cynon.  I saw him examine a few of the horses there, and I must admit they were all pretty poor horseflesh.  But Cynon was quite taken with two of the horses, but for the life of me I could not see why.  I would have placed them as eighth and ninth choices, but ... I hoped he knew what he was doing.

### To Calne

The next day saw us all riding east to Calne.  We passed through the village of Chippenham about the middle of the day.  And at the end of the day, we arrived at the fortified city of Calne.  We took over a hovel of a peasant just outside of Calne, as the gates had been closed for the night before we had arrived.  Everyone had been asleep for a while when I was woken by some sounds outside.  Taking my sword out with me as I went to check the horses, I saw a small bright wisp of light coming - it was Puck.

I do not know why, but he seems to like me.  Maybe it is because I always seem to be in the middle of an adventure.  Then again, maybe he just finds me a constant source of mirth.  Who knows the mind of a Fae?  I bespoke with him about our travails with the Red Cap in Cornwall, and Puck told me a little of the Unseelie Court and how Puck and Oberon were of the Seelie Court.  Unfortunately I think I failed to hold up my end of our conversation for I was much tired and had not slept yet since the last dawn.

Feeling little refreshed, I led my company from the outskirts of Calne eastwards.  But as the day wore on, so did the skies darken.  Hywel rode alongside me as the air started to have the tang of incipient rain.  He told me that it looked like it would be too bad to travel soon, and that we needed to find some shelter for our female companions.  Thanking him civilly for telling me what had been on my mind for the previous hour, we continued on at a harder pace.  By midafternoon, it was as dark as dusk and the heavens opened.  We rode a little more, but in a flicker of lightning saw that a building appeared to be off the road a way.

### Lady Leda and Lucienne at Avebury

As we approached, the rain cleared enough for us to see that we neared no building; we but advanced on some standing stones.  Slowing to a halt, I sent Sir Brion and Sir Cynon to investigate.  Although they were just tall stones with no roofing, they did provide some shelter from the pelting rain as it travelled sideways with the wind just as much as it came down.

All was quiet for a while until the call came for us to come, for all was clear.  Clear until Sir Brion called out that he had heard something, whereupon a lantern was lit nearby.  A tall foreign-looking knight of obvious noble bearing came forward bearing that lantern.  Announcing his name as _[[Lucifer-Morningstar|Lucienne]]_, he found we were a party escorting some women, and so announced that he was escorting the _[[Lillane-of-Clan-Bahari|Lady Leda]]_.

Sir Brion seemed to be quite taken with the newly-met pair, but I had my misgivings about them.  They said they had just arrived, but they had no horses.  They were lingering around standing stones that we were told was Avebury.  Based on our previous experiences with standing stones, I thought that this was all adding up to a questionable tale and that they were not necessarily plain mortals.  As our discourse continued, I made sure that I was always between Leda and Lucienne, and Arthur.  I would die before I allowed harm to come to him.

We camped there at Avebury, for travelling was certainly beyond the Lady Ygraine or Lady Margawse.  They were settling themselves for an extended stay here, until the storm blew itself out.  And so, I barely slept again, feeling miserable from the weather and the drawn out strain of always being on guard.  For I still harboured my suspicions, and also the Lady Margawse seemed quite taken with Sir Lucienne.  Before the Lady Ygraine took herself off to sleep, I thought to advise her to make sure the Lady Margawse stayed near her all night and nodded at Lucienne.

As dawn broke on an overcast day, our newly-met companions had disappeared with no word.  With no signs of their departure, I thought back on some of the stories I had heard as a youngster of "those" creatures of the night and shuddered in spite of myself.  I was no longer a timid youngster, but I had seen more that should only have been fables than I would have liked to.

### The Impregnable Marlborough Castle

Thankful that nothing untoward had happened, we travelled onwards to Mildenhall with the plan to turn south at the crossroads there to head south towards Amesbury Abbey.

Riding easily, by mid-afternoon we passed a track leading to a very fine castle on a large mound.  The castle was a hefty, square structure and looked very different from all others I had seen.  It also looked impregnable.  I had seen a number of castles in my travails with my father on his sojourns with the various lords, and this stronghold looked the most defensible and impressive.  Would that it were mine!

Out of all our company, looking around, I appeared to be the only one who showed reaction to the citadel ... but wait, Arthur also kept looking back at Marlborough.  Good!  It is meet that the future King should be interested in the noble arts of battle.

### On the Bridge to Mildenhall

All to soon, my short-lived good humour was chased by some buffoon on the bridge to Mildenhall.  I know in hindsight that my short-temper was from my lack of a good night's rest since Bath.  But at the time, I was incensed at this ...  But, I get ahead of the tale ....

As we approached the unwalled town of Mildenhall, we came to a bridge across a shallow stream that fed into the Kennet River to the south.  Even with the rains of the previous night, the stream was flowing slowly and was obviously shallow.  However, there was a bridge built by the Romans to continue this road from Bath all the way east to Londinium.  And on this bridge was an armoured knight astride his horse.  He was apparelled in most notably an odd fashion in red and black.  He also had couched a great lengthy spear.  He challenged us that none shall pass unless a knight was brave enough to fight him.

A flood of anger coursed through me.  How dare he challenge the party of the King!  I cried at him to ready himself as I drew my sword.  Spurring my charger forward, I found it oh so easy to avoid the lance.  A quick parry of the spear saw my sword swinging in the very same motion at the _[[010-The-Seasons#^146e71|Red and Black Knight]]_.  My blade went through the armour, beneath the raised arm holding the lance, and he went down with what looked to be a mortal blow.  The squires rushed to render assistance to this knave.  Still feeling the heat of my passion, I barely managed to command the squires to take him to Mildenhall with us as we must maintain our code of nobility.  For all his arrant trickery, he was still a knight.  I was informed that he would live, but would be helpless for many months.

### Mildenhall

The townsfolk seemed to vanish as we came into the town.  Looking around, it was obvious as to which building was the manor here.  Going there and banging in the door gained us little but sore fists, until the badly injured knight was brought forward.  The servant at the doorway seemed very presumptuous; that annoyed me, greatly!  The lady of the manor was initially ... well ... shrewish and arrogant until she saw her husband being carried in.  Then she seemed to be in a fever of activity.  Her crisp biddings to the servants to tend their lord showed who was steward of this town.  Other servants were sent to prepare a room for him.

However, my anger was exacerbated as she ignored us completely.  But as time passed, I began to calm myself for it was not meet to rail at someone who must be totally focused on the possible loss of her love and lord.  Then I started to realise some quite pertinent and important facts.  The Red and Black Knight was the Castellan of Marlborough Castle and the Steward of Mildenhall.  Unfortunately for him and his, he was in no condition to govern anything.  As these lands and demesnes were held beholden to no one, they would be taken by whoever had the strongest force at hand.

My travails with my father amongst the many lords of the lands conflicting with each other showed me what would happen to the lands themselves.  Also, these lands were very central to Logres.  It would be beneficial and auspicious if any conflicts were averted by an expeditious demonstration of puissance by someone with the strength of arms to hold it and who also had the favour of the neighbouring Earl of Salisbury.  I cannot allow the spread of discord through my King's lands … I must step forward and claim these offices.

### Return to Marlborough Castle

After asserting my privilege, we stayed in the manor overnight.  The following day, I led Sir Brion and Sir Hywel back west to Marlborough Castle.  I had to delve into this strange castle.  It was so much more than most of the others I had seen.  In fact, it rivalled (if not exceeded) all the great castles I had seen through Logres.  It was amazingly impressive ... but it did not have a great lord residing there!  I had not known that it would be so imposing, as I had barely heard anything about it, except it existed.  Mysterious indeed!

The stronghold was the most defensible fortification I had seen, located at the top of a large hill.  It had a remarkable cellar for storage and a large, very deep well.  The garrison men-at-arms were there, but no one could give an account for this state of affairs.  Well, they were just troops so they weren't likely to know.  But the people who were likely to know were either mortally wounded or not talking to us.  Frustrating!

The only thing Hywel managed to get from them was a rumour that the castle was placed over the grave of a mighty and terrible wizard.  As we rode back to the village, we laughed about that.  Surely that couldn't be so....

The next day was time to complete our final stages of our journey - escorting Lady Ygraine and her daughter Margawse to Amesbury Abbey.

21 Feb - night of the Feast of Birgit.  There are rumours spreading throughout the lands of Salisbury that there is an evil wizard imprisoned to the north and who is now almost free.  Gather the party together and head to Middleton.  Go to Gilbert (the Red Knight) and wife at manor and ask questions.  No new info except there's a (new) Druid in the forest.  Go to look for a Druid in forest.  Night has fallen and I hear some singing - see light - its somebody!  He doesn't give his name.  He appears to be a Druid who doesn't want to follow the Druid's strictures (he doesn't say so, but we can make up stories with the best of them).  He mentions he told them not to do it (build the castle there).  There is a necromancer entombed in the foundations!

The Druid gives hints as to who (Brion) can contact someone to help us - related to why / how / who the castle was built.  On way back from forest, we camp in hills.  Brion goes to pray.  While starting, Lady Leda comes and after conversation, she offers to help when Brion then offers all us knights present in a service for her.  I'm on watch when Brion returns.  When questioned he says we will have help.  Upon further questioning and prodding by me and Cynon (when he awakens), we draw out who's going to help and why.  We is pissed and surly.

Next day we go to Middleton.  Tell Aneurin to stay as Edar comes with us to Marlborough.

At castle, we wait till nightfall. We're down in the cellar when the steward comes and tells me of visitors.  I go up and welcome Leda and the quiet one of her companions (Lucienne) to my castle.  Offer wine.  Lucienne accepts and compliments me on it and seems to make some strange teasing of it with Leda.  We then go down to the cellar.

They examine it.  Lucienne, Brion and I go down the well.  Lucienne first, does something and sends the bucket back up.  I'm next and he has moved the seal and is in the tunnel.  When Brion arrives down here, we go forward.  Lucienne instructs us to keep anything from distracting him.  Lucienne's in front and us behind and to the sides.

When Lucienne reaches intersection, bag o' bones wizard is behind us.  I go forward and attack him but as I give him a mighty blow, he moves his arm and I am flying back.  Remembering my task, I tumble to avoid Lucienne.  I bounce up and run back when one of my dead brothers appears before me.  Brion goes to fight the Wiz and one of his dead cousins pops up.  As we fight our relations, another relation pops up.  The Wiz does nothing.  I kill one of my brothers, and Brion kills one of his cousins.  The tunnel has been getting steeper.  A gaping pit had opened in the centre of the tomb.

Early in the fight, I had glanced back at Lucienne.  He was now looking like a demon (big, red, horned!).  Behind us a pit had opened up in the centre of the tomb.  The tomb had a rounded (domed) ceiling.  Things had been getting hotter and hotter.  I was ignoring the heat but Brion seemed to be boiling away.

When we only had one ghost each fighting us, Brion made an attempt to dodge past his cousin and reach the Wiz, but he slipped as he tried and fell back down the tunnel towards the pit and fell over the side into it.  Angered at the loss of my companion, tried to do the same (dodge my brother and attack the Wizard, not fall in the pit).  I did it successfully.  As I got to him, he had been seemingly much more affected by the heat than us.  He'd been smoking earlier, then smouldering.  He soon burst into flames.  Looking back Brion was climbing out of the pit.  Lucienne led us back to the well.  Brion went up first, then me, then Lucienne.

When I got to the cellar, Brion was being given aid by Cynon, though Cynon seemed to be making some mistakes, then he helped.  Though I was still slightly discomfited, I felt quite good.  Feeling that my wounds would be better in a few days.  Brion seemed quite the worse for wear and would probably need a few weeks.

Cynon, Brion and Edar went to bed.  I stayed up for a while and drank wine with Lucienne.  Not drinking to excess, I stayed sober and plied Lucienne on his knowledge of the lands.  Using my knowledge of intrigue (surprisingly good), I seemed to find the right way of asking for the information for Lucienne seemed quite ready to tell me everything I wanted to know.  King Mark was not moving any further than where he was, no Briton lords were making moves of invasion or large raids, the _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^52ad1e|Duke of Silchester]]_ would not be averse to a change of ownership in Levcomagus if the current lord of it could be proven to be treasonous, the Saxons were just as likely as ever to attack some Briton lord (either Silchester or Salisbury).  Then I retired for the night.

When we awake Brion has left for his home at dawn.  I awake at dawn and send servant to warn knights and men-at-arms I will be inspecting them in an hour.  I do so.  They aren't good, but they're not Vagon level.  They have a brain between them.

### Matthew and the Banshees

After a while Cynon wakes up, he suggests we go to town to check out the current status of the haunted boy (_[[010-The-Seasons#^2a3e63|Matthew]]_).  We do so and find the villagers looking a bit haggard from poor sleep.

Cynon has to make a trip to White Horse in the north.  I'll head to Amesbury with boy to find some way of saving him from his doom.  Before going, Edar wants to know if I want anything done.

I head off with two of the Earl's knights, squires and boy to Upavon.  Camp in hills past town, so the town won't be haunted.  That night, about 11, two banshees come.  I get between them and the boy.  They approach to a point and stay at that distance.  I go to ward them off and they seem to sense my presence, but not my physical one - my spiritual one.  They move by darting quickly around the place so I can't ward them away.  Waving my sword through them doesn't do anything, so I go back to Matthew.  Up close and yell an encouraging speech at him to hearten him and then I try to go to sleep (ignoring the banshees).

Go to Amesbury and speak to the _[[010-The-Seasons#^d83703|Abbess]]_ about the problem.  At the word of banshee, she shoots through and sends for the Abbot.  He agrees to try and help.

I head off to Sarum to see how's Arthur doing.  Cover story is he's my nephew.  Speak to Earl and tell him what's happened.  We put the Wiz to rest with the help of the same people who put the Wiz to rest before.  Also thank him for his aid.  Tell him of what I have learnt from Lucienne (concerning the politics of the land).

Head home to Raven's Gate.  When Jeanne sees me, she appears first astounded then angered.  She looks at the middle of my forehead and asks who marked me.  I lead her inside and tell her the full tale of my travels - paying attention to the Red Cap, Arthur and family, Morgaine, the henges, Leda and Lucienne, Marlborough and the Wiz, the ex-druid and the banshees, and Brion's promise in return for Leda's aid.  I have a mark on me such that whoever put it on me could make a call on me and I would do it / go straight-away.  Thinking it was Leda, who I'm super suspicious of anyway, I ask for it to be removed from me after the festival.

### Spring Equinox

21st March - morning.  Leave a-horseback with Jeanne, bring harp and head southwest.  We pass lots of people who are heading in the same direction late in the day.  Greet Jeanne respectfully by name.  We arrive at nightfall.  Having experienced prior Spring Equinox festivals, I know there is supposed to be equivalent numbers of men and women.  I notice she pulls her Raven cloak out of nowhere.

Everybody is camped out around a chalk man.  Bonfires everywhere.  For actual rites, those couples who want fertility come forward and the ladies (one at a time) sit on the penis area.  Jeanne cuts their hand and they drip blood on the chalk and then rub their hand where the blood fell.  The guys go lay down on the club (for martial and sexual prowess).  After missing Jeanne's signals, she drags me to the club.  I feel a little better afterwards, but notice little difference and Jeanne seemed slightly disappointed.

I notice one of the other guys making moon-eyes at me.  From Roman stories I know of such things, but I'm not interested.  I move to stand by Jeanne.  After she's done cutting hands, she comes to me and takes me a bit aside.  She says because of her gravid state, she can't share this with me.  She cuts my hand and I lose myself (in reality I turn into a raven and fly and hunt and stuff).  In the hour before dawn, I return.

Hywel returns from Lothian in June.  King Lot is sending an escort for Margawse.  2nd week in June, two knights from King Lot arrive and take Margawse - she hates me now.

July - my first son, _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^bf9597|Caradoc]]_, is born
