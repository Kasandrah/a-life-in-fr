# Rhodri's Tales - 514 AD

**Written by [[Ben-G|Ben G.]] (Rhodri)**

Arthur is 21, Caradoc is 17, Nia is 15 and Gwyn and Gwen are 14

Wintertime:  The Fomori Ambassador arrives at the court at Carlion soon after the New Year has been celebrated.  Arthur is formally betrothed to _[[Angelica|Maeve]]_.

With the Jagent leaders eliminated in the Dorset campaign, Arthur makes Rhodri Earl of those lands and Warden of what is becoming known as Morgaine's Forest.  This is to provide a strong buffer to the troubles in Cornwall.  Everybody knows that it is in the middle of a civil war, but while nothing definite is known, it is widely believed that Mark is winning.  His is the only name that everyone considers has the strength to win all of those lands.  Also there are stories of the Sunken Lands from beyond the Scilly's providing some powers (on Mark's behalf, according to him).

I organize Jagent so Mark won't want to risk trying to expand his lands northwards, and the new Praetor of Dorset is being set-up as a place for dissident Christians - "special" people.

Caradoc kings it up in the North.  He sends word he too has proposed to Guinevere.  Jeanne and I are really concerned.  We fear that Guinevere is predisposed to causing knights / lords to fight over her.  Caradoc is totally enamoured / obsessed with her.  We (mostly Jeanne to provide the most horrid parts) see that Caradoc could become twisted and evil (a possible future necromancer and priest of Raven).  Being a priest of Raven will protect him from the wiles of the Witch of the North, but the changes to him would be from within.

I point out to Jeanne that if I talk to him, he would use what I say as a spur to go further on his path, or just ignore me and anything I ever say again.  Jeanne is distraught and in a flap.  I then suggest we may not be able to change Caradoc's path, but perhaps we could somehow change Guinevere's.  Could we convince her to be faithful and in love with Caradoc?  Although I asked rhetorically, Jeanne gets a glint in her eye and says that she can't but that kind of magic is of the Fae.  She smiles beatifically and I receive the instruction to deal with the Fae to obtain the spell.  A little off-putting as Jeanne had always chastised me for any dealing with the Fae.

The spell must be very intricate, subtle, long-lasting (as long as Caradoc is doting on her).  Where she is faithful and doting on him, in fact, hopefully so subtle that Caradoc will never know.  I know of one of the Fae who owes me such a great favour, that he would help me with such a thing … and who would be quite appropriate to provide help - Angus Og, the bard of the Fae.  I visit him and ask if he can repay the favour.  Angus is quite happy to and smugly tells me that I am extremely lucky because he was the best choice I could have made … but I knew that already.

I have been quite lucky throughout my life, but I may not have been quite wise throughout my life.  It could possibly be considered that I have been so little wise, that when I do have some wisdom I recognize it myself.

Success - Arthur will go to Caradoc's wedding after his own.

In February Brennos gets a scribe to write to Arcavius with news of Titania's new (and only) son and heir - _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^c00cba|Asterlan]]_, _[[Oberon|Oberon's]]_ target if he knew Brennos was Asterlan's father.  Arcavius and Brennos use Blaen to correspond on the issue.  Amongst many other things, it is expressed that Titania claims Oberon is the father of Niani's daughter!  Possibly Oberon knows Asterlan's parentage and has already had his revenge on Brennos.  Another thing is the possibility of fostering Asterlan with Arthur and Maeve.  Brennos is keen for his son not to be a pawn of the Fae.

As time passes, Brennos and Niani have a falling out.  Niani seems to have placed blame and onus and demands on Brennos.  Brennos writes to Arcavius that had he known Niani wanted a child, he would have warned her and tried to protect her from Oberon, even at the cost of his own life.  As it was, he thought Niani would continue to use her plant magic to prevent becoming with child.  But Niani had taken a step that was not under their arrangement, had not told Brennos of her intention, and left herself vulnerable to Oberon's deception.

In addition, Niani is still angry at Brennos for being "willingly deceived" by Titania, when it is possible, at least according to Titania, that Oberon has done exactly the same thing to Niani.  Brennos wonders that, if so, Niani knew it was really Oberon in her bed and went ahead.  Under that intimate circumstance, how could she not have known?  Brennos is now faced with the prospect of raising a daughter of his enemy in his own house.  The sooner she can be fostered out, the better.

Or perhaps Brennos could use Niani's daughter as leverage against Oberon?  Arcavius advises against this, as Oberon appears to know Brennos is Asterlan's father.  Both children shouldn't be made to suffer at the hands of vengeful step-parents.  I agree with Arcavius in this, and have asked Brennos to leave the children out of any future dealings with the Fae.  If we cannot even protect the children, we don't deserve to be Knights.

Late April.  Niani sends a message to Jeanne and I that she wants to return to her own lands.  Then later, she changes her mind as she and Brennos have a brief, passionate make-up, and then she changes her mind back as they suddenly break up again.  Jeanne tires of this back-and-forth and is considering withdrawing her offer to return Niani and Brennos to their otherworld.  I see the effort it takes Jeanne to maintain her preparedness for this great magic, and wish Niani would decide one way or the other, soon.

Arthur marries Maeve in May.  Caradoc marries Guinevere in June.
