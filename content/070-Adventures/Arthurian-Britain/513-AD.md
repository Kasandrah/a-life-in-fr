# Rhodri's Tales - 513 AD

**Written by _[[Ben-G|Ben G.]]_ (Rhodri)**

No childbirth.  Nia gets sick and cousin goes missing.  Arthur is 20, Caradoc is 16, Nia is 14 and Gwyn and Gwen are 13

Winter Time:  To keep an eye on things with Morgaine, she was invited to spend the winter with us.  As we expected, she soon showed signs of being pregnant.  Jeanne had noticed the signs much sooner than I did.  I didn't want to face Arthur as I wanted to minimize any half-truths, so I sent a messenger that Morgaine was due in March and she hadn't said who the father was.  When a conversation with Jeanne turned onto the topic, I said that I would not countenance any harm to mother or child - no matter how much the mother might deserve it.

In meetings with my friends, we can't really decide how to handle Morgaine.  We talk around it a lot, but we eventually conclude that Morgaine did this because _[[Oberon|Oberon]]_ wanted a balance between the Fae Courts.  Ailil had a grandchild who could be heir to Arthur, so Oberon had to have one as well.  We hoped that Morgaine had no other plans or concerns on this child.  Arthur sent a message back saying he wanted to know who the father was (… that could do this to his king, perhaps?) and that she must be married.

It's agreed that Arthur needs to know more about the situation of Morgaine's existing marriage, so I travelled to court as soon as we had come to a decision, to meet with Arthur.  Although I prefer action to talking, this was a time when I wanted to continue talking.  Arthur does what I expected he would do when I sent the original message, he continues to probe until he asks me outright who I thought the father was.  With the only choices being the truth and an outright lie, I have no choice at all with Arthur - I couldn't lie to him.

I tell him he will be unhappy with the answer, but he pushes and I tell him that Morgaine put on the seeming of someone else and seduced the father.  He still pressed and didn't understand the first time I said she had put on the seeming of someone he loved and seduced the father.  Then as he was coming to terms with that and the very definite realization that this could not be acknowledged publicly, I had to tell him that Morgaine was married to Ailil.

Arthur felt betrayed that I hadn't told him before and I gave him some excuses which I had felt were valid, but felt guilty about it anyway … as I hadn't told him.  Arthur was quite angry as well for involving the Fae with Morgaine.  But this was not something I had engendered.  It was an almost inevitable thing based on Morgaine's heritage and the ongoing rivalry between Oberon and Ailil, which I also told Arthur of.  Although this is an important point, I knew that Arthur wasn't ready to let go of the anger he felt.

Later, the group gathers again to discuss preventing a similar thing and we decide that at the very least Arthur needs some protection from Fae illusions (like _[[Angelica|Maeve's]]_ natural protection), so a trip to Goibniu is planned.

Returning to Arthur, he had me go through a list of possible foster parents for his new child, as obviously Morgaine had to be returned to her husband.  Jeanne, knowing as usual that this topic would come up long before it did, had already said to me that she didn't want to raise another child, and Merffyn was only a laughable option.  Lucio and Leda were not really acceptable (they had no consideration for Britain, per se) and Voklaynn and Anwyn.  But we had already asked them for a lot of favours in the past.  Arthur wanted me to ask but I convinced him to go himself to pay a visit and to ask for a foster-parenting for his child.  When he does so, she accepts with what I felt was the sighing acceptance of a mother have been gifted with a pet by her child.

We ask to be returned to Goibniu (you know, save us a trip) and there Arthur gives blood and passes out.  Goibniu has a so-smug expression on his face when receiving the blood of the High King of the Land.  This is valuable stuff for him, so we prevail on his pride on his very obviously renowned skills to make sure that Arthur received a broach which would allow him an extended kind of protection against the Fae, as well as something to aid him in the hoped-for rebuilding of the Empire and to see through things to see them as they really were - to be a Caesar.  After much unconscious frowning, Goibniu's face lightens and a smirk flutters across his face.  What he produces is exquisite - it is an extremely ornate broach of intertwined dragons which has the exact colour of Arthur's eyes.

During the early Spring, we began our final plans for our attack on the Saxons holding Dorset.  Although initially we plan for another huge force in excess of 100,000 as we had the previous summer against the Northern Kings, but the scouting done in the Autumnal months of last year has allowed us to think that the Saxons have, at most, 25,000 men there which, if we moved quickly enough with a similar number would be enough to catch them in their individual strongholds before they could gather their forces together.

Another issue is the creatures that protect the forest from the Saxons.  Would they attack us, ignore us, or could we get them to help us?  After speaking with Jeanne and sounding her out about them, she smiles enigmatically when she answers that we would have to negotiate with them and (broadening her smile) that we must have Niani's help.  I assume that it is because she knows that Niani is going to baulk just to gainsay Jeanne, but I find out later that it was for another, more amusing, reason.  I gather Niani, and bring her into the discussions.

Niani appears to be acting quite intractable and refuses to understand why her help is especially needed.  Jeanne creates a few points which raise some notions amongst the rest of us, but Niani can't seem to join the dots.  Even though she still doesn't understand why, she finally is convinced enough to agree to come with us.  Before leaving, I must tell Arthur of my plan to talk with the forest's protectors.  He is extremely reluctant to allow us to leave his realm until I tell him we sought to gain the forest's aid in fighting the Saxons.

We enter the forest with no metal.  Amils wants to bring his horse and I can't feel comfortable without the weight of a weapon, so I bring a practice (wooden) sword, but essentially we are "unarmed".  After two hours travel, which was a nice walk for all of us except for Amils whose ride seemed a constant string of suffering which culminated in him splitting his head open on a branch, Niani swears she heard lots of girls giggling.

We come to a clearing where some women are waiting for us.  These women all look like a lot like Niani, and are literally walking out of the trees.  I emphasize this point, they came out of the trees themselves.  They are all inhumanly beautiful … and suddenly it dawns on me that they are dryads.  They greet Jeanne politely as a sister and then Niani almost exuberantly as a beloved sister.

We discuss their possible assistance to us, which becomes quite impassioned in some.  Arcavius promises Dorset will not encroach on the forests anymore and that there will be more men passing through the forests (!).  The first offer was in the process of being belittled as dependent on the length of mortal memories, but the second halted the discussion … at least on our side.  After a short burst of heated argument amongst them, it was suddenly announced that they would help us.  But conditions were set upon us, our army must not take any wood from the trees themselves.  Only dead wood which had already fallen to the forest floor was acceptable.  All our army would also wear a particular kind of leaf to symbolize who we were, so the forest would not attack us and would know if one of us broke the agreement.  If any of our army transgressed this rule, then all would be punished.

Right from the start, all of us men are treated as slabs of meat.  Jeanne's got me tagged, as Niani does Brennos, even though we continued to look.  Amils, though, is just being courteous (as well as his native flirtatiousness) but he has just encouraged them and they take him away.  After a little while, we do negotiate him back.  Arcavius is quite handsome, in addition to being a chaste, married Christian - which must be the ultimate temptation for a dryad.  All the dryads work hard, but Arcavius leaves them a quivering, sweating, cowering, chaste, married Christian.

On our return, we pass on the news of the alliance and its obligations.

We launch our attack west from Sarum, through to Ilchester in the lands of Jagent.  Take opportunity to gain their fealty - but I suggest we take our new followers and leave an honorary guard in Ilchester.  Suddenly, rather than bringing their weakest, they're taking their strongest as they realize they (themselves) are coming.

We travel SE from Ilchester to Dorchester.  The forests seem easy to travel through - but some of the squires (including my new second string squire, Aeron, 16) have become guests of the dryads for an indeterminate time.

Army approaches Dorchester town.  We mock and challenge Saxons and they attack out of the town.  They're outnumbered and the forest fights against them.

First round.  Each has single Saxon warrior, next round two warriors, then three warriors and a chieftain.  I've killed four warriors and captured the chieftain.  Arcavius is using his Saxon-killer sword that screams when used against the Saxons.  It seems to take something from them and he is being given a wide enough band of space as possible.  Niani / Brennos find it slightly tougher, not being able to finish opponents as cleanly.

Emils fought with a lance near Arthur, who was also very effective.

Arcavius marched to gateway with Niani / Brennos and I following.  In the block hole as Saxons were trying to flee us and others were trying to come out.  They made one last push with a berserker who died before Arcavius.  I took one chieftain's blow and slew another chieftain as I went down.  Niani took a chieftain.  Arcavius turned on the chieftain who'd knocked me down just as I flashed a mighty, mighty blow.

We move through gates in the confusion and the Saxon council of Elders use one of their ex-slaves to tell us they surrender Dorchester to us as the chieftains are all dead and their King Port is on the Isle of Wight.  We accept initially for Arthur, but arrange for a more formal surrender to Arthur.

We try and get some info on the Saxons of the past - how they're all different tribes.  Praetor Jonathel of Dorset has been taken to no one knows where.  Other than the three forts we knew of to the east, there are no more Saxon strongholds.

We discussed that we would need to get Port to attack us or we could try to get to him.  Logistically, and for many other reasons, it would preferably be the former.  We could tempt him by saying that _[[010-The-Seasons#^9a36d4|Sissa's]]_ slayer was here, but that might attract Cerdic's attention a year or two earlier than I wanted, or by saying that the holders of "the Spear" were here (pushing on their desire for that Great Treasure for their allies), or by just insulting and challenging him, or that Arthur was here.

We could also use ransom to try and bargain for the Praetor Jonathel's return.

The messenger we'd send would be one of our prisoners and he had overheard some of the rumours and the insults / challenges to Port (i.e. he hides behind his women).

Saxon woman asks for audience with Arthur.  Well dressed woman and three kids are ushered in on Arthur's agreement.  Annifred is the widow of the chieftain I slew at the gate.  She says in her custom, she has now become mine.  There is great amusement at this situation, but no open laughter or ridicule.  I accept and place her under the command and demesne of my wife, for I feel she will be able to use this more astutely (politically) than I would … and more importantly, I love my wife and her opinion of me.  Also, even I can see that Annifred appears to have some calculated designs on me that I think Jeanne will be able to convince her would be inappropriate.  Again also, the Saxon peoples are going to be / are a part of Britain.  They must be made part of the Neo Roman Empire that is Britain and the change has to be started as soon as possible.

Ambassador solution?!  Our initial aim was to get Ailil to take Morgaine as our ambassador.  The positive is that it takes her away from us, and not in Unseelie command.  Negative is she's going to do something to stir things up.

We knew that if Ailil was going to have an embassy, Oberon would want one.  So for the Seelie we try and think of who we know that we trust absolutely and have a strong enough sense of self to do good.  Three names come up (Caradoc, Cynon and Hywel).  Caradoc's impetuosity, etc rule him out, and Cynon has a few too many issues here and there to be good.  Hywel has the Lady Etleyenne issue, but is 54 and has no ties here bar with me and my household (and there was the issue of Guinevere and the Fish's vision).

Arcavius gets all Roman crafty again and suggests we create an embassy with Voklaynn and let Oberon see so he'll ask for one and then Ailil will have to ask for one as well.  For the embassy with Voklaynn, we can use Cynon - friend to Voklaynn already, as well as Champion of Epona.  Voklaynn also has strange powers with time so he may not have to give up his life in this realm totally.

We give recommendations to Arthur and he is willing to try them.  He orders Cynon to do so (I expect he tried to refuse, but could not deny his king).  He (Cynon) vanishes.

I had tried to get us to think of an alternative to Morgaine, if that was possible - but couldn't think of anyone who could cope or be suited.  Except it would have to be someone who we might want to get rid of, maybe.

After a few days, we move from Dorchester to SE - Vexallation Fort.  I have a 2nd squire who's doing a perfect job, so mid-morning I face up to him and praise his work and bluntly ask who he is.  He says he's offended that I don't recognize him.  I point out to him that his skills in disguise far outweigh my ability to see through them.  Switching suddenly from offended to satisfied instantly, he tells me gleefully he is Puck.

He brings word that Oberon is offended that we haven't tried forming an embassy with him, so obviously our plan to entice Oberon into asking for the embassy by seeing someone else (like Voklaynn already having one) has worked.  I act like a diplomat (I can do it sometimes) very successfully and keep buttering up Puck and guide him to Arthur's command tent to "talk" straight-away to Arthur (but I know he doesn't sleep in there).  Arthur is obviously not there, as I had hoped, so I tell Puck to wait and I will get Arthur and our friends.  I track down Arthur sparring with _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^9bb92d|Lancelot]]_.  Absolutely no blood drawn, but Lancelot barely has a sheen over his flesh while Arthur is sweating.  Not the time to really distract Arthur, so I track the team down.  We discuss who we might like from the Seelie with Brennos who concludes that a Count Valen would be our best choice (has favour with Oberon and Titania), even though he is an arrogant prick.  We go to get Arthur and warn him of the embassy request.

Greetings are exchanged and Arthur silently forces Puck to present all his messages before he really responds.  Puck offers us a choice of 3 ambassadors - the first man offered, Brennos signs "no way" / "over my dead body", the second man offered is Count Valen, the least worst - the third offer was a female who Brennos didn't recognize.  We / Arthur selects Count Valen and we will select someone to return with Puck.

Puck leaves and soon enough, a commotion begins outside the command tent.  A new visitor has arrived.  Our new guest introduces himself as Count Valen - very richly dressed in silver armour and lots of ornate, silver jewellery (including over one ear).  He's as big as Arcavius.  He gives a slight (yet barely respectful) bow to Arthur, nods to most of us except for Niani (whose hand he bends over and kisses) and Brennos (whom he ignores totally).  Brennos may only have been annoyed by Valen before, but now he is incensed / angered.  Valen has arrived with his riding horse and another carrying his pack.  He, of course, grabs servants to serve him.  He makes himself very comfortable.

Speak to Hywel about him being Arthur's embassy to the Court of the Seelie, and he acts reluctant, but I have known him for many years and I can tell he is glad (even excited) to go.  But heartfelt goodbyes are said for with the Fae, he could be gone shortly or for 100s of years - maybe never to return.  But we only tell each other "until the next time we meet".

Having had this exchange of embassages with the Seelie, it was only a matter of time before we would have a visitation (from either Merffyn or Ailil's henchmen).  As the tent is the only obvious place in the camp for leaders, so Arcavius and I decide to sleep there that night.

While we are waiting, I hear the soft susurration of a blade being dropped / drawn through the tent fabric, like silk from a sword.  It is the long pole-arm of the Red-Cap who doesn't act really surprised when I greet him.  His style of persuasion is different from Puck's - he's more threatening.  We convince him that we can make the deal if an issue is resolved.  Arthur is dissatisfied with an "issue" that will prevent him from agreeing and the Red Cap eventually agrees to deal with Morgaine appropriately. After telling us that he'd heard rumours and believes them true.  We are told that Lord Otho will be Ailil's ambassador to us, and we decide that we would send Sir Sagamore of Vagon Castle and the Red-Cap tells us he would be "picked up".  Lord Otho's name sounds familiar to me, but I can't quite place it.  Red Cap looking at me with a twisted smile, tells me I amuse him and he advises us not to talk about the ambassador's appearance as he wouldn't like it and then Red-Cap leaves.

We've been advised of Lord Otho's coming, but it's not too soon before his actual arrival that I recall he was Ortho the Troll who was Ailil's doorman - big as a medium-size Fomori, but not too bright.  A big hand soon sweeps in through the hole the Red-Cap made into the tent.  We talk - mostly we talk, as Otho's not so big on words - he's a 'Bassador.  I tell him he can do pretty much as he was doing before, but he couldn't fight anyone in our court, or any other Ambassador, but he could attack our enemies.  He was happy at the promotion to Lord and at the chance at fighting (Saxons, but mostly Fomori).  We then feed him a cow - he eats any meat but horse (hey, he's no Fomori!) and shortly afterwards sleeps and snores loudly.

We go and tell Arthur's guard (as Arthur is asleep) everything on our new Ambassador, so they can tell Arthur when he awakens.  This is to allow Arthur to act as surprised as and when he wants to when he meets the new Ambassador, without really being surprised - much more dignity that way.

When we all gathered for morning meeting and Valen has made his appearance.  He says slept soundly through the night.  Niani, Brennos and I are wearing silver Fae armour.  We can see Niani's and Brennos' armour is even more ornate than Valen's was, so he is just wearing fine clothing this morning.  Otho comes up behind Valen and is greeted by Arthur.

Valen goes very pale when he spins around and stops breathing.  Otho is introduced and Otho returns the greetings with a blank smile and a long, long pause.  Valen sighs away his current breath and restarts his breathing.  Looking at him, I think he understands a number of the reasons behind his selection and Otho's … and he is not very happy about it.

In all the talking that follows, we push our new Ambassadors to find out what involvement they were willing to provide in our ongoing dealings with the Saxons and their Fomori allies.  Otho will fight.  Valen is only willing to give advice and battle co-ordination, but not get involved in actual fighting.

Our week of peace and politicking is coming to a close.  Niani received a delegation of dryads who told her that there were Saxons in the forest.  There are numbers of hunting parties and scouts, but they are not the important ones.  There is also large band of Saxons forming (~1,000).  After they leave she comes and tells the War council (Arthur and all of the inner circle).

Instructions are spread throughout the army that we will be fighting soon in the forest, and that it was essential that no-one should do any harm to the forest.  Stories of the inner circle and of our adventures have been spread through the land over the years, so the army do pay attention.  Also, this was an instruction that was given fairly regularly to the men - practically daily.  Now Otho is told too, and very strenuously.

We lead our men and, with directions provided by our friends in the forest to the lady they seem to admire, we encircle the large band of Saxons.  In the process, we can see that there don't seem to be that many leaders amongst the band.  In the fighting that follows, our leadership group fights directly to the Saxon chieftains.  I deal with three of them personally while the entire Saxon war band is wiped out.

Otho is charging around afterwards looking for more fighting.  Thankfully, he has listened to us and hasn't attacked the forest.  We entice him with food to stop his rampaging, and then we praise his fighting and keep talking until he looks calm.

After even the crows have settled to their spoils, a bard arrives, striding across the battlefield.  I recognize him as Blaen, the bard-in-training who is a valued friend of Voklaynn.  Oddly enough, I remember he played that birthday game, the one with the ball, very well.  He heads directly to Arthur, greeting him and passing on felicitations from Voklaynn.  He is Voklaynn's ambassador to Arthur's realm.  He gives Arthur a gift from Voklaynn, one that is boxed, packed and locked.  On opening, I am amazed at how apt my odd memory was as it is a perfect ball … just like the one that was played with at the birthday party.  I think Voklaynn is trying to spread this game.

After returning to our camp that night, the next day sees Count Valen quietly politicking with Arthur about fighting the Saxons.  He is talking how killing all the Saxons (genocide) will cost way too many lives.  Arcavius, once he got over his vitriolic hatred of the Saxons .. or at least was talked to a standstill on the matter, is barely able to admit that there has to be another way.  I just believe we need to smash any armies that oppose us and annex the Saxon people.

Valen's also questioning why we're not trying to separate the Fomori from the Saxons.  We have talked about it before, but other adventures always cropped up.  Our current priority is Dorchester, according to Arcavius, and when Valen turns to Arthur, Arthur looks at Arcavius obviously is thinking of his promise as he agrees.  But one interesting fact arose from the roundtable discussion was that the Fomori were allied to the Saxons because the Saxons had promised the Spear of the lost Fae Great Treasures would be theirs.

Resuming our march, we are approaching the fort Dunum.  There are two Fomori, one big and the other of … Arcavius' size at the head of another war band of Saxons.  Leaning over, Arcavius asks whether we've ever given a personal challenge to the Fomori.  I believe Brennos and myself would win in such a challenge.  Although a lot of questions were raised, the idea takes on a life of its own.  Valen offers to pass on the challenge.  Arcavius confirms with Blaen if he also speaks Fomori and then gets him to let Valen know so that Valen won't be tempted to "betray" us.

Valen goes and does his spiel - a lot longer than the basic gist that we gave him and Blaen reports that he is actually impressed at Valen's command of Fomori insults.  The big Fomori is in a heated discussion with the smaller one.  Then there's a heated discussion with the head Saxon chieftain of the war band.  The small Fomori comes and gets more details from our side.  He returns back to his leader and their heated discussion continues.  Then Valen says, well … how are you going to prove it.  Both of the giants come to our side and ask for proof.  Arcavius gathers all the Christians he can in our army and gets them to pray en-masse to ask the Archangel Michael to turn up with the Spear of the lost Fae Great Treasures.  He declares vehemently that he doesn't summon him … but the Christian god's minion does appear in between the two armies and stalks between them bearing the Spear.

After the minion returns to whence it came, the Fomori start roaring, pull out their weapons and start hewing into the Saxons near them.  We charge to attack the Saxons, trying to ensure our group gets to support the Fomori and prevent too many Saxons from ganging up on them and tearing them down.  Surprisingly enough, the Saxons break after only a little bit of fighting as the giants (who the Saxons had expected to lead the fight against us) and us have destroyed the Saxon's armies leaders.

Post-battle, we talk more directly with the giants through Blaen.  I tell them to pass the word of what they've seen back to their king, but they looked pretty blank.  Before they go, I ask if Arthur wants to take this opportunity of dialogue with the Fomori to arrange anything.  I am hoping for at least an agreement of peace between us.  Arthur puts on a little sly look and speaking directly through Blaen to the Fomori a little apart from the rest, an arrangement is made with the Fomori.  Arcavius and I assume that this is extension of the offer of ambassadors.  Arcavius convinces Blaen to advise the Fomori that they will not be offered horse here in our lands.

The Fomori leave and Arthur's inner circle go into more post-battle discussions.  We think that the Fomori will attack the Saxons in the future.  How, when and where we have no idea.  To all remaining Saxons in Dorset, an offer is made to allow them to leave peacefully in the next few weeks.  After the period of grace, all remaining must either submit or …

Essentially, the reconquest of Dorset is complete.
