## Catching Up

After some time passed, we were able to talk ... and a lot of news was heard.  I can see that bowl-shaped haircuts and clean-shaven are in vogue, just by looking around.  I hear from Arthur and Caradoc of other things that are now in use.  Reinforced chain mail is now used with closed helms (obviously by those who are rich), weapons such crossbows, lances, morning-stars and war-flails are more in use now.  The general horse stock of the land appears to have improved, as well.

Arthur has been organizing for the building of some keeps across the south of Salisbury, which I'm surprised to find are being financed by Edar (!?!) in my name.  The style is no longer the Motte-and-bailey, but now they are square, stone ones.  From Caradoc, I'm finding also that melee's are also becoming commonplace; melee's just for glory or for profit.  All extremely interesting (especially the keep building … and the fact I have that much money to afford it), but what has happened across the lands.

The year we left, Saxons led by Port had taken Dorset.  Although this was independent from Cerdic's forces, in time they soon succumbed to the rule of Cerdic.  In 503, the Saxons besieged London, which then surrendered rather than be sacked.  The following year, the Picts invaded most of the Kingdoms of the North.  In 507, the Perilous Forest in Cumbria became inundated with Picts, and 2 years ago (in 508) King Cerdic expanded northwards, closer to Levcomagus.  However, in that same year, Duke Corneus seized London back.

Also, on New Year's Day this year (510), a convocation of British Lords and church officials have called for a tournament to be held in London.  The winner of which would be declared the High King. (This is practically the first of these "French things" to be held.)  Present is King Alain of Escavalon, King Leodogrance of Camelaird, King Barant de Apres of Malahaut (the King of the Hundred Knights), King Lot of Lothian and Orkney, Duke Escan of Gloucester, Duke Corneus of Lindsey, Earl Sanam of Bedegraine, Earl Robert of Salisbury, Earl Gilbert of Hertford and Earl Macsen of Lonazep.

The tournament is never finished, because the melee is interrupted by the crowd shouting, "The Sword!  The Sword!"  Everyone dashed away, abandoning the fight to see what is going on.  Upon entering the already-crowded court of St Paul's Cathedral, it can be seen that Merffyn, Archbishop Dubricus, Jeanne, Arthur, Caradoc and Cynrain are there.  All noblemen who try to draw the sword fail, but keep trying for another chance.  From the crowd comes the cry of "Let the boy try!" which is then taken up by everyone.

Arthur steps up to the sword and, with a flourish, pulls the sword out and brandishes it overhead.  Celestial trumpets blare, a beam of light shines down from above and a dove and an eagle fly upward in a spiral (Merffyn up to his tricks again).  It is in the hubbub afterwards that Lot bellows out "What?!  Are we to be ruled by a beardless bastard?"  Most of the greater barons and outland kings express similar astonishment and disbelief.  But many lesser noblemen swear loyalty instantly.  Among those who swear immediately were King Leodogrance of Camelaird, Sir Hervis de Revil (a famous but landless knight), Prince Lanceor of Estregales (son of the King of Estregales), Sir Brastias (a famous knight), and many others.

King Lot laughed contemptuously, the Duke of Gloucester registered indignant righteousness, and others joined them to deny the fact.  The crowd grew surly, voicing resentment against the nobles and threatened to riot.  But before rioting could break out, Merffyn and Dubricus calmed the crowd and announced that they will hold another contest , with anyone who wished to try and pull the sword having a turn.  They order the word be sent out by all means possible to everyone to meet again in a month, at Candlemas (Feb. 2).

Merffyn and Dubricus then chose ten of the best knights loyal to Arthur and set a continual watch over the sword in the stone.  Each of them then chose ten more knights.  Of more than passing interest was the fact that King Leodogrance was present at the sword drawing, but abruptly departed with his knights when he received word that his kingdom was being threatened by King Ryons, a warlord out of the Perilous Forest.

The fourth member of the party we met was Jeanne's champion while I had been gone, Sir Cynrain.  While I didn't like the fact that he was required, I understood the necessity for the rituals.  This was something that was going to be dealt with later.  For the rest of the party of knights soon caught up with us.  It was 5 days before Candlemas, and we were heading to Londinium, via Levcomagus.

Asked the knights who have been following Jeanne and Arthur.  Find out about the Saxon invasion of Dorchester (ooh!  Arcavius is pissed!)  The Saxons had gone through there like a hot knife through butter, but Earl Robert and what forces he could gather stopped them.  They then tried through the forests near Raven's Gate, but Jeanne did something that no one talks about (on either side), but does get talked around.  They couldn't go through the East as there were other Saxons there, and there is no word about their moving West (I think we can assume that _[[Oberon|Oberon]]_ would have opposed that).

Sir Cynrain is the knight who had become extremely put out by my return.  I say questioningly that "he's acted as castellan?" to Sir Buddfannyn (a huge hulking knight now) who agrees and then finds an excuse to get away.  I fear I must start to pay heed to the consequences of my absence.

I need a new squire.  Jeanne knows the lives of her people and she has chosen four teens - two were squires of other knights, but the knights (and the squires) daren't even think about being offended.  The other two are eager to be squires.  They all show potential and no obvious signs of problems.  As I would be involved in battle within the month, I needed a trained squire, so I chose one named Perin, an 18 year old from Salisbury, who was a follower of the Morrigan.

## Levcomagus

Approaching Levcomagus, we are stopped by a sergeant and men-at-arms who question us.  I step forward and state who I am and King Arthur, and they still continue to question us.  When I wryly ask whether they would perhaps recognize Sir Cynon.  I guess not too surprisingly, they take a while before they do and some of them escort us to Levcomagus.

I talk to Arthur and he questions what happened to us.  I had to explain the promise originally made to Oberon and I must talk to him, Jeanne and Caradoc and tell them of what we are currently committed to.  At Levcomagus, we meet up with some of the old crowd, but Cynon says he doesn't have a bone to pick with his ex 2IC.  The herds of horses have increased in quality and quantity - made Niani and Brennos more wealthy here.

## Silchester

When we ride to Silchester, we're met by some knights before the gates.  Duke Ulfius had given his support, but not his fealty, to Arthur.  For reasons of age, method of gaining kingship, Merffyn's involvement.  We get moved through to an audience with Ulfius.  He greets us but not warmly.  Eventually Cynon requests a private meeting with Ulfius.  Afterwards, Ulfius is ready to leave very quickly and Cynon is noticeably with him more.

## London

Travelling to London, we travel as a group.  Ulfius is heartily sick of Arthur trying to convince him to swear fealty by the end of the trip.  There are a number of armed groups we ride past who are also going to London.  At London, Arthur is widely recognized, but there isn't necessarily a large acceptance of kingship.  Arthur sees a girl and goes over to her, while shooting a quick glance at me.  Jeanne sighs.  She's talked to him about it, but it obviously had no affect him.  She's Lysanor.  I don't recognize who she is, but I can recognize she is the daughter of Ailil … who we had promised to Oberon - oh bugger!

Arcavius went to convince his father-in-law, the Duke of Clarence, to pledge loyalty to Arthur, but the Duke is extremely belligerent.  Everybody and especially his daughter, the Lady Margot, had thought he was dead.  She had taken vows and resides in Amesbury Abbey, which was horrible.  But the Duke's complaints were that he couldn't marry her off again.  I believe that Arcavius placed all blame on myself.

Meanwhile, I took the opportunity to speak to Merffyn and Jeanne about what we had experienced since we last saw them and about what we had promised to Oberon.  As I was explaining that I had recognized that person already, and that it was Lysanor, they were exchanging looks which even I could see seemed to have a silent expression of "Rhodri!".  But when we continue on as to what is to be done, in the midst of their disparagements, it suddenly strikes me that hadn't Jeanne tried to dissuade Arthur from Lysanor and failed.  And I find that when I make the point, Jeanne quickly demurs the point to me and moves on to say that maybe they should get Arthur to meet his betrothed.  Feeling a little smug, smiling but not being dumb enough to say anything, I escort my beloved away from the meeting to enjoy the rest of the day and night in one of the finest beds in Londinium before the spectacle that was to follow on the morrow, Candlemas.

## The Sword in the Stone

At mid-morn the following day, all are congregated around the Christian church and various contestants are trying to pull the sword out of the stone.  Then Arthur drew the sword once more before another assembly.  Placing ourselves among the highest nobles of the land, I could see that it was done so easily because the sword seemed to jump into his hand a fraction before he pulled it out.  Looking around, I see that I was probably the only one who noticed and begin to relax … for now many knights and noblemen accept the witness of their own eyes and pledge loyalty to the Boy King, Arthur.

Especially important among these new royalists are those ranking noblemen of Logres who had been close the old king Pendragon; as well as myself.  These Eager Vassals include Earl Robert of Salisbury; Duke Ulfius of Silchester; Duke Corneus of Lindsey; Duke Idres of Cornwall; Bishop Bawdwin of Britain; Earl Gilbert of Hertford; Praetor Jonathel of Dorset; King Leodogrance of Camelaird; King Lak of Estregales; and many lesser lords and bannerets.  But it is noticeable that Duke Eustance of Clarence and King Lot of Lothian and Orkney did not.

But before we get too far, one of those pernickety Christian Bishops starts complaining that not all have sworn, so we must have another drawing on Easter.  Not only do the Christians want to have their part in this crowning, they want to have it on a Christian celebration.  Surely there is no time to waste on this mummery for Christians?

At the dinner in the hall at Londinium (one of the only ones left from the Saxon occupation), there is early discussions of military matters.  I lead the talk of the need to gather knights to begin our reclamation of Britain from the Saxons, perhaps beginning with an attack towards Hantonne, and then moving to eliminate Cerdic and his sub-chiefs in Dorset, before sweeping eastwards.  Praetor Jonathel is quite keen, while Duke Ulfius and Earl Robert consider this sage counsel and agree to gather an army at Silchester.

## Lysanor and Oberon

After such weighty matters, the meal changes into a feast.  Amongst the ensuing swirls of conversations, Lysanor is interested in what happened to us.  Although I try subtly to move the conversation towards Niani, Cynon blindly ignores (doesn't notice) and starts explaining about the Fae.  As he realises why I was trying to avoid explaining the Fae and what happened and begins to peter out, Niani joins in and starts selling the Fae to Lysanor - and Lysanor is keenly, coldly, and calculatedly interested.  In the process of which, I find that I lose all qualms that my conscience had held.  Niani is brilliant, laying bait at the right times and backing off perfectly at other times.  Lysanor is hooked brilliantly.  I am impressed (and feel I owe her for this).  We arrange to meet her outside later.  Niani, Brennos, and I go and we separate the maid away from Lysanor and then ride to the nearest grove.

Oberon came when summoned, but he used glamour to enhance his impressiveness.  He sweeps Lysanor away by being courtly, and in leading her away with arms intertwined, he gives me a pleased nod.  Before we return, Brennos asks what is going to be my story, and I decide my story will be no story at all.  I do not know what happened to Lysanor.  The next morning, I answered Arthur by telling him I don't know where she is (exactly, sotto voce).  As he is going to continue looking and asking, an unlikely ally comes.  Word arrives of the Dorset Saxons moving east, presumably to join up with the Sussex Saxons and move against Salisbury.

Arthur will remain in Londinium, while Duke Ulfius will return to Silchester with Earl Robert, who will then proceed to Sarum.  Our group will ride ahead to scout the western Saxons.  Initially, we rode hard to Levcomagus and found out that the Saxons had already crossed the Witham River, below where it becomes the Avon River.  So we rode south from Levcomagus along the Test River to try and find the trail of these Saxon forces.  To our surprise, Hywel fails to find the trail until after Niani points it out to him.  We trail a horsed group who joins with another, forming a group we estimate at 80 to 100.  As a plan of action, I aim to grab some Saxons and question them.  Thankfully, Cynon's new squire had been captured in the past by Saxons and could speak their language.

Just when Niani lost the trail, Hywel who had been taking pleasure in just riding along while someone else was tracking just looked around and pointed out the way they had gone.  We eventually hear some Saxon voices ahead (we guesstimate about a dozen).  We make a quick plan of us knights charging in and continuing to press forward and the squires take wounded Saxons as prisoners.  As us knights charge in, Niani shoots her bow wounding a Saxon who alarums every Saxon there.  This is something that we will have to discuss with her, it's just not knightly.  As the rest of us charge in, we each find two Saxons turning to face us.  Arcavius is knocked unconscious right at the start.  We fight them and all but two die.  I instruct we ride back to Levcomagus.

## A Gift from Morgaine

On the way, we meet a Red Cap who verbally spars with me a bit, but I say we are in a hurry, in a war, and don't have time.  He tries to tempt me with info, but I don't give in.  He gives me regards from Queen Morgaine.  As I'm about to go, he asks me if I'm refusing Morgaine's gift to her uncle.  I am curious and wavering when Cynon slaps my horse forward and rides forward, forcing my horse to follow it's sire - I don't mind.  Further along, Cynon sees a glint of metal ahead and warns us all, but we find a silver box.  Against Cynon's wishes, I feel obliged to take the gift.  We ride on.

Saxon prisoners are jailed when we get to Levcomagus and Arcavius is taken to a room and is first aided, but he still needs to be chirurged.  We try questioning the Saxons, but we can't get answers.  Cynon realizes most of us can't stomach torture and sends the rest of us out.  His squire, Gwyn, has been dying to get medieval on the Saxons.  They talk!

King Cerdic took Dorset in 508.  He's going to Sussex to have a powwow with the Saxons in Sussex to plan their next conquest … Sarum.  These 80 Saxons were being sent as part of the delegation.  I head to Sarum to warn Earl Robert and then we will head to Londinium.  The rest will head to Silchester and then to Londinium.

On the way, they encounter some woman named Leri.  Stuff happens and she will be put in a convent in Londinium.  But wait!  She's a Seer!  She sees herself going with them and that they let her and … wow!  She was right.  They travel with the army of 100 knights.  Leri occasionally wakes up screaming.  She says the Fomori are still with the Saxons.  And when she sees Arthur, she shows signs of recognition and gives the impression she's not telling everything.

Meanwhile, I had gone to Sarum and warned Earl Robert.  I stay a day to help with the defences and then head back up the road to Londinium.  I meet up with the army of it's 2nd night since leaving Londinium.  We travel to Sarum with nothing else untoward happening.  I get told of the Seer.

At Sarum when a War Council is held, Cynon talks of the Fomori and their effect on weapons (bar magical ones).  Arthur has stated that he wants to meet the Saxon army before it gets to Sarum.  I suggest he chooses precisely where the battlefield will be, as the Saxons won't be able to afford to let our army behind them.  Niani, Brennos and Arcavius take command of individual units.  Cynon, Hywel and I are staying with Arthur as part of his personal bodyguards.

## Battle of Sarum

It is the 11th of March of the 1,263rd year of Rome, or the year of the Christian lord 510 AD, our armies had come together, and after some hours our forces appeared to hold the edge (Niani and Brennos units performing quite well).  But some confusion started appearing in the centre and from the flank came 2 Fomori leading a band of Saxon chieftains.  Cynon and I made sure we faced up to the giants.  Inspired to protect Arthur, I take mine down fairly quickly and also slay the other as Cynon had been having abysmal luck and kept being knocked away.  But as I was approaching them, I saw the Fomori swing at Cynon's head when he was caught flat-footed.  It was plainly going to be a mortal blow … but it didn't land.  There was some flashes of silver protecting Cynon.  I guess the Fae Captain was still protecting Cynon for the future duel.

With the blunting of this flanking attack, the Saxon chieftains were overwhelmed and the Saxon army was pushed back into a rout.  This Battle of Sarum was Arthur's first … first of many to come, pray Mithras.  But even as we are part of the congratulating masses, Arcavius and I could see in the distance Merffyn wandering through the battlefield using his feet and staff to turn over bodies, looking for what we didn't know.

We were offered boons by Arthur for helping him win the battle.  Arcavius asks for aid in liberating Dorset and Arthur gives him a new suit of mail.  I ask for nothing as I am here to serve him.  Niani wants some land so she can have her own home while she remains in our realm.  Cynon can't decide yet.  Cynon returns to Levcomagus to recover from his wounds, and Arcavius goes to Amesbury Abbey to do an Officer and a Gentleman (which I believe he got wrong!)

## Ailil's Ring

When they had left, I showed Jeanne Morgaine's "gift".  As I did, she took a few steps back with a quickly indrawn breath - "she gave you something like the Pandora's Box … it's Ailil's ring (oh-oh).  It is like Angus Harp (oh-oh).  And Ailil obviously doesn't yet know that Morgaine has given me the ring as a gift (oh-oh)."  I need to return this to Ailil and hope it's enough to convince him to stop involving us humans (and to not involve Aneurin, which is something I fear in this Fae rivalry of one-ups-man-ship).  I plan to visit Mildenhall and re-establish my domain … as well as gift this gift to Ailil, but Arthur's third drawing of the sword approaches too closely.

## The Sword in the Stone - Again

We travel back to Londinium slowly enjoying the acclaim of the people we pass.  On Easter day, Arthur draws the sword before a third assembly.  This time more of the rest of the Logres noblemen pledge their loyalty.  These include Arcavius father-in-law, the Duke Eustance of Clarence, Earl Artgualchar of Wuerensis and the Earl Tegfan of Jagent amongst others.  However, word also comes with some of the more northerly nobles that some of the foreign British kings have mustered against "the beardless bastard boy".  King Lot has publicly claimed that, as any reverently superstitious Pagan knows, you cannot trust a sorcerer.  Merffyn is obviously trying to foist off this lad as his puppet king.  So King Lot has called the dissident kings to meet in his northern stronghold to discuss their options.  The names spoken of are the Centurion King of Malahaut, King Carados of Escore, Duke Ansirus of Benoice, King Nentres of Garloth and King Urien of Gorre.

The ceremony is magnificent, with preparations having been undertaken by Merffyn, Dubricus, Jeanne and other lords for months.  First, the Christian Bishop Bawdwin and Duke Corneus of Lindsey knighted Arthur while I beamed on proudly.  Then, at the St Paul's church, Arthur is crowned King of Logres.  The full pomp and ceremony is exercised to impress everyone with the King's power and sacred character.  He takes the arms of Logres as his own: a gold shield, with 2 green dragons back-to-back.  During the ceremony, the last of the recalcitrant Logres nobles grudgingly pay homage to King Arthur.  After days of feasting and celebration, Arthur announces to all that he will now proceed on a Grand Tour across Logres to Carlion in Escavalon (not mentioned, but I strongly sense Merffyn's involvement in this decision).  This Tour will pass through all towns between Londinium and Carlion (Staines, Silchester, Donnington, Wandborough, Cirencester, Gloucester and Caerwent).

## Mildenhall and Marlborough

Between Donnington and Wandborough, I detour to pass through Mildenhall.  When I arrive, I find it is a much, much bigger town than when I left.  It has exceeded Levcomagus in size and is starting to rival Cirencester.  The manor house is also much larger, almost a tower in it's own right.  Huge numbers of merchants pass through here using some of the large buildings built to house travellers.  The income from this explains my apparent large wealth.  I need to go to Marlborough (which has also been improved) to see Edar and Aneurin to see my old friends, to show that they still have a living liege lord, and to warn Aneurin of the possible threat from Ailil.  I tell her of Morgaine's marriage to Ailil and of Oberon's method of evening the stakes.  After the all-too brief visit, I returned to the moving Court at Wandborough.

## The High King

At Carlion on the 10th May, the Supreme Collegium is summoned to select the new high King, an office which has been empty for many years.  Most of the kings do not attend, but those present are sufficient to elect their leader.  King Arthur of Logres.  Thus King Arthur is elected to be the Imperator and Caesar of Britannia, and Wledig (Chief Warlord) over the British Tribes.  He now takes the arms of the High King as his own: a red shield with 7 gold crowns.

The festivities are tremendous, and no one is disappointed.  Many of the sub-kings came, including King Lak of Estregales and King Alain of Escavalon.  Amid the celebrations, we learn that the six Northern lords under the command of Lot have arrived at the city.  Arthur sends them presents and bids them welcome using our party as messengers, but King Lot and his allies insult us to our faces and also insult Arthur.  It was only discipline instilled by my father and the jostling of Cynon and Arcavius which stopped me from bringing dishonour on me if I broke the bounds of being the messenger.

## Lot and the Northern Kings

Respecting the larger army (which outnumbered us 3,000 to 1,500) and it's commanders, Arthur ordered the city gates closed and the city to prepare for a siege.  After a couple of days, Arthur realizes that no battle can be won by hiding behind walls and he must show everyone that he is the rightful King, and so he orders the army to assemble and sally forth out at the enemy.

In the battle that follows, it is a strangely even tussle for a while where I capture some obviously rich Northern knights.  But then Lot and his men charge with lances lowered against where Arthur is and many of Arthur's immediate defenders are downed.  But then Arthur draws Excalibur, his Sword of Victory, and by its magical flash of power blinds his nearby foes and awes (or shocks) the rest of the enemy to hesitate.  It was about then that the citizens of Carlion, who had been surging from the city even as the battle was being fought, fell on the flank of the Northern army.  Seeing the position that his army is in, Lot sounds the retreat and his army withdraws in an orderly fashion from the field.  Although individually we may have covered ourselves in Glory, for Arthur it was an indecisive result.  Lot still opposes Arthur and he still has a larger army.

After the battle, Arthur calls on his major vassals and allies to advise him for the northern army may have retreated, but it is still ranging through Logres pillaging and plundering.  A lot of the dead from Lot's army are wearing the green armour, which was the same as the one I had encountered in 496.  It, and they, were made by the Witch of The North - at least according to Merffyn.

Duke Ulfius asks us to come help with the prisoners and assess them for ransoms.  I recognize one of them as the mercenary captain I had met in 496, who's wearing the green armour.  He is much more bilious than I remember him.  But back to our assessing, 8 of them are significant and the rest are ordinary knights.  Word is spread by the squires that we wish to speak to anyone who fought any green-armoured opponents.  From all the responses, we establish that there were only about 12 of them and that they had only been mercenary captains or sergeants - none of them Lot's vassals.  Lot knows it corrupts the wearer and won't let his vassals have the armour as he's extremely loyal to them (and vice versa).

Arthur decides to send Duke Ulfius to French Kings as an envoy of suitable rank for aid.  We are assigned to be envoys to Lot.  Follow the trail of destruction to Lot's armed encampment and a herald is sent ahead to allow us to be escorted in.  Lot gives us sparse welcome, and I give him his due greeting and I parleyed with him to find out was there anything that Arthur could do to convince Lot that he was the true High King, and Lot said nothing would.  I then asked whether there was anything that anyone else could do that could convince him, or was there someone else's judgement on this matter that he would accept.  Again Lot gave a straight negative answer, when he paused and gave a half-smile and said that he would accept the use of the Stone of Kingship as the sign of the true King, and he would accept the judgement of the Red Dragon of Cymry - at which, his nobles began to laugh.

## Test of the Dragon

Cynon asked what had Lot against Arthur - and his reply was that "Merffyn had meddled in the fortunes of our people for too long.  He had the same opposition to Arthur as he had against Uther."  We need to visit Voklaynn, for we have seen a red dragon making itself at home at her castle.  We arrive in the garden in front of the castle where a game of ball is being played.  It hits me in the head from behind as I arrive.  We speak to Voklaynn, and I explain what is happening, and what Lot has said and ask if we can get the aid of the Red Dragon of Cymry to prevent its people from fighting each other.

The way the Red Dragon proves the true High King is by roasting him and him surviving (critical roll).  I have absolute faith that this is the solution - for Voklaynn agrees to ask.  We stay for a couple of days and even see Illisander, Voklaynn's second son.  Meet up with other friends there.  While Cynon and I play the game, Arcavius visits the Fish … Wise One.  When he returns, he looks upset.  We find out from him that Arthur is to face tests of the Dragon for valour, justice and honour.  One of the servants had told us of the test being breathed on Arthur by Dragon.  Arcavius was shown words in his mind that Arthur would pass the first test, but would have huge trouble with the second.  This doesn't say how long between the tests.

We are returned to when we left directly to before the gates of Carlion.  I spring on Arthur Lot's demands and advise of the Red Dragon of Cymry's willingness to apply the tests and I tell him what I had heard.  While Arthur is in shock at the bizarreness of the demands and my news, in complete faith as to the rightness of this course of action, I continue to reassure and cajole.  Still with doubt in his eyes, Arthur agrees after Cynon and Arcavius point out that it was just hearsay from the Fish.

We go to Lot and invite him to a parley at Carlion to await the Red Dragon of Cymry test.  After dawn on 15 May 510, Lot arrives with his army near Carlion and halts it on top of the nearby hill, while our army places itself in the valley below.  If this goes to battle, it still looks bad for us as they outnumber us 2½ to 1, and they have the high ground.  Cynon is the 1st to see a speck in the sky directly at the sun.  When it becomes clear that it is the Red Dragon of Cymry and it grandstands in the air, a lot of panic sets in.  It lands on the slope between the armies.  No horses can approach it so Lot starts walking towards it and Arthur follows from our side just ½ a step behind.

They meet before the dragon.  They speak to each other, but they are too distant from me to hear what - but at one point, the Dragon glares at Lot and he backs away quickly.  The Red Dragon of Cymry blasts Arthur and smoke fills the valley.  Both armies, as well as the horses, panic.  I run (on foot) towards where Arthur was.  Smoke clears and he's alive as I reach him.  His armour has melted around him.  I take his hand and fall to my knees, and Lot comes and submits (in his mind as the first of Arthur's vassals).  Both of the armies (at least those who have remained) cheer.

At the party afterwards, everybody is involved in a huge celebration - including Voklaynn and a huge number of the castle staff.  In the late night or the early morning (about 4 am) after I had been kicked out of bed by Jeanne and I am trying to sleep but feeling very drunk still - Oberon came by and had discourse with me but I fear that it was more at me.  I can recall that he was there, and that I responded but I can remember nothing more and my throbbing head prevents me from feeling much concern about it.

## Hywel and Guinevere

In the days that follow, Arthur and I prepare the army to be taken north east to relieve King Leodogrance against King Ryons of Nogales.  When our army does arrive near Camelaird, it is to find that King Ryons has taken his army and fled with no battle.  While at Camelaird, we meet Guinevere.  She's most beautiful (bar a few unearthly beauties).  Unfortunately she's the red-head of Hywel's nightmare.  Hywel is head-over-heels in love with her, and is practically willing to give up everything for her.  She's not pushing him away - she's quite pleased about it, actually - but Arthur's jealous.  Hywel says that I don't want to see him happy, but I try and emphasize she's the King's betrothed.  He says that she's not married yet, and I respond that that doesn't make a difference to royalty.

Later, the rest of us talk about what to do with him.  I'm going to send him back to Raven's Gate with a number of men.  Arcavius suggests sending Cynrain with them to make sure he stays there.  Arcavius has also counselled Arthur on how to impress Guinevere.  He manages to convince Arthur not to take her hunting, but he does suggest a ball like the Fae court hold (bad idea!).

## The Autumn Ball

Late Autumn.  This ball is the last major event of the year.  Of course, Guinevere suggests masks.  I say no before she can finish asking, and Niani intrigues critically and convinces her that the Fae don't do that - let her beauty shine through.  Jeanne has a bad feeling about this, as do I.  She tells me to get my band of followers to watch.  Niani and Brennos and Arcavius and Margot do dance successfully.  Feeling totally out of place but trying to do right by my friends, I try and build up Cynon for Arianwen and her mom, who is the Countess of Rydychan, but I bore them.

Niani notices that Arthur is dancing with a strange young lady.  Niani tells Arcavius, and then she and Brennos cut in with Arthur.  Meanwhile Arcavius tells me, Jeanne sees her and hisses that it's Morgaine.  Jeanne is unnerved.  I send my page with Arcavius' to get Ailil's ring.  It's discretely handed to me.  I cut in.  She has an almost Saxon-like face, but the eyes are still hers.  We conduct a stilted conversation.  She is pregnant and is due at the end of October.  If it's a girl, it will be called Durandel, but they haven't decided for a boys name.  I say I will try to be there.  She points out that the court has changed and the Unseelie are reigning.

Meanwhile, Arthur is mingling normally and mostly with Guinevere.  Niani has been hijacked by someone who asked her to teach them how to dance.  This goes on and on and her danger-sense is blaring.  After a long time of her trying to get away, until she started walking away from him and he appears instantly before her.  Its Ailil, but it doesn't get through to Niani until he starts to raise the point of Lysanor and instantly Niani wants to get out of there … big time.  Arcavius notices, grabs Brennos and Brennos then leads the way.  Brennos and Niani move away while Arcavius stays with Ailil.

Arcavius spills the beans about the ring.  Ailil's eyes flash and he looks directly at Jeanne and he starts walking away while Jeanne is compelled to follow.  Morgaine forces me to dance another time, but I promise her I will make her pay if Jeanne is hurt by this.  Raising an eyebrow, she smiles enigmatically.  After the dance stops, she turns around and vanishes.  I turn and face Arcavius and Cynon who have come to tell me Ailil has Jeanne, but I brush past them to where I had last seen Ailil and Jeanne disappear to.  I brush past people dancing, causing a scene until I realize I'd be quicker if I walked around the dancers.

I finally see Ailil bowing to Jeanne with her hands between his.  Ailil was walking away when I grabbed the boxed ring and held it out to the side before shaking it.  He stops immediately, focuses on me and then starts to walk to me.  We chat fairly bluntly, but when he starts to make some blatant threats I quieten him and gift him with my gift.  He tells me he doesn't like things being taken from him.  I agree with him and tell him I feel the same.  We smile at each other and part.

Jeanne has that gleam in her eye again.  I start to speak to her when she puts her finger to my lips and leads me to the wall which is decorated with flowers and takes one.  With another smile at me, she cups it in her hands and it turns to stone and offers it to me.  While bystanders draw back in shock, I laugh and take her in my arms, so glad that she has regained the spark, the self-assurance, that was so important to who she was.

Meanwhile, Hywel has been challenged by an ugly Fae.  I think it was an Unseelie, but I check with Puck and he says it was some of Lady Etleyenne's friends and family who are trying to obtain revenge on her behalf for Hywel's breaking of the betrothal.  But Hywel is resisting.  Speaking with Lady Etleyenne, I offer to create an embassy consisting of Hywel with the Fae, but she only wants him if her love is returned.

## Sir Cynrain

Concerning the ongoing issue with Sir Cynrain, I take him aside and speak with him.  I praise him for what he has done for my troops and for my castles.  I tell him I fully understand the part he has played while I have been absent, but that it ceased when I returned.  I will accept his word that it won't continue, but his life rests on his honour, of course.  I ask him to be a steward and companion like Hywel and he accepts.

Cynon is also betrothed to Arianwen this autumn.  Through Jeanne, I find out that Arianwen had set her sights on Cynon 10 years before, as a 10 year old, but he disappeared when she couldn't marry him and he returned when she could marry him.  She considered it her destiny but, unfortunately for him, her mother, the Countess of Rydychan, hates him.
