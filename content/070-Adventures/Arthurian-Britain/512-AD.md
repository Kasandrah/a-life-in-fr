# Rhodri's Tales - 512 AD

**Written by [[Ben-G|Ben G.]] (Rhodri)**

## Notes

Beginning. no childbirth and twins get sick and brother goes missing.  Arthur is 19, Caradoc is 15.

Winter Time: Court moves to Silchester during the previous Autumn.  Caradoc stayed behind at Carohaise.  I see that Arthur has no suspicion of what is happening with Caradoc and Guinevere.  Niani stayed at my home during the winter.  Emissaries from the Pope came for tributes.  Arthur sent them packing.

## Oberon's Heir

About Christmas time, it's obvious that Lysanor is pregnant with _[[Oberon|Oberon]]'s_ child.  It's not discussed (at least amongst the nobility) but everyone knows.  Lysanor has been accepted into my care as I had wanted to have some say in Uther's (Arthur's illegitimate unproclaimed child) upbringing, but with this second child it will involve Oberon and I don't want any more involvement with him than I already have.  In the past, when Lysanor was originally assigned to my care, an alternative was offered in that her father would install her as Castellan of Karadigan Castle.

So I proposed we see her off married, as well as send some more knights who have some loyalty to me.  Lysanor is the child of the Lord of Bedegraine Castle, but she has been shamed and would not have high prospects.  Jeanne raises the issue, but I had already put some thought into the matter and stated that Sir Cynrain should be rewarded for his faithful service.  Jeanne frowns and starts suggesting others, but I am so determined that it will be Sir Cynrain.  Having called her bluff, she backs down.  I suggest we arrange for the wedding after April to allow everyone interested the chance to attend and Jeanne says that's probably a good idea.

For in March, Lysanor gives birth to a boy.  She's trying to think of a name for him as she hadn't expected this.  Oh damn, the consequences of this are starting to come to mind again. Oberon's only male child, something Titania has failed to provide ... think about it guys!

A couple of nights later, I woke to find beautiful, unearthly music playing in the distance and Jeanne asleep, dead to the world. another Fae visitation.  I dress up a bit and follow the music out my room and off towards Lysanor's room.  I'm not surprised. knock. music stops and Oberon says "its your castle".  I enter and find Oberon walking back and forth with his son in his arms.  Everyone else is asleep.  We talk.  He wants me to be the child's guardian, but I keep telling him I won't be able to protect the child from Titania.  I'm only mortal.  I suggest maybe a deal might be made with the Lady Voklaynn, but it takes a fair amount of time before I seem to have convinced him.  Not being able to take this pleasure from him, I leave him time to spend alone with his son, just like I had with Caradoc.

Tell Jeanne everything in the morning.  She also thinks that when I speak to Voklaynn, I should ask her about how she's done with getting Niani home.  Jeanne also points out that Oberon probably expects me to ask Voklaynn, which I definitely hadn't agreed to but I guess if I'm not going to be saddled with this then I will have to do it.

## Foster Parents for a Fae Prince

Ask Arcavius' help to speak with Voklaynn and he suggests we also ask the Fish, but when we arrive, it is almost instantaneously that we're ushered to Voklaynn and Anwyn.  We're greeted and Anwyn points out that us alone means that there is something to be done?!  And could we explain it in short fashion.  Only slightly put out by the fact that he was right, I succinctly say that Oberon has achieved a male issue without Titania's involvement and he has chosen me to be the foster father.  I tell them I can't protect him from Titania due to my mortal limitations and that I am already in Titania's debt concerning the Captain's challenge.

Anwyn laughed heartily until I made him choke on it when I told them that I suggested to Oberon that they should be the child's foster parents as they had the ability, and that would be worth a great deal to Oberon.  Anwyn started saying quite strenuously that it wasn't worth it when Voklaynn cut through his sputtering to agree to this if Oberon would aid her in getting what she wanted. The Cauldron.  Anwyn said that he would probably want to visit his child and I said that too was worth something.  I told them I would pass on the offer to Oberon but that they would have to negotiate the final deal face-to-face (allowing Oberon entry to do this).

In the excitement of passing on this problem, I didn't ask Voklaynn about Niani's situation and returned home with the good news, but was soon back.  Greatly amused, they told me they were unable to return her. It was in the hands of Raven.  In fact, Jeanne would probably be the one to come up with a way … more so than anyone else.

Returning home again, this news led to a splitting of energies.  Jeanne set to with the arrangements for Lysanor's wedding and also worked on returning Niani to her homeland.  Niani had always treated Jeanne with antipathy (because Jeanne followed Raven, but Niani never told Jeanne the reason) and Jeanne was quite happy to see the back of Niani.

## The Lady of the Lake

Mid-February. we have a raid into Saxon Hampshire.  We burn and pillage a village (Arthur is strong on removing all reasons for them to want to stay) but then we come across one of their war-bands.  We charge at them, I got two against me and hit both of them, knocking one down.  Arthur is down on first round.  Arcavius and I see it.  Arcavius knocks his opponent (a chieftain) down.  I ignore my enemy and charge to save Arthur.  Arthur is wide open on the ground, stunned and his opponent has a free (huge) swing with his axe at Arthur's stomach.  As the blow falls, I've gone critical inspired to avenge him.  I'm charging the Saxon chieftain , focused on him but meanwhile the Saxon has gone on his knees and is doing nothing now.

I'm hit from the side. By ARTHUR.  Cynon's horse is going berserk but there is no sign of him.  Some of the other knights say that he took a huge blow and looked dead.  The battle is over, but no one can approach the horse.  Arthur finds tracks leading from where Cynon fell to nowhere in particular and then it stops in mid stride.  As if I needed any proof, I had felt that the Captain had stepped in again.

Arthur had struck me.  Arthur was alive.  He lives!

A couple of hours after the event, Cynon pops up again, all healed.  We also have five Saxon prisoners.  Riding back, we find that we're on a lake that has frozen over, but the ice broke and Arthur and his steed went under.  They are helped out, but Excalibur has fallen out of its scabbard.

Arthur is held back and I go in the water for it (?)  The water is heating up and starts bubbling.  A bubble of mid-summer surrounds the lake.  I'm still in the lake, and have drawn my sword.  Excalibur is being held out of the lake by a woman's mailed arm.  Arthur takes his armour off and dives in and swims to the arm, puts his hand over the hilt and woman's hand and is dragged down.  I try to swim to him, but I'm in armour and can't swim (?!)  Eventually, as I'm still panicking, Arthur walks back to shore (while underwater).  He says that was interesting but can't explain it further.  We return to court.

Caradoc's back looking smug.  I have grave misgivings if the betrothal of Arthur and Guinevere continues.  Merffyn says he sees Arthur met Lady Vivienne and then leans forward and warns him not to lose it again.

## Battle of Bedegraine Forest

Rumours of Northern Kingdoms banding together and fighting Lot are heard in March.  All Arthur's counsellors (including me) advise gathering forces for this summer and help him, so when these Northern lords have sent about 3,000 Picts down south, we have a similar number (made of knights) to fight them.  We meet them west of Newark.

Battle of Bedegraine Forest. Merffyn's with us against the horseless Picts.  At the end of the first day, a black dragon from Ben Nevis (!) is burning forests.

Check with Merffyn who is like when he called the Mist, ask should we call on the Lady Voklaynn?  We arrive before the gates and a ball hits Arthur on the side of the head.  Girl / lady comes over and they stare wide-eyed at each other.  Arcavius gets the gargoyles to be at attention.  We go through the gates and send a messenger for the Lady.  While we wait Maeve and Arthur talk together off to the side.  They appear quite taken with each other (could this possibly be the knife to cut the Gordian coil).  We talk to the Lady instead and tell her of what's happened and she then takes Arthur for a walk.  We then talk with Maeve.  Arthur returns set-faced.  Niani takes us back with her token from the Lady (it goes both ways. Arcavius is astounded).

The Lady set Maeve up and sends her with us.  My heart is tremulous in that the possibility is growing, but more immediate matters are pressing.  Arthur says we need to move away.

We all see the Black Dragon high above.  Arcavius sees the plummeting Red Dragon above the Black.  We run.  The dragons plummet and hit the ground. end result was fighting being won by us, but indecisively.

We have a feast at Newark. both Maeve and Arthur.

Two days of chasing after stragglers.

Glory. witness Arthur survive chop. 50 pts, Glory. see Dragons fight. 300 pts, Glory. Battle of Bedegraine Forest (60/round plus the modifiers). 460 pts.

## An Iceni Queen

Need to find out more about Maeve.  I speak to her and ask about her lineage and she says that the Lady told her to be circumspect about that.  I take it as it is the Lady is the one I should be asking, but later with Cynon we ask for her to come see us and we do find out she's the daughter of Queen Boadicea and she pointed out she foresaw possible problems if the relationship progressed in that she would be wed in the manor of the Iceni as who she was and that she would be no meek, tapestry-making maid.

At the end, she asks about Lysanor and we tell her about her.  We also tell her of Morgaine's origins and she shows her views on the Fae (she considers them "of loose morals") and she throws in that her family is proof against the Fae.  I immediately step in and ask her to clarify that and find out the family sees thru the Fae's glamour and the Fae can't charm (or such) her family.  The (very) little hesitation I had before disappears and I am very happy for this love-match to occur.

We let the matter lie for a few days to see how the relationship will progress.  Eventually, Arthur comes to see me to tell me he wants to call off the betrothal (be still my pumping heart).  As calmly as I can, I ask him if it's Maeve that he is interested in.  He's surprised that I know (ah, youth), but is happy when I tell him it can be done.  I suggest that Guinevere could be pointed to Caradoc and Caradoc would not be averse to this as a favour for his friend and Guinevere is beautiful and noble.  I also point out to Arthur that it will be a matter of imposing his will on Leodogrance.  It may not be just, but it will be the way things are.

Cynrain and Lysanor's wedding was planned for May, but a lot of lords are here now, so a large number repair to their new home castle in southern Bedegraine Forest, with some of Cynrain's family and a number of hers, including her putative father, Earl Sanam of Bedegraine.  She is officially the middle daughter of three, and her real father, Ailil. although I didn't stop him, or even acknowledge him, I was kept sober constantly keeping an eye on him (something that he was finding greatly amusing).

Soon afterwards it is time to return to the Court in Silchester.  But the night before we go to Silchester, I speak to Caradoc on the matter and tell him that Arthur has asked for the betrothal to be called off and that I would be going to Camelaird to speak to Leodogrance on behalf of Arthur.  I would also be broaching an alternate betrothal.  Caradoc wishes me a safe journey, which is more affection shown to me than since he was a young child.

Cynon and I leave, travelling ahead of the army's return to Silchester and head to Carohaise where we ask for a private hearing with King Leodogrance.  After greetings, we break the news of Arthur breaking the betrothal.  Leodogrance is not impressed (!), and he wants to know who he is seeing and we tell him Maeve is the intended.  He questions her heritage which I had said was of the Royal Family of the Iceni.  Leodogrance barely holds his temper.  He accepts that Arthur is going to make it not happen, but he is very unhappy / dissatisfied.

I get back to army and tell Arthur of the news having been accepted, but badly.  Tell Caradoc who then goes to Camelaird for an indefinite stay.

Maeve will still make her impressive appearance to Silchester, but no announcement of their betrothal.

## French Knights in Briton

In Silchester, while escorting Arthur on a little tour about the town, we encounter a French knight who I recognise as the noted _[[010-Knights-of-Arthurian-Britain#Sir Emils|Sir Emils of Champagne]]_, and he has come to deliver a message to Merffyn from his King, as well as greetings to King Arthur of Briton.  I greet him and lead him to Arthur and make introductions.  Arthur accepts the French King's message and returns them and then assigns Sir Amils into my care.

Thinking about this, and can't think of any way to bring Merffyn here (he comes when he wants to) but maybe Jeanne has some way of contacting him.  On speaking with her, she says she can "send word" but that not only Merffyn might hear.  She asked whether the need was worth the risk.  As I thought of all the enemies I know of, I knew that the need must be great for me to want to risk my beloved.

When I tell Amils, he would not explain what the message was.  That is his right, but I will not risk my family for that. so Amils sighs and says he can only stay a week. and thinking of his country, the thought of another method of attracting Merffyn's attention comes to mind. Although I think that charging at each other with lances on horseback is very stupid, but I think it is a grand idea to have one of Emil's "tourneys".  I will sponsor and subsidize this, unless the Duke of Silchester or Arthur decides to take precedence.

## The Grand Tourney

22 Jun 512 is St Alban's day.  This is when we will hold the tourney.  It has been taken over mostly by Duke Ulfius, with ill grace by myself.  Amils explains slowly and repeatedly but it still seems slightly silly and quite laborious.  Arcavius and Brennos will be Marshals of the Lists (mostly because they have the ability to write, but the fact that they have the ability to enforce their decisions when required doesn't hurt).  The Mass Joust and the Grand Melee will consist of two opposing forces, who will be led by Duke Ulfius and myself.

On the first day, there will be a Mass Joust which will be team versus team, with each team charging the other, then after the first pass all still ahorse will line up again and charge the other side again, and this will continue until only one remains.  This will then be followed after lunch by a Grand Melee in the afternoon which is essentially a battle with training swords with each side having a safe area where some can rest and prisoners can be held.  On the second day, any personal and individual challenges would take place.

It is to be noted that the feasts that would intersperse and follow these events attracted just as much attention as the actual events.  Amils gave great rise to amusement for everyone when he tried convincing everyone that we should obtain the favours from a lady to carry with us.  After the laughter and jokes died down, he explained that the lady's favour in this instance was a scarf or item of clothing which represented that the lady "favoured" that knight to win.  As bemused as I was by this, I wasn't averse to riding with something showing my wife's love and affection.

On my team was Amils, Caradoc and Niani and opposing us was Duke Ulfius, Captain Bellator, and assorted others.  In the joust, I led our forces charge and aimed directly at Duke Ulfius and unhorsed him, while Amils unhorsed Captain Bellator.  In then following pass, an unknown, fresh faced, foreign knight organized the other side.

As I was trying to organize my side, I must have accidentally spurred my mount for it leapt forward before anyone else was ready and then my saddle-girth broke and my saddle and I fell off my steed before the foreign knight could reach me.  Amils seemed inspired and he rallied my side and charged the others. he scored a perfect blow which caused great damage to this other French (maybe) knight, but this opponent still managed to get up and stagger gingerly to the side.  Morgaine and Guinevere rushed to his aid (oh, I think that means trouble). this greatly upset Caradoc.  In the end, Amils was the last on his horse and was the Winner of the Joust.

In the Grand Melee, each side was split into four groups. three to form a vanguard, main body and rearguard (consisting of about 20 men each) and one to form a reserve (of about 10 men and woman).  Amils was in command of the vanguard on the left, Caradoc was commanding the rearguard on the right, Niani was in command of the reserve which would be directed as required.  I was in command of the centre, and overall command. but that overall command wouldn't be applying in the middle of the combat, due to the vagaries of being in personal combat.

Amils was opposed by _[[030-Arthurian-Britain#^9bb92d|Lancelot]]_, Captain Bellator opposed Caradoc, and I face Duke Ulfius.  In such confines, there can be only very basic plans made … but even the basic plan we had was thrown into disarray when Caradoc charged his formation across the field to attack Lancelot.  Calling Niani's reserves to oppose Bellator and free some of Amils to create a new reserve, all I could do was bring Ulfius low, but we were too evenly matched, as we fought each other through the hours of the melee it was everyone else who was being brought low.  The Marshals led the vanquished off the field, including Amils and Caradoc (who was in a rage and behaving almost shamefully) who were both beaten by Lancelot, and Bellator who was defeated by Niani, until only four were left on the field.

Ulfius and myself were still locked in battle, and Lancelot was refusing to fight Niani. so the Marshals called a Draw of Champions.  Leaning on our swords and with sweat pouring down our faces and panting heavily, we looked at each other and grinned. we had wagered £5 (Librium) with each other on the outcome of this Melee and neither of us had lost (nor had we won). but now it was time to feast.

Arrive late to the feast with Jeanne.  Caradoc is trailing Guinevere, holding a monologue with her to her back as she's walking away.

Arcavius is trying to garner support for an attack on Dorchester. and he starts with Earl Robert, who I can hear agrees that the Saxons need to be dealt with, but the Northerners could possibly be eliminated as a threat for a long while if we attacked them this season.  The Saxons should be watched and information gathered on them so that attack early in the next year could be made. a spring 513 raid, perhaps.  Arcavius wants more, and sooner, but bottles the frustration down.

As the feast progresses, Amils offers to sing a song, and I offered to accompany him.  I don't think he was paying much attention to any of us males, so I shouldn't have been too surprised when he also grabbed a harp from someone else and accompanied himself.  Afterwards, he is approached by Morgaine, who leads him off.  I notice, but I am more interested in having a little talk with Caradoc, but I fail to catch up with him.  The upshot of it is that she was pumping him for information on the unknown knight (who was Lancelot), but Amils kept his mind focused on what he considered important and continued his own efforts until he began to notice that everyone around them is showing fear of her. whereupon he tries to disengage himself, but although I think she had used him for information and was "satisfied", the fact that the prey was trying to run challenged her "hunting" instincts.

Meanwhile, Arcavius and I have noticed separately that we can see two separate Niani's dancing.  One is with Lancelot, and one is with Brennos.  I tell Arcavius we'll try and handle this subtly, by trying not to cause a scene, but to speak to Brennos and Niani separately.  But when I try to separate them, the Niani makes sure Brennos doesn't leave her side.  When I then try to lead them to the other strange pair, I fail to move them.  To me, Brennos appears befuddled and veritably glamoured.

While I am trying to separate the pair, Arcavius goes to the other pair to tell them that Brennos is in trouble, but when he leaves, the Niani pairing with me says they want to dance and quickly disappear into the milling crowd behind a dancing pair but don't appear on the other side.  I search, but part way through I advise Ulfius and Cynon (who had been in discussion on the dais) but they say that it is my problem to deal with.  When Arthur receives word of the situation, he storms over to me and orders me to deal with it, but to not travel to the Fae realms.  He can't afford to have his greatest knights disappearing for an unknown amount of time, maybe forever, just when he is going to be campaigning to take the north and then the Saxons.

Niani has looked and wakes up Merffyn.  As she starts to really annoy him, Amils comes and tells him that Morgaine was mightily amused that Queen Titania was at the feasting.  A big discussion ensues and Merffyn blames me.  I then tell him of Oberon's (and Ailil's) heir.  A plan ensues to apologize to Titania and to possibly get the Stone of Destiny for her.  But this will create more of imbalance with the Fae, with the Fomori losing their only Great Treasure, but it was worked out that if we could distract the Fomori King with the Jute Queen (as shown in the Fish … Wise One's vision) and use her to break the alliance between the Saxons and the Fomori.  For the Saxons won't be able to provide the Spear for the Fomori and we could leave Fomori gear if we kidnapped the Jutish Queen.

To get to see Titania, I could contact Angus Og (who I have a good connection with, as he owes me … as I recall a levelling of a kingdom, destruction of a dragon, influence a king, or such) and see Titania.  But at the mention of Angus name, Niani says that he was a friend of Brennos … that Angus had, in fact, blessed their union.

So, now our far-fetched plan was brought to something only difficult, rather than impossible. an appeal would be made to Angus to help his friend.  Niani wants to go now, however, all my previous successful contacts with the Fae have been done in the forests, so I say we need to get to a forest, but the creatures of the night are uncommonly rife around Silchester, as Brion and Cynon attested to in 497.

Niani was determined and went to don her armour and I went to see Ulfius about the "creatures of the night".  I had tried earlier to convince her to wait, but failed and after Ulfius, I go to try again.  But checking with Ulfius, we grab a survivor of one of their attacks.  He was the only survivor of a group of a dozen.  They were slaughtered by only three, and they let him go laughingly.  But even his testimony isn't enough to deter her.

Niani spoke to me and when I asked her to explain why she had to go tonight, she initially would only answer that I wouldn't understand.  But when I pressed, she asked what would I do if Jeanne was taken by Oberon.  I would go and chase them and rescue her, so she said I did understand.  I did, but I couldn't risk losing more time in the Fae realms for Jeanne and because Arthur needed me just as much now as he had before.  I could get her to a meeting, but I couldn't cross over.

We go to Merffyn. get him to talk of the creatures and their weaknesses.  He tells us that beheading and intense fire is their weaknesses. but we needed to beware of their bite, for it would make us one of them.  Again this was not enough to deter Niani.

After some thought, I think I have another way which might work.  We need to have some isolated spot, and I will try to play some music to see if Angus Og will come.  Niani, Arcavius, Amils and I crowd into Niani's room.  I play the song that I remember Angus Og playing on beloved harp.  After my playing, we are all looking at each other when a knocking comes from the door.  Niani opens it and bids the youngish servant girl with new blankets to enter.

As everybody starts to assume this was a mistake, I recognize her as Angus' love.  As the discussion continues, she tells us that Brennos is on a mission for Titania to retrieve a sword from the smith Goibniu in exchange for a key.  Niani thinks she believes what she's saying.  With a little more discussion, this is enough to settle Niani enough to wait for Brennos to return, for a while.  We all split for the night, Arcavius to the chapel, Amils to find a companion for the night, I to my beloved.

Returning to Jeanne in our commandeered abode, I tell her what has happened and she was glad that I wasn't going, but she said something that disconcerted me.  That Titania was politically astute to put this seeming on.  We went to bed and went to sleep after a goodly time.

The following day, we have the Challenges to fight.  Caradoc had challenged Lancelot the previous night, while I was trying to deal with Brennos kidnapping.  No one will challenge me for whatever reason, so I am challenging Lancelot.  I believe that Ulfius is after Arcavius, while Amils got quite a few from all the companions to the ladies' favours he had accepted the previous day.  Caradoc came to me saying that he had the greater right to fight Lancelot, but I pulled rank as I had the greater glory.

Arcavius faced an unknown knight, but this poor unfortunate was majorly wounded in one blow.  Amils had an almighty (almost bland) combat which seemed to last for the entire morning.  I face Lancelot and he gets the first blow in.  It was a mighty blow that sent me flying backwards more than a score of feet.  As the blow had landed, I had noted that Lancelot's shield was "special" (it gave him the strength of two men).  As I get up, stretch and walk back to him, I calmly ask him if it was fair to use that shield.

He looks at it, and then looks at me apologetically and says sorry.  We go and change our shields. his magic one for a normal one, and my now-½ shield for a whole one.  We return to the Melee Field, and trade mighty blows. taking turns to knock each other down (although I find it hard to admit that I was being knocked down more than he was).  After much spectacular swordplay, I ask him whether we can call it a draw and offer him my hospitality, which thus takes him away from a fight with Caradoc (at least, so I hope).

Niani had tried to speak to me as I was about to go out to fight, and I had to brush her off. I found out after the fight that she had left a message with Hywel for me.  Niani had spoken with Arcavius and had then disappeared to visit Voklaynn.

Lancelot and I had been chatting for a while with Nia (14) and the twins (13) and Jeanne.  Nia is quite smitten with Lancelot.  I leave them alone in conversation as much as possible, but all too soon Arcavius tells me that Niani had told him that Maeve had told her that she strongly believed that Arthur had slept with someone else, but that Arthur was acting as if he hadn't.  Acting as in ignorance, not with any guile.  He doesn't seem to cope well with that.  Maybe someone who could take on another's appearance … not Morgaine … surely … but Jeanne nods.

Niani also comes in and says she wants to talk to me about getting Brennos back before the end of the day.  I know that something must be done about this but the issue with Arthur was but another version of the vision of Arthur's most greatest danger which was granted to us by the Fish … Wise One.  I immediately start searching for Merffyn.  Find him in the midst of the festival being held behind the noble pavilions.  He's eating some pie and making a pig of himself.

Whisper to him that the vision of danger to Arthur has come to pass.  We all talk about it.  Merffyn asks if there are any reasons why he shouldn't kill Morgaine and we come up with the following 1) she is Oberon's daughter, 2) she is Ailil's wife (maybe lapsed, maybe not), 3) it could upset the balance between the Fae, 4) she's Arthur's sister (but this doesn't seem to be all that relevant anymore, if she will be the source of greatest peril for Arthur).  Looking at us, suddenly Merffyn seems to vanish before our eyes for such a short time that some of us doubt that he had gone.

Arcavius says we need to cut all ties with the Fae, but I know of no way to do so.  But Merffyn tells us that we need a strong enough king to be able to do so.  Maybe with the Stone of Destiny with Arthur and the Cauldron with Voklaynn, who supports Arthur, might be enough.

Catch up with Caradoc and have a number of private discussions with him.  He must never place personal vengeance above his responsibility as a leader.  If the Melee had been a real battle, his men would have been decimated in seconds and overwhelmed in minutes, and left the rest of the army with its asses hanging in the air.  I don't yell, but I'm greatly disappointed with him.  His followers deserve more from him.  I also make sure that I don't mention these discussions with anyone, including Jeanne.

In a few days, we will be mobilizing Arthur's army to begin it's move North and I have been given the task of mobilizing it.  In the midst of the organizing, Niani breaks into the midst of a conference and says she needs my help with Angus again.  After dealing with the immediate concerns, I look in a few tents to find an empty one and lead us in.  Probably due to my other concerns, I failed to give the music it's due consideration and only played barely adequately.

As I come to a halt, we are interrupted by a mostly cloaked "knight" with a scroll for me looking askance at the known married commander with the beautiful female strange lady.  Initially I was surprised at being caught in this situation which could be interpreted wrongly, but then I knew who it was.  I accepted the scroll, which had some runic strokes on it. passing it to Niani to read.

She read "Be not afraid" in Brennos' hand on some blue silken material which I assumed was Titania's.  I then pointed to some much smaller writing in the silk and Niani said it was in another's hand saying "the sword is for the child".  Looking askance at each other, we could not decide whether this meant that the sword was going to be given to a child, or the sword was going to be used against a child.  Although a child might mean trouble in the future, I would oppose quite violently any harm against one.

He tells me I will lose, looking meaningfully at Arthur.  But even though it breaks my heart to say it to this seeming, I don't believe it is my father.  I tell it so, and if it was really my father, then he would have been forced to say this for he would know I would not bow under this weight.  As I stride forward to cease this temptation, I am almost betrayed when I have to physically brush him aside.  Arthur, of course, encounters "Uther".  Arthur is struck dumb and is stunned until I verbally prod him by asking does he want to continue or turn back, it being his choice, and his alone.  He splutters back to life and asks how I could ask him that and he moves on past Uther.

Amils meets his "King Clovis".  Brennos encounters a young boy who is never explained to anyone else.  Drust comes upon a young, tattooed woman.  To me, all these "ghosts" felt like they were malicious and offered us nothing but disapproval and words of despair.  Whether they really were the spirits of our sorely missed, dearly departed or not, the intelligence that controlled them was inimical to us.

We travelled ever onwards, but now we could really enjoy the never-ending rain in it's natural sounds.  In fact, the rain was changing from coming straight down, to sleet, and now to coming almost horizontally at us.  Eventually, after an indeterminate, miserable time of scrambling upwards, Drust bundles all of us into a cave.  The crone who had accompanied us, but never came near to any other than Drust, pointed out the cave to Drust.  As we all come the realization that we are all out of the rain except for that crone, we turn back and see that she is changing shape into a crane.  Brennos recognizes it as a spying creature from the Summer Country, similar to what we had all been thinking … well the spying part at least.

We go down the tunnel after Drust prays to a rock he calls Crom.  Whatever else it may have done, it's made Drust happy.  But as we go, we can begin to hear the sounds of armour creaking and clinking, and weapons hitting rock, so much sound that we fear that it may be an entire army in here with us.  Eventually, we reach a cavern which is larger than the area our torches light.  In trying to follow the most obvious path across the cavern, we are ambushed by those small rock-like creatures which were below Silchester all those years before.  Kobolds.

Although we were outnumbered three to one, we slew most of them and the survivors bolted into the darkness.  As some of us are giving in to the urge to follow and finish the fleeing Kobolds, we hear a deafening roar come from ahead.  Ahead, that is, in the direction we had been heading.  Reaching the far side of the cavern, we find another tunnel leads to wooden and stone constructed landing which has a rope bridge (a la Indiana Jones) which leads across a gaping chasm to a matching landing.  There is a blue-green phosphorescence spread throughout these caves, which is disturbing as well as illuminating.  The chasm is about four to five hundred feet deep, and about thirty feet across at the top, but thinner at the base.  Also, at the base of the chasm, a river can be seen running lengthwise along it.

## Varis the Black

On the landing opposite us stands a tall, cloaked, black-garbed, distinguished-looking figure with smoky green glowing eyes.  Although Amils recognizes this as probably the Dragon in human form, I doubt anyone had any other idea.  It arrogantly, yet coldly smiles across at us and gestures sweepingly at us; the gesture from which a green, sparkling ball of energy forms and flies sharply across the bridge at us.  While everybody's instinct was to scatter and avoid this ball, I have been in battle enough now for me to use some judgement.  I chose a very risky course by charging directly at the dragon, dodging its strange missile and attempt to quickly cross the bridge so that the others would be able to soon after.  Unfortunately, as I was partway across and dodging the missile, I slipped and almost fell.  But as I slipped, my concern was for Arthur so I managed to espy him swinging Excalibur and hit the projectile with the blade, whereupon the missile careened to the ceiling and exploded.  I am pretty sure that the Dragon was incensed by this, for with a snarl and a series of wild gestures and from the base of the chasm comes a grating roar.

The Dragon has summoned a ghost lizard thing from below the waters and the flying ghost sweeps rapidly upwards while I struggle to get back on the bridge again.  As I try this, Arthur scampers past me and is almost across when the ghost smashes into the bridge between Arthur and I.  As the bridge begins to collapse between us, the ghost flies up and inverts through itself directly back at where it came from, smashing the bridge apart.  As the two halves of the bridge fall to the walls of the chasm, I am buffeted but my eyes are only on Arthur.  Arthur appears unhurt and again is scrambling up to the other landing.

My half of the bridge is still shaking, so I look up and see Arcavius scrambling upwards.  Unfortunately, as I had been closer to the break and in a more precarious position, I had become entangled in the ropes.  When the ghost lizard came in once more, I thought I was done for as it appeared to head directly for me.  However, it smashed into and through the ropes above me.  The good news was that I was no longer entangled, but the bad news was I now had nothing but water below me some distance and was wearing armour.

I plummet and land badly in the river.  Although I am slightly hurt and winded, I find that I am sorely motivated to swim (critical, not inspired).  I swim to the side to get to Arthur, and find that there are paths along both sides of the chasm going up in carved stairs to openings 100' below the landings.  I climb to the opening on my side and attract my companions attention and try and tell them that there must be a way down from where they are.  Eventually Arcavius seems to understand.  They go back and find the passageway down to the chasm stairs but they are taking way too long for me.  The sounds of battle and roars comes from above, Arthur is in mortal danger so I can only hold back for so long before my patience is frittered away completely.

I forge ahead in the tunnel hoping that the slight luminescence will be enough for me, but I took a wrong turn and found I had walked into a proper maze, the likes of which would be a good castle defence.  But I used my wiles before I became too frantic, and found my way back to the proper path.  By this stage, I find that my detour has allowed my companions to get in front of me.  They had encountered a few skulls which apparently had screamed in alarm once, but were now completely shattered and they ended through a doorway into a finely hewn corridor with a dead Fomori slashed in half.  It is here I catch up with them.

Arcavius is badly wounded.  We enter a richly furbished … throne room, you could say.  There are very fine Dwarven decorations, says Niani … whatever Dwarves are.  There is also a fine painting of a beautiful blonde woman which is hung behind a bejewelled cedar chest, with a handle on its top, from which Arcavius can hear soft female singing.  As everyone stops in amazement, Amils is being put to sleep by the singing.  I rush forward and take the box by its handle, which makes the singing increase in volume and power, but still I resist its siren call.

Opening the chest and looking in … at a skull.  I greet it aloud and am not too surprised that it returns my greetings with a fine soprano voice.  Although the lack of movement of the skull and jaws is off-putting, it is offset by the fact that the somnolent singing has ceased.  Slightly nonplussed, I introduce myself and in return find that it is the Lady Bethannon, wife of Varis the Black.  I can guess that Varis the Black is, in fact, the Black Dragon who practices the necromantic arts, but I think Arcavius recognized Lady Bethannon's name in her own right for he blanched at the name and whispered for me to be very polite.

Introductions are made genteelly all around.  So, in the polite discussion that followed, we find out that she has the power to foretell things.  Like she knew we were coming, but she only told Varis that some adventurers were coming, not who we were nor that Excalibur would be used.  Apparently, she has a fond desire to be a widow.  As this fits in with what we want, too, as well as Amils particular peculiarity with dangerous, mystical females with dangerous amounts of power, Amils manages to promise her anything she wants from us in return for help.  Oddly enough, she graciously accepts and we also convince her to aid us in retrieving the cauldron.  But when we try and find out if there is any other treasure in Varis' hoard, she becomes quite agitated and fractious.

With Arthur still in danger and Varis still to lay to rest as well as make our escape, there is only so much time we can waste.  We take Bethannon in her chest and the oil painting of her, at her insistence, of course.  We race quickly to get the Cauldron. and then only Niani and I are unencumbered, so I take the lead and she takes up the rear.  As we pass through the rooms and hallways, we can see they're richly furbished and dwarf us in size, but they are only giant-sized, not dragon-sized.

Now we have to cross what Bethannon called Varis' observatory to reach the duelling pair and escape.  We reach a huge, double-doorway which is closed and barred on our side.  It takes four of us to lift the beam and put it well away to the side.  Looking in we at first focus on the floor, for it is well-covered in sigils and mystical symbols.  As Bethannon is telling us that they allow you to see better, some of us are looking up at what we would assume normally opened to the sky, but now was covered in an inky blackness which had no naturalness to it.

My feeling of franticness, which had never disappeared since being separated from Arthur, arose quite powerfully and I lead us directly across the centre.  But as we were crossing it, a magical gathering of power occurs (something like electricity, but different).  Some were damaged, but most of us weren't.  We continue on towards the stairway down, which is on the opposite wall.

However, a spectre rises from the stairs.  A number of us try using our different religious rituals or just simple faith to avert it, as we knew our weapons were useless against it.  I trust in my faith to protect me and walk through it. but I am still chilled dangerously by it.  Then Amils is reminded of the cross he wears and waves it at the spirit. it remembers it is supposed to fear the symbol, so it did flee … through me and down the stairs, again chilling me.  But as it fully passed through me, I also felt some of its feelings of general malevolence and its intense fear of Varis.

We go down the stairs and begin to hear distant sounds of combat.  We enter a huge cavern and in the distance see Arthur is continuing to fight Varis.  Arthur is amongst some rocks, dashing out to strike Varis, who is trying to entice him into the open.  They look very tired and wounded, with Varis looking as if he has exhausted his spells and his breath, and his wounds look worse.  I am so happy that Arthur has that magic scabbard.  As we are greeted by this sight, Arthur gets in a blow which makes Varis roar especially loudly and the absolute darkness from above is lifted and the normal, overcast night sky becomes visible.  Immediately, Varis claws Arthur away and he is sent flying back amongst the rocks.

Now, just as we start to rush towards Varis, he turns and faces us.  As we realize that this may be our last battle, a different roar comes from above. a red dragon dives in and attacks Varis from above.  The impact starts the dragons tumbling end over end away.  We continue rushing to Arthur, but he is just starting to hobble after Varis.  He brushes our help aside and we can see that he is physically healing before our eyes.  Then we follow Arthur's lead, who is following the dragons who went off the ledge's side and down the abyss that cuts into Ben Nevis.  As we are taking a path down, the struggling dragons fly up and crash into the mountain-side above us and the debris almost knocked Arcavius off the trail.  But it did knock the oil painting from his hands.  We had to recover it for Bethannon.  Thankfully it was only 15 feet down and Arcavius recovered it after being lowered by rope.  By this time, Arthur has left us behind.

We continue down and not soon enough leave Ben Nevis' side, and after some effort find Arthur's trail.  We come to a dark hummock, which we find to be a dead Varis … and we're standing in his blood.  Because of this, we have lost Arthur's trail.  Calling out, we hear a soft-voiced reply which does turn out to be a tired Arthur.  He is covered in this black blood, and my heart goes out to him for he is slightly rocking back and forth and wringing his hands together.  As we also find a badly wounded and unconscious Red Dragon nearby, Arcavius manages to catch a glimpse of Arthur's eyes to see that they are totally black.  Arthur needs no first aid, and Voklaynn needs a lot of it but how do you heal a dragon?  I try and cauterize one of the scores on her chest with a torch, but even I can tell that it won't be enough.

As we discuss what we can do, like transport us using Voklaynn's medallion that Arcavius and Niani have, but Niani calls Anwyn's secret true name.  As we then tried Arcavius' medallion and found it wouldn't work, and Niani was confident that Anwyn was on his way, I knew that Dragon parts would be useful for mages, so I gathered as much of his blood as I could, as well as a couple of claws and a tooth.  Niani and Arcavius are watching around for any incoming danger, and Arthur is getting quite frantic about Voklaynn and the need to save her, including trying to rock her awake.  But as we think there is something much more serious with Arthur than shock, we want to have someone like Anwyn available to help "heal" him.

A strange unicorn of great power appears, and then changes into Anwyn. and he has brought the Horn of Ilvilon, which he pulls out from its wrappings with the words: "Now I bring you out of darkness…".  He uses it on Voklaynn.  She arises after a very short while and glides over to a higher position.  We point out to Anwyn what appears to be wrong with Arthur.  He tries to approach Arthur, but for each step he takes, Arthur takes an involuntary one back.  Anwyn tries again, but now Arthur's eyes begin to glow smoky green, like Varis' had, and he draws Excalibur.  I use my shield to block his blow on Anwyn, but I then attempt to brush his sword aside and close in where he can't swing Excalibur, and grapple him to the ground.  As I do so, I unfortunately trip over his extended leg. just as I'm totally open to attack, Anwyn steps forward and tried his magic over a distance and exorcised Arthur of Varis.

But then almost immediately, Brennos started acting possessed.  Then Anwyn does the ritual again for everybody.  Everyone now felt at peace and happy and spiritually cleansed.  Arthur doesn't remember what happened after he had gotten underneath Varis and stabbed upwards at his underbelly and was drenched in his blood.  To help staunch his confusion, I ask him to help me gift the Cauldron to Voklaynn.  Finding some focus now, he quickly regains his poise.

We travel to Voklaynn's castle after we finalise our scavenging of Varis' body (including the entire head as a trophy for Voklaynn) and including some skin for Arcavius.  When Varis returns, as we are assured by Anwyn and Voklaynn he will return, he will be very annoyed at all of us, but he will really count Arcavius as his enemy.

Bethannon is shown Varis dead, but she still portends that he will return.  As we have already been told of this, this is the last straw for us.  We must get rid of her.

We find out from a reluctant Anwyn that he had to make an arrangement with another family of unicorns to get to us, but he won't explain any further.

## A Gift for Oberon

In a plan as fully Roman as ever I heard, Arcavius gets us to give Bethannon to Voklaynn as a gift from her to Oberon.  As this amuses her greatly, Voklaynn is quite happy to accede.  Obviously sensing the shift with the Great Treasure, Oberon appears a few days after we have arrived.  Although he comes unannounced, the staff have done wonders and have a feast fit for a king ready for him and everyone else.  Voklaynn makes a big deal of the presentation, wrapped as it in a bejewelled cedar chest, and as time passes during his visit, it is obvious to all that Oberon is like a cat in that his curiosity is almost uncontrollable.  But he does control it enough to continue to visit his son and leave … so he can open it as soon as possible.

Also Niani scares the fish (?) maybe.

We visit Goibniu.  Arcavius gives him the dragon skin because he wants it made into a suit of armour.  He also gives some of the dragon's blood … just as a gift.  Niani gets a shield which protects her from any attack.  I get a stronger shield which is practically unbreakable.  Amils gets a sword of sharpness (+1D to damage).

We will visit Voklaynn during the winter.
