# Parry, Riposte ... Checkmate

>**GM's Note:**  Deak Freehold is sent to investigate an unreliable Intel operative, one Sari Blackwood, singer.  Sari is involved with Imperial General Leith and no longer trusted by Intel.  Deak tests Sari's loyalty to the Alliance by using her to obtain information about and access to an Imperial research station developing biological warfare viruses.  Deak forces Sari to return to the Alliance with him, as she is too unreliable to remain with the Imperials.

## Written by [[Jenny-H|Jenny H.]] (Sari)

Sari sat in front of the mirror and stared sightlessly at it, frowning slightly.  She couldn't define what was disturbing her, just that there was uneasiness in the air, like the build-up of pressure before a storm.  She tried to picture the people in the bar, but there was no one who had struck her as unusual, just the collection of officers and civilians that she normally attracted by her singing.

Sari sighed and rubbed her eyes.  She hadn't felt safe since Brormal had come.  The Rebellion had been right, after all.  She didn't have the temperament to be a spy.  Once the pain of her parents' death had eased, she kept wondering why she was doing this.  With luck, they had forgotten about her.  No one had approached her for months and she no longer had a channel for information anyway.

Sari was putting the finishing touches to her party face when the door opened.

"I don't..." she began, then turned and saw the grey uniform.  "Yes?"

The man looked at the woman he had been sent to find and wasn't impressed.  She was satisfactorily pretty and more sophisticated than his briefing had led him to expect, but he didn't like the softness in her expression.  She'd also been far too ready to change her mind about letting him in when she'd seen the Imperial uniform.  Was it part of her cover or not?  If the rumours were true, she didn't need to cower before a junior Imperial officer any more.  Deak closed the door behind him.

"I came for information."

Sari raised one eyebrow in disbelief.  "Oh?"

"I want any information you have on Rebellion troop movements."

Sari stared at him.  "I think you've got the wrong room.  Lieutenant..."  The pause was a question.  Deak ignored it.

"I have no idea why you're asking me this.  I just sing to the troops.  They don't talk to me about their missions.  Surely you ought to be talking to their commanders?"

"Considering your background, I thought you might have additional information."  Let's see whether she was stupid enough to answer a direct question.

"You can't be serious.  Any additional information I had would be two years out of date and don't you think I would have told the investigating officers years ago, if I'd known anything?  I wish I had been able to tell them something.  I wasn't there when the Rebels murdered my family."  Sari turned back to the mirror.  You're raving, girl.  Stop or you'll lose it all.  She didn't have to force the tears, they came unbidden to her eyes.  "If you don't mind, this is a painful subject for me.  Please go."

At least she could act, thought Deak.  But that didn't mean she was still loyal.  "Very well, we'll discuss this later then."

"Oh, Lieutenant," she added, as he turned to go.  "If you do want to discuss this matter further, I'm sure you could make arrangements through General Leith to do so in a more convenient place."

I'm handling this poorly, she thought.  She was playing a dangerous game here.  The situation was becoming more complicated by the minute.  First, Brormal, her Intel contact, tried to set her up, now this unknown asking questions about her family.  Was he Imperial or Alliance?  An Imperial would be easier to get rid of, given Leith's rank.  Either way, she knew who had really destroyed her home.  The Imperials had gone to great lengths to cover their tracks, but she'd found out the truth.  If either the Alliance or the Imperials ever found out how she'd discovered the truth, they could use it against her.  That was something she couldn't afford to have happen.

Once he had gone, Sari sat still for a moment, pondering her strange visitor.  Then she quickly gathered up the matching white shawl to her dress, wrapping it around her short, black hair, so as to blend more easily into the stark walls.  Then she hurried down the corridor he must have left by.  It was late and the stranger led her into one of the less populated areas of the domed city so that, even for an untrained shadow like herself, it wasn't that hard to keep track of him.  Besides, she knew the intersecting corridors and squares reasonably well.

Sari was mentally congratulating herself that she wasn't too bad at this game, when she rounded a corner and walked straight into her visitor.  The casual way he leaned against the wall worried her more than any physical threat would have.

"Well, well, well.  You know, if you'd really wanted to talk to me some more, we could have stayed in your dressing room."

"This is my way home."

He shifted his body, subtly reminding her of just how much bigger he was than her.  "Try again."

Think, girl, think.  She reached inside herself for her inner sanctuary and cleared the fear from her thought processes.  "I wanted to know why you came to see me."

"I told you."

"If the Imperials thought I had information about Rebel troop movements, they wouldn't send a single, low-ranking officer to ask me polite questions in my dressing room.  I'd be in a centre somewhere answering questions from a mind probe."

She'd surprised him with her forthright answer.  "And do you know anything about Rebel troop movements?"

"Absolutely nothing," she replied, truthfully.

This tack isn't going to work.  I'll have to figure out where she stands another way.  For now, it'd be better to get out of here and into some place more private.  He had more questions he wanted answered.

He wasn't Imperial.  She was sure of it now.  And her instincts were never wrong.

"Come with me."

Deak took her arm and tucked it into his.  Despite the appearance that they were just another couple out for an evening stroll, Sari didn't doubt that she wouldn't make it far if she decided to run.  He took her down a side street and triggered a palm lock.  Sari noted that it looked like a temporary bolt-hole at best.

"Now, talk."

"My mother taught me never to talk to strangers."

Hang on, this is my territory.  Isn't she supposed to be nervous?  He smiled enigmatically.  "Allow me to rectify the situation.  I am Lieutenant... Vader."  Two could play at this game.  With a cold twist in his stomach, he realised he'd have to kill her now if she didn't come up with the right answers.

Something had changed in him.  It was serious now.  Her life hung in the balance.  Sari licked her lips nervously.  This had to sound good.  "The unit insignia you're wearing.  They left last week.  And they don't do this kind of questioning anyway.  Your information is out of date.  So, if you're not Imperial, who are you?"

"I thought you said you didn't know anything about troop movements."

She chose her words deliberately.  "You asked me about Alliance troop movements."

Finally, I've got her nervous again, he thought to himself, triumphantly.

Sari started to wonder if her instincts had been right after all.  Ah well, she had no choice now.  "You'd only be approaching me in this way if you were no longer sure if I could be trusted."

If she's gone Imperial, I need to know fast.  "And can you be?"  Damn!  He caught himself.  I might as well be announcing who I am with a question like that.

"The last contact was a spy.  What did you expect me to do?"

"So what did you do?"

"I.. I made sure he couldn't report to anyone."

"Wouldn't that have made them suspicious?"

"It looked like an accident.  It's been a while, they haven't come after me."

"What if they're waiting to see what you do next?"

Sari took a deep breath and shrugged.  He'd better be who she thought he was.  She was compromising herself badly here and he was giving nothing away.  Why couldn't he just come out and say if he was Alliance or not.

Deak watched her, quietly assessing.  He noted her agitation.  She had to be pulled, loyal or not.  It was obvious she was starting to crack.

"So what now?  You want out?"

"No!" Sari said, quickly.  "No, I'm useful here."

There was one last thing.  "But what have you been doing lately?  Anything worthwhile?"

Sari winced internally.  There was a sting in that question, despite the careful couching of it.  "There have been complications.  I couldn't get you the information without compromising the source.  And he's far too useful to just drop."

So the rumour was true.  Her reaction proved she was getting a little too close to one of the Imperials. "What's the point of keeping you here then?"

Sari was feeling hounded on all sides, but she had a last hand to play.  "There is something.  I haven't been able to get the details, but the Imperials have been doing some kind of research.  Something big.  Some sort of disease they plan to let loose on Alliance-controlled planets.  Easier than fighting them."

"You've been sitting on something as important as biological warfare?"  Deak was appalled.  He'd seen the results of that before.  "For a fragile source?"

Sari hurried to defend herself.  "I didn't have a reliable contact, remember?  Did you really think I'd risk using just anyone for this sort of information?"

"Surely you've got something for us to work on?  Anything?"

"No, I haven't been able to get close to it.  Nightclub singers don't usually get to casually poke around in secret research labs!"

"How can I trust you?" he asked, sombrely.

"How can I trust you?" she responded.

"Point taken.  But we still need the information."

"I can't get it on my own.  But there is a way."

Quickly, Sari outlined her plan.  It involved telling Deak more than she really wanted to, but she had no choice.  This was too important to keep to herself.  Not when the cost was so high.

"How can I get in touch with you, if I need to?" she asked.

He smiled and laughed.  "Just call the base and ask for Lieutenant Vader."

She looked at him coolly.  "You'd better change that uniform then, Lieutenant Vader.  Out here, nobody but a fool questions an Imperial officer.  But don't go near the base in that without changing the insignia.  You might get past a sloppy guard who just checks to see that everything is where it should be on a uniform, but I wouldn't count on it."

Sari waited impatiently for the next night and not just on the Alliance's account.  Leith was coming home.  That was, if his schedule was to pattern.

There was nothing artificial about her greeting of him.  Sari had been initially uncertain about starting a relationship with an Imperial officer, especially such a high ranking one, but there was something different about Leith.  He had none of the darkness she sometimes felt in the officers she came in contact with.  She even had suspicions sometimes that he disapproved of the direction the Empire had taken, having risen in the ranks in a time when talent had meant something.  All too soon, the possibility of useful information from him was ignored, as Sari didn't want to put him in danger as the source.

Afterwards, Sari lay with her head on Leith's chest and hoped Deak had fulfilled his part of the plan.  It should have been easy for him to collect the security passkey from the scattered clothes in her antechamber.  And there was no possibility of Leith being implicated, provided Deak didn't get caught.  Those few who were issued with the passkeys could go where they wanted without it being recorded.  Sometimes the Imperial's paranoia worked in her favour.

Sari was suddenly afraid.  The feeling of storm build-up was still there.  She looked up at Leith and said, urgently, "Whatever happens, remember I do love you."  There, it was said at last.

Leith looked searchingly at her. "Is something wrong?"

Have I said too much?  I can't let him suspect.  "It's just that you're away so much lately.  I'm afraid you'll find someone else and never come back."

Leith laughed, a low rumble of pleasure.  "Not if you keep greeting me like this!"

"Well, if that's all it takes!"

Much later, Sari relinquished Leith to his duties, the passkey thankfully back in its place, and went to meet Deak.

"It wasn't there."

"What?  But the passkey was in his pocket.  I saw it."

"Not the passkey," he replied impatiently.  "The research lab.  I overheard two techs talking though.  Something was moved to Complex MW under tight security."

"That's easy then.  You can get in that through the service ducts."

"The what?"

"There are some old service ducts the original inhabitants left behind when they were forced off.  Most people are too small to get through there, so they don't bother guarding it as well as they should."

"Don't they know that?"

"Don't be silly.  If they knew they don't guard it well enough, they would guard it better.  Besides, it's only a temporary holding bay.  They're probably planning to move the stuff out fairly soon."

Deak grew impatient with her.  Her casual attitude and over-confidence annoyed him.  It was a wonder she hadn't been caught long before now.  It better not be a trap.  "Something this big will be guarded, all the same.  Before we go charging in, we need a plan.  Now, tell me what you know of the area."

Several hours later, crawling through the ducts, Sari wondered how she got herself into these situations.  She just kept on reminding herself that if the Imperials were ready to move the research out, then there had to be an antidote ready as well.  They were too careful not to have one.  Even if she were wrong, the Alliance could manufacture one with the base virus.  Or whatever the damn thing was.

Deak's surmises about the area were correct.  He'd obviously done this sort of thing before.  The service duct ended in the storage area, which was guarded by two stormtroopers.  The virus would be in the bay beyond.  Sari contemplated her options, mentally mapping her way around the bay.  She needed to get to the bay door and inside it unseen.  Fortunately, the guards were looking out, rather than in.  She screwed up her courage and moved cautiously out.

Sari reached the bay door with little trouble.  There were advantages to being small and light.  She waited until the guards were distracted, then punched the keypad, ducking and rolling in as the door opened and whooshed shut behind her.  The device Deak had given her flew from her hand.

Sari breathed deeply, calming her nerves.  The droid hovered precariously in the air, one appendage with its weapon pointing directly at her.  Blue sparks crackled about it.

Deak had been right.  She hadn't thought this through.  She tried not to think of the consequences and silently thanked him for the device that had disrupted its electromagnetic field.  She only had a short time though.  The droid was already starting to move slightly, its back-up systems calculating a counter to the effect.

She worked as quickly as she could.  Fortunately, the Imperials had little imagination when it came to storage canisters.  They were all much the same.  That, combined with what training the Alliance had given her, meant she could open the storage container with relative ease.  The Imperials were getting too complacent in this sector, too complacent by far.

All the same, she decided not to copy the information, but to just take the whole canister.  It would take too long for anything else and the droid wouldn't stay immobile for ever.  What she was after was small enough to take entirely and, with luck, they wouldn't notice that it was gone.

The guards gave her a bad moment on the return to the ducts, choosing to give the area a brief check, but she made herself as small and insignificant as she could and they missed her.  Once she was back in the duct, she gave a heartfelt sight of relief.

At his post, Leith frowned slightly, considering recent events.  He took out his passkey and looked at it thoughtfully.  He reached out and operated the console.  "Sir," he said to the answering face.  "I suspect my passkey has been used.  The only person with possible access to it was Sari Blackwood.  I suggest you check the Research Room."

Deak was waiting for her.  "How are you getting this out?" she asked.

"I've got transport waiting for us."

"Us?  But I'm not going with you."  Didn't he care what she wanted?  The alarm sounded as Sari spoke.

"We'll argue about it later," Deak said.

They were challenged almost immediately.  "Halt.  This is a restricted area.  What..."

Deak cut off the conversation with his blaster.  "That settles that argument," he said, running down the corridor.  "They now know you're involved."

Sari panicked as she digested the implications.  "But if they know about me, they'll follow my trail to Leith.  I've got to warn him."

Deak forestalled her leaving.  "You come with me or I shoot you where you stand," he stated, coldly.  He couldn't afford to leave this foolish woman behind.  She knew too much and people could get killed because of her.  In her current state, she was useless as a contact.

Damn, he really means it thought Sari.  Her mind raced through possibilities, but there were few.  She went with him, hoping for an opportunity later on.  In this dress she wasn't readily identifiable and she might be able to bluff her way through.  She just had to wait a chance to elude this man.  Damn it!  She was in this because of her parents, not stupid idealism and the situation had changed.

Deak grabbed her arm and raced her through the corridors.  He'd certainly mapped out his escape route well.  They paused at one point till the sounds of pursuit had subsided for a space.  Deak keyed a door.

"Don't bother.  It's coded and your palm print needs to match."

"Fine," Deak turned away slightly, then grabbed Sari from behind, grabbed her hand and forced her palm against the pad.

Sari stared in horror.  The pad would register her print and they would have conclusive proof it was her.  Bluff was no longer an option.  There was no sympathy in Deak's expression, as he gently let her go.

He had a two-man shuttle ready not far away.  Sari tried a last minute struggle, unwilling to trust herself to this man, but Deak pushed her inside, at the same time clearing away the last opposition he encountered.  They were away before Sari had another chance to react.

Leith smiled in satisfaction at the console.  His timing had been right.  He reached for a button and started the necessary action for the next stage.

They were silent until well after they rendezvoused with their transport.  Deak was regretful at her distress, but the Alliance was more important than one person's dreams.  You couldn't afford to stay sentimental in this game.  Sari contemplated her life and tried to decide if it was better being manipulated for idealism or for power.

"I want you to send a message to the Imperials, offering to swap me for an Alliance agent they hold."

"What will that achieve?"

"It's the best I can do for Leith."  She half-smiled.  "I was hardly a willing passenger.  It's not much, but you might give them cause to think I was an Imperial agent."

"They'd know you weren't one of their agents."

"There are factions in the Imperials.  They use agents against each other, as well as against you.  They won't be able to be absolutely sure."

Deak shrugged.  "If you're not one of their faction's agents, they'll be glad to be rid of you."  But it was little enough to give.  And any confusion, however small, in the Imperial ranks was worth achieving.  "You really care for him then?"

Sari looked away.  "If I'd really cared for him, I wouldn't have placed him in this position.  I'd do anything to change that."

If the universe were fair she'd have that chance.
