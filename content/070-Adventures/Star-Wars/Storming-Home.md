# Storming Home

Jess is ordered off Generis Base by General Caroth, the newly arrived Alliance Commander now in charge.  With her goes Leith, Sari, and Nichelle (or does she?).

## Caroth Takes Over

General Kryll, Commander of one of the Rebel Alliance's strategically crucial Outer Rim Communications Centres (ORCCs), was rapidly developing an intense dislike of the newcomer and his party.  For the second time since the Command Group's unexpected and untimely arrival on Generis Base, Kryll found himself counting slowly to three before he spoke.

"General Caroth, I believe your criticisms of this base and its personnel are overly harsh.  While I agree that things aren't run strictly by the book here, our personnel are highly dedicated to the Alliance and our record of achievements in Atrivis Sector speaks for itself."

"I know what's been achieved by Generis Base personnel Kryll," replied Caroth.  "I have recently spoken with General Vernan and it would appear that Alliance Intelligence has collected quite a bit of interesting information on this base's activities."  Kryll noted the growing menace in Caroth's tone.

"What surprises me greatly" continued Caroth, "is that you have achieved what you have in spite of lax discipline and failure to adhere to basic Alliance procedures, particularly in the matter of regular reporting to Sector Command.  Having up-to-date information on this base's activities is imperative for Secom's planning efforts."

Travia Chan, Commander-in-Chief of Atrivis Sector, stared coldly back at Caroth.  "Secom has shown little interest in our activities since this base was established.  Our requests for more mechanics, trained pilots and spare parts have all been ignored, in spite of being submitted in accordance with procedures outlined during our initial Alliance briefing sessions.  As no support has been received from Secom, we have ceased wasting valuable time preparing and submitting regular reports."  As usual, Chan was blunt, to the point and expressed herself with all the welcoming warmth of deep space.  The force of her words was unexpected, coming as it did from a frail, aging woman in a repulsorchair.

Caroth appeared momentarily taken aback, looking down at Chan more closely than he had when he first arrived.  Kryll cheered mentally for his superior, once again reminded why her enemies called her the "Icewoman".  However, Caroth regained his composure quickly, leaving Kryll wondering if the man had enough sense to recognise the anger behind Chan's coldness.

"Commander Chan, you requested the same scarce resources that every other Sector Force requests, with no indication of why those resources were needed," replied Caroth.  "If you had provided Secom with more details of your intentions, their analysts would have assigned a higher priority to your requests.  In light of my conversation with General Vernan, I can say quite confidently that had you informed Secom of your intentions, your requests would have been approved."

Chan was not about to concede to Caroth.  "I question the wisdom of providing details of this base's plans and intentions to Secom.  Recent events indicate our communication channels to Secom may have been compromised.  I have no intention of exposing this base and its personnel to unacceptable risk just so some analyst can get their sums right.  As for "lax discipline", I will remind you that this base has suffered the least casualties and accounted for more Imperial personnel, ships and supplies per head than any other Alliance Sector Force with the exception of Yavin Base.  I too have contacts in Intel."

There was an uncomfortable silence between Chan and Caroth.  Kryll became acutely aware of the murmured queries and responses passing between his technicians as they went about their jobs behind him.  Even the background hum of the ORCC equipment was unusually loud.  He glanced at Loom Carplin, Chan's Chief-of-Staff, who had been standing behind Chan's repulsorchair in silent support of his Commander-in-Chief.

"Well General Caroth, why are you and your people here?" asked Chan, finally breaking the tense silence.  Caroth didn't answer immediately but appeared to be considering his reply.

"After learning about your recent successes from General Vernan, I have persuaded Secom to build on the demonstrated abilities of Generis Base by supplying additional mechanics, pilots, starfighters and spares in line with your earlier requests".  Kryll could hardly believe his ears; everything they had given up asking for was about to be dropped in their laps.  There had to be a catch.

"That still doesn't explain why you're here," remarked Chan.  Kryll looked at Caroth and realised why the General and his party had descended on Generis Base.  Secom didn't believe Chan was up to the task of commanding the expanded Sector Force and had sent Caroth to take over.  The man wasn't even making any attempt to hide his satisfaction at the direction the conversation was taking.

"Secom has assigned control of the expanded Atrivis Sector Force to me.  This includes the ORCC, this Generis Base space station in which it resides, the attached starfighter wing, the Alliance ground troops on Fest and Mantooine and the additional personnel, when they arrive."  Caroth looked at Chan, regarding her with an expression bordering on pity.

"Secom appreciates your efforts to date, Commander Chan, but feels I am more suited to command the new Atrivis Sector Force," he said, handing the orders to Chan.  The Alliance crest and the Chief, Sector Command's signature was prominent for all to see.  Yes, thought Kryll sourly, there was always a catch.

"General Caroth, we've handled those recent events you keep mentioning without any assistance from Secom," stated Carplin, joining the discussion for the first time.  "We can continue to manage without your assistance."  Even Carplin was riled by Caroth's manner.  Big, bluff and immensely strong, Carplin discouraged most intelligent beings from angering him without even having to open his mouth.

"Yes" said Caroth, looking calmly at Carplin despite Carplin's radiating anger.  "Yes, you have been successful in the past, but Secom believes Generis Base can be even more potent under an experienced commander, one who appreciates the need for rigorous procedural control and tight security.  General Vernan's personnel have raised doubts about the continued secrecy and security of this base.  Secom has given me the authority to address Intel's concerns however I see fit."  He looked at Chan.  "I also do not wish to expose Alliance personnel to unacceptable risks.  If I do not have the full support of all senior officers on Generis Base I will reassign the starfighter wing and relocate the ORCC."

Chan was quiet, remembering the time before the Alliance strategists came to Atrivis Sector, before the treaty was signed and Generis Base established.  The Atrivis Resistance Group was struggling with poor communication channels, little warning of Imperial movements in the sector and no starfighter support.  Casualties on Fest and Mantooine were high.  She still saw some of their faces in her dreams.  "Very well then" sighed Chan.  "Where would you like to start, General Caroth?"

"I intend to begin my stay here by establishing proper order and adherence to procedures.  Beginning immediately I want all ships arriving and leaving this base to be fully scanned and properly identified before clearance is given."

"But General, a complete scan on a fully loaded transport will take"... began Carplin

"I don't care how long it takes, the security of this base is paramount" Caroth snapped, glaring at Carplin.  "And furthermore, beginning immediately this ORCC will institute the correct procedures for communications between this base and all other ships and personnel.  If you need clarification on what those procedures are, consult your Alliance Briefing Manuals."

"Hi, honey, I'm home!" Captain Jess Storm's customary greeting rang through the ORCC subspace COM link, her bright tone in complete contrast with the prevailing mood.

"Who or what was that?" demanded Caroth, glancing furtively around the room.

"That was Jess Storm, Captain of the _Red Storm Rising_.  She and her crew have been major contributors to our recent successes here in Atrivis Sector," replied Chan.

"Storm is one of General Vernan's concerns," stated Caroth coldly.  Kryll was stunned.  Surely Caroth didn't seriously suspect Jess of being a danger to the base, the suggestion was ludicrous.

Corporal Scol, one of the three technicians on duty in the ORCC, struggled to remember the correct protocol and then responded to the incoming ship.  "Unidentified ship, please transmit correct landing codes" requested Scol.  The ship, having just dropped out of hyperspace, was now plainly visible on the holographic long-range sensor display in the centre of the room.

"Come off it," replied Jess, "I just gave it!"  She yelled into the freighter's main cabin, "Ash, get up here pronto, we may have a problem."

Scol, conscious of Caroth's baleful stare, tried again.  "Unidentified craft please transmit correct landing codes."  Kryll sighed inwardly.  He was genuinely fond of Jess and particularly intrigued by her knack of getting beings rapidly offside with what appeared to be very little effort on her part.  Kryll looked over at Caroth's darkening face and feared the worst for the Corellian.

"Send out two X-wings to intercept that ship and escort it back to base.  If there's any trouble, tell the pilots to destroy that ship immediately" ordered Caroth.  Scol complied, quickly switching to the base's internal COM link channel and passing the order on to the duty pilots in the landing bay below.  Kryll decided it was time to intervene.

"General Caroth, Jess and her crew have risked their lives on numerous occasions on behalf of the Alliance."

"However I see fit," reminded Caroth, turning to watch the display.  The two X-wings appeared, speeding out toward the flashing red visual of the incoming ship.

 The crew of the _Red Storm Rising_ were becoming concerned.  Wing Commander Varth slid into the navigator's chair alongside Jess.  Ash, the _Red Storm Rising's_ navigator and more properly the occupant of the chair, made no objection but leaned over the main flight console between Varth and Jess.  Lou and Roger crowded into the cabin doorway, the tall thin scout wrestling the burly ex-pirate for space, but without much success.

"Think they've been compromised?" Jess asked Ash.

"From this distance?" responded Ash, surprised at Jess' faith in his Jedi skills.  Her open scorn of Jedi in general and of his mentor in particular was well known among the crew.  The woman never failed to amaze him.  However, one look at Jess' set face convinced Ash and he concentrated on the task at hand.

"I think he's nervous, but not, er, angry, er, aggressive. Whatever."

Varth switched on the link again.  "This is Wing Commander Varth. Is that you, Corporal Scol?"

"Yes, sir."  Even Jess could tell Scol was nervous now.

"Corporal," Varth continued, "if this ship ever transmits the correct landing codes, you are to consider it a hostile target.  Is that understood?"

"Yes, sir, but", Scol said as he was cut off by Caroth, who had unexpectedly closed the COM link channel.  Caroth turned to address Kryll, Carplin and Chan.

"Do I understand from this incident that the Generis Base Wing Commander is actively encouraging personnel to ignore correct procedure regarding communications contact with incoming ships?"

Kryll and Carplin jumped to Varth's defence, but were interrupted by Chan.  "Gentlemen, please."  She turned to Caroth.

"General Caroth, as Commander-in-Chief of this sector I strongly support any action taken by Wing Commander Varth.  He and General Kryll have been invaluable in establishing and protecting this base.  Varth has had to meet my requests for assistance with offensive operations in Atrivis Sector, Secom's requests regarding protection of the ORCC and Starfighter Command's requests for support in their combined starfighter operations.  He has managed this while woefully under equipped in mechanics, spare parts and trained pilots and in the most inappropriate and unsuitable maintenance facilities.  Generis Base was not designed as a starfighter operations centre and facilities are makeshift, to say the least.  Varth is a brilliant soldier whose flawless tactics have kept our pilots alive and victorious against terrible odds."

"Be that as it may, Commander Chan, I still believe that procedures would not have been devised unless they were necessary.  See that my orders are carried out and send a detachment of troops to the landing bay to await arrival of that ship.  Their orders are to shoot if there are any signs of trouble."

Chan sighed and nodded in Scol's direction.  Scol relayed Caroth's orders to the on-duty SpecForce Marine Sergeant.

"We've got company, Jess.  Two X-wings," commented Lou.  "An escort.  How sweet.  Hi boys, what's up?" queried Jess in her friendliest tone.

"Unidentified ship, you are to follow our lead and dock in the indicated landing bay.  If you attempt any other manoeuvre we will be forced to fire on you" replied the pilot of the lead X-wing.

"That's Carly," said Ash, startled at her blunt and deadly manner.  The X-wings were clearly within visual range.

"Can't she see it's us?" queried Jess.

Ash concentrated again, reaching out through the Force to touch Carly's mind.  He read confusion and fear, not of the _Red Storm Rising_ and its crew, but of the newcomer to the base.  He calmed and steadied her, just as he would have had she been in his arms.

"Follow us please, and land in the indicated bay" repeated Carly, more relaxed after Ash's mental soothing.

"Just what I was planning anyway" replied Jess.  "Got my usual spot ready?"  She flicked off the link.  "Go away for a while and the whole place goes crazy on you" she commented to Varth.

"They start enforcing the dress codes and I'm out of here!" added Roger from the cabin doorway.  Ash was quiet, worrying over how Jess was going to react to Caroth and vice versa.  The crew fell silent while Jess piloted the _Red Storm Rising_ into Generis Base's landing bay, following the guide lasers until the freighter was properly docked.

The detachment of SpecForce Marines was waiting for them.  Jess walked casually down the _Red Storm Rising's_ ramp and stood in front of the troops.

"Hi guys.  I'm not sure I'm ready for a party.  Got to do the post-flight checks yet.  Tell them to start without me." She went to check the _Red Storm Rising's_ outer hull, making no overt move towards her blaster.  Jess wasn't worried about the hull but she was making a point.  She had never paid attention to procedures before and she wasn't going to start now.

Varth followed Jess down the ramp.  "What's going on?" he queried the Sergeant, his tone icy.

"New base commander, sir. General Caroth from Secom."  The Sergeant was apologetic.  "I have orders for you and Captain Storm to report to him for debriefing."

"I'm sure you do, Sergeant.  Very well, we will accompany you as soon as post-flight checks have been completed"  Varth replied sharply.

"But, sir".

"That will be all, Sergeant."

"Yes, sir."

"Whoever this new base commander is, he's got them jumping through hoops for him," commented Jess as Varth joined her.  "With your rep Varth, I'm amazed the Sarge dared to question you."

"Well Jess, Caroth's presence explains that landing query.  I met him before I was assigned to Generis Base, he's a totally by-the-book man.  I suggest you tread carefully."

"Varth, I will start as I mean to go on.  If he doesn't like it, he can stick it."

"Jess, are you sure you mean that?"

She took a long, hard look at him.  "You don't like this man Caroth, do you Varth?"

"Whether I like him or not is irrelevant, Jess.  If he is now in command of this base, his word is law."

"I bet Carplin and Chan love that," added Jess ruefully.

"Well Jess, why don't you finish the post flight checks and then we can find out what's going on.  One more tap on the hull should do it" Varth said, eyeing the battered exterior of the _Red Storm Rising_.

## In Caroth's Office

General Caroth and his Command Group had moved into quarters in one of the less frequented sections of the base.  The one resource this relic of the Clone Wars wasn't short of was crew accommodation.  Jess and Varth were announced by Caroth's aide, a young man with an air of crisp military precision and not a hair out of place.  On entering the hastily converted office they found themselves facing a solidly built desk, behind which sat a large man, ruddy of face with a severe cut to his red hair.  Caroth reminded Jess a little of Kryll, but without the redeeming features.  She stood at ease, her arms crossed despite Varth's warning glance.

"This base has been run in an entirely lax way" began Caroth, ignoring any preliminaries.  "You, Storm, are a case in point."

"Captain Storm," interrupted Jess, casually.

Caroth glared at her.  "It is my understanding that you have refused to commit yourself to the Alliance" he snapped.

"That is correct," replied Jess.  "However, it is customary for a freighter captain to be given that title."

"It is not customary for unaligned personnel to come and go from an Alliance base as they please," replied Caroth, his face darkening again.  Jess felt her anger rising, but remained calm.

"Whatever" she said.  "I have a deal to do just that."

"Really.  What sort of deal allows a security risk like yourself to make use of Alliance resources whenever you see fit?" enquired Caroth.

"In exchange for one cloaking device which I retrieved from Minos Sector, Yerkys ne Dago was to be gotten off my back.  Until then, I'm permitted to use this base as a retreat, whenever I choose."

A look of triumph spread across Caroth's face.  "That deal has been fulfilled.  Your payment for the cloaking device, 250,000 credits, was shipped to a location nominated by ne Dago.  Intel has since received confirmation from their agent on Farstey that ne Dago has collected the payment".

Jess was stunned at the man's naivety.  "If the Alliance is stupid enough to believe that it can..." she began.

"Your opinion of the Alliance is quite clear," snapped Caroth.  "The deal is fulfilled.  Any further problems are your affair."

"Sir," interrupted Varth, "I hardly think.."

"You, Wing Commander, are not here to think," snapped Caroth, cutting Varth off.  "The subject is closed."  Caroth turned once again to Jess, missing Varth's hallmark stillness and the dangerous glitter in his eyes, well remembered by those who had crossed him, at least those who had survived.

"I want you and your ship off this base immediately" continued Caroth, glaring at Jess from behind his desk.

"I doubt the Commander-in-Chief and Chief-of-Staff see it your way, General. I made the deal with them, I'll discuss the details with them."

"I decide the details here now, Captain Storm.  I want you gone."

"Fine," replied Jess.  "I'll collect my crew and get going.  Time I stopped working for free anyway."  She turned to go.

"Captain Storm." Caroth sounded smug.  "I wouldn't call 250,000 working for free and your crew all joined the Alliance some time ago, or don't you remember?  You'll be taking that pile of junk out of here alone."

Jess turned back and put both hands on Caroth's desk.  He wasn't fazed by her obvious anger, but it felt good all the same.  "Listen, General, I don't give a bantha's breath what you decide.  If my crew want to come with me, they're coming with me even if I have to blast my way out of here.  It may have escaped your notice, but they all volunteered for this chicken outfit.  They can all unvolunteer too.  You might like to remember that next time you talk with Chan and Carplin."  With that parting shot, Jess stormed out of Caroth's office.

"Well, what are you waiting for, Commander?  Don't you have a starfighter wing to look after?"  Caroth waved his hand dismissively in Varth's direction while looking at the vid screen on his desk.  Varth couldn't believe the man's arrogance and left without saying a word to Caroth, not trusting himself to speak again without causing an incident he might later regret.

Caroth leaned back in his chair.  Vernan had been right, the woman was a loose cannon and a danger to everyone around her.  Alliance regulations regarding desertion were quite specific.  If her crew attempted to leave with her, he would have no hesitation in ordering her ship destroyed, especially if she initiated a fire fight on the way out of the docking bay.  He had seen such a lack of "esprit de corps" on the part of Corellian traders before.

If Storm was serious about her threat to blast her way out, Caroth would see to it that Wing Commander Varth was in the landing bay at the time.  Caroth's information on Storm included the fact of her relationship with Varth.  With Varth in the line of her turbo lasers, perhaps Storm would hold her fire.

If Storm's crew didn't leave with her he'd see to it that someone was ready to keep an eye on her, someone who would kill her if she became a more active threat to base security.  Caroth smiled.  He liked to be prepared for all possibilities.  In the war with the Empire, lack of preparation was fatal.

Outside of Caroth's newly established office Varth had finally succeeded in gaining control of his anger.  He remembered criticising Jess for her quick temper and here he was struggling with an urge to strangle an Alliance General.  He looked up and down the corridor, but saw no sign of Jess.  He decided her quarters would be his best chance of catching her.  There were some things he needed to tell her before she left Generis Base indefinitely.  He headed down the corridor trying to make sense of the situation.

## Farewells

Jess found her crew in the base lounge.  Roger was already well into the "refreshments".  Her explanations for her departure were short and sweet.  "I've been given my marching orders.  I'm out of here.  Are you coming or staying?"

Ash was dedicated to the Alliance, an organisation that Jess had frequently criticised.  He found her cavalier attitude to the Rebellion frustrating because he did care about her and hoped she would see sense.  Between the Empire and the Alliance there was no middle ground.  Also, his Jedi teacher and his lovers were all here on the base.  He said no.

Lou was somewhat taken aback at Jess's sudden departure but also said no.  He was only too aware of the possible outcome of leaving with Jess.  He was disappointed that Jess expected her crew to disregard their commitment to defeating the Empire and become deserters.  He hadn't thought her that selfish.

Roger had other friends among the base personnel he didn't want to leave.  He was looking forward to the challenge of life under General Caroth.  After all, where there's a will, there's a way.  He also said no.

They all wished Jess luck.

"Take care of yourself then," she said, expertly hiding her disappointment under a mask of bravado.  "You'll need to with that megalomaniac in charge.  If you change your mind, you know where my haunts are.  And hey, thanks."

Jess was not one for flowery speeches, even that one made her embarrassed.  She left the lounge quickly, hurt that not even one of her crew had chosen to come with her.  So much for the loyalty of her shipmates she thought bitterly as she strode towards her quarters.  Still, for all the good times they'd had together, none of them was a patch on Flis.  She had learned to manage without Flis; she could learn to manage without them.

Flis had been Jess' first navigator and her best friend.  Jess's pace slowed as she neared her quarters, Flis' face still clear in her mind.  She'd kept her promise to Flis.  "You promise me that if I die first, you'll rescue my body from my grieving lovers and send me out to wander space forever".  Jess had laughed so hard she'd nearly fallen off her bar stool when she first heard, but Flis had persisted.  Jess made the pact with Flis never thinking she would be the one to carry it out.  Well, she had dragged Flis' body from the tangled wreckage of an "accident" meant for both of them.  Yerkys ne Dago had a lot to answer for.

As Jess approached the door to her quarters, she concentrated on the task at hand.  Cleaning out her gear wouldn't take long.  Her mental list of the few things that were there was quite short.  Distracted, Jess palmed the lock and stepped into the room when the door slid open, not noticing the light from inside.  She preferred to sleep on the ship anyway or in...

"Jess."

The man could read minds, she thought, looking around to see Varth sitting on her bunk.

"You're really going then?" asked Varth softly.

"I don't think I've been given an option, Varth," said Jess, turning to rummage through the locker.

"You could join up" he said, then realised what he was saying.  Jess turned and stared at him in disbelief.

"Under that numbskull?  He represents everything I dislike about the Rebellion.  Forget it."  Jess looked at Varth in the subdued light and noticed an expression on his face she hadn't seen before.  Was it uncertainty?  Had he just asked her to stay?

"You could come with me," she said, hopefully.  Varth looked away from her, uncertainty becoming resignation.

"I'd like to Jess, but I can't.  You know that.  Our pilots are young and inexperienced and we can't afford to lose even one of them."  He looked back at Jess.  "It wouldn't have hurt you to humour him a little," he said.

Jess sighed and continued her packing.  When would he learn to accept her for what she was?  "Nothing I said would have changed his mind" she replied, shoving the last of her things into her kit bag.  "He'd already categorised and dismissed me.  I don't see why I should crawl to the likes of him."

Varth watched her seal the bag, knowing he couldn't say the words that might cause her to reconsider.  He stood and looked at her, trying to freeze her face in his mind.  "Well, take care of yourself, Jess."

Jess turned to go, then changed her mind and looked back at Varth.  "If this is goodbye, then fine, but let's be perfectly clear about the reasons.  This is nothing to do with Caroth or anyone else on this base.  It's to do with you and me.  You can't handle the commitment.  Half of you is buried with your wife and son and you won't let go.  You don't want to let yourself get involved again with anyone.  Especially not with someone like me." She kissed him, hard. "Goodbye Varth."

Jess picked up her kit and turned away.  She thought she heard a whispered "Jess" as she left, but she wasn't stopping to be sure.  The door slid closed behind her.

Varth sat back down on Jess's bunk and looked around at the empty room.  It was fitting somehow, just like he felt inside.  She was right.  It was revenge that had driven him to join the Alliance, the opportunity to avenge his wife and son and the thousand other innocents murdered on that space liner.  He should have been with them, but he wasn't and he had lost them both.  Now he had lost Jess as well.  He couldn't go back and he couldn't go forward.

He had accepted Jess's attentions while dwelling on her faults in order to keep her at arm's length.  He had said to himself that it wouldn't last, they were too different, her attitude toward the Alliance grated on him, her temper disappointed him, her stubbornness regarding her grandfather infuriated him and her behaviour in front of the most senior Alliance officials embarrassed him.  But she was brave, exciting and unpredictable.  He stared at the wall, breathing in the faint hint of her perfume that lingered in the room.

## Leaving Generis Base

_Red Storm Rising's_ last mission had been short and uneventful so there was little that had to be done to ready the ship.  Jess always kept the _Red Storm Rising_ in flight readiness anyway, just in case.  A detachment of SpecForce Marines was waiting for her.

"Wanted to give you a little send-off, Jess" said the Sergeant, grinning at her.  "We're actually here to make sure you don't steal the X-Wings on the way out, but you know how it is."  He was one of the ones she'd rescued from the Imperial base orbiting Mantooine and he reminded her of how many friends she would be leaving behind.  "By the way, there's a clean-up crew inside.  Just make sure some of them come out again, will you?"  He winked broadly. Jess grinned.

"You take care, you lot.  I know what your fighting's like" she replied.

"Yeah, not half as good as our drinking!" one wit muttered.

There was more good-natured ribbing on both sides before Jess took her leave and headed up the ramp.  Inside the freighter the clean-up crew were packing their scanning equipment back in the container in which it usually resided.

"You're clear, Captain" the technician said with a wink in Jess's direction.  "Didn't find anything unusual," the other technician added with a wide grin.  Jess stood aside as they clambered down the ramp with their ungainly container.  More people she'd miss.

"Captain?"

Jess was just closing the ramp and turned to look out at the little delegation that stood before her.  She'd expected the "clean-up crew" to be Ash, Lou and Roger, but there was no sign of them.  Instead, General Leith, Sari Blackwood and Nichelle stood before her, a small pile of bags at their feet.

"More escapees from the nut-house?" she asked, wryly.  She was disappointed.  She'd really expected at least one of her crew would have come with her.  So much for her magnetic personality!  General Leith spoke for the group.

"Let's just say we've been reassigned to another location.  I've been de-briefed and given new orders.  I don't think Caroth trusts me, so he's happy to let me go.  Sari's been like a three-legged gammon since she got here and..."

"And I'm not officially part of the Alliance," finished Nichelle.  That surprised Jess.

"But I thought your little Jedi reunion was going well."

"Frankly", admitted Nichelle, "Troy bores me to tears and I'd prefer a few more light years between Boddi and myself."

Jess laughed.  "My sentiments exactly!  Let's get this show on the road."

The three new crewmembers picked up their gear and waited while Jess lowered the ramp for them to come aboard.  Neither Leith nor Sari had been aboard the _Red Storm Rising_ before.  They had arrived at Generis Base aboard a stolen Imperial shuttle, piloted by Jess and carrying two Moffs taken prisoner during Leith's defection from the Imperial Army.

Leith looked around at the inside of the freighter, but thought better of commenting on the small, uncomfortably close quarters or the non-standard and unlicensed extras and alterations which would never have been approved of by the Corellian Engineering Corporation.  He suspected that Jess's ship carried a highly illegal collection of armaments, counter measures and defensive capabilities that were probably cobbled together in a manner guaranteed to ensure that one component or another would fail in a crisis.  All of this was in stark contrast to the highly ordered, cold and sterile Imperial Navy ships in which he had travelled in the past.

The contrast between Alliance and Imperial ships was further reinforced by the fact that his shipboard company consisted entirely of women, three very beautiful ones at that.  Women were a rarity in the Imperial armed forces, usually serving the Emperor as assassins or spies.

Leith followed Jess around from the ramp past what could only be a gun well and into the ship's lounge, thinking of how he had met Sari.  Even the supposedly morally and ethically superior Rebel Alliance used women as spies, on the tacit understanding that the more beautiful ones would seduce the required information out of their Imperial targets.  Well, she had seduced him, used him for information and abruptly disappeared along with the results of some highly secret biological warfare research, leaving him terribly compromised in the eyes of the Imperial Security Bureau, a deadly situation for him.

But he had suspected she was an Intel agent and had initially assumed she was feigning affection for him.  He had made sure she found out about the biological research, one of the many reasons for his disillusionment with the Empire.  He had covered his tracks by alerting ISB to Sari's activities, but not before he was sure she would have time to get away.  Leith had been able to use what he knew of her to contact the Alliance and arrange his defection.

That had been a year ago.  Then she had followed him aboard the Kauri Princess and tried to contact him, stumbling into his arrangement with the Alliance.  To complicate matters further, Jess and her crew had been told, incorrectly, that she was trying to prevent Leith's defection.  Jess had no alternative but to take him and Sari back to Generis Base and let the base's Intel officers sort out the mess.

Once they were all in the lounge, Jess quickly outlined the basic shipboard arrangements and left them to sort out which cabins they were going to use while they were on board.  Nichelle claimed one by the simple expediency of throwing her gear onto one of the bunks, and then went to join Jess in the cockpit.

Leith looked at Sari and decided that he really did want to mend their relationship.  Now was the appropriate time, as in these close quarters they would be spending a great deal of time together.  He picked up his and Sari's gear and carried both bags into one of the remaining passenger cabins.  Sari followed, radiating uncertainty.

Leith turned to her and took her in his arms.  "Sari, we're Rebel spies now.  Where we're going and what we have to do will be very dangerous.  We will need to be able to rely on each other, to trust each other."

## Fest and Beyond

## Omze's Incredible Travelling Starport

"So, that's what happened to the old Allanthia," muttered Leith darkly.  He leaned over Nichelle to get a better look out of the _Red Storm Rising's_ starboard cockpit window.  "Looks like I would have won my bet after all.  That shark Admiral Ozzel always said the Allanthia had been scrapped, but I knew she'd defected.  Looks like her crew didn't defect to the Alliance, though.  Look at that!  They've even fitted her with docking bays!"

Or had been made not to notice.

Standing quietly next to Nichelle and trying hard not to give offence, Sari concentrated on the green, snout-nosed Rodians.  The mercs cordially and coldly hated every one of the crew.  Their disgust at dealing with humans was barely concealed.  Their leader pocketed the docking fee, then walked away without a backward glance.  "Have a nice stay, humans," said the last Rodian slyly before he followed the others.

On the way back to the docking bay, Jess was distracted.  A good-looking man with obvious intentions had his arm about her waist.  Her intentions should have been the same as his.  Then why did she feel less enthusiastic the closer they got to his ship?  Wrestling with this dilemma, Jess missed the bounty hunters.

They were jumped from behind.  Jess heard the lead bounty hunter's instructions as potential witnesses ran from the companionway.  "Dead or alive but preferably dead.  They'll be easier to transport."

Jess and Ross tried to draw their blasters.  Ross was hit before he could fire a shot and fell to the deck.  Jess was fast enough to return fire and hit one of the bounty hunters.  She was drawing a bead on a second when something hit her in the back.  Her legs went out from under her and Jess collapsed to the deck.  She'd been hit by a nerve disruptor and couldn't move a muscle.

Jess cursed herself for her inattention.  She knew she had enemies who would pay for her disposal.  It was embarrassing to be caught so easily.  Still, despite the bounty hunter's words, they'd used a nerve disruptor.  They didn't really want her dead and that meant possible opportunities later.

Jess was roughly picked up by one of the bounty hunters and slung over its shoulder.  From this vantage point Jess could see Ross lying very still on the deck.  A deep blaster burn smoked from the centre of his ruined jacket.  Jess hoped Ross wasn't dead, but she'd seen wounds like that before.  A different pain racked her as she remembered his green eyes.  She'd never forgive herself if her inattention had resulted in his death.

The bounty hunters left Ross behind and began moving away with their captive when they were stopped by the largest, fiercest Twi'lek Jess had ever seen.  A tall, muscled fighting machine wielding a force pike who was deliberately blocking their way.

"Remember me, Ulkarg?"

"You want to die too, Hal'noan?"

The Twi'lek's reply was to wade in with the force pike, deadly and much more effective than a blaster in these close quarters.  Jess was dropped to the deck as the bounty hunters fought back.  Although greater in numbers, the companionway was too narrow to allow them all to attack the Twi'lek at once.  He was fast, savage and effective, giving the hunters no quarter.  This from a race of sly, willowy connivers who avoided direct conflict!

However, the Twi'lek's luck wasn't with him.  Although he had killed the bounty hunters, he too was wounded, fatally so.  He was lying on the deck in a pool of his own vital fluids, dying. Jess couldn't do a thing to help either of them.

Nichelle stepped into Jess' limited field of vision.  She passed by Jess and knelt beside the Twi'lek, who moaned at her touch.  She rolled him gently onto his back and placed her hands tenderly over his wounds, one by one.  The Twi'lek responded to her touch, recovering quickly.  When she finished he got up from the deck and stood before her, uninjured.

Nice trick, thought Jess.

"Goddess of the Bright Lands, I owe you my life.  My name is Hal'noan.  I was a slave gladiator.  I won my freedom in the fighting pits of Loovria with my fellow gladiator, Omze, who I am proud to call my friend.  Now I am his bodyguard and travel the space ways with him.  By saving my life you will have earned his gratitude, as well as mine."

Nichelle looked the Twi'lek over, a speculative expression on her face.  "Do the Twi'leks remember the Sellenni?"

"Sellenni?  They are a myth not spoken of outside of Twi'lek company.  We lived for their light but feared their dark.  Pleasure and pain."

Nichelle moved closer to Hal'noan, took his face in her hands and kissed him, long and deep.  He staggered and fell to his knees, held fast by her kiss.

Nichelle stopped kissing him, but still held his face in her hands.  "The Sellenni remember the Twi'leks.  I'm as real as you are."  She looked down into his eyes.  "Hal'noan."

He was astonished and touched his fingers to his lips.  "Yes, this Twi'lek remembers."  Hal'noan gently took Nichelle's hands from his face and held them.  Still on his knees before her, he bowed his head to her.

"Hal'noan, do you also remember the rituals?"

His head jerked up and he stared at Nichelle.  "Yes" he stammered.

"Good, I want ... your company".

"Me?  Why me?"

"Because you're not like other Twi'leks."

"How can you possibly know what I am?"

Nichelle smiled at Hal'noan, but this time her smile reminded Jess of Boddi.  It was that same enigmatic smile, the one that proclaimed a knowledge of secrets only guessed at.

Hal'noan stood, wrapped Nichelle in his arms and head tails, lifted her off the deck and kissed her like his life depended on it.  What seemed like minutes passed, and then he put her back down and released her.  He took a step away and bowed to her.  "As you wish."

"Yes, I wish."

Nichelle turned to look at Jess lying motionless on the deck.  "I know you can still see and hear me, Jess," she said, moving to kneel beside her.  "You're not injured but the nerve disruptor is blocking any voluntary movement.  Hal'noan, I need you to hold her down."

He stepped over Jess, stood with a leg on either side of her then knelt down, sitting on her thighs and effectively immobilising her legs.  He placed one forearm across the front of her shoulders, pinning her to the deck, and then wrapped her arms in his head tails.  He leant on his other arm so Jess wasn't taking all his considerable body weight.  This close to him Jess could detect a faint hint of the same alluring scent she'd noticed in the bar.  Memories of Varth again confused her.

"Gently" said Nichelle to Hal'noan.  "You're not subduing an opponent.  She'll kick and thrash but just hold her down and stop her from hurting herself.  If you annoy her she'll be a complete pain" Nichelle added, smiling down at Jess.

"Jess, you'll feel some muscle spasms after I negate the disruptor's effects, but they will wear off quickly.  Just relax and don't fight me."  She leaned a little closer to Jess.  "And whatever you do, don't fight him!"

Fighting wasn't what Jess had in mind.  Hal'noan's scent was driving her crazy.  "It's a natural body pheromone, just try and ignore it," said Nichelle quietly as Jess began to feel pins and needles all over her body.  That was followed by several violent involuntary muscle spasms, all effectively blocked by Hal'noan, his head tails giving him an unfair advantage.  Once the spasms had passed Hal'noan helped Jess up off the deck and steadied her until she had regained complete control of her limbs.

Nichelle was already tending to Ross.  Jess watched as Nichelle placed her hands over the burn on Ross' chest and closed her eyes.  A wave of calmness and peace settled over Jess, stilling the adrenalin rush of her recent fight.  She became aware of Hal'noan standing close to her in an attitude of quiet reverence, his head bowed.  The silence in the corridor was total.  Jess couldn't even hear the hum of the cruiser's energy generators.

The light in the companionway suddenly brightened.  Jess looked around but couldn't detect the source; the light seemed to come from everywhere.  She looked back and saw Ross breathing shallowly, cradled in Nichelle's arms.  Unmarked skin showed through the hole in the centre of his jacket.  Nichelle smiled at him and brushed her fingers against his lips.

"Sleep, rest and be at peace, Ross.  Remember your journey to the Light.  Remember that the Force is with you, always.  The Ashla awaits your return. Spend this time well, Ross.  You have a second chance, a chance given to very few.  Learn from what has gone before, and make what follows better.  Listen to the Force within you; let the Ashla be your guide.  Sleep now. Rest."

Nichelle glanced up at Jess as she laid Ross gently on the deck.  "Not too bad.  He'll make a quick recovery now but we can't leave him here.  Hal'noan, would you please pick him up and carry him.  We'll take him back to our ship until he recovers."

Ross appeared to be sound asleep.  Hal'noan scooped him up with ease and carried him over his shoulder.  He used his free hand to take the wicked looking force pike from Nichelle, who had picked it up off the deck.  "I'll leave the rest of the mess for Ne'Chak and his boys" he remarked as they set off towards the docking bay.

Jess turned to Hal'noan. "Why did you rescue us?"

"I wasn't rescuing you, I was trying to take out those bounty hunters.  I recognised them, they're ne Dago's scum.  Yerkys ne Dago was my master before I won my freedom.  He made millions of credits off me and I suffered for it.  He buys and sells his own kind with no remorse, no compassion, no honour."  The last was said with great bitterness.  "If I ever get my hands on his throat..."  The outcome remained unsaid, but something dark glittered in the gladiator's eyes.

They made their way through the crowds in the companionways.  None seemed to take any notice of the group, not even of the body slung over Hal'noan's shoulder.  At one junction several Rodian guards were watching their leader arguing with a trader.

"Carrying out the rubbish now, Hal'noan?" asked the leader.

"Haggling for bargains like a mama, Ne'Chak?" replied Hal'noan.

Ne'Chak glared at one of his crew who'd been short sighted enough to laugh at Hal'noan's reply.  Jess and Nichelle hurried past while Ne'Chak berated the hapless merc in native Rodian.  Jess didn't understand the language but she understood the tone.  She heard Nichelle laughing quietly behind her.

"What did he say?"

"Well, if Ne'Chak carries out his threat there won't be any more little Rodians for that one."

The _Red Storm Rising_ was locked up tighter than a drum.  Jess keyed the entry code into the side hatch's security lock.  The resulting alert tone should attract Leith and Sari's attention, if they were on board.  Sure enough, when the hatch opened Jess spotted Leith standing just inside.  He was pointing a heavy blaster in their direction.

"Jess, what happened?"

Jess ignored the question.  Once the ramp was completely lowered, she waved Hal'noan and Nichelle on board, took a precautionary look around the docking bay then ran up the ramp.  "Lock it up again, Leith.  We don't want company," she said, showing Hal'noan the way to the remaining spare cabin.  Here Hal'noan laid Ross down on one of the bunks.

"He'll be fine," said Nichelle from the cabin doorway.  "Just let him rest, he'll be up and about soon."

Hal'noan promised to see that they were contacted when their purchases were ready.  He warned them to otherwise stay on the ship out of the way, as there may be more of ne Dago's bounty hunters about.  Jess was happy to comply with Hal'noan's advice.

As Hal'noan left the ship, Nichelle informed Jess that she would be spending the remaining time with him.  As Jess watched them cross the docking bay arm in arm, Nichelle winked at Jess over her shoulder before turning back to Hal'noan.  Jess thought that she knew Nichelle well enough to know what the "rituals" were about and hoped for Hal'noan's sake that he was letter perfect.

Some time later Ross awoke and sought Jess out.  She was sitting in the pilot's seat with her feet up on the console thinking about recent events when he entered the cockpit.  "A credit for your thoughts?" he asked, looking appreciatively over the ship's controls.

Jess replied sombrely, "I don't think they're worth that much."

"Well, Jess.  I wanted to say thankyou for saving my life.  I owe you one," he said, sitting in the co-pilot's seat and turning to face Jess.  Jess was surprised at how unnatural he looked sitting opposite her.  Ross was the wrong height, the wrong colour.  In short, he wasn't Varth, or even Ash.

"I think you owe Nichelle one," replied Jess.  "It was me they wanted after all."  She shifted awkwardly in her seat and tried to explain to Ross that she didn't want to start anything with him.  This was something she'd never needed or wanted to say before now.

"It's nothing to do with what happened, it's just that ... oh Bantha's breath!  I don't even understand it myself.  How in the stars am I supposed to explain it to you."

Ross leaned forward in the co-pilot's seat and looked straight into Jess' eyes.  Jess was acutely aware of his proximity.  "Whoever he is Jess, he's a lucky man.  I hope he appreciates what he's got."

"I hope so too, but I doubt it" Jess muttered darkly.

"Don't doubt it, Jess.  You feel it and so does he, that's why it hurts.  Do a new friend one more favour and don't throw it away.  I've learned a few things recently."  Again he looked straight at her, his eyes an intense green.

"Time is precious and you never know when it will end, so tell the people you love how you feel about them.  You've got nothing to loose and everything to gain."

Ross stood up to leave.  "Tell Nichelle thankyou from me.  Tell her ... tell her I know we'll win.  Goodbye Jess, and may the Force be with you."  Ross left the cockpit and a stunned Jess.  Just what had Nichelle done to him and why hadn't she done it to Jess?  Some minutes later Ross waved to Jess from outside the viewport.  Jess saw him climb aboard the freighter docked next to hers.  Within minutes he and his ship were gone.

Some time later Leith entered the cockpit.  "A trader sent by Hal'noan has just delivered our purchases, Jess.  Everything seems to be in order.  This datapad is for you, one arms load out permit," he said, handing Jess the small datapad that had set her back 6,000 credits.

Nichelle returned with Hal'noan just as Sari was starting to worry about them.  She meet them at the bottom of the ship's entry ramp, but then wished she hadn't intruded on their final moments together.

"I'll be back soon, Hal'noan, on another ship with other faces.  Until then." Nichelle kissed Hal'noan like she was kissing him for the last time, then reluctantly walked past Sari and up the ramp.  Hal'noan pulled his cloak over his head and turned and walked away.

## The Jump to Draenell's Point

Seated next to a subdued and introspective Jess, Nichelle set course for Draenell's Point.  Jess was full of questions about recent events that she wasn't sure she wanted answered.  She contented herself with asking Nichelle just one question.

"Is there anything I need to know about what went on back there?"

"What would you like to know?"

Jess answered levelly, without emotion.  "I'd like to know what you did to Ross.  And what was so important on Omze's that it cost me a small planet in licence fees and put us in danger.  And why I should trust your judgement from now on.  Other than that, there are lots of things I'm curious about, but not enough that I want to involve myself in them too much."  Jess didn't like being used and knowing too much might commit her to whatever the Jedi were up to.  And she didn't think she wanted to know that much.

Nichelle took time to consider her reply.  Jess' cynical spacer ways blinded her to the Light within herself, the same Light that was within all beings.  She would probably dismiss the truth or at least fail to see that it was as applicable to her as it had been to Ross.  However, if Jess chose to listen, to really listen, then perhaps she would begin to question her assumptions about herself, about the galaxy she lived in and about other beings.  If Jess began to consider other possibilities she could move forward from her current painful situation.

"Jess, Palpatine threatens to extinguish the Light in all the systems under his domination.  As he tightens his grip on those systems, millions of beings will suffer, lose hope and fall to the Dark Side.  You cannot imagine the horror of the Dark Side, of the Bogan.  To have even one being fall to that chaos of hate, fear, pain and hopelessness is a tragedy, a loss so profound that it defies description."

"Palpatine believes that he uses the Dark Side to make himself powerful, that the terror he inflicts on other beings makes him stronger.  The reality is that the Dark Side, the Bogan, has Palpatine completely under it's control and is using him to spread terror throughout the galaxy, terror on which it will grow strong.  When the Bogan is strong enough, it will attempt to consume the Light, the Ashla.  During the struggle every living thing in the galaxy will either join the Light or fall to the Dark."

"Eons ago the Jedi Bendu, the Servants of the Ashla, overthrew the Dark Lords of the Sith.  The defeated Lords and their minions fled this Galaxy.  Without the Bogan's predators, the Galactic Republic throve and grew.  Peace endured for centuries and we withdrew from the stage.  Now the pendulum swings back the other way.  The Galactic Republic has rotted from within, Palpatine gains more power with every fallen system and out past the Galactic Rim the Bogan marshals its forces again."

"Boddi is gathering the Jedi Bendu for another stand against the Dark.  Each of the Circle members are gathering what resources we have and creating other resources for the coming fight.  Hal'noan is one of my resources, a very important one.  Boddi not only engineered him, but also a recent conversation between myself and Bursurra which alerted me to his existence and whereabouts."

"I needed to see Hal'noan, to see for myself whether he really was a true Twi'lek.  He is, therefore I am oath bound to protect him.  There are no other Sellani left to fulfil that oath.  I didn't tell you what I was up to because you don't want to know and you don't want to be involved.  As for the danger, I know you like the excitement, the rush that it brings, otherwise you wouldn't be a smuggler, Jess, you'd be a bureaucrat.  Its danger that makes you feel alive."

Nichelle rummaged in her jacket pocket, then brought forth the most brilliant red gem Jess had ever seen.  It's savage fire reflected in Nichelle's dark eyes.  "This should more than cover your additional expenses," said Nichelle, placing the dazzling firestone on the console next to Jess.  Jess left it there.  Later she'd put it in a safe place in her cabin, but she had no intention of selling it.

"Now I will tell you what happened to Ross.  He was shot in the chest with a heavy blaster at point blank range and died on the floor of the companionway.  I healed Ross' body and asked the Ashla for permission to summon him back from the Light.  Up until now, the only Jedi Bendu, the only Servant of the Ashla to ever have been granted permission to summon a being back from the Light is Karan.  He is the truest Light of our Circle.  The Ashla has never refused him."

"Now, on this one occasion, I too have been granted permission to summon a being back from the Light.  I don't know what's in store for Ross; I don't have Boddi's foresight.  I do know that his journey to the Ashla gave him strength and peace.  He knows that the Ashla is strong, far stronger than the Bogan will ever be, and that ultimately the Ashla, the Light, will triumph.  He has regained hope.  With hope he can inspire others.  This hope spreads and behind it will follow courage, then action.  Palpatine and his minions will be defeated and the Bogan will crawl back into the small, Dark place where it dwells."

"You might wonder why I asked permission in the first place.  If the Ashla allows a being to return, that being is always strong in the Light.  Their contact with the Ashla changes them, they have hope and they are unafraid to walk the harder paths.  The galaxy is a better place for their return.  I know that Ross will aid our fight."

Nichelle's expression softened.

"Jess, you've made it quite clear that you don't want to know what we're up to and you don't want to be involved.  Why did you agree to take us on board in the first place?  You know Sari's training to be a Jedi and you know I'm a member of the Circle of the Ashla."

"One of the Jedi's basic tenets is that each being is free to exercise their own will.  All I can do is engineer situations that will guide you in making choices and in exercising your free will.  My fervent hope is that you choose the Light and not the Dark.  There is no in between, Jess.  You cannot remain neutral in this battle between the Bogan and the Ashla, between the Empire and the Alliance."

"If you feel used, its because I need your help and as you don't want to know what's really going on, you leave me little option.  Quite frankly Jess, there are much bigger things at stake here than your sensibilities.  However, if you still don't trust me and still don't want to help us, then its time we parted.  I'll get us to Draenell's Point, then you'll need to find a new navigator."

Jess hadn't said a word during Nichelle's explanations.  She sat, stunned, in silence while Nichelle turned her attention back to the nav computer and completed programming their next jump.  "There you are, Jess.  You can take it from here.  I have other things to do before we arrive on Draenell's Point".  She rose from the navigator's seat.  "I'll be in the lounge with Sari if you need me" she said, and left the cockpit.

Jess stared after her.  How could Jedi be so powerful and yet so obtuse?  It was a sobering thought and made her even more determined not to get involved in their dealings.  Helping the Alliance was one thing, taking on the … what did Nichelle call it? … the Bogan, was something else entirely.  Let them do that, she would potter around at the human level where it was far safer.  All she had wanted was for Nichelle to inform her she wanted to go to Ozme's for personal reasons, rather than pretending it was to help Jess.  It would have been the honest thing to do.  She hadn't needed a blow-by-blow explanation.

But to have Nichelle feel that lack of enthusiasm to take on the Dark directly was an expression of non-alignment stunned her.  What did Nichelle and the others expect?  She wasn't a Jedi!  Did no one at all trust her motives?  Did Varth?  Did words mean so much that actions were unimportant?  Was that why he had let her go so easily? Jess felt suddenly more alone in the universe than she ever had before.  She desperately wanted to talk to her father.  At least he understood.

Sari had questions she wanted answered.  She waited until they were safely in hyperspace on their way to Draenell's Point and Nichelle had returned to the lounge.

"Did you know Hal'noan?  Is he why you wanted to go to Omze's?"

"I knew of Hal'noan from Bursurra on Generis Base, but I hadn't met him.  Did you know that Bursurra was also a slave gladiator?  That's how he lost his eye."  Sari didn't but, then again, she hadn't spent much time with the Wookie.

"In the past the Twi'leks and the Sellani were very close.  We gave them knowledge and they gave us companionship.  They inspired us with their passion and we took them to the stars.  We learned from each other and brought each other joy.

In the eons since that time, the Dark Sellani worked their pain on both races and both races suffered.  The Sellani passed into myth and the Twi'lek became slaves, adapting to survive.  We weren't there to protect them.  
Hal'noan is what every Twi'lek could be again, strong, honourable, loyal, sensual and passionate.  I believe he's the outcome of some interesting genetic engineering.  I suspect Boddi's been tinkering in an attempt to bring back some true Twi'leks.  She's the only one with the skills to do what's required and she's been here the whole time.

We went to Omze's so I could find Hal'noan.  I will be returning to Omze's later to bring him back to Generis Base.  I will fulfil my obligations as his protector, and perhaps we will bring each other joy."

Nichelle smiled at Sari.  "At the very least, our nights will be warmer."

Sari was pleased Nichelle was happy.  She liked people to be happy.

Several days passed as the _Red Storm Rising_ traversed Nichelle's hyperspace route to the Trax Sector, then on to the Bissillirus System jump in point.  Late in their journey Leith came across Jess in the lounge.  She was seated at the small table with a bottle of Corellian brandy.  Leith considered it was time to try and learn more of Jess' motivations, so sat down across from her, uninvited.

Jess had been uncharacteristically quiet and withdrawn during this leg of their journey.  Leith suspected she was suffering through a personal crisis and had begun to question her life, her comfortable assumptions about the galaxy around her and her place in it.  He recognised the struggle, he'd been through it recently himself and was still trying to find a way forward, to find a place for himself in the Alliance so he could begin accomplishing the new goals he had set himself.

"Jess, I've had time to review the information we obtained on Omze's.  It's more recent than that on which my original mission was based.  Sari and I will have to rethink our approach and what we are going to Bissillirus to accomplish.  I don't think our original cover story will be appropriate, or even safe, given some of the recent changes that have occurred there."  Leith leaned over the table toward Jess.  She was struck by the intensity of his gaze; something was going on here.

"Last time we spoke about this Jess, you mentioned a more appropriate replacement.  Who you were thinking of and how capable are they?"

"I've been trying to contact Deak Freehold and Tor Skylow.  Tor you might know.  She used to be Imperial. They're good and they are good undercover workers.  That's what they do."

"Have you been able to contact them?  Are they meeting us on Draenell's Point?"

"I've sent them a message.  I don't know if they'll get it in time.  I don't know what they are doing now and if they'll consider it more important that what we're doing."  She shrugged.  "Frankly, it's a long shot.  Whatever the outcome, you still don't have to play by Caroth's rules.  Nosing around before you jump in is perfectly sensible.  I can just trade and you can be casual tourists until you get a feel for the situation.  When are you going to tell me what you found out at Ozme's that has you worried?"

Leith paused to consider what Jess had told him.  When he did speak it wasn't to answer her question, but to ask another of his own.  "OK.  Now, why are you helping the Alliance?"

Jess answered facetiously, "why not?  Do you think there's much of a choice?  No one who finds out my family background is going to believe I'd keep my nose clean.  A father who is part of the Rebellion, a mother who is the daughter of a traitor to the Empire, a grandfather not good enough to be Bantha fodder who is a Minister in the Rebellion hierarchy.  No one would believe I wasn't up to my eyeballs in it."

Leith took a moment to refill Jess' glass.  "So, you're doing this because it runs in the family?"

Jess eyed Leith curiously.  She replied, quietly.  "I started working for the Rebellion long before I found out most of my family history."  She wondered briefly why she was telling him personal details she'd told no one else, even Varth.  Maybe it was a sudden desire for just one person to understand her.  She continued more matter-of-factly.  "One of the major problems with the Empire is that its leaders don't understand people.  They don't understand that people are very complex and their motivations are seldom simple.  One of the major problems with the Rebellion is that most of its leaders are taken from Imperial turncoats and they bring that lack of understanding with them."

"It's like this.  I have a choice between allied to the Empire, allied to the Alliance or neutral.  It's becoming harder and harder to be neutral without getting stomped on.  If you are allied to the Empire, sooner or later you have to do some pretty despicable things.  If you are allied to the Alliance, you're on the losing side."

"Why are you assisting the Alliance if you believe they'll lose?"

"I don't think from what I've seen that the Rebellion has it in them to win.  But perhaps enough people like me can inject some sanity in the proceedings and make the loss less devastating.  And at least I'll be able to sleep at night.  Smugglers have a short lifespan anyway.  I might as well be on the right side when I get blown out of the skies.  I'll just be pretty annoyed if someone like Caroth gets to do the blowing."

"Why haven't you joined up?"

"Then he and all his ilk would have the right to order me around and I couldn't stand it.  I don't have a death wish and I refuse to take stupid risks with either people, other valuable property or myself.  Did you know I was expected to use this ship for your rescue and just dump it?  Do you know how valuable this ship is?  How long it took to get the armaments it has?  And they were prepared to sacrifice it on a single, low priority mission!  That's insanity!  Too many Imperial officers too used to unlimited resources."

"But if I was actually part of the Rebellion, I'd have had to say yes, sir, right away, sir, and any refusal would have landed me in the brig.  I couldn't argue them out of suicide missions or waste of resources.  I'd be in there dying with the rest of them.  Forget it."

"Why compound the problem with obvious antagonism then?  Just refusing to use the word 'Alliance' is a slap in the face."

"I want the parameters clearly defined.  I am not part of the Rebellion.  I am an independent.  Treat me with some respect.  I am not a lackey.  Get used to it.  And anyone with an ounce of brains will look at my actions, not my words and know that I am as much a part of the Alliance as if I'd signed my life away.  Do you think Carplin or Kryll were fooled by my words?  Only idiots like Caroth will take me at face value and they're the ones I'm protecting myself against."  Jess was protecting herself against them, Nichelle and her crew and probably Varth too.  The wounds felt very raw.

"But why make it difficult for yourself?"

"Because I want to keep some integrity.  Do you think it will really make any difference to me who wins?  Most of what I do will still be illegal under an Alliance government.  They're not suddenly going to revoke the laws against smuggling.  They'll need the taxes even more.  And they can't make it legal for those that helped them and illegal for those who didn't.  I won't pretend I'm going to change.  Why should I?  This is my life.  I have no interest in being a commander.  Why should I pretend I do?"

"So you don't believe the Alliance will win and, if they do, you don't believe you'll be rewarded.  Why bother?"

"You're a general.  Haven't you had any kind of feeling for the men under you?  Didn't you feel any kind of guilt for what situations you sent them into?"

"What do you know about that sort of guilt?"

"One of the first things the Base asked me to do when I got there was to transport troops to the surface, young, fresh-faced, idealistic troops, and half of them didn't come back.  I wasn't responsible for sending them, but I still swore if I could stop more dying, I would."

Suddenly embarrassed by the baring of her soul, Jess stood up abruptly, picked up her bottle and walked off to her cabin.

Well, that's the end of that conversation, thought Leith.  He sat for a moment; beginning to understand why some beings found Jess Storm so damned annoying.  Then he went to his own cabin to consider what she'd told him.  Sari was with Nichelle and he wasn't expecting her for some time, so he stretched out on the lower bunk, clasped his hands behind his head and closed his eyes.

That was the first he'd heard about Jess' family's involvement in the Alliance.  The heat in her comment about her grandfather had been very revealing.  He'd obviously hit a nerve.  Just for an instant he'd caught a hurt little girl in Jess' face.  So, under all her cynicism and bravado, she did have some real feelings.

Leith was quite sure that Alliance Intelligence knew about Jess' grandfather.  Experience had taught him that Intel was exceptionally good and extremely motivated, both in gathering information and in withholding it from the enemy.  They most certainly knew that Jess was the granddaughter of an Alliance civil government Minister and that her father was also involved in the Alliance.

Known relatives of highly placed Alliance personnel were the first targets of ISB kidnappers.  Knowing that your loved ones were at the mercy of Imperial torturers made you far less effective for the Alliance.  In many cases it had opened the way for ISB to control senior Alliance personnel and initiate the release of considerably damaging information.

To combat this threat, Intel would have learnt all about Jess and her family, including where they were and what they were up to.  Intel would have done all they could to ensure Jess and her family's safety.  The fact that neither Jess nor her parents had fallen into ISB hands was a testament to Intel's efforts, efforts of which Jess was quite probably completely unaware.

There were only two options for keeping the families of Alliance personnel safe from Imperial retribution; Alliance safeworlds and well fortified and defended Alliance bases.  Both of these options suffered the drawback of discovery and subsequent attack by Imperial forces, but they were the best options available to the Rebels.  Generis was the finest example of an Alliance base Leith had ever seen and, up until recently, Jess had been allowed to use Generis Base as her retreat.  She would never have been permitted on the base without Intel approval and support, let alone been allowed to remain there for over a year.

Intel had protected Jess by allowing her access to Generis Base.  Why?  Because they knew the effect news of Jess' capture by ISB would have had on the Minister.  In spite of what Jess thought about her grandfather, Intel must have believed that the effect would have been catastrophic.  They had allowed Jess free reign on Generis Base even though she was a self proclaimed unaligned neutral, one whose attitude to the Alliance had convinced some that she was a security risk.

Having witnessed the effect that Jess' attitude had on more committed base personnel, Leith suspected that the base's Intel officers were reporting her every move to their Secom superiors.  He knew from his own debriefing that they were watching Jess very closely.  However, any requests to remove her from Generis Base must have been overridden by the Intel officers assigned to Secom, and by other more positive reports from Generis of her contribution to the Alliance.

Secom's Intel officers would have been aware of her relationship to the Minister and the subsequent need to protect her.  Positive reports from Generis Base would have included her assistance in Leith's defection as well as the numerous times she and her crew had risked their ship and their lives in pursuit of Alliance objectives.  Leith had learned from Kryll that the senior Generis Base personnel had no doubts about her loyalty.  Jess and her crew had participated in several very dangerous missions and many of the base personnel owed the crew of the _Red Storm Rising_ their lives, Wing Commander Varth included.

Leith chuckled to himself.  Base gossip had it that once Varth had sufficiently recovered from his injuries, Jess had exacted payment from him in a manner that had left him falling asleep over his breakfast in the mess.  All training flights were off that day and he was even too tired to give his pilots their usual haranguing.

Leith continued to think Jess' situation over.  Things had gone along for over a year without any change in the status quo. Jess had continued to come and go from Generis Base as she pleased.  Sometimes she assisted the Alliance, sometimes she traded, and sometimes she did both.  Her behaviour was no better and no worse than hundreds of other "honest traders" that the Alliance did business with, either knowingly or unknowingly on the part of the trader.

Then Caroth had arrived and thrown Jess off the base.  Why?  Leith considered Caroth.  Secom had assigned Caroth to Generis Base to command the expanded Atrivis Sector forces.  Secom's Intel officers would have briefed Caroth on the Generis Base situation before he left.  That briefing had prompted Caroth to order Jess off the base.  Why had Secom's Intel officers changed their minds about Jess after all this time?

As far as Leith could see nothing had changed substantially on Generis Base.  In fact, the _Red Storm Rising_ had recently fully and wholeheartedly participated in a very dangerous mission.  The senior base personnel had no real reason to question Jess' loyalty.  The change in Intel's intentions towards Jess wouldn't have been prompted by recent reports from Generis Base.  The content of those reports would have been the same as it always had been.  Something bad outweighed by something good.  The change in Intel's intentions must have come from the Secom Intel officers who had briefed Caroth.

Leith thought about that some more, and then he remembered something his instincts told him was important.  When Caroth had given Leith Secom's orders regarding Bissillirus System, he had informed Leith that the information on which those orders were based had come direct from General Vernan.  Caroth had smugly told Leith that he had spoken with Vernan personally.

Leith had thought at the time that Caroth was big noting himself.  Well, Caroth's tendency to gloat had given Leith a clue as to what was really going on.  Secom's Intel officers hadn't briefed Caroth; the Head of Alliance Intelligence had briefed him!

Why had Vernan briefed Caroth in person?  Vernan certainly didn't need to come all the way to Generis Base to give briefings.  Generis was one of the Alliance's vitally important Outer Rim Communications Centres.  Kryll was in charge of long-range hyperspace transmission capabilities that used encrypted signals that were nearly impossible to break.  Vernan could have transmitted the information over the hyperspace channel and left Caroth's briefing to Secom's Intel officers.

Now, perhaps Vernan had been at Secom for some unrelated reason and had simply taken advantage of an opportunity.  That still didn't make sense.  Vernan should have left Caroth's briefing to his subordinates.  In conducting Caroth's briefing personally, Vernan would simply have undermined the confidence of his subordinates for no appreciable gain.

Leith opened his eyes and stared at the underneath of the top bunk.  He had nearly enough pieces to come to a supportable conclusion; he just had to arrange them in the right order.  He tried putting himself in Vernan's shoes.  When had he ever stepped outside the chain of command to pass information on to someone else?  When he didn't want his chain of command to know what he was doing!

Leith sat up abruptly, nearly hitting his head on the top bunk.  Vernan didn't want his subordinates to know what he had told Caroth!

Beginning to smell a womprat, Leith swung his legs over the edge of the bunk, stood up and began pacing the short distance between the cabin door and the bulkhead wall.

There appeared to be a significant difference between Intel's standing policy regarding Jess, a policy which had been in operation for over a year, and Vernan's briefing of Caroth.  Why would the head of Alliance Intelligence step outside his chain of command to give Caroth information that had prompted him to act contrary to standing Secom policy and, given Jess' capability and commitment, contrary to the interests of the Alliance?

Added to this, Vernan's information regarding Bissillirus and Draenell's Point was dangerously out of date when compared with the more recent information Leith had obtained from Omze's.  Leith's new information led him to believe that his and Sari's cover, also provided by Vernan, would have put them in an exceedingly dangerous position.  Why had Vernan provided uncharacteristically and dangerously out of date information outside his chain of command?

The womprat was growing!

Vernan had chosen to go around his chain of command because, as far as Leith could see, he was not acting in accordance with Intel policy regarding Jess and he was passing on information that was likely to get two Intel agents killed.  If Vernan had gone through his subordinates, they may have questioned his intentions towards Jess and checked his information on Bissillirus.  They may have found him out.

Intel would be keeping tabs on all members the Alliance High Command.  The risk of ISB infiltration at that level was far too high for Intel not to watch them closely.  Vernan would be a prime ISB target.  The damage a compromised Alliance High Commander and Head of Intelligence could do would probably cripple the Rebellion.  The Alliance would take a long time to recover, if they recovered at all.

Leith sat down again on the bunk and folded his arms.  Now, was Caroth also a problem?

Caroth followed procedures.  Alliance procedures stated that potential threats to security were to be arrested, locked up and questioned by Intel.  Why hadn't Caroth followed procedures?  After all, he was someone who did everything by the book.

Because Caroth was following the procedure that said when an Alliance High Commander gives you a direct order, you jump.  Vernan had told Caroth to order Jess off the base.  Why?  When Jess was away from the base she was a much easier target for ISB spies, assassins and kidnappers.  Caroth, acting on Vernan's orders, had thrown Jess out into a very dangerous galaxy.

This galaxy probably knew, courtesy of Vernan, that she was the granddaughter of an Alliance civil government Minister.  The best bounty hunters and ISB spies in the galaxy would be on Jess' trail.  Her run in with the bounty hunters on Omze's confirmed Leith's suspicions.  Vernan had access to the Alliance's encrypted hyperspace transmission capabilities, so the news about Jess had spread far and fast.  He had probably included recent holos of Jess just to make sure.

Vernan wasn't Jess' only problem.  Caroth, again acting entirely in accordance with Alliance procedures, would probably "assign a capable individual to follow and watch the suspected security risk and liquidate that risk if necessary".  Caroth was highly suspicious of Jess, thanks to Vernan, and would assign someone to keep an eye on her.  Caroth's agent, whoever that was, wouldn't leave finding and following Jess to chance.  So, how do you know where someone's going when they don't even know where they're going?  You plant a transmitter on their ship!

Leith had every intention of combing the ship inside and out for possible transmitters.  However, there was a dedicated Intel agent somewhere out there with instructions to follow and, if necessary, kill Jess.  It was highly likely that Leith and Jess would wind up shooting at one of their own.  Sari would have to help him out with that one, if she could.

OK.  Jess was in serious trouble.  But why had Vernan taken a personal interest in the Bissillirus mission?  Jess' trouble was personal, but Leith new better than to assume his was too.  Leith and Sari were simply on hand when Caroth arrived on Generis Base.  There hadn't been anyone else available to assign to the job.  Leith knew that Caroth didn't trust him and he knew why.  Leith was, as Jess had so bluntly put it, an "Imperial turncoat".

Leith didn't believe that Vernan was after him personally.  Vernan was after whoever undertook the Bissillirus mission.  Whoever used that cover would be in a very dangerous situation.  Now, why did Vernan want the Bissillirus mission to fail?  The Alliance depended heavily on food and personnel from Trax Sector.  Both the food and the personnel were routed through Bissillirus, specifically Draenell's Point.  If the Alliance failed to protect that route then, as he'd told Sari earlier, they'd starve.

Leith became aware of a dull, throbbing pain in his head.  He was getting to old for this!  Something had to be done about that transmitter.  Even though Jess had swept the _Red Storm Rising_ for bugs just after they had left Generis Base, his instincts told him there was still something to find.  He left the cabin and went in search of Jess.

His knock went unanswered.  He wondered how much of the bottle she'd finished off and how long before she'd be able to comprehend what he had to tell her.  "Jess, we've got a problem!"

The door was opened remarkably quickly and she looked far more alert than he'd expected.  He noticed the bottle on her desk looked no emptier than the last time he'd seen it.  "I think we have a transmitter on board.  Any ideas where it might be hidden?"

Jess went back into her cabin and took out the scanning equipment.  "What makes you think there's one on board?"

"Caroth's following procedures.  He would have assigned someone to follow you and as you didn't know where you were going, that someone would have planted a transmitter on board before we left Generis Base."

Jess stopped and turned to face Leith.  "You think Caroth sent the bounty hunters after me!  He really does consider me a security risk?  He really thinks I'd put my friends in danger?"  Jess was stunned.  "He probably thinks I really would have blasted out of the base if I'd had to.  The man's an imbecile!"

"No, Jess.  Caroth has sent one or two Alliance personnel to keep an eye on you.  It's General Vernan who told the bounty hunters about you.  He's the real problem here."

"Who's General Vernan and why would he want me dead?  Are you sure you're not reading more into this than ought to be?  You do realise that na Dago probably has bounty hunters after me.  I assumed it was his men that were after me on Ozme's.  In fact, Hal'noan confirmed they were na Dago's men."

"General Vernan is one of the Alliance High Command and the Head of Intel.  I think he engineered Caroth throwing you off the base so you would be easier pickings for any ISB spies and assassins or bounty hunters of any alignment.  Who's ne Dago?"

"The nastiest, slimiest crime boss in any galaxy.  My family fell foul of him a while back.  I blew up his home, so I'm not the most popular person on his guest list."

"Don't assume that Vernan and ne Dago aren't connected, Jess.  If I were in Vernan's place I would make my move against you look like a strike from a pre-existing enemy.  That way suspicion would be diverted away from me.  Vernan would simply have made sure that ne Dago knew you were out and about.  That's all he would have to do if ne Dago was already after you."

"Why would Intel have it in for me?  Didn't they like my cloaking device?"

"Cloaking device?"

"I gave them one in exchange for a haven on Generis Base and to get na Dago off my back.  Maybe it blew up on them, but that wouldn't be my fault."

"OK, well that goes some way to explaining why you were allowed on Generis Base in the first place.  Then you were allowed to stay there for over a year, so I don't think Intel has it in for you.  Vernan briefed Caroth personally before Caroth took command of Generis Base, then you were out on your … ear.  Vernan changed the rules regarding you and now you're out in a very dangerous galaxy.  One which knows who your Grandfather is, thanks to Vernan, and one which will definitely try and take advantage of that situation."

"How can you be sure of this?  I doubt very much if Toiyabe has told anyone of the relationship and I certainly haven't broadcast it.  He made it quite clear he was ashamed of my father and I.  Until a few months ago, neither my mother, father or I knew that Elkior Toiyabe was still alive.  He staged his death, leaving my mother to fend for herself.  I found out he was alive when he addressed a victory rally I was present at.  I've been told he'd decided I was worth getting to know after all.  Probably decided I'd become useful."

Leith looked at Jess closely.  If he could just get her be objective, they might survive long enough for him to come up with a suitable plan of attack.  "Intel would know this, it's their business and one they are very good at.  Trust me on this, Jess.  I've lost enough men to Intel's plots and schemes in the past.  If Intel knows, then so does Vernan.  If, as I suspect, Vernan wants to serve you up to the Imperials for them to use you to get at Toiyabe, then the very best ISB spies will be after you."

"Frankly, I doubt Toiyabe would be terribly concerned if any of us were captured.  He certainly made no efforts to rescue my father when na Dago held him.  I think his only worry would be that his lack of concern would look bad.  If he had really cared, he would have stayed dead.  Alive and trying to make contact, he's simply put us in danger.  My mother has already been a target of kidnapping.  She was still angry with her grandfather and she had no reason to believe he had altruistic reasons for his actions.  If he had, he would have moved more quickly - about 30 years more quickly.  "Then again, the Imperials won't know he doesn't give a damn about us."

"That's right, Jess.  You've said Toiyabe wouldn't be terribly concerned if any of your family was captured, but your mother has been kidnapped before.  That was probably ISB trying to get at Toiyabe.  You are just as much a target as she is."

Jess abruptly changed the subject.  "I have an idea about your transmitter, Leith.  It's probably in the cargo area.  I didn't bother sweeping it because there's no room to hold interesting conversations there and there would be no point in Roger planting a bug there."

Jess had only searched half the cargo bay when the scanner indicated the presence of a high tech item under the floor.  Leith helped Jess remove some of the panels and they found the transmitter partially hidden behind a floor brace.

"Let's not remove it just yet," warned Leith.  "It may change transmission frequencies and alert them that we've found it.  They've probably plotted our course from our last jump point and picked the most likely destinations and arrival times.  If Caroth knew I was going to Trax Sector, to Draenell's Point, then they'll be waiting for us.  This transmitter may simply have been to make sure we were coming.  If we move or destroy it, they'll know that we've found it and will change their plans."

"I wonder what they made of Nichelle's jump."

"What about Nichelle's jump?"

"It's way ahead of current technology.  We made it in a fraction of the normal time.  One of the advantages of having a Jedi on tap."

Leith wondered what other "advantages" Jess had been making use of and whether he could persuade Nichelle to assist with the current problems.  "Then they may know our destination, but they won't know our arrival time.  If we're that far ahead of schedule, we may still have an advantage.  Besides, the Alliance personnel Caroth assigned to follow us aren't the real problem, Vernan is."

"So, let's assume you're right, Leith.  It's possible Intel just had orders, not the reasons why.  That means that someone has got to Vernan.  Far more important than securing the Bissillirus System is checking out the possibility that Vernan has turned, and plugging that hole."  Jess rubbed the knot that was beginning to form on her brow.  "This is really not my field, you know."  She sighed.  "Nichelle sent a message to Deak Freehold.  Deak knows about Alliance politics, he might have some idea about what hold ISB might have over Vernan, if indeed they do."

"Failing that, there must be someone we can talk to."  She ran through the slim list of Alliance personnel she knew that might conceivably know what to do next.  Maybe Han Solo.  The Corellian smuggler seemed to be on good terms with some of the higher-ups; that not-actually-dead Princess for one.  "So what we really need to do is a) find someone to install as chief supplier on Bissillirus and b) go spy-hunting.  All the while avoiding wandering bounty hunters.  Great!"

"Look, I think it's time to go and find my father.  He's into the Rebellion supply stuff.  He'd probably eat this assignment for breakfast.  Besides, if there is movement against the family, he needs to know about it.  And he'd probably know where to start with this Vernan thing.  I don't."

"OK Jess, but let's start with Deak Freehold.  We have some time and if he's meeting us on Draenell's Point, then we can brief him on Vernan and Bissillirus and see what he comes up with.  Then we can contact your father.  Meanwhile, you can jettison this transmitter.  I don't want to lose the advantage Nichelle has given us.  Caroth's people will just have to find some other way of keeping tabs on us."

"By the way, the information that I got from Omze's - there's a new Imperial supply base orbiting Draenell's Point, the one I was supposed to destroy before they finished it.  Its fully operational and the Imperials have cracked down on all independent supply operations in the system.  Our Alliance front companies on Draenell's Point were wound up with the rest.  I wouldn't be at all surprised if the Imperials netted the Alliance personnel along with the other suppliers.  Our cover would have brought us the undivided attention of the largest Imperial presence in Trax Sector.  That's one I owe Vernan."

"Then the Alliance still won't be able to use Bissillirus System as a supply source, even if we do go there.  We still need to get supplies from elsewhere.  So tell me again, why are we going to Trax?"

"We have, depending on Nichelle's navigation ability, several days up our sleeve before anyone will be expecting us.  That's if they don't notice that we've jettisoned the transmitter.  We can use some other cover to find out what we can first hand.  That may give us enough information to change our approach to the problem."

"The fundamental point is that the Alliance can't afford to lose Bissillirus System as a source of supply.  If your friends are already on their way there and they really are as good as you say, then our chances of succeeding will be improved considerably.  I hope I don't need to remind you that one farm boy took out the Death Star.  I saw the crew of the _Dark Heart_ once on Generis Base and I know what you're capable of when you set your mind to it.  I also believe that putting the pieces together this early on is a good sign.  We could have just flown straight into a trap, but know we know enough about what's going on to change our plans."

"Jess, we're going to Trax Sector because we've still got a job to do."

"Then let's check with Nichelle first of all whether or not Deak and Troy got the message and are on their way.  If not, let's meet somewhere else.  Have you told Nichelle and Sari about all of this yet?"

"No, not yet, but now is probably as good a time as any.  You know Nichelle better than I do Jess, is it reasonable to expect some assistance from her?"

"Hell, if she won't do it for me, she might do it for Sari.  We can only ask.  If it fits in with her plans, we're fine."

"OK. Let's ask."

Leith and Jess joined Nichelle and Sari in the ship's lounge area.  Leith was momentarily relieved that he hadn't interrupted another "lesson", as Nichelle and Sari appeared to be in the middle of a conversation that stopped abruptly when he and Jess walked into the lounge.  Leith decided from Sari's downcast eyes that the conversation had probably been about him.  Now wasn't an appropriate time to find out.  He wanted Nichelle on side when they arrived on Draenell's Point.  Leith outlined his suspicions to Nichelle and Sari and reiterated the important points.  At worst the head of Alliance Intelligence was working with the Imperials, Jess was in danger of being kidnapped by ISB and used as a pawn against the Alliance Minister of Supply, their mission in Bissillirus had been compromised and Caroth had arranged for Alliance personnel to follow and potentially eliminate them.

Nichelle looked thoughtful, but said nothing.

"You don't do anything simply, do you," Sari commented.  "What now?" she asked, acutely aware of Leith's raised eyebrow.

"Were you able to contact Deak and Tor, Nichelle?" asked Jess, interrupting before Leith was able to respond to Sari's comment.

"Yes," replied Nichelle.  "They will be arriving on Draenell's Point a day or two before us.  They are going to lay low and see what they can find out prior to our arrival.  Tor's been looking forward to catching up with you, Jess.  I think she misses like-minded female company.  Deak's not so sure he wants the two of you together again.  He's worried about your combined influence on Hela."  Jess laughed.  She remembered the last shopping trip she and Tor had on Corellia.  Which reminded her of what she and Varth had done with her purchases.  Damn it, it shouldn't hurt that much!

"Just quietly though" continued Nichelle, "I think its already too late for Deak to be worrying about Hela.  She always was a free spirited individual.  She seems to have thawed the Iceman's clone out nicely though.  He's nearly human.  It looks like Boddi was right there, too.  The latest generation of clones do have the potential for emotional attachment.  That will make them that much harder to spot.  But anyway, that's another problem for another day."

Jess hadn't heard that news.  Her head was still trying to encompass the bombshells Leith had dropped on her already.  She didn't need more!

"So we go to Draenell's Point as planned then," said Jess.  "The identification codes for this ship have been altered, so unless we attempt to use any of the contacts Vernan supplied, we should be fairly safe.  You're tourists and I'm an honest trader looking for more work and deals on insulating material, heating equipment and farm machinery parts."

Leith nodded, then turned his attention to Nichelle.  "Can we ask you for some special assistance, Nichelle?"

"I'll do what I can without attracting undue attention, Leith.  However, you must understand that the purpose of the Jedi Bendu is to encourage the growth and development of sentient beings towards the Light, towards the Ashla.  This development is not usually achieved without some ... growing pains.  Intelligent beings frequently learn more from their mistakes and their failures than from their successes.  If you believe at any time in the future that I have abandoned you, then understand that I have given you an opportunity to grow without my intervention, to become stronger and wiser.  The Force, far more powerful than any one Jedi, will always be with you."

Convenient, thought Jess.  She was fast revising her opinion of the Jedi downwards.  They were far too involved in cosmic events to be any help in day-to-day matters.  Nichelle obviously wasn't an exception to the rule.

"With all of you" Nichelle concluded, her gaze resting on Jess, who wondered uncomfortably just how often Nichelle read minds.

"Very well then" said Leith, getting up from his seat.  He knew that this was as good as he was going to get from Nichelle.  "Let's do it."  He looked at Sari, an expression crossing his face that Sari hadn't seen before.  "Come on, Sari, it's late" he said with a smile, holding out his hand towards her.  Sari instinctively "read" his mood before she could stop herself.  Leith's anticipation wasn't laced with doubt or suspicion.  He had finally accepted that she loved him.  It also confirmed she'd made the right decision in telling Leith she was a Jedi.  Sari didn't need a second invitation.

"Goodnight Nichelle, Jess" said Sari as she took Leith's hand.  He led her from the lounge without a backward glance.

"Good night, Sari" said Nichelle quietly, smiling as the couple disappeared around the corridor towards their cabin.

Jess couldn't bring herself to reply.  The intimacy between Leith and Sari and their obvious intentions opened up her pain like a raw wound.  She was suddenly hurting too much to think straight and angry with herself for being so affected by Varth's absence.

"Jess, take my hand" said Nichelle.

Jess stared at Nichelle's outstretched hand, wondering if she was making fun of her.  "Why?"

"Each of the Circle members has particular strengths, Jess.  We work together to achieve control over life, thought and matter.  My strengths are space and time, that's why astronavigation is so easy for me.  I want you to understand something, Jess.  The Force holds everything together; it connects every moon, every planet and every sun.  The Force also unfolds time from moment to moment.  It governs space and time, as you know them.  By using the Force I can will myself from place to place and from time to time, within a limited period, all within a heartbeat."

"I'm here with you now, but I'm also sitting in the bar in Generis Base having an interesting conversation with Varth.  He's tired, a little drunk and in more pain than he knows how to deal with.  He has a dangerous mission soon and he knows his judgement is impaired.  He can't sleep, he can't concentrate and he's concerned that he will endanger his pilots.  He's drunk enough that he's told me he misses you intensely and that he's been a complete fool."

"You're in an equally dangerous situation and you're not at your best, either.  Those bounty hunters jumped you on Omze's because you were too wrapped up in trying to numb the pain of your separation from Varth.  I think you've been apart long enough and felt enough pain to know that your feelings for each other are real.  There is nothing else to be gained by dragging this out any longer, so I'm intervening now before anything irrevocable happens to either of you."

Jess was angry with Nichelle for her interference.  At the same time, she was digesting the news that Varth missed her.  Not that she could do a lot about that at the moment.  "Oh, I see.  Helping us with a potentially Alliance-shattering problem is not necessarily something a Jedi wants to do, but the chance to interfere in other people's personal lives is just too good to pass up!  You Jedi are all the same.  Varth may be drunk, but I'm not.  If you think I can bare my soul in front of a third person, you are sadly mistaken."

Suddenly, Nichelle was standing right in front of Jess, who started.  There had been no movement, she simply disappeared from her seat at the table and reappeared in front of Jess in the same instant.  "I can arrange this so that you and Varth can talk to each other without me being present.  However, your suspicion tells me that you don't trust me.  You won't be open with Varth because you'll think I'm listening in, no matter what I say to you."  Jess didn't answer.  She was uncomfortably aware that Nichelle was right.

"Varth has a question for you, one which you won't answer under these circumstances.  He'll probably take that as a rejection of his feelings, so there is no point in continuing.  I could give you my word of honour as a Jedi Bendu that I wouldn't allow anything to happen that would embarrass either of you, but you don't believe we have any honour."

"That's not true, I just think Jedi honour is different.  You have your own agenda."

"My agenda was to stop two people I care about from hurting so much that they risk endangering not just their own lives, but the lives of others as well.  In spite of what you think Jess, I wasn't playing games at your expense.  I was using the abilities I have for the purpose the Ashla intended them, to bring intelligent and compassionate beings together so they can resolve their differences and learn from one another.  Well, Varth's question will go unanswered."

Nichelle looked at Jess like she was seeing her for the first time.  "What's your agenda, Jess?"

Jess was startled into answering without thinking.  "I don't know.  Just to help out where I can."  It sounded lame, even to her ears.

"You're capable of more than that, Jess.  You can make a real difference if you set your mind to it."

"Not on my own.  And you've seen how effective I am at working with other people."

There was a brief silence between the two women, and then Nichelle took a step away from Jess and visibly relaxed.  "Perhaps you're right, Jess" Nichelle said in a much more subdued tone.  "The Servants of the Ashla may be all the same.  We've been Jedi Bendu for so long that we've forgotten much of how it is for other beings.  It would seem that my efforts only push us further apart.  Well, it's time I stopped.  Boddi said we could never go back."

Jess decided not to point out that Boddi was hardly a good judge of how things were for other beings.  Nichelle folded her arms and regarded Jess.  "Sari's not ready to commit to full-time Jedi training and I won't take her any further without the necessary isolation from Leith.  Distracted apprentices are a danger to themselves.  She has enough Force ability to contribute to the success of Leith's mission and that will have to do for now.  It's time you found another navigator, one you can trust."

Suddenly, Jess was alone in the lounge.  She hoped that Nichelle was still aboard somewhere, but now wasn't the time to find out.  Jess went to bed secure in the knowledge that Varth missed her.

Some time later Sari awoke and saw Nichelle sitting on the opposite bunk.  "Shh" whispered Nichelle quickly with her finger against her lips.  With her other hand Nichelle pointed at Leith, still sleeping quietly next to Sari.  Sari gave a barely perceptible nod and Nichelle smiled warmly at her.

"Sari, remember what I have taught you.  Have the courage to live by the Jedi Code.  Now is the time to prove to me and to yourself that you are a Jedi, but do not draw unwanted attention.  There are many who would seek to turn you to their will.  Be wary.  Trust only Leith and those you read as true.  I will see you again. Farewell."

Sari blinked and the cabin was empty but for herself and Leith, who's regular breathing told her he was still asleep.

## Draenell's Point

"Jess, where's Nichelle?" asked Leith as he and Sari came into the cockpit.  Jess glanced briefly at the empty navigator's seat on her right.  "We had a difference of opinion, Leith.  Nichelle decided to pick last night to try and resolve some of my personal issues and I took exception to the timing.  She got huffy and left.  Of course, it wasn't as polite and open as that.  We both said some fairly rude things to each other.  I was hoping to have the chance to talk to her about it today, but obviously she's decided it isn't worth it."  Jess sounded awkward about the situation and clearly didn't want to discuss what had happened.

Leith exchanged a quick glance with Sari, and then slid into the navigator's seat next to Jess.  Sari had already told him that Nichelle had left; he just wanted to see what Jess' take was on their parting.  Nichelle had never struck Leith as being "huffy".  In fact, he had never seen her short tempered, upset or angry.  Trust Jess to get her offside.  He was just hoping that Jess' navigation skills were good enough to get them to the landing field in one piece.  After that Leith would reconsider his options.  Jess had a knack of attracting just the kind of attention he wanted to avoid.

Leith was distracted from his musings by the sight of Draenell's Point looming large before them.  The yellow-blue planet's only moon, Unillian, wasn't visible from their angle of approach.  Extensive cloud cover hid all three major landmasses and most of the oceans.  Jess was heading towards the only civilian star port on Draenell's Point, Starpoint, which was located in the West Unillian Mountain chain on the continent of Mee'r.

He watched nervously while Jess manoeuvred the _Red Storm Rising_ from the western Mee'r coast east into its mountainous interior at breakneck speed.  He'd heard a rumour about Jess crashing her ship into a mountain on Farstey during a previous mission and fervently hoped she'd learnt from the experience.  Even with the sensors mapping the terrain ahead of them, at this speed they were relying on Jess' piloting skills and reflexes to keep them alive.

After several minutes of threading through the mountainous passes in near zero visibility, Leith came to the conclusion that either Jess would have made a superb Tie fighter pilot or it just wasn't his time to die.  He was considerably relieved when the nav computer locked onto Starpoint's beacon and Jess slowed to a more pedestrian landing speed.  He caught Sari's barely audible sigh of relief from behind him, but resisted the urge to turn and see if she was OK.  He didn't want to embarrass her in front of Jess.

Starpoint's Controller requested they identify themselves.  Jess responded, obtained an approach vector for the landing field and dropped the ship below the cloud cover.  Leith looked out of the cockpit ahead while they cruised at low altitude toward the now visible field.  He recognised the distinctive _Dark Heart_ and pointed it out to Jess.

"Looks like Freehold and Skylow made it after all, Jess," he said as Jess landed the ship in an out of the way corner reserved for smaller vessels and tramp freighters.  Leith turned around to Sari and was surprised to see her looking calmly back at him.  She had recovered quickly.

"Sari, we'll need to catch up with Freehold and Skylow straight away.  If they have anyone with them that we haven't met back on Generis Base, tell me ... what you think."  Leith trailed off, still not sure how to phrase that question.  He turned back to Jess.  "Well, let's all be careful and try not to attract attention.  Shall we see if your friends are home, Jess?"

They spent the next several minutes scrambling for cold weather gear, blasters, credits and whatever else might prove useful in a strange new star port.  Leith was first down the ramp, his breath clouding in the cold air as he looked around and got his bearings.  Sari joined him as Jess secured the ship.  Invigorated by the biting cold, the smell of real earth and the solid ground beneath his feet, Leith swept Sari up in his arms and kissed her, hard.

"Now, isn't this better than being cooped up onboard ships and space stations?" he said, putting Sari back down on the ground.  "I've always preferred planets.  They have that real energy that everything else lacks.  They're alive, you can feel it."  He took Sari's gloved hand in his and they headed off with Jess towards the _Dark Heart_.

Jess trailed behind morosely.  She didn't hate planets, but give her a ship any day.  Planets had too much sky for her liking.

"Well, well.  You run half-way to the Core Worlds and you still can't get away from your friends, hey Jess."  Tor Skylow stood at the top of the _Dark Heart's_ entry stairs, hands on her hips and a huge grin on her face.  "You must have brought this weather with you, it was perfect yesterday!"

Jess responded with her own grin.  "Wait till you see the bill.  Transporting snow is horribly expensive, you know".   Jess could see Deak standing just behind Tor, smiling and shaking his head.

"Come aboard, Jess" added Tor.  "You probably need to stretch your legs and we've got plenty of room."  She indicated the spacious interior of the _Dark Heart_ with a grand wave.

Jess climbed the stairs, followed by Sari and Leith.  It had been a while since Jess had been aboard the _Dark Heart_.  The ship's sensual elegance was the perfect setting for Tor, but Jess couldn't reconcile her impression of Deak with these surroundings.  Perhaps Tor brought out a hidden side of him, a side that he reserved for her alone.  After all, Jess knew she bought out a side to Varth that his pilots would never have believed.  She warmed to Tor's friendliness, balm to Jess' hurt feelings.

Boddi's niece, Hela, and her bodyguard were also aboard.  Jess remembered Nichelle's comment about the Iceman's clone and noticed his new demeanour.  There was something very protective in the way he stood near Hela, his right hand resting on his blaster and his ice cool blue eyes watching their every move.  Hela in turn radiated a new confidence.  There was something in her smile and her direct gaze that suggested to Jess she had turned a corner recently.  Jess noticed the light sabre at Hela's hip and remembered that this young woman was Boddi's apprentice.  Hela could protect herself quite well without the clone's assistance, but very few would know that.

Once onboard the _Dark Heart_, Tor took Jess up to the flight deck to show her around and to catch up on Jess' doings over the past months.  Given that those doings were likely to be outrageous, Tor made sure she and Jess were out of earshot of Leith and Deak.

Deak took Leith and Sari into the ship's lounge, followed by Hela and "Ice".  Once all were settled, Leith outlined the situation to Deak.  In the course of the discussion that followed, Leith came to the conclusion that the crew of the _Dark Heart_ would be more help in an undercover assignment than the mercurial Jess Storm.  Leith was also aware of this crew's reputation within the Alliance for getting the job done with a minimum of fuss, not something for which Jess was renowned.  He didn't think twice about enlisting their help at Jess' expense.

Deak agreed, and Leith felt that his chances of success had just improved greatly.  Sari however, was shocked at the speed of the changeover and embarrassed when Leith informed Jess of the "change of personnel", even after he thanked Jess for her help and wished her well.  Jess hid her disappointment under her usual air of bravado and, after warning Leith that she wasn't hanging around just in case, left the ship.

After she had gone, Sari took Leith to task.  "You've been around Imperials too long.  Jess wasn't one of the hired help and you shouldn't have treated her like the Alliance has treated her.  She was a volunteer and could have been useful.  She'd like to be useful to us."  Tor was equally annoyed.  She hadn't been consulted and would have supported Jess' continued involvement.

Leith looked hard at both women.  "I'm not going to have this mission and our lives compromised by Jess Storm's unpredictable temper and selfish attitude."  Deak nodded slowly in agreement.  As an Intel agent, Deak appreciated the delicate, exceptionally difficult and dangerous nature of undercover work.  The thought of Jess going off under the noses of the Imperials made him shudder, for her and for anyone with her.

Leith's decision held.
