# Calm Before the Storm

>**GM's Note:**  Jess Storm loses her best friend and navigator, Flis, killed in an "accident" after Jess dumps cargo owned by Yerkys ne Dago.  On the advice of her father, Taran Storm, Jess flees to Minos Sector to contact "someone" (actually Tor Skylow, a friend and business partner of Taran's).

## Written by [[Jenny-H|Jenny H.]] (Jess)

Jess settled into the bar and ordered her favourite.  They could afford it.  That last run to Farris was particularly profitable.  Flis nudged her and Jess automatically ordered her friend a drink, before turning to see what had caught her eye.

"I should have known!" she muttered.  "Don't you ever think of anything else?"

"Be fair.  How often do I find another Togorian?"  Flis purred.  "It's all right for you.  You've got a man in every port!"

"I wish.  Well, what are you waiting for?  Go get him."

"You'll never understand these things, Jess.  There's the thrill of the chase."

"Just remember, we get a contract and I'm out of here."

Flis stuck out her tongue.  "Spoil-sport.  Look, I'll find a man for you and we'll be even."

"That's what I like about you, we won't ever get into a fight over a man."

"Not unless you decide to see sense and go for fur!"

"Go on, get lost. I'll mind the store."

There was merit in their arrangement.  The Bantha's Breath might be a good place to find a contract, but it wasn't the best place to let go without someone to watch your back.  And Flis and she had been watching each other's backs for a long time.  Flis' father had been a friend of Jess' father.  The two of them had followed in their respective fathers' footsteps; so that now Jess was the captain of her own ship and Flis was her navigator.

On the long trips between planets and systems, it helped to have someone you could live with.  The _Red Storm Rising_ wasn't a big freighter and crews of that size (two) weren't noted for their people skills.  Flis was irrepressibly cheerful, but Jess had a temper that could stop a Wookie in its tracks.  And she didn't always have the sense to realise that one of these days that Wookie was going to tear her head off!

 Business was slow, so Jess just watched the other patrons for trouble and admired Flis' effortless charm.  That woman could put a Jedi on heat!  Some time later, Flis returned to her side, her hips waving seductively, a good sign.

"Don't tell me you struck out!"

Flis smirked.  "Not a chance.  I'd retire and hide in my den if I did.  No, just made arrangements for later on.  Got to give my captain a chance."

"Hang on.  Looks like we've got a customer."

The bartender was talking to an insectoid alien in grubby robes and indicating them.  Jess wasn't keen on what she saw and her hand inched a fraction closer to the blaster at her belt.  There was the clink of metal as the alien handed something to the bartender, and then he approached the two women.

"I hear you have ship hire," the alien said, the words slightly distorted by the translator at his throat.  "I have cargo you carry."

"What and where," asked Jess, all business now.

"Passengers.  Ten them.  Two month.  Location later.  How much."

"I don't normally carry passengers.  Haven't the quarters."

There was something about him she didn't like.

"No need.  They come own space."  The alien shrugged expressively.  "I take care."

"I take it we should avoid any undue attention then.  That will cost extra."  She gave a show of considering.  "Ten thousand."  It was outrageous and she knew it.  Even so, the alien looked as though he might just consider it, so for good measure she added.  "And the cost of extra life support systems.  I won't take the risk of your 'space' being faulty.  You might decide to hold me liable."

That settled it.  The alien made his excuses and left.

"Why didn't you just say no," asked Flis, brushing down her face fur in a thoughtful gesture.  "We don't take prisoners."

Jess laughed shortly.  "After all this time, Flis.  You surprise me.  Never let them know your limits.  They know that, they know your price, your weaknesses.  And they can use them.  If they're stupid enough to believe my words, not my actions, that's their look-out."  She rubbed the tension out of her neck.  "Look, you go along.  I'll stay here a bit longer.  If there's nothing doing, that droid repairer lives around here."

"Ah, the cute one with the long fingers."  Flis made an expressive gesture.

Jess grinned.  "Never think of anything else, do you."

Flis sighed and looked archly over at the Togorian waiting expectantly.  "Is there anything else to think of?"

Jess made her drinks last, but it was late and just as she was considering leaving when Car Riesheff found her.

"Got a job for you."

Jess looked expectantly at him.  She'd worked for Car before and the merchant usually did well by her.

"Got some crates need to be taken to Sici Three on the quiet.  Only small.  Pay you five thou'."

"Not bad."

She thought quickly.  She could pick up some machine parts with the profits from her last job.  They'd sell well there.  She might even be able to get a deal on some ore for the return trip.  Do some proper trading for a change.  Car took her pause for possible refusal.

"Can make it seven, if it's out of your way."

"All right, Car, you've got a deal."

Car seemed relieved.  "Good, I'll bring the stuff tomorrow.  You in the usual place?"

Jess nodded.

Car and the crate were waiting when Jess got back in the morning.  She'd spent a night mixing business with pleasure and expected the rest of her cargo shortly.  Flis got back after it had all been safely stowed, looking very pleased with herself.  It wasn't long before the paperwork was completed and they were underway, the special cargo safely stored in one of Jess' cubbyholes.  It wasn't until they were well underway that Jess realised just what it was that had been bothering her.

"You know something funny, Flis, Car doesn't normally haggle, but he did for this run.  Let's have a look at that cargo".

Undoing one of the crates didn't take long for an old hand, even one as securely done up as this one.  Jess gave a gasp of amazement.

"By the Emperor's..."  She broke off the curse to say, urgently.  "Don't touch it!"

Flis brought back her hand hurriedly and looked in puzzled anxiety at her friend.  Jess sat down and swallowed hard, cradling her chin in one hand.

"Toxins.  They're nerve toxins.  Seen 'em once."  She shook her head at the memory and spoke almost to herself.  "Imperials use 'em.  They do horrible things to a body.  The lucky ones die."  She added angrily, "Hell's teeth!  Car doesn't deal in this stuff.  What the hell's he think he's doing?  This is a class 1 infraction if we're lucky!"

"Even if he hadn't been dealing with us long enough to know we won't touch something like this," added Flis, a soft growl starting low in her throat.

"He doesn't either, normally, that's the point.  Shit!"  Jess ran her hands through her hair, thinking furiously.

"What do we do with it?"

"We dump it, that's what we do.  I won't be held accountable for what that stuff does, let alone what they'll do to us if we get caught with it."

"What about Car?"

"We got boarded by a customs ship.  The Imperials are usually thick around here anyway.  Won't take much to doctor the log."  Jess had reason to thank the day she'd installed automatic ejectors in those holds.  "Ashes to ashes," she muttered, as a quick shot with the lasers finished the job.

In fact, they were boarded by a customs ship near Sici Three.  The rest of the ship was clean, so Jess made no attempt to evade them.  Made the log look that more realistic.  The Imps were a lot more thorough than usual and Jess was seething by the time they'd gone.

"Just wait 'till I get my hands on Car," she promised.

He wasn't in evidence when they returned.  Jess wasn't surprised he was avoiding her.  The news that he was rumoured to be deep in debt didn't surprise her either.  The whole thing smelt of a con job.  What did surprise her was the man who turned up the next day.  She leaned against the hatch in a deceptively nonchalant manner and wondered why he was there.

"My boss is rather upset with you, Storm," he started, his voice a silken, even tone.  "His cargo didn't arrive as scheduled."

"It may have escaped his notice, but I'm not on his pay-roll." Jess replied, equally calmly.

"You were for the last run to Sici Three.  Yerkys wants his property."

"I haven't got it.  Got boarded by Imperials and had to dump it.  I'm sure he'll understand."

"I'm sure he won't."

"He can take it up with Car then.  He hired me and I'll do any explaining to him."

"Car has sold his business to Yerkys.  You'll explain it to him."

Jess could see this wasn't going to end up her way no matter what, but gave it one last shot.  "There's nothing to explain.  Whether I'd dumped it before boarding or if the Imperials had confiscated it, he wouldn't have a cargo.  Either way, same result.  So what's the difference?"

"Yerkys doesn't care what happened.  He wants his property, or compensation.  Twenty five thousand."  He could have asked more, but it didn't really matter.

"Standard practice for cargo carrying.  I don't owe him anything."

"Do you think he cares about standard practice?  You've been warned, Storm.  First payment's due tomorrow."  The man gave a mocking bow and left.

Damn! thought Jess.  What now?  Flis was off on shore leave.  Her male was still in the area and Jess hadn't had the heart to stop her taking off.  This was becoming more complicated by the minute!  Even if she were inclined to pay up, she didn't have that kind of money.

The following day was a nightmare.  No matter how hard she tried, there weren't enough favours she could pull in to get Yerkys off her back.  The biggest crime boss in this sector was not someone they wanted to tangle with.  And no matter how hard she thought she couldn't figure out why Yerkys had gone to this much trouble to reel her in.

Jess never decided whether the explosion was meant for her or Flis.  It didn't matter really.  When she pulled Flis' body out from the tangled wreckage, it felt like part of Jess had died with her.  There was a sense of unreality about the whole thing.  It couldn't be Flis.  She wasn't supposed to be back till the next morning.  Jess automatically filled in the endless forms and answered the officers' questions.  Mechanical failure was the official verdict.  Death by accident.  Jess knew better.  Her father's message in response to the incoherent vidcom she'd sent was the only touch of sanity in the whole proceedings.

"Get out of the system.  Go to Minos Sector.  Someone will contact you there.  Take care."

She hired the first navigator she found who was willing to go out-system.  He wasn't the companion she would have chosen, but then, no one was.  She went berserk at the authorities, until they decided there was no harm in releasing Flis' body to her.  She didn't think they cared enough to argue with her.  She got Tak to jump a short distance, and then fulfilled her last promise.  A drunken pact that she'd never thought she'd have to carry out.

"You promise me that if I die first, you'll rescue my body from my grieving lovers and send me out to wander space forever."

It broke every rule about the disposal of bodies, but she didn't care.

"The undiscovered path is yours now.  As part of the Force that is in all of us, become one with life, with hope, with the universe.  Wander the pathways and explore them well, until we meet each other again, in the eternal space lanes."

After it was finished and Flis was on her way, Jess ordered Tak to lay in a course for Minos Sector.  He started.

"What?"

"I said Minos Sector," she barked, in no mood to argue.

Tak wisely didn't.
