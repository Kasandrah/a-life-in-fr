>**GM's Note:** I didn't accept this player story as **canon** for my Star Wars campaign. The writer's assumptions regarding my NPCs' motives and behaviour are incorrect.
>
>_[[Boddi|Boddi]]_ doesn't hate Jess; Boddi doesn't hate anyone or anything. She's learned hate is a fast path to the Dark, to the Bogan.  Boddi is familiar with many ways of using the Force. She prefers the way of the _[[Jedi-Bendu|Jedi Bendu]]_ for long-term galactic stability, but sees value in other Light-side approaches.
>
>In this player story, Jess invents a way of using the Force that is "something completely new". In Boddi's long experience, Jess' wild and untamed dancing isn't new. This form derives from ancient shamanistic traditions among tribal and clan-centric Force users. _[[Jedi-Bendu#Dermod|Dermod]]_, being an ancient Jedu Bendu himself, would have recognised this "new" form for what it was as well.
>
>Boddi agrees Jess isn't a Jedi Bendu, that much is obvious, but is more a Witch of Dathomir with a rare Force-cloaking ability, for example.  This being the case, Boddi would have acknowledged Jess' fresh path and commitment to training, and encouraged her to seek a suitable teacher. One who had already walked the path Jess was starting out on.

# Lines of Storm Force

**Written by _[[Jenny-H|Jenny H.]]_**

Jess had had enough.  She and Jayme had been waiting to receive their new assignments for long enough, especially as Jayme informed her that the delay was an argument about whether she really was a member of the Alliance and not just a stand-in for Hadra.  Jess made a signal to him and they went into action, Jayme distracting the adjutant who had kept them waiting in the reception area, while she entered the conference room.

Ignoring the half-uttered protests and the beginnings of concern, she put a data projector on the table.  "Excuse my interruption, sirs.  In the interests of saving time, I think this may solve the issue you have been concerned about."

An image of General Madine appeared and spoke.  "You have before you Commander Jess Storm, an officer in the Alliance.  As such, we expect that under the rules of engagement she will be treated as an enemy combatant, rather than a common criminal."  The image bowed and faded.

Jess pocketed the device.  "I hope that answered any questions about my loyalty.  I assume General Madine is unavailable to confirm my appointment."

Jess knew when Leith approached her.  She'd started to learn the difference between some of the headaches the Jedi gave her and she had more to do with Leith than most.  She continued her pre-flight checks without acknowledging his presence.

"Running away again?"

That was predictable.  Everyone thought she was running away.  "Running to is more accurate," she replied without turning around.  There was no response, and with anyone else she would have said no more, but Leith was the only one who might conceivably understand.

"Have you considered that if Boddi had wanted to keep me here, no transfer order would have arrived?  I'm being thrown out rather than running away.  But it's time for me to take my duties more seriously anyway."  That wasn't the first time she'd told him something like that, but this time she was going for good.

"I've told you before that whatever it is that you're going to do needs a solid base behind you.  Anyone who thinks the Empire will dissolve just because the Emperor is dead is dreaming.  Power vacuums attract power fights.  The Alliance needs all the help it can get to consolidate its victory."

"What about your Jedi training?"

"I'm not a Jedi."  To forestall any argument, she added, "I don't know what I am, but I'm not a Jedi.  Even Dermod admits that.  He can do no more for me.  I need to find my own path."

"What about Jayme?"

For the first time Jess paused in her check.  "I'm not taking him with me.  I can't deprive him of his training."  She frowned.  "I've sent him to the planet on an errand.  When he gets back, can you give this to him please?"  Not quite meeting his eyes, she handed Leith a data com, the kind that would project a message.  "I've got to get going."

"Jess."  Leith waited until she turned back.  "The Force be with you."  She smiled wryly.  "I don't have much choice in that these days."

In the cockpit Jess completed the startup routine.  It was just as well she could do it in her sleep; she needed something methodical and mindless to avoid the fact that she was leaving for what might be the last time.  Her heart felt like a lead weight and she didn't want to think about why.  She'd left this station before - she could do it again.  She told herself that this time was no different to any other time, but she knew she lied.

"Our passengers strapped in?" Jess asked the footsteps that came down the passageway.  She wasn't the only one being rotated.

"All present and accounted for," replied Jayme.  Startled, Jess looked around at him as he strapped himself into the jump seat.  He calmly returned her stare and Jess knew, as surely as if he'd broadcast it on a tight beam, that Jayme was disappointed that she had not given him the choice of coming or staying, that her attempts to be self-sacrificing had been misplaced, that in her untrained state she was ill-equipped to take on the galaxy alone, that she wasn't going to trick him so easily and that one way or another she needed him.  And that even if Flar hadn't chosen that moment to take his seat and begin the navigation calculations, none of it would have been said aloud.  It was enough that she knew.  Jess turned back to the controls with a heart suddenly lighter.

Which should explain what Jess has been up to lately...

Jess was beginning to hate her sessions with Dermod.  No, that wasn't true.  She had almost always hated her sessions with any Jedi.  Despite some breakthroughs, the headaches were becoming even more tiresome, she kept failing any tests (however minor) that Boddi threw at her and the simplest Force tasks were still like trying to swim through molasses.  There were a couple of things she could do and do well, but they were hardly of immediate use in any actual mission.

Her few attempts to head off and steal ships, a welcome relief from an increasing feeling of failure, were met with an attitude that she was playing hooky.  Which, of course, from a Jedi point of view she was.  Ironic that she'd finally actually joined the Alliance and it was now treated like she'd joined the Imperials.

Dermod interrupted her bad mood.  "I think you're getting better.  Must be all those good feelings from finally getting laid.  I should have arranged it sooner."

The crate Jess was trying to levitate crashed to the ground.  "I thought emotions interfered with Force skills.  Boddi certainly disapproves."

"Boddi disapproves of everything.  I wouldn't let it worry you."

"What worries me is that she's right.  I really am a waste of space as far as Force skills go."

"Nonsense! They're there.  Somewhere buried deep inside you.  Even Boddi has to acknowledge that.  The difficulty is getting them out.  And we are making progress, whether you think so or not."

The crate began its wobbly path upwards again, and then tilted slowly over.  Next to Dermod's three doing lazy cartwheels in the air it looked pathetically like it was drunk.  Which was what Jess wanted to be.  Her crate lurched to the ground, despite her best efforts to get it airborne.

A grunt of pure frustration escaped Jess.  She swung out mentally and Dermod's crates suddenly dropped like marionettes whose strings had been cut.

"What did you just do?" asked Dermod in astonishment.

Jess felt contrite.  It wasn't his fault she was useless.  "I'm sorry," she sighed.  "That was rude."

"No, that was amazing.  How did you do that?"

"Do what? All I did was drop your crates."

"You cut my hold on them.  You didn't oppose it, you..." He struggled for a description.  "You cut it."

"So?" Jess was really puzzled now.

"So you can't do that.  You oppose it, you hide from it, you defend from it, you deflect it - you don't just make it not there."

"But... oh."

"I think we just found out why Boddi doesn't like you.  And probably why you have so much trouble with this stuff.  Your use of the Force is something completely new.  Just wait till Nadoc finds out.  He'll love it."  Dermod sounded like all his birthdays had come at once.  And he'd had quite a few.  "Let's try that again."

They soon discovered that she could do it again. Dermod changed the strategy of his training and Jess found herself unlearning almost everything she’d managed so far. The headaches lessened the more often they left the concentration of Jedis and Force powers flying left, right and centre. Let them think she was running away. She no longer cared. If she ever really had.

Of course, it all had to come to an end sooner or later. Boddhi wasn’t going to let her get off that easily.

___

Jess got the summons from Boddi on the return from one of her Alliance missions.  Well, in theory they were Alliance missions and usually she had something concrete to show for them, but in practice it was a Jedi training session with Dermod.  They had discovered that the development of her skills progressed much better when she was away from the ubiquitous lines of Force that seemed to permeate the base.  She could concentrate on the development of her own abilities rather than trying to avoid interfering with the others'.

Neither she nor Dermod were ready to reveal what she was actually developing.  The fact that her skills seemed to work better with her own temperament shining through, with all its emotions and raw energy, would only confuse the issue.  The Jedi treated with suspicion anything that wasn't measured, controlled, and emotionless.  That was of the Dark Side.  Dermod could detect no element of that in Jess, but the misunderstanding and dislike that was already evident against her wasn't going to accept his assessment, especially as he wasn't exactly part of the accepted status quo.

So they had conducted the training sessions, which were more like scientific experiments, away from prying eyes and Jess' headaches.  Dermod had taken on the role of a mentor, rather than a trainer, suggesting possible methods or uses for her raw talent.  Some had worked, some were disastrous, but as neither had a clear idea of what she was capable of or what form her skills should take, this was the best they could manage.  Interestingly, Jayme's use of the Force didn't cause her the same angst as the others'.  She could tell he was a little jealous that she had finally discovered her talent, while his was still hidden, but he was mostly reassured that one day he could discover his own specialty.

Increasingly, this activity was removing her from the circle of descendants.  Their attitude to her ranged from open distain to uncomprehending sympathy.  Her former crew's barely-hidden rejection hurt the most.  It was a sobering thought that she could spend that long with a group of people and completely fail to create any kind of loyalty or sympathy.  If the Circle was correct, one day they would have to work together.  That day might be a long time off.  A very long time off.  The longer the better!

Boddi's summons was not unexpected.  It had been too long since the last test she'd failed.  To be charitable, Boddi might be trying to get her to take Jedi training seriously.  Jess might be wrong in thinking Boddi was enjoying Jess failing far too much.  For the sake of the Circle, she hoped so.  But with a spare descendant or two, it wasn't like Boddi needed her.  The other option was that Boddi would like her to leave and never come back.  It would be a lot more comfortable.  For both of them.

Being the stubborn cuss that she was, Jess arrived on the planet with the rest of the descendants that were on the base at the time.  Boddi arrived and announced that the test this time was a simple form of capture the flag.  One apprentice would take the flag to the designated fort.  The other apprentices would try to stop them.  They all had to avoid the traps and Force snares that she had set up.  And the local fauna and flora.  The first person or team to reach the fort with the flag was the winner.  There were no other rules or restrictions.  Although her face was impassive, Jess could almost see the satisfaction with which Boddi handed her the flag and told her she had an hour's head start.

In the first 1/2 hour Jess made no real attempt to hide her passing, though she set off on a tangent and set a couple of simple diversions.  The one Force snare she encountered she found long before reaching it by the pain it caused.  Rather than dissolving it, she formed a protective bubble and slid through it, finding the blanks in its effectiveness, rather like taking a land speeder sideways through a gap.  That gave her an idea.

By the time the rest of the apprentices started to hunt her, Jess was invisible.  Not just cloaked, but simply not there.  She had stopped interacting with her environment and was simply moving through it.  She felt the tendrils of Force that were trying to locate her and moved aside.  The dance lessons her mother had insisted she take had taught her to move in time with a partner, matching his movements but never touching.  She did the same now with the Force movements that sought her.  Sway, slide, turn, gracefully teasing but never coming together.

Some of the more determined Force attacks she treated as Flis had taught her to fight, using the body to dodge attacks and allow the fighter's energy to uselessly dissipate.  But this form of fighting was much more graceful.  She moved around them and let the Force attacks find other, less useful targets.  She moved through the landscape like a ship through space, silently, never leaving a trace of her passing.  No footprints, no signature, no trace, just as she had passed through countless lives without forming a bond, without creating a connection.  Once she passed by a beast that had lost interest in attacking a creature that was unappetising and without challenge.

As she moved along the threads of her life were being woven together.  The losses, the rejection, the misunderstandings.  She made them into her own snares and let them go.  The skills that had seemed useless were now becoming part of her abilities.  At the edge of her awareness were pressures she recognised as the others, but she could silently move them around her without touching the patterns they formed.  She danced her life, gracefully moving forwards, invisible, uninterrupted, unconnected, unnoticed.

Jess was at the fort without even realising it, coming back into reality with a vague sense of surprise and loss.  She couldn't remember much of her passage, just the dance that accomplished it.  She stood at the mound of the designated fort and tried to clear her head, feeling the earth beneath her feet as something foreign.  She heard movement behind her and quickly pushed the flag into the earth.

They came in then, in singles and in pairs.  Leith and Sari were first.  At Sari's urging, they had followed Jayme, but even Leith's strategies had not brought them there quickly enough.  The sight before them was not at all Jedi-like.  Jess was still dancing, wild and untamed.  Her strawberry-blonde hair had escaped from its customary pigtail and had taken on a life of its own, bristling with the Force that still was undetectable to her on-lookers.  When enough of their presences got through to her, she slowed and finally stopped.

Boddi came then, followed by several of the Circle.  She looked at Jess with a hard stare.  Jess saw Dermod behind her, and she answered his smirk of satisfaction with one of her own.  On impulse Jess thanked Boddi.  The test had been the most useful thing she had ever done for her.  Boddi turned on her heels and left without saying a word.  Jess nodded to the others, collected Jayme and returned to her ship.  She might not have their friendship or even their respect, but she now had direction.  And with Jayme to watch her back, that was enough.
