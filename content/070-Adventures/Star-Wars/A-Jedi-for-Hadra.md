# A Jedi for Hadra

## Written by _[[Jenny-H|Jenny H.]]_ (Hadra)

Hadra leant against a rim support, surveying the scene below her with a pair of macros.  All quiet, as expected.  "Is everyone in position?" she asked and listened to the team reporting in.  It felt odd stealing ships, especially with what had happened to hers.  However, the Alliance needed transports and she made sure that the ones they stole belonged to people who deserved to lose them.  A kind of revenge with a purpose.

Their target today was a relatively new, sleek model.  The Z-103LR Moonrunner was one of the latest upgrades for the Z-93 Headhunter and capable of long-range space travel.  Give it a few more guns and it would make a fine Alliance fighter.  She'd prefer something a little less obvious, but Morgan wanted to have a go at the new security systems and this was a sort of reward for her good work lately.

"Ready when you are, Commander."  That was Jayme.  He'd been invaluable in helping her adjust to her new role.  It felt good to have a back up she could depend upon.

She was about to give the go-ahead when she hesitated.  Everything was running smoothly, but Hadra couldn't help feeling uneasy.  Nothing logical, just prickles down the back of her neck.  There was something wrong, all her instincts told her so, but there was absolutely nothing to justify it.

"Morgan, do you have a tracer in your kit?"

"Yes, why?"  Morgan's voice was justifiably confused.

"Install one on that ship and fall back."

"Commander?"  That was Jayme, as it should be.  She'd just changed several days' planning without notice.

"You'll have to trust me on this one.  I'll explain later.  Just install it and everyone fall back."  Hadra hoped she could explain it later.  She didn't understand it herself, but there was a definite compulsion to follow, rather than to take.  She hoped her reasoning would become clear soon, at least to herself.  Then she might be able to explain it to the others.

Morgan worked quickly.  She was one of the best technicians Hadra had ever seen. If Morgan wasn't so easily distracted, she'd be a genius at anything mechanical.  Hadra kept watch for any guards, but the landing bay was quiet.  It was too early in the morning for much activity around the ships.  The next shift wasn't due yet and they'd picked this time because the guards liked to play Sabbac while they were working.

"Done, Commander."

"Good, now fall back, all of you.  We'll meet back at the ship.  Start getting her ready to leave."

Hadra didn't follow her own instructions.  She had to see what would happen next to make sure that her instincts were right.  Although 'instincts' wasn't quite right for the urgency she felt.

She didn't have long to wait.  A dark figure came into the landing bay, looked around and headed for the ship they'd targeted.  No, not dark.  He was actually fair-haired and dressed in the pale clothes the people on this planet favoured.  He just felt dark.  The man hesitated and looked around the landing bay.  Hadra shrunk back further into the shadows, trying to remember everything Jess had heard about hiding herself.  All those overheard lessons of Ash and then Sari from the Jedi Masters.

That was the real problem with the Jedi.  Their skills were undeniable and extremely useful.  It was just that Jess had never been able to relate to any of them.  They were too remote, too removed from everyday life.  Their skills had changed them too much.  They had forgotten what it was like to be without Force skills.  Perhaps had never known.  Jess had thought Nichelle was different, but even there she had been mistaken.

And more threatening still was the knowledge that Jess had the potential to be the same.  Her family was descended from one of the Circle and she could be strong in the Force as well.  As could Hadra.

Apparently, whatever Hadra had done was enough to allay the man's suspicions.  Or perhaps he just thought her too insignificant to bother with.  In any case, he dealt with the security systems of the ship, entered and the sounds coming from it indicated he was making preparations to leave.

Hadra made her way back to _The Maker_ and her waiting team.  "What was all that about?" asked Jayme, suspiciously.

Hadra had already been thinking about how to explain her actions.  Nothing but what little she knew would satisfy them.  "What do you know about Jedi?"

"What?" asked Morgan.

"Weren't they some crackpot religion that died out?" asked Nord.

Flar was silent, waiting to see what Hadra had in mind.  Characteristically, Jayme spoke last.  "They were a religious group all right.  They saw themselves as the protectors of the universe.  But they haven't died out, have they Commander."

"No," replied Hadra, slowly.  "They haven't."

"And we're dealing with one now?"

"There are two kinds of Jedi, light and dark.  The Emperor is a Dark Jedi.  The Dark Jedi tried to wipe out the Light.  They probably think they've succeeded."

"You're not telling us you're a Jedi?" exclaimed Morgan.

"Not me," answered Hadra.  "Jess came across a few of them.  That's why I recognised the one today.  He's definitely a Dark Jedi, but I can't explain why I know that.  I just know.  And I know we have to stop whatever he's up to.  Whether he's on the Emperor's side or not, whatever he has planned would be bad for the Alliance if it succeeded.  I'm sorry I can't be more specific and I can't explain it.  You'll just have to trust me on this one."

Hadra held her breath.  She could have ordered them to act, but not with something like this.  She choked back a sigh of relief when Jayme calmly stated, "I think we've given him enough of a head start.  I've cleared our departure with Port Authority.  We can leave when you're ready, Commander."

It didn't take long before _The Maker_ left the spaceport.  She was always ready to take off with a minimum of preparation anyway.  Hadra could feel the questions of the crew, but for the moment they were prepared to give her the benefit of the doubt.

"He's not leaving the planet's atmosphere," stated Morgan behind her.  "Whatever he's doing, he's doing it here."

They followed the ship; Flar making sure they were out of range of even the most advanced sensors.  The tracer made it easy.  He was heading to the unpopulated equatorial regions, where the hot and dry conditions made human life impossible.  Hadra ordered protective clothing and goggles to be made ready.

The land became a maze of sand dunes and craggy rocks carved by the wind.  Flar reported that the sensors were becoming confused by the conditions, but Hadra knew her way now.  She didn't need the tracer to tell her where she needed to go.  She set _The Maker_ down in an area protected by cliffs shaped in grotesque patterns.  Unfortunately, the ground wasn't as stable as she thought and one of the landing supports ploughed into soft sand, giving the ship a drunken look.

Hadra and Morgan inspected the ship.  "There's no damage, Commander, but we'll need to dig out some of the sand before we take off."

"You and Nord see to that.  Jayme and I will find whatever the Jedi is after."

"Why should I have to do the manual work?" asked Nord.

"Do you really want to go chasing a Dark Jedi?"  The medic didn't know what a Dark Jedi really was, but from the tone of Hadra's voice deigning to do a little manual work was preferable.  Hadra added wryly, "Besides, you're our backup if this goes wrong."

Shortly afterwards, Hadra almost wished she'd taken him with her.  Traipsing through blinding sand with a visibility of only a few feet, even with protective goggles, might have been good for toughening him up.

"Are you sure you know where you're going, Commander," came Jayme's voice on the COM link.

"The tracer in my head is still going off.  I don't think it's far."

And then they were there, a rock face with a narrow entrance that Hadra would never have found on her own.  It opened up into a small cavern.  On one wall was a small carved recess of intertwined snakes that Hadra recognised.  She wore the reverse of it in green stone around her neck, a legacy of Jess.

It was supposed to be useless once it had freed Troy, but the pendant fitted perfectly and the stone wall slid open, just as it had with the first Jedi Master.  But this time, the room revealed was a mastery of technology.  The workspace was covered with odd pieces of machinery, wires and notes in a language that Hadra didn't recognise.  There was a sphere with lumps that had who knew what purpose and a couple of other pieces of equipment that looked finished or at least close to it.

"What is this place?" asked Jayme.

"It's a Jedi's dream," answered a voice behind them.  They spun around.  Jayme's weapon, already drawn, went flying out of his hand and clattered against a far wall.  Hadra decided drawing hers would be a waste of time.  "It's a place for enhancing Force powers and you don't have any."  He paused and considered Hadra.  "Though you have potential, which could be interesting.  I might even be persuaded to keep you with me."

Hadra considered her options.  The Jedi's arrival must have been masked by the noise of the wind outside and she didn't have the skill to effectively fight him.  She'd led him here and if he was right, he had to be stopped.  And the only weapon she had was words.

"If it enhances Force skills, then it's not much good to you.  This place looks pretty fixed to me," she commented.

"Ah, but it's not fixed," the Jedi replied, smugly.  "It's one of these machines."  He turned to search the workbench.  Jayme took the opportunity to try to grab him from behind.  The Jedi didn't even pause.  With a flick of his hand, Jayme was sprawling on the ground.  "It's not that easy now.  I think you can stay there for a while.  You too.  With this I can challenge the Emperor himself, you might as well get used to it."

Hadra found herself kneeling, unable to stop the movement.  Whatever he had tapped into, it certainly made him powerful.  She hadn't sensed any such level of power at the spaceport.  Her mind raced with possible outcomes.  Worse still was the suspicion that he wouldn't have been able to enter here without her pendant, which made her in part responsible for any future actions on his part.  Their only hope was that at the moment at least, the Jedi was more inclined to brag than to destroy them.

There was a shimmering movement near one of the walls and suddenly there was another occupant, a youngish man with rumpled clothes, sandy-coloured hair worn over his eyes and a distracted look.  He dusted himself off and commented to no one in particular, "Oh good, you're here."

The Jedi gave a shriek and grabbed at one of the machines on the workbench.  "It's mine.  You can't take it from me!"

The young man turned towards him, as if seeing him properly for the first time.  "You can't make it work, you know," he said casually.  "It won't respond to your kind of Force."

The Jedi clutched the machine closer to his chest.  "No, you're trying to trick me.  I studied this.  I've been looking for it for years. I know about it."

The response was soft, coaxing almost.  "I made it.  I can teach you how to use it, but not as you are.  You would have to give up your schemes.  And I can show you a future you can only dream of now."  He put out an open hand.

"You can't have it.  You won't trick me.  It's mine now!"  He fiddled with the controls.

"No!"  The cry came from Hadra and the young man simultaneously, though for different reasons.  There was a flash of brilliant light, like a thousand landing lights winking in and out, and when Hadra's sight cleared, she found herself able to move again.

The sandy-haired man stood where the Jedi had once been.  He said softly, "Why wouldn't you listen to me?"  Then he bent and retrieved the machine from where it had fallen.

Hadra bent over Jayme, who was picking himself up from the ground.  "Are you all right?"

"I feel like I've done a round with a Wookie, but I'll be fine.  What happened?"

"You did a round with a Jedi.  And I think I was had."  She turned to the other Jedi.  "You brought us here, didn't you."

"I'm sorry for that.  You probably felt like you had no choice.  It's hard being subtle with the untrained."  Jess was astonished.  That was the first time she'd ever heard a Jedi apologise for anything at all.

"Why did you need us here?  You dealt with him quite well on your own."

"You're here to collect me, aren't you?  The Circle is gathering.  I thought you'd need directions."

"I wasn't sent to collect you.  The last I'd heard there was only Troy, Boddi and Nichelle, if you mean the ancient Jedi.  There could be others, but there has been no contact with them since Jess left Generis Base, so I can't be sure."

"Yes, Jess.  Could be a problem there."  He looked at her carefully.  "Or maybe not, we'll see."  He rubbed his chin in thought.  "Troy, Boddi and Nichelle, you say.  Well, if they didn't send you, maybe I can hang around with you for a bit.  I don't have to join them just yet.  I'll just get a few things together."  He sounded almost plaintiff and Hadra smiled, despite herself.  He didn't sound like he thought any more of the Jedi than Jess had.

"Can you leave all this?  What if someone else finds it?"

"Oh, I'll take the important things with me.  The rest is just spare parts."  He waved his hand dismissively.  "It won't matter if they find it, they won't be able to do anything with it.  I'm Nadoc, by the way."

"Jess and Jayme."

"I know.  You should really develop that talent of yours, you know.  It would save a lot of trouble."

"We'll see about that.  In the meantime, I have an extra ship to pocket.  Jayme, let's get Morgan back on that security system she wants to play with."
