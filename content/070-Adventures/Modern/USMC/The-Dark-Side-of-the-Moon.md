## Background to Events

### Kate’s Lagoon, northern Belize - late summer 1994

An archaeological expedition from the University of Pennsylvania, headed by Dr. Paul Hughbanks and including Kyle Woodson and Susan Donnelly, carries out a survey and dig.  Rumours of a huge unexplored site in the nearby jungle called El Cacao led Hughbanks to set up a team to survey and possibly excavate.

Cocaine traffickers are using El Cacao as a base.  A misunderstanding leads to a shootout and the deaths of four students.  The expedition flees, the project collapses, careers are destroyed and the survivors go their separate ways.

### In the interim …

Woodson and Donnelly find work separately with National Geographic.

Donnelly’s scholarship and original research quickly came to the attention of Angela Hamilton, a member of the National Geographic Board of Trustees, who began assisting Donnelly by placing her in several Mayan field expeditions.  One of these is the Early Copan Acropolis Program in Honduras, South America, where Donnelly first meets Colonel Snow.  Jefferson Dean subsequently recruits Donnelly.

Donnelly continues to keep in touch with Woodson, who conducts several field seasons across Central America under National Geographic’s patronage.  He publishes several successful books about the Maya and then begins work on a new book about the Pre-Classic Maya.  Woodson’s research takes him to the Sanbourne Institute in Los Angeles, Arkham in Massachusetts, and then back to El Cacao, Belize.

### Late March, 1999

Woodson informs Donnelly that he is returning to northern Belize to “take care of some personal business”.  Donnelly is very concerned for his sanity and his safety, given what happened when she and Woodson were in Belize in 1994.

### San Francisco - early April, 1999

Woodson returns home to San Francisco and is injured in a hit-and-run car accident while walking between the San Francisco International Terminal and the central parking garage.  He dies later that night in a Cal Med/San Francisco emergency room.

After reading about Woodson’s death, Donnelly informs Jefferson Dean.  Dean sets Agent Tara Banks and Agent Dale Simonson of the San Francisco office of the FBI the task of investigating what’s going on.

Agents Banks and Simonson obtain information from Detective E. G. Rolston, Homicide Squad, Investigation Bureau, San Francisco Police, Dr. Herman Zanca, Cal Med/San Francisco Emergency Room Admitting Physician, Mr. Morris Beachy, Woodson’s neighbour, aimless retiree and friend of animals and various other witnesses, paramedics and reporting officers.

After a thorough analysis of the information obtained, Agents Banks and Simonson determine:

- a man or group of men were following Woodson late last year, as reported by Woodson to police at the time (because of lack of information on who the men were and no threats or attacks on Woodson, the matter rested)
    
- Woodson’s hit-and-run manslaughter or murder at San Francisco International Airport was carried out by the driver of a metallic gold 1997 Ford Taurus with no licence plates, as reported by witnesses and determined by evidence left at the scene (broken glass and paint flecks on Woodson’s clothing)
    
- Woodson’s most serious injuries when admitted to Cal Med/San Francisco were to the top of his head, almost as if he had dived headfirst into the front of the car, and his face was badly lacerated, as reported by emergency room personnel
    
- a Mrs. Felice Woodson was contacted by emergency room personnel after they saw her name and phone number scrawled into the “next of kin” field in Woodson’s passport - she took Woodson’s passport and personal effects (a tentative description of her has been provided by witnesses)
    
- subsequent testimony from Woodson’s colleagues, including Donnelly and his neighbour Beachy, states that Woodson was a loner who had never, to their knowledge, been married - his employment records confirm this
    
- the police and the FBI have no records of a Mrs. Felice Woodson
    
- no records of Woodson exist at Cal Med/San Francisco save for admitting doctor Herman Zanca’s examination report, and Zanca’s treatment and prescription orders (no HMO computer database records, no master file, no pharmacy records - all apparently deleted after the fact)
    
- the hasty transport and cremation of Woodson’s body was arranged illegally by Felice Woodson and carried out by hospital, mortuary, funeral home, and crematorium personnel - these serious irregularities in procedure involve violations in city regulation and criminal statutes of state law
    
- a break in occurred at Woodson’s house on the same night as his death - Woodson’s neighbour, Maurice Beachy, reported a burglar next door and when police investigated, they found the Woodson’s house ransacked, no sign of forced entry and the burglar long gone
    
- Woodson’s autopsy revealed no malaria or malarial antibodies in his blood, even though his medical records show he caught malaria in the field a few years ago
    
- the possibility of an unknown corpse and a missing person (Woodson), based on the above autopsy irregularities.
    

Agents Banks and Simonson investigate Woodson’s house, on a slope of Russian Hill in San Francisco.  Here they disturb a woman answering Felice Woodson’s description, but dressed as a Pacific Gas & Electric (PG&E) meter reader, who is ransacking the house.  An exchange of gunfire results in Agent Banks being shot in the upper arm, Agent Simonson being critically injured by a bullet in the chest, and “Felice Woodson” being shot dead.

Subsequent investigations and a thorough search of Woodson’s house by Agent Banks and her new partner, Agent Casper Green, determined:

- PG&E knows nothing about “Felice Woodson”, though her picture ID and equipment are authentic
    
- witnesses confirm her picture is that of the “Felice Woodson” who obtained Woodson’s body and wrangled its speedy and illegal cremation
    
- her autopsy reveals that she suffered a massive and fatal embolism to the brain, that there is much more cerebral fluid in her skull than there should be (her brain is too small for her skull), that a foreign body not unlike an IDE connector joins her brain to her spinal cord, and filaments of an unidentifiable orange-grey substance run through her entire brain _(the connector and samples of the orange-grey substance are provided to Dean for further analysis)_
    
- DNA testing reveals that her brain has been transplanted from someone else’s body
    
- Woodson’s computer has been “hard scrubbed”, and all disks remaining in the house have been demagnetised.
    

Agents Banks and Green send Woodson’s research papers to Dean, who sends them to Donnelly for analysis and authentication.  Donnelly determines that Woodson’s research is taking him in startling new directions.  She also determines the existence of Woodson’s secret attic room from Mayan hieroglyphics in his notes.

Dean sends Agents Banks and Green back to Woodson’s house to find and search the attic room.  Here the agents find:

- small clay Mayan idol
    
- more research papers
    
- travel receipts for Woodson’s trips to the Sanbourne Institute in Los Angeles (September last year), the Henry Armitage Library in Arkham in Massachusetts (mid-October to February this year), and Belize (late March)
    
- a small key and the contract for a safe deposit box at the First National Bank of Arkham.
    

Agents Banks and Green send the additional material to Donnelly (again via Dean).  Donnelly analyses the new information and has an interesting and nearly fatal encounter with the inhabitant of the Mayan idol before destroying the idol.

### Los Angeles - Early May, 1999

Donnelly and Agents Banks and Green visit David Cox, Assistant Director of the Sanbourne Institute of Pacific Antiquities, in the remote suburb of Santiago, three hours drive from Los Angeles.  Here Donnelly convinces Cox to part with photocopies of the same notes studied by Woodson when he came to the Institute in September last year.

After leaving the Institute, the team are followed by four men in a black sedan.  The driver of the black sedan loses the team when Agent Green turns their car around to confront the men in the sedan.  The team returns to its Los Angeles hotel rooms for the night.

After waking from a horrible dream about El Cacao just after dawn, Donnelly discovers that her room has been searched - little things are subtly out of place.  The searcher(s) have gone to a lot of trouble to conceal the search.  She informs Agents Banks and Green, who inform Dean.  Dean sends the team to Arkham.

### Arkham, Massachusetts

An hour’s drive out of Boston lies the dark little town of Arkham, a tiny suburb of Beverly.  Here Donnelly retraces Woodson’s steps through many arcane and dangerous texts while Agents Banks and Green try to do something useful.

The contents of Woodson’s safe deposit box at the First National Bank of Arkham are revealed to be further research papers, summaries of his readings from the Henry Armitage Library restricted collection, and an outline of his new book.  Donnelly is by now quite boggled at where Woodson was going with his research.

That night Donnelly meets “Kyle Woodson”, gets in the middle of a pitched battle between “Kyle Woodson”, Agents Banks and Green, two of the men in the black sedan now dressed as hotel waiters, and two creatures in the bathroom.  The outcome is Agent Banks killed in the line of duty, Agent Green critically injured, two waiters and “Kyle Woodson” missing, and three piles of unidentifiable greenish slime puddling in the bathroom.

The following night there is a highly unusual lightning storm over Vermont.  Analysis of satellite and “hurricane hunter” aircraft data shows that the lightning storm is not natural but has all the hallmarks of an upper atmosphere battle between two unidentified sides, each wielding laser pulse weapon technology.

Dean calls in the Marines for an operation in Belize.  Why Belize?  Just after Woodson returned to Belize, he called Donnelly and told her that extraterrestrials were present at El Cacao.  Donnelly put it down to overwork and the stress of being back in Belize after the events of 1994, and begged him to return home.  Woodson didn’t listen.

## Highlights

### Belize, Central America - Mid May, 1999

Belize is a British protectorate with a small English defence force.  As an ally of the United States, Belize will co-operate with US DEA “anti-drug” operations that extend into Belize.  This is the cover story for Dean’s investigation into Kate’s Lagoon, El Cacao, and surrounds - drug interdiction.  Dean directs _[[Michael-B#^2a3d68|Aaron Ford]]_ to Belmopan, Belize, to prepare the way for Colonel Snow and his team.

### Belmopan, capital of Belize

Ford’s instructions are to contact Andy Lee, DEA Belize, a Chinese ex-Hong Kong policeman now working with the Belize police drug enforcement group.  Ford has to talk Lee into obtaining official Belize support for a major US-backed raid on drug runners operating in northern Belize, specifically Kate’s Lagoon, El Cacao and surrounds.  Once he has support, Ford is to contact Donnelly, now also in Belmopan, and travel with her to Belize City.

Prior to leaving Belmopan, Donnelly contacts Program for Belize (PFB), a local archaeological and conservation organisation with links to National Geographic.  PFB supported the 1994 dig at Kate’s Lagoon, a half hour's drive from Orange Walk Town in the Chetumal Bay region of northern Belize.  Donnelly asks that someone from PFB meet her at the airport on the outskirts of Belize City.

### Belize City

Jackson Leroix is a Caribbean Creole archaeologist working for PFB.  Leroix meets Ford and Donnelly at the airport.  Donnelly can find out recent news regarding Kate’s Lagoon and surrounds from Leroix, plus whether Leroix saw Woodson recently.  Leroix can also assist Ford and Donnelly in hiring a helicopter to take them to Kate’s Lagoon.

Possible encounter with another Mi-Go pawn sent to watch the airport _(statistics on page 29 of “A Resection of Time”)_.

Before boarding the helicopter, Ford is to contact Colonel Snow and inform him of their ETA at Kate’s Lagoon.  Snow’s team will fly in from a US warship off the coast of Belize, timing a landing at Kate’s Lagoon shortly after Ford and Donnelly’s helicopter has departed the area.

Snow has detailed satellite maps of Kate’s Lagoon, El Cacao, the nearby Mennonite village and Orange Walk Town, and instructions to investigate and secure the site.  Once the site is secure, additional US military personnel will arrive from the warship to support the operation.

### PFB camp, Kate’s Lagoon

After an uneventful flight, Ford and Donnelly’s helicopter lands at Kate’s Lagoon.  Fifteen minutes later they are joined by two US helicopter gunships carrying Colonel Snow, Majors Ilsenburg and Morrissey, Captain Goldfarb, Jamie Mossman, and several other marines, including Gunnery Sgt. Anderson and Joe “Cthulhu” Marine.

In attempting to secure Kate’s Lagoon, the Mennonite village, and El Cacao, Snow’s team may cross paths with Mennonite villagers, Brothers of the Yellow Sign, Mi-Go pawns, and the Mi-Go themselves.

The team should go through the gate at El Cacao to the Mi-Go Station in the Peruvian Andes, destroying the gate behind them with explosives so the Mi-Go cannot use the gate to move between the Station and El Cacao.

### Mi-Go Station, Peruvian Andes

Once in the station, the team’s order of business should be:

- pass through the Xibalba gate
    
- destroy the Mi-Go breeding cave at Xibalba (flamethrowers, explosives, etc. will bring the roof of the cavern down)
    
- return through the gate to the Station, destroying the gate behind them with explosives so the Mi-Go cannot use the gate to move between the Station and Xibalba
    
- pass through the moon gate to the Mi-Go base on the dark side of the moon, using one thermonuclear device behind them to destroy the station.

### Xibalba, Chetumal Bay, northern Belize

In 500 BC Chetumal Bay was the site of Xibalba, the fearsome capital of the depraved and bloody Tlachan or I’winik Ka (“ee-WEEN-eek HA”) people, the “people of the fish”.  The Tlachan worshipped Chac, Ix Chel (the Mayan goddess of fertility and the moon) and their ancestors.  Around 400 BC other peoples in northern Belize rose and destroyed the Tlachan.

Chetumal Bay is also the site of a great cavern used by the Mi-Go of the Andes for their dry season reproductive rites.  The Tlachan called the cavern Ix Chel’s (“EESH chell”) cavern.

### Cerenarian Sea, north of the island of Mtal

Five black galleys manned by moon servants (men of Leng) close in on the Princess Ark, tired now from her journey. Outnumbered, outgunned, and slower than the galleys, the crew of the Princess Ark put up a mighty fight against their foes.

If all is looking hopeless, Donnelly and Cooper will pray for help. Perhaps Ilsenburg will as well. If so, a comet appears on the horizon that closes with the galleys and engages them. Unlucky galleys! _[[Abdiel|Abdiel]]_ and _[[Si-Ling|Si Ling]]_ combine to destroy them all. Abdiel takes great delight in slaying the black dragon spirits that emerge from the wreckage of each galley.

“So many find us only in dreams. But in dreams, we are most potent.”

After joining the crew of the Princess Ark, Abdiel reverts to “holy paladin” guise while Si Ling reverts to bird form and stays on Abdiel’s shoulder.

“On the nature of life, death, and the spaces in between.”

Abdiel sorts the Living (bodily in the Dreamlands) from the Dreamers (bodily on Earth) from the Dead (just passing through) from the Dreams (constructs created through use of the Dreaming skill).

Abdiel and Si Ling then leave. They have a ball to attend, and Abdiel has to find his bow tie.

### Voyage to the Enchanted Wood

The Princess Ark sails past Hlanith and into the mouth of the Oukranos River. Sailing upriver, she passes the city of Thran (to starboard) and sails under a bridge over the Oukranos. A little further upriver she stops, and the crew weigh anchor in the lee of the Enchanted Wood. Here the team from Earth disembarks. Cooper, Athos, and O’Shea remain aboard for the return voyage to Hlanith.

### The Enchanted Wood

After warnings to beware of the Zoogs, the team travels into the depths of the Enchanted Wood looking for a point of connection with the Waking World. After seeing many wonders (and a successful spot hidden) they see a very large bison standing in a clearing. Walking towards the bison is an elderly man waving a large, square, red cloth at it.

The team has unknowingly passed from the Dreamlands to the Bialowiecza National Park, Poland. The man is the park’s former Bison Master, Wlodzimirez Piroznikov, and he is attempting to get the bison to move out of his way so he can continue his afternoon walk.
