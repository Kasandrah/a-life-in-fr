## Involvement

Charles was furthering his bootlegging interests off the coast of North Carolina when the Silk Scorpion crossed paths with a party of academic researchers investigating the disappearance of the first British colony on Roanoke Island.

## Written by [[Ben-G|Ben G.]] (Dr. Alexander Notting)

My companion, _[[The-White-Magicians#Professor Edwin Occultree|Professor Edwin Occultree]]_, and I had an amazing adventure in the summer of ‘25.  Let me tell you about it.

Sir Andrew Buckinghamshire, “of Oxford University” (NPC) contacted our University to investigate the mystery of the first British colony in the United States - Roanoke Island.  He wanted us to find out what had happened to the missing settlers; find any descendants; establish what caused the failure of the colony; and establish whether the site is worth investigating.

A bit of investigation finds that he had been involved in a symposium of the occult at this university the year before.  Some things had occurred, and it was reported in the papers as weird things happening, but nothing definitive.

Background information that we find out is that the climate around Roanoke Island is sub-tropical, so at this time of hear it will be extremely humid.  Any investigations we do should not be too strenuous for too long, or we might suffer heatstroke.

But the history we find out about the colony is that on March 25, 1581, Sir Walter Raleigh found Roanoke Island and convinced others to settle there.  But it wasn’t until 1586 that some ships were gathered and their leader, someone named Grenville, and the Royal Governor, Ralph Lane, led them to the island.  The Indians who were there dealt with settlers in a friendly manner.

But the settlers were not friendly themselves.  At the end of that summer, Lane led the settlers in a pre-emptive attack against their friendly neighbours.  Oddly enough, the natives weren’t so friendly after that.  As time passed, the settlement was not so successful without the support of the natives, so Grenville took the settlers back to England (more like fled, really).  However, he left 15 men behind with a tiny boat.

The following year, 1587, 125 new colonists came under the leadership of White.  They were supposed to pick up the 15 men stranded/left behind and then they were to settle on Chesapeake Bay, but their ship left them on Roanoke Island.  These new settlers only found the remains of one of the 15 men.  It was assumed that the mainland natives had come and killed some, and the rest of the 15 had fled to another island and vanished.  Governor White returned to England for more supplies, but his return was delayed by the Spanish Armada for 3 years.

In 1590, he returned with 2 ships, but they found no one.  The houses previously built had all been torn down, and palisades had been raised.  In the gate of the palisade was scratched CRO in Roman letters.  There are 2 theories about what happened, but with no proof either way.  The settlers were attacked, and they fled to the south or to the north.  Apparently no one asked the Indians.

Since then, rumours of buried treasure on the island arose during Drake’s & Grenville’s privateer activities.

Another story out of the ordinary is that there have been reports of ghost sightings on the island.  A couple reported seeing the ghost of a little girl.

The natives of the island and area had been Powhatan Indians, but there are no known descendants of this tribe.  Not unexpectedly, as they wouldn’t have been happy with the Chesapeake settlements.

We contacted Indiana University about the word CRO and found that it meant “Sound/Voice With Power Behind It” in the Powhatan language.

We travelled down by rail to Elizabeth Town with some student assistants, and we tried to find out if there was a local history club or group that could give us some of the local tales of yore.  The few locals who knew something of the past had little before Elizabeth Town was begun.  So we then tried to charter a boat to the island.  A local fishing boat captain, named Mack, agreed to take us there when we reached an acceptable price for him.  Damn, I hate dickering.

As we approached the island, we passed a rich yacht, which Mack told us was the Silk Scorpion, and it was owned by the van Burens, a couple who were big in Elizabeth Town and elsewhere.

Our archaeological team was dropped off on the southern part of the island, and we could set up camp easily.  The first days were nothing out of the ordinary, but some of us suffered from nightmares and sleepless nights.  But then everybody was affected.  We explored the island, including the lighthouse.

We found it only 12 years old, but it had already been abandoned and the light panel was boarded up.  In the north-western end of the island, we easily found the Civil War settlement, but the surprise was a shack on the eastern side that, with a little risky work of breaking and entering, saw us find some illegal hootch.  A little further and we saw the Silk Scorpion at anchor.

Visiting the yacht, we met Charles van Buren and his wife, Sarah Bateman van Buren.  They had Sarah’s uncle and aunt also on board, Matthew and Ninian Patterson.  They were from Cornwall in England; you know.  They all seemed quite knowledgeable on esoteric things.  In the more relaxing environment, we found that this fabulously rich couple had some strange encounters themselves.

Back at the dig site, we found a bone where the palisade of the original settlement had to have been.  The bone itself didn’t excite us, but that it had been gnawed on by something larger than a dog concerned us.  I fear that the word cannibalism was thrown most injudiciously.  We had to finish this dig!  There must have been something planted by the palisade by the Indians to curse this place.  I know it doesn’t quite seem a logical jump, but the situation suited itself to such illogical things.

Working as quickly as we could, we thought we had narrowed it down to a certain area, and in fact we had just found a largish piece of Indian pottery that was sealed.  It looked like it was a major find!  As we discussed that maybe this held the curse, we were attacked by animals.  Animals of all sorts and all sizes came from the surrounding forests, and everyone ran.

I am afraid to say that we could do nothing but run by the creek into the sea, where the Silk Scorpion was now at anchor.  We lost 2 of our students.  As we were sailing back to Elizabeth Town, we destroyed this thing to destroy the curse.  Although reluctant to destroy something we were trained to search for, everybody felt it had to be done.

I fear we didn’t report things as truthfully as we used to.  The students were lost in a boating accident.  There was just a small incident with animals.  We only found these broken shards of Indian pottery.  The child's bones we had found inside were interred respectfully, but without being included in our report.  Roanoke Island is no longer haunted, and I am proud of being involved in that, but that too is not in our report.

Officially, we told Sir Andrew Buckinghamshire that we could not find out what happened to the settlers and that we believed that a major investigation would not shed any further light on the matter.  He was also told that any strange stories were sure not to happen again.
