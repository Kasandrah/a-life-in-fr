## Highlights

It must have been the look on my face that told Charles there was something seriously wrong.

“Sarah, what’s happened?”

He came over to where I was sitting and gently took Mairi's letter from my shaking hands.  I was barely aware of him as he stood close by me and read her hesitant script.  My mind slipped back to the horrors deep in the Arabian desert, to the journey that nearly claimed our lives and to the death of our beloved friend.  Mairi's letter brought back memories I had carefully buried.

Now some last remaining piece of that horror was reaching out to tear her mind apart with its claws, filling her head with nightmares and forebodings and sapping her strength.  I turned my attention to Charles and waited for him to finish reading the letter.

“We must help her, Sarah.  She’s our friend.”

I thought of Mairi's gentle face and knew we could do no less, but what if we had to go back there again, back to Iram of the Pillars?  “But Charles, the last time was so...”

I shuddered at the memory and was lost for words.

“Sarah, we must help her.  Mairi wouldn’t have sent us this letter unless she was desperate.  She was there with us, remember.  She, of all people, would know what she was asking of us.”

I agreed with Charles, but I raised one more objection, the most important one.  “We nearly died out in that desert; you almost lost your mind and Magnus lost his life.  Please Charles...”

“Sarah, all we have to do is contact this Rabbi that Mairi mentions at the end of her letter.  He is here in London.  We don’t have to go any further than that.”

But we had to go further than London - we had to return to Iram of the Pillars before we were finally done.  We experienced the strangeness of travelling through time and space via a magical two-way gate, and our reward for the hardships we suffered was to discover Magnus alive.  He returned with us to London with the power to dispel Mairi's nightmares.
