## Written by _[[Ben-G|Ben G.]]_ (Dr. Alexander Notting)

Sir Andrew Buckinghamshire received a request for help from a Miss Rebecca Haughton, a resident of Framingham, a small town near Boston.  What kind of help or the problem wasn’t specified, but that it involved her grandfather and something about the Civil War.  Even though he was told it required discretion, Sir Andrew still asked us to investigate this.

We asked the van Buren’s if they were interested, and found they were and that Matthew and Ninian had returned to England.

Before we go, we search the libraries for Framingham, which is cross-referenced with the Civil War. We learnt of the Framingham Vets Hospital that actually has a surviving member of the war.  This Edward Haughton must have been a drummer boy in the war.  There also is a quite famous Civil War Memorial for the Union Forces, which is in Framingham Cemetery on the outskirts of town.  Finally, Framingham sent 50 volunteers into the 23rd Massachusetts Regiment.  They were involved in the 1862 Peninsula Campaign and the Second Battle of Bull Run.

Unfortunately, in that battle alone, 27 of the Framingham volunteers were slain in the bloody conflict, and they were buried in the town cemetery.  A full list of names of the volunteers is taken down, but then we learn of 1st Lt Upton Wheedon, who was also from Framingham, led a charge that almost turned the battle around.  He rallied some men and led a charge where practically everybody but a handful died.  After the battle, he was promoted to captain.  But he had been wounded and lost his leg, and so was repatriated home.

Interesting!  All the news of the time said there was only one survivor, and that was Wheedon.  Dr Lucas B. Wight wrote a book, Our Boys In Blue: Framingham and The Civil War, and that too only talks of Wheedon surviving, even though it has photographs of both Wheedon and Haughton.  One written by Beasley Faraday, named Massachusetts and The Civil War, mentions Wheedon’s charge, but it also mentions Haughton had survived and had gone on to several other battles.

Contacting the Framingham Historical Society gave us the information the Memorial was funded in February 1866, originally by the mayor.  The following year, there was a small newspaper article on the Framingham Drummer Boy, but I stress it was small.  And surprisingly, just yesterday, their Civil War Memorial had been vandalised.

Travelling to Framingham, we met up with Charles and Sarah at the only hotel in town.  We brief them and decide to visit the Memorial first before settling in.  We find it cracked, which Sarah and Edwin say it looks like it was structurally done from within.  There’s some ooze coming from the cracks to support the idea.  I take a sample of the ooze.  While Edwin talked with the cemetery caretaker, Gerber Pender, to find out if he knew of anything.  The only new piece of information he gives us is that Upton Lamar Wheedon is still alive, but he is living as a recluse outside of town.

While Charles and Sarah return to the hotel, we proceed to Miss Haughton’s address.  From her, we find out that her grandfather was hospitalised in the Veterans Hospital just last year, but that he has been neglected while he has been there.  Edward has been more agitated since the vandalism, but he had said in the past that there was some evil out there.

So soon afterward, we proceed to the Framingham Veteran’s Hospital and get a Nurse O’Malley to let us in to see Edward.  From him, we find he is reliving a nightmare of Wheedon rallying everybody to attack a gap in the Confederates' line, but just as everybody attacked, Wheedon fled. Afterwards, the night nurse tells us the same story.

Returning to the hotel, we sent the goop sample back to the university to be examined, but no response is expected anytime soon.  Just for a constitutional walk, Edwin and I walked to the cemetery for a leisurely look around and then back.  But where Edwin sees absolutely nothing, I see dead bodies and winged things and more all over the Monument.  I admit I did lose hold of my sanity, but as I started to get a grip on myself I had to explain it to Edwin, and that set me off worse.

Ushering me back to the hotel, we all have dinner and have a big discussion on what we had found. We plan to confront Wheedon, for we believe it is he who is summoning whatever is happening.  Based on Charles and Sarah’s experiences, it may be necessary to shoot the summoner to halt the summoning!  Off-putting in the extreme, but what must be done must be done.

But after the dinner, we learn of how they became involved in these kinds of … adventures, really.  Charles begins with a story of South Yemen, and then they had a trip on the Orient Express, followed by a visitation by a Celtic Faerie Knight.  Sarah chipped in by pointing out to us that this one differed completely from the others.

It was continuing on from this interruption that we learnt it had been a pursuit of the Cup of Tristan and Ysolde.  I had been coping well with all this until they concluded this adventure with returning it to its rightful owners … Tristan and Ysolde!  By then, I had to retire for the night, for my head was spinning.

The following morning, Edwin goes for a constitutional to the cemetery again, but he finds the police have cordoned it off.  He eventually attracts the attention of the Chief of Detectives, a Luther Hardin, and convinces him of our relevant abilities by saying we’re from Harvard and Yale.

When I arrive, I go loopy again and I believe that’s where I gained my phobia of bats.  Edwin speaks with the caretaker again and finds out that at midnight, Gerber had heard a sound out there.  When he went out to have a look, he saw the monument glowing, and its surface was moving and doing “stuff”.

Ushering me back to Charles and Sarah, Edwin tells them of what had happened this morning.  In return, Charles tells us of the dangers of summoning.  If the summoner succeeds and can control it, then it is good for the summoner and bad for everyone else.  If the summoner succeeds and can’t control it, then it’s bad for everyone.

We travel to the Wheedon Estates out of town, and a Roderick Aspen answers the door.  We learned he had been a boarder there for 3 weeks, but he looked quite unrested.  While we have tea, we learn he has been having the same dreams as Edward Haughton!  While he is blathering on but not making much sense, Edwin mesmerises him and convinces him he has “seen” us out the door and that he wants to go to sleep now.

With the border asleep, we find stairs going both up and down.  We were told by Aspen that Wheedon was asleep upstairs.  We go downstairs and find a locked door.  Charles picks the lock, and we find a sleeping Wheedon.  He awakens and sort of confronts us, and after we try to talk to him, we say we’ll leave.  In fact, we split up and investigate some more.  Sarah and I check upstairs while Charles and Edwin check out the buildings out on the grounds.

Upstairs, we find a map room.  While there is a diorama of the 2nd Battle of Bull Run, I recognise that the actual landscape is the same as outside of the house.  There is also a wall-hanging of the same detail.  Most of the rest of the upstairs is unused bedrooms, and one library.  While I’m drooling over the very interesting collection, Sarah finds Wheedon’s diary.  It mentions his name and shows a map of a tunnel from near the house to the woods.  Looking out the window, we can work out that it is to the well house.

Outside, Charles and Edwin had checked the barn, where Edwin had almost fallen into a spiked pit trap, but then they tried the well house.  They go down the well and spot a secret door at the bottom.  We all gather and enter the tunnel heading back to the house, just as the map showed.  As we travel, hands from within the ground below us try to grab us.  We fight free and run on.  Going through the door, we find a book in a separate basement area.  Taking the book, we then find a doorway into the main basement area.  We need to regather ourselves and work out what we’re going to do.

Returning to the hotel, Edwin studies the book and finds out it is a Boden Dandee book named Corpus Modus.  It is a treatise on animating the dead by West African witch doctors.  It also talks about raising an effigy of hate.  Discussing this amongst ourselves, we think that Wheedon wants to change the past … to succeed in his charge and to lead it successfully.  But this is wrong.

Calling Matthew Patterson, we find out that we will need to kill the summoner and destroy the “totem” that is being used to summon the Effigy.  We all know that there is little time to spare.  It feels as if it is almost complete.  Gathering our tools and some weapons that Charles and Sarah seem to have, we make our return.

Driving back to Wheedon’s Estate, we enter a fog and as we coast near the house; we see giant figures moving in the fog.  As everybody squints to see, they are acting as stretcher-bearers, but they are not human.  Nobody has the desire to face these monsters, so we flee into the house. Inside, I have a feeling that Wheedon will be up in the map room.

Climbing upstairs, we find a youthful Wheedon.  While Edwin and Charles try to play along with the shockingly young Wheedon until the rest of us could get in a position to jump him.  But as we do, he goes berserk and starts throwing us around.  Both Sarah and I are flung out the room, while the others were struggling with Wheedon.  Then Sarah uses her shotgun.

Unfortunately, she failed to aim properly, and I was struck down from behind.  As I lay on the floor in shock, I saw that eventually Wheedon is clubbed down and I think it is Edwin who shoots the map-table.  Obviously this was the “totem” for then the house came alive.  I think they set fire to the house, or maybe a lamp was spilled. I fear I was not in a condition to pay close attention to this.  Charles and Edwin got me out, as Sarah found Aspen, and we all fled.

Because of this adventure, the vandalised monument has not been vandalised again.  Edward Haughton is having restful sleep once again, and is being treated better.  Personally, I’m having an extended leave of absence in a specialist hospital that Sarah has recommended as I heal both from the shock and the shotgun wounds.  She is very apologetic and is paying for my brief holiday, but I think I don’t want to be called to help for a long while.
