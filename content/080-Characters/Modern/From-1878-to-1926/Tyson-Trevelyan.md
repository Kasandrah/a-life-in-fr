# Baron Tyson Trevelyan

**Written by [[Ben-G|Ben G.]] (Tyson)**

Born: Delhi, India, 1890

Parents: Baron Robert Trevelyan (known as "The Colonel" to his children) and Lady Amelia.  Lady Amelia died of malaria in 1895.  The Baron remarried in 1899 to Lady Mary Northcote.

Siblings: Robert Jr. (1888-1915) and Katherine (1902-)

## Background

The Trevelyan family of Sussex has had a family Regiment since the Napoleonic Wars.  The regiment has been stationed in India since the Indian Mutiny of the Native Regiments of 1857.  Since 1870, the regiment has been stationed in the Punjabi State.  Due to the length of time spent in India, there are a number of family holdings spread through the State and in Delhi and Calcutta.

While Robby was fathers favourite, mother favoured me.  But when she passed on, I spent a lot of time with my friends … my Punjabi friends.  I was treated like one of their own.  For 3 years, the Colonel didn't care.  It was only when my brother left for England that I must be with him and my tutors.  Robby was sent to Sandringhurst to follow in the family tradition of serving in Her Majesty's army.  In due time, the year of the arrival of my new mother, I too was sent to Sandringhurst to follow in my brothers footsteps.  She may have tried to be nice to me, but I guess there wasn't time for me to stop resenting her for taking the place of mother.

Time passed slowly in Sandringhurst, especially since the only person I knew didn't want to be seen with me.  But I survived and graduated in 1908.  When Robby left Sandringhurst, he went straight back to India to the Regiment.  But, I was sent to a Fusiliers regiment in Oxford.  I guess coming first is more important …  But anyway, I didn't make many friends …  In fact, I guess I only made acquaintances.  I suppose that I wasn't outgoing but I felt so out of place in England and it was always so cold.  Ah well, whatever.

In 1911, I finally went back to India to serve as a Lieutenant in the Regiment.  By that time, my brother had already become a Captain (!?).  I finally met my sister, Kitty.  The one family member whose company I enjoyed.  Just as much as we were family, we were friends too.  Although it was a time of drills and military boredom, it was also a time of exploration of the unfamiliar and the strangely familiar.  I spent most of my time stationed in Jullundar in the Punjab, where I formed a number of attachments with many of the Brahmin families.  I also met some of my old friends, but I couldn't get the same camaraderie that we had before.  I took to taking my leave in many varied places, including a trip down to Singapore.

A trip which I deeply regret, for I caught the same disease that killed my mother.  I got Malaria soon after getting there.  Never having anything worse than a cold before, I know I didn't act too bravely but I always felt myself being calmed by mother.  Delirium, the doctors said, but I don't know….  I survived that hellish time, but it still likes to visit me occasionally.  A year after recovering, I took to going climbing.  You see … I found that I could restore at least my strength and my dexterity, if not my constitution.  I also found that I enjoyed pitting myself against nature.  That through such clean struggle, I could achieve things done by few before me.  Something that was Tyson, and not Trevelyan.

Everybody knows of the War, of course.  As soon as word reached India, our regiment was one of the many that was sent to fight in France.  Within 2 months of the Empire declaring war on Germany, we were on French fields.  Within 12 months, my brother died leading an assault.  Within 14 months, my father died of broken heart.  I know it was on an assault, but he wouldn't have …. Umm, yes … well, I survived.  Assault after assault, I survived.  I made it to Captain soon after The Colonel's assault.  But month after month, I found it harder and harder to convince the men to go over the top.  I kind of felt that was why I was promoted to Major by 1918, but I know it was because I had survived long enough while others fell around me.  Humph.  The War To End All Wars did, in fact, end.  And the regiment returned home ... to India, naturally.

For all that I was home, I still didn't feel all there.  The old drills seemed so out of place, like we were boys pretending to be soldiers.  The family regiment was now mine … I was second-in-command, but it was now only a matter of time before I would assume command.  I was now Baron Trevelyan.  I had been since the end of 1915, but now I had the time to experience it ... brood about it.

While we had been gone, there had been a rising number of protests against the Empire in India.  I didn't think much about that as an idea, but it did involve British forces in India.  Troops were used more to police and to suppress any violence.  In April 1919, I found my regiment involved in an incident at Amritsar.  I won't say much of this except to say that over 1,000 Indians were massacred needlessly and I keep thinking I could have stopped it.  I shocked the regiment and resigned my commission, and "Went Native".

I have become a Hindu.  Whether it was an act of atonement or that I felt that this was a better way does not matter.  I had spent most of my happiest times immersed in the Punjabi culture, so I found … contentment with my choice.  My stepmother has refused to accept my choice, but it is not her decision to make.  Kitty, as always, is comfortable with this.  But she has always shown an ability to easily switch from dealing with different people, whether the difference was race, religion or caste.

Although I had always been with, and had been treated as a Brahmin, I found that I spent large amounts of time with the priests.  I have learnt much of Hinduism, and much of the Veda and the mythology.  I have learnt more of how to deal with people and to make assessments of people.  I have travelled much through the north of India, and can mingle quite happily anywhere there.  I have even learnt a form of self-defence that doesn't involve using guns or knives.  And I still climb to … what, get away or to help find more of myself.
