## Appearance

Charles is very attractive - tall with dark curly hair and dark brown eyes and a mouth that curls upward on one side when he’s amused.  He likes to spend a good deal of money on his clothes and is always particularly well dressed.

## Interests

Charles makes his money as a bootlegger and smuggler, using the [[The-Silk-Scorpion|Silk Scorpion]] to transport large amounts of alcohol and restricted goods from Britain via Cuba to secret locations along the New York coast.

## Education

Charles was educated at home.  He learnt French, Latin, geography and history in the main, with a smattering of other subjects as suited his parents.  Since returning from the war, where he learnt a great deal of first aid, Charles has concentrated on developing skills in business and accounting.

## Background

Charles was born into a middle-class family in Boston.  Prior to WW1 Charles spent several years living in Japan, where he learnt Japanese language, history and swordsmanship.  He brought two katana swords back from Japan and maintains his considerable skill in using them.
### World War 1

Charles served as a pilot officer - Lieutenant (brevet Captain) - during WW1 and was awarded the Silver Star for his efforts in the conflict.  He joined the 90th Aero Squadron, organised on 20th August 1917 at Kelly Field, Texas.  The squadron was re-designated as the 90th Squadron (Surveillance) on 13th August 1919, and the 90th Squadron (Attack) on 15th September 1921.

The 90th Aero Squadron was posted to Garden City, New York from 5th to the 27th October 1917, then to France.  While in France Charles’ squadron was located at:

- Colombey-les- Belles from 20th November 1917
    
- Amanty from 19th April 1918
    
- Ourches from 13th Jun 1918
    
- Souilly from 20th September 1918
    
- Bethelainville from 29th October 1918
    
- Belrain from 15th January 1919
    
- Colombey-les-Belles from 18th January 1919
    
- Libourne from 25th January 1919
    
- St. Denis-de-Piles from 29th January 1919
    
- Libourne from 2nd February 1919
    
- Bordeaux from 10th to the 19th April 1919.
    

The squadron was attached to I Air Depot from 20th November 1917, I Corps Observation Group from 19th April 1918, IV Corps Observation Group from 13th June 1918, III Corps Observation Group from 21st September 1918, I Corps Observation Group from 30th November to December 1918, and Army Surveillance (later 1st Surveillance; 3d Attack; 3d Bombardment) Group from 1st July 1919 until the end of the war.

Charles saw combat in Sopwith 1, Salmson 2, Spad XI, and Breguet 14 aircraft while undertaking corps observation for the American I, III, and IV Army Corps, and French XVII Army Corps, from the 16th June to the 10th November 1918.  The 90th was posted to Hazelhurst Field, New York from 5th May 1919, where Charles left the squadron to rejoin civilian life.

### After World War 1

Charles returned to his family in Boston, then struck out again to make a success of himself in a variety of business opportunities, some of them not strictly legal.  Once Prohibition became a reality, Charles took to bootlegging and became very successful quickly.
