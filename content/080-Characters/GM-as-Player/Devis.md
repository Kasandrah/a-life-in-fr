# Devis

Yes, a lazy character choice on my part. I had a lot going on and [Wizards](https://www.dndbeyond.com/en) placing this half-elf bard example in the 3.0 Player's Guide made things a lot easier.

That said, Devis developed into one of the most intense RPG characters I've ever played.

## Appearance

![[dd_devis.bmp]]

**Devis**

>**Player's Note:** Devis became a half-dragon over the course of his original campaign.

6' tall, 200 lbs - Devis' hair is now copper-gold instead of white, his eyes have slitted pupils like a cat, his skin has a perpetual all over light sunburn, his lips are a little fuller and wider than before and he'll have to adapt to playing musical instruments with clawed fingertips.  Even his toes are clawed.

He has red/copper/gold scales across the top of his forehead, down around both his ears, and down the back of his neck.  He's less half-Elven and more Draconic by the day.  His body temperature has even gone up a few degrees.  Devis doesn't fully appreciate the effect of his enhanced appearance and personal charisma - think a reddish-gold Mick Jagger in studded leather.  Some people just can't keep their hands off him.

Devis has a magical tattoo - "GI" on his left butt cheek and "NI" on his right - encircled artistically and in a large heart shape by tiny flowers, hearts and vine leaves.  The whole thing covers his butt from the small of his back to the top of the back of his legs, and from hip around to opposite hip at the widest curve of the heart shape.

## Background

Although he behaves and reacts like a human, Devis grew up with elves.  His Elven mother was cold, distant, and uninvolved in Devis' life, but exceptionally beautiful and made music to move the soul. The two things that can easily be considered Devis' fatal flaws - great beauty and great music.  There probably isn't anything he wouldn't do to possess either.  Becoming a bard was a natural progression.

As a half-elf in an Elven community, he was always on the edge of things - didn't quite fit in with the others.  His mother considered him the inconvenient consequence of a moment of passion. However, she believed in taking responsibility for her actions and raised Devis as her own.  In this she considers herself better than his wandering human father.  Adventurers!

Devis didn't fare any better with his first love - not unrequited, but an Elven royal heir was an unwise choice for a bastard half-elf. Like I said - great beauty and great music will get Devis every time.  _[[Shandell|Shandell]]_ possessed both.

To Devis' delight, Shandell responded in kind but neither could withstand the combined censure of the rest of the community.  A long, sensuous summer became a nightmare of public restraint and carefully planned secret meetings that kept them both on a knife edge.  Devis couldn't stand it any longer and left, none too gracefully, to pursue life on his own terms.

An over-confident young half-elf with a broken heart makes mistakes, and Devis made a biggie not long after leaving his homeland.  Travelling alone in dangerous territory, Devis was captured by a small band of orcs.  They liked him so much they shared him around for three days before Shandell, hot on Devis' trail with an Elven war band, tracked him down.  Nothing like an enraged Elven paladin with company to brighten up an orc's day.

Shandell's reputation as a scourge of the orcs was made that afternoon, as six orc skins were pegged out on the ground.  All that Devis remembers is that it was Shandell that found him - naked, filthy, badly abused and injured, tied up and just barely alive.  Devis managed (just - but I think he's kidding himself) to deal with events, but he could never reconcile the ugly expression on Shandell's beautiful face with any future together.  After that it really was over as far as Devis was concerned.

Shandell took Devis back to their secret place in the Elven woods.  After several weeks here recovering some measure of sanity, Devis convinced himself that Shandell couldn't bear to touch him.  Had Devis but asked he would have learned differently.  Elves understand suffering - it finds expression in the poignancy of their music, music that entrances, heals and strengthens the listener.  Shandell was giving Devis space and time in which to heal.  Devis, accustomed already to too much space in his relationships with elves, misinterpreted this and chose again to leave, again without discussing it with Shandell.  This time, wisely, Devis left in the company of other elves.

Devis' travels took him to Greyhawk - safety in numbers.  The rest - as they say - is history.

## First Dream - Written by _[[Simon-M|Simon M.]]_

Devis has experienced the dream as confusing and disturbing fragments that have built up into a comprehensible whole over a number of nights.

Normally elves experience 'the contemplation' instead of sleep, they relive moments of great importance and aspects of the days activities that were unusually good or bad.  This allows them to fit them into their life views and learn from their mistakes.  Elven religious doctrine is that the gods of the Seldarine participate in this process, showing their followers directly what is acceptable and what needs to be improved.  It is believed by scholars that it is this process that truly moulds the Elven psyche.

Half elves tend to experience one of three possible options:

1. Sleep. (Those who embrace their human heritage)
2. Contemplation. (Those who embrace their Elven heritage)
3. A mixture of contemplation and sleep. (Those who try to combine both heritages).

Devis tends to sleep like a human.

(First Person)

He exists in darkness, although he does not know.  At first he floats - warm, secure and content.  Gradually he acquires a new sensation, weight.  Sensations literally shake his world; vibrations soothe him and keep him company.  Hunger.  It comes slowly but inexorably.  Souring his contentment, wearing away at his security, stealing his warmth.  Anger.  It flares in response.  Its blaze provides all the warmth he could ever need.  All need for protection vanishes in the warmth of the confidence it inspires.  Deep inside the contentment fades to be replaced by exhilaration.

Instinct directs his anger against what until then has been a comforting presence, the wall about his world.  It resists which only causes the anger to flare even higher.  Again he smashes at the wall of the world and again, yet still it denies him.  Anger turns to Fury, far too great to be contained … by anything.  To survive he thrusts the fury from him and against the wall.  With a flash of heat inconceivable, he is launched from the remnants of the old world into the new.

He is assaulted by sensation.  Dozens of senses bombard him with information he does not know how to interpret.  Pain and confusion cut at the anger that sustains him, for a time it seems that he will fail.  Again it is instinct that saves him, allows him to ignore everything else and pursue the scent of food.  Instinct that allows him to make his first kill and take his first meal.

Afterwards he retreats to a fissure to sleep and allow his mind to organize itself.  In many ways it is the most profound thing that he will ever do.  The confused newborn settles to sleep, but what arises a day or so later is far more.

(Third Person)

Eyes of ruby flame open suddenly.  They sweep across the extent of the chamber, even as his other senses supply him with information on the surrounding area.  Creatures approach!  From the sound and the smell they are of the same type as his first kill.  Idly he looks down at the cracked and broken bones; all that remain of his first kill.

He rises and jumps outwards from the ledge towards the chamber floor, wings unfurling and beating once to arrest his fall.  His every movement is graceful, disturbing in a creature of his size and apparent power.  Moving quietly despite his size he approaches the entrance of the cave and then steps out to face three creatures carrying wooden clubs and torches.

The creatures react with evident surprise as he steps into the cavern, his tiny crimson scales gleaming in the light.  He stalks forward, that disturbing agility holding his quarry frozen.  Suddenly his wings snap out to the sides, startling the creatures and causing them to cower together for a split second.  In that instant his mouth opens and a blast of flame covers all three creatures that scream in pain and fear.  Before they can react the area goes pitch black.  From within the darkness come screams of pain and panic followed by the sounds of combat.

Shortly these give way to the sounds of ripping flesh and splintering bone.

For Devis' information:

1. The three creatures were ogres.
2. Red dragons do not get the ability to create darkness, except as spells.  That was not a spell.
3. Dragons can 'see' in darkness.
4. Ashardalon was medium-sized, i.e. human-sized, which is normal for a wyrmling red dragon.
5. Ashardalon is the child of Timat, goddess of the coloured (evil) dragons, and her Red Dragon consort.

## Second Dream - Written by _[[Simon-M|Simon M.]]_

![[dd_ashardalon.jpg]]

**Ashardalon**

He moves quietly through the dark tunnels.  The only sound of his passage is the occasional rasp of a scale against stone or the click of a talon on rock.  Yet to senses like his even this quiet passage is too loud, he is constantly astounded that the food does not hear him, smell him or just feel him.  For he can feel them.  Always he is aware of the other life around him, the sounds that echo through the mountain, the smells, but mostly the feel of life - little glows, some near, most far away yet always there at the edge of his awareness.

He has learnt much since he came into this world that he knows as the feeding ground.  Not that he thinks of it in words, for he does not really use language.  Some of the prey have used sounds and he has deduced enough to realize that they are able to transfer information with them.  Often he has imitated the sounds, at first this caused the prey to flee, which puzzled him until he realized that he was warning them of danger.

But by spying on them when they thought themselves safe he has learned enough to use their own sounds against them.  He has learned a series of sounds that attract them to him if he waits out of their sight and calls.  He has also learnt sounds to panic them.  The best of these is the sound they use to represent him.

Normally however he avoids places where the prey are concentrated, for although they can not hurt him they will run from him and then he must work harder to find enough food.  But now he hunts for something other than food and to find it he must go into the inhabited areas.

As he lay in his lair, drowsing, yet still aware of the feeding ground, he detected a new and intriguing sound.  At first he had simply lain quietly and listened, not even stirring until hunger finally drove him out into the feeding ground.  He quickly found and killed a passing pack of the strange tough ones.  They are tasty enough once past the metal skins and he eats his fill for he is about to start a new hunt, one that will continue until he has found this new sound.

He has hunted for a considerable time, through dark corridors that echoes this tantalizing sound back and forth so that even his hearing is deceived into dead ends and false trails.  But he is relentless and each setback simply makes him more determined to find this sound and finally he is drawing close.

His senses searching, trying to work past the sound that is now nearly overwhelming, he creeps down the dark corridor.  Gradually the darkness gives way, almost grudgingly, to a grey twilight and then as he proceeds to a white glow that streams out of an opening in the wall.  It is from this opening that the sound comes and he can feel the glows of several creatures within the room.  He continues forward slowly, since he has discovered that sudden movements draw the eyes and often cause the prey to bolt.

Renark shifts against the rough stone of the wall in a futile attempt to find some way of sitting that does not hurt his wounds.  His squad had been forced to seek shelter here when the never sufficiently damned Baklunish had caught up to them on the mountain slopes above.  He had personally killed an entire four man section of the scum, but in the end there had just been too many.

If not for Lord Galthais they would all have died.  He had thrown a bolt of lightning right into the middle of the enemy formation that had killed many and caused the commander to jump down a steep slope in order to avoid the blast.  By the time that the enemy had rallied it was too late.  The battered Suel squad had retreated into one of the deep caverns that covered the area and Lord Galthais used a spell to bring down the roof, sealing them in and their enemies outside.

Of the original ten man squad only four remained.  Saln the Ranger, Lar the scout, Lord Galthais their leader and of course Renark himself.  It was impossible in this sunless world to be sure of exactly how much time had passed, but Renark knew that they had to have been underground for at least a week.  The food was running low and the only water they could obtain was from his spells.  He had trained to be a cleric until he transgressed one of his orders numerous rules and they threw him out.  Actually he considered himself lucky for if the war had not flared up again he would have ended as the monthly offering.  Still he could make enough water for the four of them, and under his ministrations their many wounds were slowly healing.  At least they had light for Saln and Lord Galthais had carried light stones that provided a clear white light when uncovered.

Gradually Renark became aware of a quiet melody, although he was sure that it had been there for some time before he noticed it.  Without turning he knew that it was Lord Galthais playing his sitar, as he had much of the time that they had been here.  He claimed that it helped him think and though Renark was certain that it was unwise to make noise down here he was certainly not going to complain.  Lord Galthais was considered strange even by other nobles of the Suel Empire, the mildest of whom would have you slowly skinned alive over a period of weeks if they thought it would advance their own position.  His blood was as pure as any in the empire and his family were much favoured by the emperor and court, even other nobles trod carefully around him.  Oh yes and he was an accomplished combat mage, able and even eager to kill with but a word.

Habit instilled as part of his training caused Renark to check his surroundings when in dangerous areas and according to the information from the kobold they had caught and tortured yesterday that was the perfect description of this place.  The kobold swore that something lived near here, something that killed and ate even groups of Ogres.  That had impressed even Saln, he and Lord Galthais had discussed the possibility of trying to find this creature and take it back to the empire.

It was just chance, the bad luck he always complained of, that caused Lar to move to look out the doorway.  He looked out the doorway and screamed, whether in warning or just in terror was unclear.  Something dragged him into the darkness and the sound of his scream was cut off with a dreadful suddenness, replaced by a wet crunch.  Then his headless body, fountaining gouts of blood, flew back into the room.  It smashed into Renark who had just managed to stager to his feet and knocked him sprawling.

As he tried to free himself from the body that had once been a companion the room was rocked by the blast of a lightning bolt and then a roar that seemed to freeze his blood in his veins.  Pushing the body off him Renark saw two eyes, pits of flame each the size of a mans fist regarding the room with a blazing malevolence that held him frozen with terror.  Saln had somehow gotten to the left side of the doorway and now he darted through to strike a blow at those eyes.  He jumped back his sword stained with dark blood.

The creature followed him into the room with a sinuous grace that belied its size and the light revealed what they faced.  A dragon, a red dragon!  It was as tall as a man although far more massive, covered in fine red scales that had obviously been penetrated to the side of its right eye.  Fear radiated from it, an unnatural fear that made shook Renark to the core.  The head turned to follow Saln and the mouth opened wide, revealing rows of razor sharp teeth, as it released a blast of fire towards the Ranger.  He tried to dodge but slipped on Lar's blood and the fire engulfed him, turning him into a living bonfire as his clothing combusted.

Lord Galthais was casting a spell even as the ranger rolled out of the way and another blast of lightning smashed into the creature.  Again it roared although it looked barely effected, but now its attention was on the mage who looked surprised at the spells lack of effect.  Almost casually the dragon reached out a clawed hand and battered the mage across the room to hit the wall with a sickening thump.  Gathering his courage Renark charged the dragon, his sword held in front of him like a lance but the blow failed to find a way between the scales, instead bouncing harmlessly off the iron hard surface.

Saln, his clothing still afire and his skin already blistered and weeping, attacked the dragon again.  His sword slid between the scales on its shoulder and the dragon roared in pain, then turned to snap at this persistent adversary.  Saln danced nimbly back out of range and stabbed again towards its eye causing the dragon to withdraw its head.  Renark struck at the creature again and this time his sword dug in.  Although the blow would have killed a strong man, it had little noticeable effect on the dragon.

Suddenly the dragon spun in place, to strike at Saln with both claws and then its mouth.  Saln dodged the right claw, but the left claw caught him and tossed him into the air where the dragons mouth closed with a snap on the lower half of him.  He screamed briefly before the top half crashed down to lie on the floor in a spreading pool of blood, his clothing and hair still smouldering.

Renark meanwhile had been hit by the creatures tail as it spun and knocked with bruising force to the floor where he lay momentarily stunned.  As he lay there he saw Galthais rise to his feet, his left arm hung limply at his side, twisted at an unnatural angle, but he was chanting words of power his face intent.  The dragon turned to face the mage.

Eyes of flame briefly met eyes of pale jade.  Galthais bowed to the dragon, a slight smile on his face and then green energy surged up around the mage obscuring him from sight.  Moments later the energy disappeared but the mage was gone.

For a time the dragon regarded the place that the mage had stood, then it turned towards Renark who screamed and charged, his sword once more outstretched.  He barely saw the paw coming and certainly had no chance to dodge.  Darkness claimed him.

1. Devis shares the Dragons senses during the dreams and it is overwhelming.  The dragon sees about twice as well as a half elf in normal and low light conditions.  In darkness he can see at least 200 feet and even in magical darkness he can track creatures within 60 feet by a combination of hearing and scent.
2. Every sensation is more intense and there are several senses that he is barely aware of that are entirely strange.  One is indeed an awareness of life, its presence, nature (sentient or not) and strength (level / hit die) although it is not as precise as vision in terms of locating the creature's position.
3. The dragon can cause magical darkness that blocks even its sight, although its other senses more than compensate, up to 3 times per day at will.  His lair has changed since he took residence, it is noticeably more pleasant for him to be in the lair than outside of it.  By willing it so he can create areas that other creatures actively avoid although he finds them comforting.  These fade unless he renews them.
4. The sounds are clearly identifiable to Devis as music although the instrument is unknown.  The music is vaguely sinister and arouses thoughts of violence.  It is not quite Bardic, but is clearly related.
5. These are Duergar Dwarves.  The same race that killed Erky in the halls of Durgeddin the Black.  They are emaciated and nasty-looking, with grey hair and skin.  Most are bald (even the females), and they dress in drab clothing that is designed to blend into stone.  They can turn themselves invisible which is useless against the dragons senses, and enlarge themselves.
6. The Suel used specially trained hunter / killers in their war with the Baklunish.  They were known as rangers and were the origin of that class.  Indeed it is tales of the Suel Rangers, eventually becoming legend and myth, that inspired and kept alive the fear and distrust that many of the common people feel for Rangers.
7. The Suel Rangers were cold, efficient and remorseless killers.  Trained from childhood to hunt and kill their own race (humans), they were very good at it.  They often combined their ranger skills with monk training, allowing them to move more quickly and survive the rugged border lands between the Suel / Baklunish empires.
8. A saving throw involves trying to get out of the way, which the dragon did not do.  Instead the lightning coursed over its body without doing anything substantial in the way of damage.
