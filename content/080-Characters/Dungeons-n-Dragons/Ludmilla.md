# Ludmilla

![[dd_ludmilla_02.jpg]]

**Ludmilla Vladimirovna Dangov**

## Written by _[[Jenny-H|Jenny H.]]_

>**GM's Note:**  This is a play-by-mail for Ludmilla.  It establishes the relationship between Ludmilla and Meyric, the Blue Dragonmage who she rescued from the magical Knight of the Traldar, bane of dragons.

Ludmilla checked everything compulsively.  The dragon statuette was carefully wrapped for travelling.  Also packed were a couple of stunning blue gowns from Vlad's castle, courtesy of a dead mage, other more practical clothes, jewellery, and makeup.  All the necessities of a holiday romance.  Her long black hair was brushed so well it gleamed.  Her makeup was immaculate.  Her brown, fur-trimmed travelling jacket and trousers looked great.  Why was she so nervous?  Meyric had been just a one-night stand, a pleasant end to a nice evening.  That's all.  So why did she feel so excited and worried at the same time?  As long as he didn't have someone with him when she arrived!

After the teleport was completed Ludmilla sent her magical owl named Meyric (after the one who gave her the owl) down the passageway.  The owl would provide Meyric with an early warning of her approach, if he was there, and would warn her of any danger ahead.  She followed the owl cautiously down the passageway and stopped at the entrance to the main cavern.  "Anyone home?" she called out cheerfully.  Her voice echoed around the empty cavern unanswered.  Ludmilla sighed as disappointment settled over her.

## GM's Reply

She began the long walk to the far end of the cavern where she knew the throne to be, her way lit by softly glowing orbs hanging far overhead.  As she approached the throne, the soft sand under her feet gave way to sandstone.  The throne and its base were made of sandstone, all the colours of the sun.  These colours ran throughout the cavern and were fired into a perpetual dawn by the light from the magical orbs above.  Her owl was perched on the back of the throne, blinking at her in the soft orange light.

When she had first seen Meyric he had been lounging in this throne, a small smile curling the corners of his mouth.  It took concentrated effort on her part to resist the magnetic pull of his attraction and stay with the business at hand: the recovery of the Knight of the Traldar.  That business led to her and her companions saving his life, though she alone had stood between him and the mighty, magical, dragon-slaying Knight.

Meyric had revealed himself to be a Dragonmage.  He had stolen the Knight to protect his kind.  Ludmilla and her companions had followed him north at the request of the Church of Karameikos, who claimed the Knight as their own.  Here in the cavern, their confrontation with Meyric was interrupted by another Dragonmage, a woman with fiery red hair.  She was bent on destroying Meyric, and in the ensuing battle, both changed into their dragon forms.  Ludmilla and her companions released the Knight, who despatched the red dragon.  When the Knight turned on the exhausted Meyric, Ludmilla stood between them and convinced the Knight not to slay him.  This earned her and her companions Meyric's gratitude.

As Ludmilla put her pack on the floor she remembered the warmth of his hands sliding over her skin.  At the end of the evening she had gone willingly into his embrace.  In a smaller, more private cavern hung with tapestries and in a magnificent four-poster bed, she discovered he was a man, with a man's desires and a man's skills.  However, he was far older than he appeared and had the advantage of great experience.  It was a memorable night.  They had parted amicably the next day.  He had shown no desire to follow her, and she had not asked.  That had been their understanding from the start.  Ludmilla sighed again, stuffed her hands in her pockets, and wondered what to do next.

As she stared at the empty throne she felt a sudden breeze from behind stir the sand and lift her hair about her.  The sound of great leathery wings folding caused her to turn around, but his transformation was too quick for her to see.  Meyric walked towards her, all animal grace, clothed like a prince and surrounded by an aura of power that almost rippled the air between them.  The same small smile she remembered curled the corners of his mouth.

He strode up to her and, without speaking, took her in his arms, one hand at the base of her spine holding her tightly against him, his other hand gently pulling her head back by her hair.  She barely had time to gasp his name before he bent and covered her mouth with his, taking her breath away with his passionate kiss.  After many moments Ludmilla's knees gave way, but he picked her up and without breaking his kiss carried her to the small cavern hung with tapestries.

## Written by _[[Jenny-H|Jenny H.]]_

Ludmilla lay in bed, snuggled deep into the luxurious comfort, Meyric's arm wrapped possessively around her.  In sleep, he looked at peace and fully human.  She should be asleep as well, but she had a decision to make, and it was not her nature to delay difficult choices.  This one in particular would affect her entire life's direction, and she wanted it made and over with, preferably before she left in the morning.

It was not an easy decision.  She loved Meyric with a passion she hadn't known she was capable of.  He was a wonderful lover, but it was more than that.  He was a fascinating person.  The trouble was, he was so hard to get to know.  Either because of natural reserve or what he was, Ludmilla wasn't sure.  To stay with him meant changing her whole life, and she wasn't ready for that yet.  She needed to know how he felt about her.  Was she just a pleasant bed partner or more?  She wouldn't be satisfied for long with a one-sided relationship, and if she chose wrongly, it would be too late to change.

She slipped out of bed, careful not to disturb him, and found her well-worn pack of cards, laying them out in the familiar cross pattern, concentrating on the future.  Queen of Clubs - that was a good sign.  The reading was a strong one, with her symbol in the central position.  She was very much involved with events.

Five of Diamonds - renown and wealth.  A positive card, and certainly true of her life so far.  Ten of Diamonds - always it came back to the Artefact, and very soon, brought about by found treasure.  King of Clubs - Meyric, part of her future, bringing the Ace of Hearts - true love.  Ludmilla played with the cards thoughtfully, straightening them compulsively.

Her hand stopped over the King of Clubs.  It wasn't Meyric's card, she suddenly realised.  His was the King of Hearts.  Was this a new person altogether?  But she'd thought it was Meyric.  As she'd tried to explain to Meyric, reading the cards was more by feel than scientifically.  She tried to think through the implications.  If it was Meyric, then it was a Meyric changed by troubles.  Perhaps the Ace of Spades earlier had been his.  Which meant it didn't mean death at all, but death of something else.  Or grave danger.

If it took great danger of change to make Meyric truly hers, then she wasn't interested.  It must be someone else entirely, some as yet unknown person.  Or she was too tired and confused to read the cards properly.  That must be it.

Ludmilla scattered the cards suddenly, mixing them together.  Then, from force of habit, she gathered them up, shuffled them briefly, and put them away.  She returned to bed, less certain of the future than when she'd left.  Meyric was awake and waiting for her, folding her in his arms.  She responded passionately.  Let the future worry about itself.  She had only a short time more of this.

Much later, Ludmilla stirred sleepily and rolled over to snuggle closer to Meyric.  The place he had been was warm, but empty.  She opened her eyes.  He wasn't in the room.  She stretched lazily.  In the month she'd been there, they had been together constantly.  A self-satisfied smile spread over her mouth as she contemplated just how constantly.  He was a very satisfying lover. She had tried to get to know the rest of him as well, but there was a core of reserve that was hard to break.

Ludmilla pushed back the bedclothes, pulled on a robe that was discarded by the bed and padded off to find Meyric.  He was sitting in his throne staring into the distance.  He didn't turn at her approach, but continued to stare, a light frown creasing his brow.  Ludmilla wrapped her arms around her lover, wishing she could shut out the world and its worries.

"Don't you think it's about time you told me what this is about, my love?" she asked softly, the endearment passing her lips without her realizing it.

## Ludmilla and Meyric

Ludmilla jerked awake. Her head had been slowly sinking towards the now mostly rolled scroll. Time to finish. She was coming to the conclusion that searching the records for information on dragonmages was pointless anyway. What she had found was based on rumour and innuendo and in some cases clearly contradicted what she already knew. And given she knew a dragonmage rather well, she was inclined to trust her own experience over… who was it this time? Some scribe recording the ramblings of Magus Speculum Spectrum. A pseudonym if ever she’d seen one. No, she’d have to solve her dilemma some other way.

Once Ludmilla was back in her rooms, she felt strangely wide awake. She heated a mug of mulled wine and sat in front of the fire to think. She felt trapped between a rock and a hard place. If she refused Meyric’s offer, she’d regret it. And when she was with him, she felt like she could agree to anything. If it had been almost anything else, she would have by now. But if she agreed she would lose everything that was her. And that would make her boring. And she’d probably lose him anyway. There had to be a way she could be both cleric and dragonmage. There had to be.

The well-worn cards scattered on the floor caught her eye. The servants knew better than to touch them and they still lay where Ludmilla had thrown them in disgust. She sighed, so far her readings had been as useful as her research, but she couldn’t expect the servants to keep cleaning around them. She carefully gathered them and put them on the side table. Time for bed.

Then she turned back. When the cards failed her it was because she was asking the wrong questions. Perhaps less ambitious questions would work. Ludmilla shuffled the cards and asked simply what her next step should be.

The cards this time were new and confusing. A young man had brought about this situation. Meyric? Or someone she had not considered or not yet met. But the other cards were all completely insignificant. Small dangers, small rewards, small people. They were the answers. Not the artifact, not Meyric, not her. Insignificant things brought by insignificant people to counter something someone had done a long time ago.

She shuffled the cards again. But why? The same item card appeared in the centre of the reading. It was a stronger reading now, with her signifier. She had to find an item, an insignificant looking item, but which held the key to her future. There was some danger, nothing great. No, not even that, just a small effort and a lot of patience. Not her strong suit.

But how could such a minor thing be so important to her? A confusing array of cards leapt out of the deck. Meyric’s card in connection to another woman in his past. The red dragon? And other people. Men, an older and a younger man. The same one who had appeared in the first reading. Did she have to do what Meyric had done to become a dragonmage? But who were these other people? They brought success.

Ludmilla started to shuffle the cards again, then stopped. No, she had already pushed further than she could understand. Any further would negate what she had learnt so far. Small steps. Small questions. Patience. That would solve the riddle.

The archbishop was looking for someone to go to Threshold to look after the next batch of young shorn ones. It was time to clear out White Lodge again. It was a nuisance. Not pleasant enough a location to set up a permanent settlement, just strategic enough to be unwise to let be taken over by the Dark. It was a job for a much junior cleric, but it fit the reading. Small danger, small rewards, small people. It was a start.

Ludmilla yawned, suddenly sleepy now that she had decided what to do. There was a flapping at the window, and she reached over to let Meyric the owl settle on her wrist. She stroked his head. “I think we’ll go on a little journey tomorrow.”

___

Ludmilla yawned and stretched. More scrolls. She hated them. Give her a mace to wield and a skeleton to cleave, and she was happy. She preferred learning by doing, not by reading. But this was something she couldn’t solve by action.

Back to Ivan the Under-Bishop of Olenek’s reports on the transgressions of his charges, reported in the most excruciating detail and destined to be a primer of mischief in the wrong hands. At least it was giving her a few laughs as she remembered her own time as a novice. A welcome relief from the reports of grain and meat supplies that someone had felt necessary to keep.

Ludmilla yawned again, but she couldn’t afford to stop. Selenka the Loremistress was already becoming more insistent about getting her hands on these scrolls. She skimmed a report of one Luka, a cow, a bucket of water and an older novice and went onto a complaint about the studies of Petros the Brown.

Her hand froze over the words and shook slightly. She read them again. And again. Then, in a flurry of movement, she tipped the remaining scrolls from the chest and unrolled them, hoping against hope that the one she needed was there. Petros had discovered a way to enhance the trace of dragon blood in his veins and was becoming a dragon himself. And it was happening so slowly that he was well advanced in the attempt before Ivan had realised what he was doing. The ability of Petros to continue in his clerical duties without pause or incident confounded Ivan. It was well known that those few who remained from the interbreeding with dragons centuries before, who chose their dragon side, were unacceptable to serve the Immortals.

There it was. The scroll Ivan had confiscated from Petros. A large, elaborate eye stared out of the vellum at her. Was it possible? Had it been like calling to like? Ludmilla placed her index fingers gently at the outer corners of the eyes and spoke the command word that would reveal any heritage of dragon in her. The eye stared back at her, unchanged. She poured the strength of her will and longing into staring back at it, but it remained as she had first seen it. A single tear splashed on the page, making the eye look like it was weeping with her.

She let the scroll curl up and then stared at her fingers. She had been concentrating so hard on the eye that she hadn’t noticed the gentle golden glow around them. Ludmilla clapped her hands over her mouth to contain the shout of joy that threatened to explode from her. It was possible!

She read the rest of the report. The artifact that Petros had been using to effect the change had been removed and sent to the Temple in Arakan for safekeeping. The artifact that had been in her readings all along. The key from White Lodge probably opened wherever it was being held. Ludmilla felt like dancing.

Her newest novice needed more experience as a cleric. Arakan was now in the wilderness. A perfect excuse to do some more exploring with the shorn ones.

## Things to Consider

Dragon Disciples, according to the rules, need to be able to cast Arcane spells. Half-Dragons can be clerics (which is the opposite to what we’ve been saying.)

As Meyric is a Dragon, not a Half-Dragon, the object is to turn into a Dragon, not a Half-Dragon.

Pending replies to the above, let’s assume that both Half-Dragons and Dragonmages can’t be clerics. But what if the artifact by-passes the problem completely? It could be something that simply enhances the existing ancestry of a character, slowly turning them into something else. This sets up the reason for Christian’s character turning into an Animalist. When it explodes, the proximity of the character determines how big a blast (= how quickly the change) they absorb. Some careful party order required here!

That gives us two options: either as far as Ludmilla is concerned, the 10th Level of Dragon Disciple actually turns her into an immature Dragon. Or the artifact exploding bypasses the whole problem and turns her into an immature Dragon. In game terms there would probably be no difference - as an immature Dragon she has the same stats as a Half-Dragon, but with the potential for Dragon levels. The extra abilities of a Dragon can be gained as Feats as she progresses levels. Or she could trade in the extra one or two levels for them. She stays a Cleric because Gold Dragons have some clerical connection (they can cast _bless_ spells, after all). So far she doesn’t have a particular patron, but afterwards she could become the Cleric of a particular Traladaran Immortal, possibly ignored or forgotten until now.
