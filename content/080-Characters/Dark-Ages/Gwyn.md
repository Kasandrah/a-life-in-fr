# Gwyn (Guinevere)

**Written by [[Jenny-H|Jenny H.]] (Gwyn)**
## Circa 515 AD ...

Guinevere of ? was a fairly typical dutiful daughter, except that she was something of a tomboy.

Growing up with a long-dead mother and several brothers, she tended to want to do the kinds of things they did.  She was adored and pampered and let go her own way to a great extent.  If she wanted to play at doing knightly things, then what harm could it really do?  Nothing really serious, of course, but they didn't know that Guinevere practiced on her own.  (And who was going to say no to the lady of the household?)

Guinevere in due course was betrothed to Owain, the son of an allied lord, and whom she adored.  Despite her foibles, she would probably have lived a perfectly normal life, apart from an incident that changed her life forever.  One day she was captured by an enemy of the family with the intention of ravishing her.  Only she neatly chose the right moment to take him by surprise, beat him over the head with the chamber pot, tie him to the bedpost and make good her escape, disguised as a servant.

She arrived home, expecting a hero's welcome, only to find out that nobody believed her.  They had known about the kidnapping, but neither her father nor her brothers believed she had escaped unscathed.  Her story was regarded as a need to pretend that it hadn't happened.  Told that her betrothed would never accept her now (in fact, she believed he had said as much), her only option was to retire to a convent.

Guinevere was incensed.  If she had been a man, they would have believed her story.  If she were a knight, her word of the events, however improbable, would never have been doubted.  And so she conceived another improbable action.  She cut her hair, tied up her breasts and disguised herself as a knight.

Claiming to be Gwyn, she set out to achieve heroic deeds of valour and glory.  The only trouble was that, given her size and relative strength, this was a hard task.  Whenever she did defeat a person in battle, Gwyn always sent the person to report to her father, as a token of her honour, truthfulness and chastity.  Her father, having no son called Gwyn and believing Guinevere safely in a convent, was very confused about this and probably suspected an illegitimate child behind the events.

The inevitable happened and Gwyn was injured badly in battle.  What frightened her most was the near discovery of her gender before her plan was far advanced.  Her companions already suspected she was homosexual, how long before they realised that the hunk of hair and dress she carried in her saddlebags were actually hers and not her ladylove's, as she claimed?

Guinevere decided she needed time to think through her plans more rationally.  She decided to retreat to the forest to give herself time for this.  Coincidentally, Lillane was heading that way herself and Gwyn became part of the protective entourage pressed upon her for the journey.  The two had time to converse and Lillane discovered that whatever Gwyn lacked in fighting prowess was made up for in a lively, learned and creative mind.  She may even have guessed Gwyn's secret.

The entourage was attacked by one of Lillane's enemies.  Gwyn, not realizing that Lillane was quite capable of looking after herself, was determined to protect her at all costs.  She deliberately took a sword blow that was aimed at Lillane and compounded the wound by continuing to fight on, when the most sensible thing to do was collapse in a bloody heap.

When the battle was over and Guinevere did collapse in a bloody heap, she was dying.  Lillane, impressed by her actions, offered her the option of a second life.  Guinevere took it.  Over the following years, Lillane and Guinevere became friends.  Guinevere became Lillane's champion and with her enhanced powers, was able to become a much more effective knight.  After a delay, the defeated knights became much more common at her father's castle and the mystery of Sir Gwyn deepened.

Despite Lillane's knowledge of her true self and her new status, Guinevere was still determined to clear her name.  She still loved her betrothed and was hurt by his betrayal.  She dreamed of the truth being finally accepted by him, her father and brothers and life returning to what it should have been all along.  How that was going to be possible given her new state, was another matter.

I can't remember names and locations.  I think Guinevere was from a northern state, though given her name, it probably should have been Welsh.  She claims to be the knight of an isolated, aged lord with a large number of knights, also from the north (or Wales), someone who is unlikely to ever come into contact with her or question her claim.  She is nominally Christian, but not religious apart from the accepted social patterns.

Guinevere has blond hair and blue eyes.  As a woman she is beautiful (though the loss of her long, flowing hair decreases that).  As a man she looks younger than she is and is fastidious about her appearance, especially in trimming the hair of the non-existent beard.  She is careful never to be seen remotely unclothed.

She is intensely loyal, honest, and takes her responsibilities seriously.  Claiming to be a knight is the worst thing she has ever done.  She is very grateful to Lillane for giving her the abilities she has.  She would never willingly break a commitment to her.  While she is fast becoming a competent knight, she actually deplores the fact that to be considered honourable, you have to be tough, rather than intelligent.  Still, when in Rome…

## Owain

Owain also loved Guineviere. He wasn’t given the option of believing her story or not. By the time he arrived on the scene, she had already been packed off to a convent. He went there to try to talk to her, but was sent word that she wished to see no one. Guineviere’s maid was posing as her. It was imperative to Guineviere’s plans that it was thought she was in the convent. The maid was happy to comply with her mistress’ wishes. In addition to her loyalty to Guineviere, as a supposedly high born nun she had certain privileges that the maid had only dreamed of until then.

Owain decided that the best next step was to lay siege to the enemy’s castle and to challenge him to single combat. He proceeded to beat the enemy into tiny pieces for the shame he had brought Owain’s beloved. He then went back to the convent, where the false Guineviere still refused to speak to him. Owain swore to never marry until he could re-win her love and went of to fight the Saxons (or whomever it was they were fighting at the time, preferably well away from where Sir Gwyn was doing her thing.)

After many years he returned to his lands and was asked by Guineviere’s father to look into the matter of the mysterious Sir Gwyn. Owain found her and travelled with her for a time, adding the cliché of feeling attracted to a supposed man. Gwyn refused to answer any questions about her parentage, replying only that when the time was right, she would present herself to her father for him to acknowledge her or not, as he chose.

How the denouement happens, I’m not sure. Suggestions welcomed (for playing though later!) Should Owain see her bathing? Just she just admit it to him after he is made a ghoul? I’m not sure. Either way, she doesn’t tell him until after the ghoul-making business. In the fullness of time, he is mortally wounded. Lillane is far away, so the only option is to make Owain a ghoul. If he hasn’t recognized her before, he does when he finds her weeping over his battered body. There is no time to explain to him what she is and what she wants to do to him. She merely asks him if he trusts her, then does it.

The explanations and reconciliation will come afterwards. Owain will be shocked, as he is more religious than Guineviere, but will accept the change as necessary under the circumstances. The first thing Guineviere will want to do is to take him to Lillane and present him to her. (OK, so ghoul-making is allowed. It still seems the correct thing to do.) Then Guineviere will beg leave to return to her father and live out a normal lifespan with Owain.

Back at the castle, still as Sir Gwyn, she is presented to her father as a worthy knight by Owain. As an after dinner entertainment, she tells her own story, but changed to suit a man in that position. She asks her father if he feels the knight was hard done by and should have been believed. The father replies that of course he should have been believed, especially as his mighty deeds prove his assertions. Guineviere reveals who she is and asks why the same courtesy cannot be applied to her.

In true legend fashion, the congregation are suitably amazed, the father repents of his lack of faith and Owain and Guineviere are married with much pomp and ceremony and much rejoicing. They live out a normal lifespan with their happiness only marred by a lack of offspring. They eventually fake their own deaths (lovers dying together is essential for the heroic feel) and return to Lillane for the next few centuries until the Dark Ages campaign.

Both would probably have preferred that Owain had the opportunity to become a vampire as well, but the mutual support and dependence suits an Arthurian legend anyway, so they will be happy with the situation. They will both keep an eye on their respective families, but not closely.

## Circa 1212 AD ...

Gwyn is not overly quick-witted or highly intelligent.  She is thoughtful, but takes a long time to come to conclusions, and not always the right ones.  She is also firmly a product of her time (with some obvious exceptions), thinks in feudal hierarchies and is fanatically loyal to Lillane.  This means that Lillane has the right to make whatever decisions she chooses.  Part of her loyalty stems from the fact that Lillane has always been fair and just so far.  (I assume.)

Gwyn's unhappiness hasn't come overnight.  It probably had its seeds centuries ago when Rollon was embraced and has been building up since then, gaining momentum in the last few years.  It is also not a simple situation, having several threads of problems.  (She's also trying to hide it, knowing that Lillane knows.  She's trying to tell herself it doesn't matter, but it really does.)

_[[020-The-Antediluvians#Roland|Rollon]]_ - He is Gwyn's friend.  They have similar interests, temperaments and attitudes.  For this reason and the fact that he is a better fighter than she is, it was hard to feel upset that he supplanted her position in Lillane's household.  She feels guilty that she wasn't clever enough to understand the situation with Angelica or quick enough to stop Rollon making her a vampire.  She is grateful to the slimy Ghoul for saving him and also angry with Lillane for breaking her own rules.  (She's also feeling guilty at being angry Rollon wasn't killed.)  She knew the moment the sword passed over Rollon's head that he and Angelica would have no consequences for their actions.

_[[020-The-Antediluvians#Angelica|Angelica]]_ - Being a romantic, Gwyn is happy that Rollon found her.  However, Angelica is now the second-best fighter in the castle, pushing Gwyn down to third.  If Lillane had to introduce her family again, Angelica would come second in the vampires.

>**GM's Note:**  Rank amongst vampires and ghouls is determined by Sire and age alone.  As the oldest of Lillane's progeny at Sadaba Castle, Gwyn has rank over all the other vampires and ghouls present.  That Gwyn doesn't exercise her power, doesn't maintain her rank among her "siblings", and sees herself as less capable than younger vampires is a source of disappointment to Lillane.

_Hierarchy_ - Gwyn was introduced last in the vampires and Owain was introduced last in the ghouls (apart from Maria and Angelica, whose situation hadn't yet been clarified, but below [[020-The-Antediluvians#Romegio|Romegio]].)  Well, that puts them in their place, doesn't it?  The list wasn't in order of embracing, or Rollon wouldn't be at the top.  It had to be in order of usefulness.

>**GM's Note:** Out of game, I introduced the characters to Cain in the same order that their respective players were sitting around the table, from left to right. The order of introduction had nothing to do with perceived usefulness, and everything to do with making sure I didn't miss any PCs. I was quite shocked to find out later via an email that Gwyn's player had interpreted this as an in-game insult.
>
>In hindsight, Lillane's introductions would have been in order of embracing/power, i.e. Gwyn, Wolfstead, Raushan, Rollon, and Rafi, then the ghouls - Tomas, Monico, Romeigo, and Owain.  Owain last of all because he is Gwyn's ghoul, not Lillane's.

_[[Cain|Cain]]_ - Gwyn was worse than useless in this situation.  The other members of the family thought so highly of her that they didn't even include her in the discussions.  She was so observant that even though Gwyn was regularly making rounds of the castle, she didn't notice that Cain and Tomas were never together.  She didn't even notice that there was something wrong with Lillane.  She never drew a weapon; she bungled the one job she had to do and overall might as well not have been there.  No wonder Lillane needs to bring in other people to help her.

_[[020-The-Antediluvians#Romegio|Romegio]]_ - Even he was included in the council of war.  Lillane praised him for speaking up for Rollon.  (As Rollon's friend, Gwyn should have done that.)  Maybe she should start breaking the rules.  (Owain gently pointed out that he didn't want to be a vampire and neatly stopped that train of thought.)

_Usefulness_ - This is the major key to Gwyn's problem.  Lillane now has better fighters than her.  She's got Rafi to organise the soldiers.  Gwyn is a knight.  She is not smart enough to learn magic.  She doesn't have the social inclination to be a trader.  She doesn't have the people skills to be a diplomat.  She's not learned enough to be a scholar.  She's a knight.  Over the time that this has been brewing, Gwyn has become less innovative, more traditional; more tied to routine and is now unable to see the woods for the trees.

_[[020-The-Antediluvians#Owain and Juanita|Owain]]_ - He is in a dilemma.  He knows exactly how Gwyn feels because he's never had a place in Lillane's castle.  Monico already takes care of any skills he has.  But while he's content to potter around, he knows Gwyn needs to feel needed.  When Gwyn comes to the point of deciding to leave, Owain will feel it's probably the best for now.  It has gotten to the point that she'll put the worst slant on anything else that happens.  The trouble is that if Gwyn goes under these circumstances, she won't come back.  (And in another few centuries, Cain could be clever enough to roll up with another one of Lillane's discards...)

>**GM's Note:**  Cain won't blood bond Gwyn while she's suffering such a debilitating lack of confidence.  Cain values aggressive, bloodthirsty arrogance and a violent disposition. Rollon would be Cain's first choice.

## Leaving

>**GM's Note:** What follows, including the Nosferatu and the timeline, is a player write-up of events that didn't happen in-game.
>
>I've learned through hard experience not to let any single player have a disproportionate influence over my campaign. I decide whether to accept **as canon** any player write-ups that 1) alter, redact, or add to in-game events, 2) use my NPCs out of character, or 3) drive my campaign in a direction that has major consequences for my setting.
>
>My earth-based campaigns share a single timeline. What happened in the past influences what happens in the future. In-game events in [[030-Arthurian-Britain|Arthurian Britain]] and the [[040-Dark-Ages|Dark Ages]] impact my [[050-Modern|Modern]] campaign. Long-lived NPCs and, for better or worse, PCs, appear in more than one of my earth-based campaigns.
>
>If my Lelia had declared war on the Kindred in **1212 AD**, I would have no reason to run a modern vampire campaign. Of the original White Wolf Dark Ages [vampire clans](https://whitewolf.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ages:_Vampire#Clans), only Clan Bahari, posing as Ventrue, would remain. While I agree roleplaying games are a collaborative effort, I do not accept **as canon** any out-of-game writing that imposes such major consequences on my settings.
>
>My favourite grognards explain this further:
> - [Who Has Narrative Control Over the Game Mechanics and Setting in Your Campaign?](https://youtu.be/9FW-qg17QSI?si=-y8sNdD1EdnKQUY_)
> - [“My Backstory Says…” — The Fastest Way to Break a Campaign](https://youtu.be/TxoEcfjCQTg?si=B7-6i2ZNjSh-u-9R)
> - [Setting agency in D&D](https://youtu.be/txfPuo6dw3Q?si=YaD7SXF75kmbeLfG)
> - [The When, Where, How, and What of Player Agency: Or How I Learned to Loathe Sidequests](https://youtu.be/2C9UiQ9aAig?si=nDnfPz7Bvy0aKrHU)
>
>There is a lesson for GMs here; one I didn't learn until it was too late to save my most loved campaigns. As a GM, agency over my setting and my narrative is mine alone, not my players'.
>Take ownership of your setting and your NPCs. Learn to say **no** to players; otherwise, you'll be running their campaign, not yours, and you risk them [[The-Dark-Circle|scuttling player agency]] for the rest of your table.

As she was preparing to leave for Lisbon, Gwyn asked to speak to Lillane.  Gwyn's manner was formal, almost remote.  "Milady, I wish to clarify my mission to Lisbon."

"You are to deliver some documents to the Prince of Lisbon.  I'm sending you because the documents are important and the Prince is treacherous, as are all Ventrue.  He doesn't want to receive these words.  I know you will carry out my instructions ... to the letter."

"Milady, there are murmurs amongst your family that I am not clever enough to realise that I am not meant to be coming back.  Am I to understand that you are releasing me from my oath of fealty?"

"Yes.  Once you have completed this task, you are released from your oath.  There is nothing more I can teach you."

"I see."  Gwyn bowed formally.  "Milady."

Some time afterwards, Lucio came to find Lillane.

"Why is Gwyn clearing out her room?"

"I'm sending her to Lisbon."

"Why is she packing as though she's not coming back?  She's distributing a lot of her things to the staff."

"I have released her from her oath of fealty.  Gwyn is the oldest of my Progeny here at Sadaba, but she has let younger and weaker ones take precedence over her.  I have two choices.  I can slay her out of hand for the disappointment she has caused me, or I can release her from her oath.  Gwyn has been my loyal servant and companion for over half a millennia.  I've chosen to release her.  Perhaps, on her own, she will find her strength, her power, and honour the Blood I have given her."

"Lillane, Gehenna is coming for Cain's Kindred.  Clan Bahari will need all the warriors we have if we are going to carry out Lelia's orders."

"Gwyn will bring Gehenna to the Kindred when instructed, as will all of Lelia's Progeny.  Its in our Blood.  None of us have any choice in that.  Perhaps this will be Gwyn's opportunity to learn what it means to be a Bahari, to be the strongest, the fastest, the deadliest.  We are the sword at Cain's throat.  He may strut and preen and threaten, but in the end, he will reap what he has sown."

Gwyn took a last look around the room that had been hers and Owain's for so long.  It didn't look the same. What they could comfortably carry was in saddlebags on the horses.  Everything else had been given away to the servants or to the farming families she knew in the valley.  All that was left was the heavier furniture and no doubt that would soon be reassigned.

Sensing her mood, Owain put his arms around her and held her close to him.  After so long together, he always knew what to do.  She felt like crying, but that was impossible.  She had been with Lillane longer.

"I was faithful.  Whatever the situation, I never complained.  When she replaced me, time after time, I never said a word of rebuke.  Even though I failed her with Cain, I didn't think I deserved this."  That was why Gwyn was in her best armour, polished till it shone.  She would ride out like a knight, not slink away like a common criminal.

"Shh.  We are together.  No situation could possibly be as bad as if we weren't."

Gwyn knew Owain wasn't sad to be going.  He felt for her, but was excited at a new chance.  They had so many options, they hadn't decided on just one yet.  Jerusalem and the Crusades beckoned.  There were several countries that were begging for competent rulers.  Gwyn had even mentioned a monastery for a time, though that had special challenges for them.  They would fulfil Lillane's commission in Lisbon and then see which way the wind blew truest.

He was right though.  They were together.  It would be all right.

Gwyn took Owain's hand and without a backward glance, they made their way to Isabella and the horses.

## No. 1. Nosferatu, Lisbon

Gwyn had been subdued from the time we had left Sadaba.  Lillane's dismissal of her had hit hard.  She had been overly sensitive about her perceived failure during Cain's visit, but still felt that Lillane had been unreasonable in sending her away.  My Gwyn is a creature of habit and behaves as though we still live in the feudal age.

An event on the way had left Gwyn even more introspective.  What exactly had happened she refused to discuss, but she claimed to have had a vision of Lelia, Lillane's Sire.  None of us had ever met Lelia, but much as Gwyn would have liked to deny all responsibility to the clan, her ties to it are more than to just Lillane.  What Lelia had revealed to Gwyn I had no idea, but I was concerned about her mood.  She would not discuss her plans with me, and that was not like her.

After completing Lillane's final task, Gwyn had decided to deliver two gifts to the Head of the Nosferatu clan.  We had met with him before, so knew the way and the clan retainers led us readily enough to his presence.  They observed the general courtesies and then Gwyn presented him with her first gift.  Appropriate words of thanks were spoken, which Gwyn accepted graciously.

Next Gwyn gave the Head a plain but serviceable dagger, presented hilt first.  This time he looked confused.  "And this?" he asked, taking it.

"A gift from my grandsire," replied Gwyn calmly.  "A declaration of war."

She noted the change in his expression from confusion to alarm, and in the split-second after he had grasped the dagger more firmly to attack her with it, she thrust his hand into his own chest.  Another, longer dagger took off his head a moment later.  Then it was on for one and all.

They fought hard and well, but were no match for a determined Bahari.  I guarded the door so that none escaped.  Even a Bahari's ghoul is a match for one as they.  We were injured, but nothing serious.  Gwyn licked at a spray of blood on her fist, but spat it out.

"Their blood tastes rancid," she complained.

"What did you expect?  They are Nosferatu.  What now?"

"We take the rest of them out.  All of them.  Every clan.  Everywhere."

She didn't sound like my Gwyn at all, just hard and tired.  This then was the nature of Gwyn's vision from Lelia.  I assumed hers was not the only one.  I thought for a moment.  It was unexpected, but it made sense.  The way the clans were growing in power and influence, humans had little future.

"I will not come with you."

That got her attention.  She stopped cleaning her dagger on a rag garment and looked at me in astonishment.  We had been together for over 700 years and the days we had spent apart could be counted on two hands.

"Owain?"  Her question sounded plaintive and with an undercurrent that gave me hope.

"I will not watch you kill yourself.  I know leaving Lillane is difficult for you, but openly taking on Cain's entire descendants armed only with a sword is suicide."

She did not even try to protest, just blinked at me.  I doubt she'd even thought through the implications of what she was attempting.  I chose my words carefully.  "If you want to successfully carry out your duties, you need subtlety, craft, political intuition, knowledge of your enemies' disposition...  Even an army would be a better option than the path you have chosen."  I could almost see the thoughts chasing each other around in her mind.

We were interrupted by a ghoul servant bringing some papers.  She took one look at the carnage in the room and fled.  Gwyn strode after her, picking her up and turning her back to the room.

"Look well.  Then go and warn your fellow rats.  We Ventrue will no longer tolerate the Nosferatu crawling beneath our magnificent cities.  Paris, London, Prague, it matters not.  Leave your sewers and scurry to the wilderness where you belong or we will burn you out of your nests like the vermin you are."

The girl took one look at her tormenter's face and ran.  Gwyn turned back to me with the beginnings of a smile I had not seen for a long time and I knew she was back.

"What now?" I asked.

Gwyn smiled in earnest.  "Now we need a bath, new clothes and food.  Then I think I know where we can find your army."
