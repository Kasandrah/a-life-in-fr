From Jenny,

I’ve spent some time recently thinking about what it is that I enjoy about our sessions and some of the areas that frustrate me.

In general, If you’re feeling burnt out and need a break from GMing, then I am happy to take over for a while. I’d still like to run Tharamalor and can always find plenty of CoC or Star Wars to run. Even a bit of simple Kobold-bashing might be therapeutic. However, if it comes to the crunch, then I will not be held responsible for gaming winding down. I will simply bow out of the picture and leave you guys to it. I especially do not want gaming (much as I enjoy it) to interfere with a friendship that I value. 

I do enjoy Friday nights. It’s my break from reality and a social time where I can be creative. I enjoy developing characters and working on ‘what ifs’. These benefits far outweigh the occasional frustrations. 

I do not enjoy having the lowest character in the pecking order in every single campaign. (Except for CoC. I think I’ve got the second lowest in the chain of command, but I could be wrong there.) I realise that having been away for 3 years made things difficult, but I’ve been back that long and have to stop being the new kid on the block sometime.

I have wondered if it’s having female characters in almost exclusively male-dominated campaigns. I’ve also wondered if I’m being unreasonable and am allowing real-world disappointments to interfere with my gaming. I’ll have to think that one through a little longer.

It has also occurred to me that you may be sick of non-Friday night additions. I know you’ve done one-on-one stuff with the guys when I couldn’t come and thought this was a way of getting in my one-on-one stuff without taking too much time from the group. I can always do this stuff on Friday nights if you’d prefer. In the meantime, I decided to rethink the Niani-Brennos situation and simplify it. The results are attached as well. 

With regard to Star Wars, Simon is right, it’s really about the Jedi. (Well, the books and movies are about the Alliance; our campaign revolves around the Jedi.) I’ll drop Hadra to secondary character and pick up Sari as the primary character. By ignoring skill increase limits, I can make her roughly equivalent to the others in terms of Force skills and if the guys will be spending points on lightsaber I can catch up on other skills as well. As this is obviously an issue, I suggest you discuss a new Force-sensitive character with Michael.

However, no matter what the outcome of her test, Sari will not leave Leith for any length of time. She’s done that once, she won’t do it again. She doesn’t believe the Jedi’s plans will collapse without her. She’s not that indispensable.

If there’s something I’m consistently doing to achieve this, then please let me know! Next time this happens, I’ve decided that I’ll either simply not play that campaign or someone else can make the supreme sacrifice!

Whatever the results, I’ve decided that life is too short to obsess over gaming!

Jenny.
