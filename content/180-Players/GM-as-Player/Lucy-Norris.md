# Captain Lucy Norris

Lucy started the campaign in the Australian Army Medical Corps.

## Character Sheet

**_[[gm_lucy_norris.pdf|Captain Lucy Norris]]_, D&D 5E, [D&D Beyond](https://www.dndbeyond.com/characters/65716389/LzY7aT)**

## Campaign Background

**Somewhere in Time**

### 2018

The characters are members of the international forces based in Kandahar, Afghanistan.

Character Concept

Can be from any of the nations in the alliance (USA, Great Britain, Australia, Norway, India, etc.) and part of the branches of the armed forces stationed there, e.g. army, marine, air force, etc. Also determine the department of the force, e.g. special forces, medical, armoured, recon, etc.

Character Generation

27 point buy human

Class Prerequisites

1 level fighter and 1 level of either fighter, monk, or rogue   
STR, DEX, CON, WIS, INT minimum 11

Lucy and Wit have been in the country for 5 months and have a solid record in their respective services. Kandahar Airport is its usual bustling hive of activity, with strike, supply, and intelligence flights leaving at a pace most western airports would be envious of.

To save a 10-minute, extra walk from the mess hall back to their billets they usually cut through a quiet maintenance section of the airport and today is no different except today instead of the usual selection of choppers and ground attack aircraft there is a C130 Heracles taking up a significant amount of area. If that didn’t raise questions, the lack of registration on the plane and the 3 Blackwater private security personnel certainly did (show covert ops isn’t subtle).

“Nothing to see here” is the look you give each other so you start to move past the plane as if it wasn’t there. All is looking great until it hits the fan; 3 Afghani police bring up weapons over near the maintenance hangar and unload on the service crews scattered around, killing at least 5 within as many seconds. Everyone's mind starts adjusting to the Green on Blue attack when a 4-tonne truck with wheels being changed (obviously to get it into maintenance and to disguise its intent) explodes with the shockwave staggering you both even at 150m.

Running to the hangars means charging the hostiles, and AK47s versus your side arms not a great idea. Running to the airfield equals a lot of open ground again, not a healthy option, which leaves using the C130 as cover. A quick nod to each other and you start legging the 50m to the plane. One of the Blackwater goons is down with a very obvious headshot, and as you close the distance, you see the other two drop almost at the same time. SHIT, you think, a sniper team.

Two snipers plus spotter’s, suicide guy in the truck and 3 Rogue police so at the very least 8 (scratch that make that 7 bad guys) you think as you fly up the ramp at full sprint with small arms pinging on the airframe with the occasional round going through the side. 2 things go through your minds 1/ what is the response time for airport marine security and 2/ shouldn’t have had the extra pudding.

The plane is about 1/3rd filled with two air-deployed pallets and no one else on board. Looking out the Port side window you see the bad guys finishing with the immediate friendly’s in the area and turn their attention (more accurately) their weapons on the plane and the volume of fire increase as they in a rather unsporting manner produce a LMG from the back of their vehicle and attempt to put as many holes on the plane as possible.

As you watch and consider getting to the relative safety of the other side of the pallets one of the Afghanis obviously feeling like he was losing a bet with the “hole in the plane game” produces a RPG 7 and even as his companion franticly tries to stop him the armour piercing warhead streaks to the plane.

DOWN, DOWN your training kicks in and you both fall flat as the rocket impacts just behind the port wing but high on the plane so the impact is only really bad as opposed to catastrophic. There is a smoke up front but no fire and a plume of acrid white gas so you are guessing something pressurised was punctured and a weird hum has started and is rising in intensity (great, what have they damaged).

MOVE, MOVE! You push yourselves up and go forward to take cover behind the sizable pallets and see what has been hit by rocket fragments. 1st pallet looks like a standard resupply drop but the second pallet is different with the frame reinforced and heavy wood supporting that but a disconcerting pulsing light is coming from the hole in the crate and your skin is crawling in a very wrong manner plus is making you feel a little nauseous.

“Get them guys” you think as a Lav25 all 8 wheels screeching, speeds into view with its 25mm chain gun dropping the hammer on the bad guys ending any conversation in a spray of body parts and reaction forces in Humvees following closely with their 50 cals chewing up the rooftop where the suspected snipers lay with a comforting roar. You grin at each other as it looks like you will escape with your skins after all, but that changes as the pulse changes abruptly to a constant whine, almost ear-splitting in intensity. A frantic dash to the tail ramp ensures, but you guess something happened.

It feels like every fibre in your body was attacked and the screaming sound you hear, you realise, is you. As you start to regain some vision and the agony subsides to an angry snarl, you realise you are still in the plane …correction, part of the plane. You can see a stormy sky and that it's a lot colder than you remember. It looks like a surgeon’s knife cut the plane midway through the wing and again halfway down the ramp a few meters from your limbs.

No sign of the airfield, Marines, city or anything except a hillside flowing down to what looks like a pine forest. You look over to your companion, obviously in as much distress as you, but besides the pain, multiple cuts, abrasions and bruising looks basically intact ….. your foggy brain is starting to reboot like a 1997 windows edition “was that glow always surrounding them? Your brain kicks back into full speed ………
